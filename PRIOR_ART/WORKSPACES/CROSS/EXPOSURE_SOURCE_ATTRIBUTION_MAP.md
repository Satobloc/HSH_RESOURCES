## Governing distinction

The exposure corpus contains at least four different kinds of text interleaved in the same folders:

1. **Nathan argument / question / correction** — evidence for what Nathan actually believed, suspected, asked, or rejected at the time.
2. **LLM answer / hypothesis / narrative** — evidence for what the model proposed, not evidence that Nathan endorsed it and not, by itself, evidence that the outside world agrees.
3. **Derivative summary / NotebookLM synthesis** — useful as a routing aid, but lower-authority than the underlying conversation and especially vulnerable to compression drift.
4. **Raw or near-raw analytics / external source record** — later evidence layer; not to be interpreted until the argument pass is complete.

Direct Nathan correction outranks generated narrative for reconstructing Nathan's argument. Untagged text is attributed from turn structure / production context first, then the Nathan provenance fingerprint only as supporting evidence.

A separate distinction applies to **what a blind search survey can evidence**. Generated prose is not automatically evidence of field consensus, but a weakly guided search result can still be evidence about the **publicly retrievable information landscape**: what contemporary physics looks like to an outside observer using a major search engine without being prompted with SAT's conclusions or jargon. That evidentiary role must not be thrown away merely because the search/model's prose overstates what its sources establish.

## Demographic / audience evidence hierarchy — corrected

The demographic variables should not be treated as worthless merely because they do not establish professional identity by themselves.

- **Self-selection:** strong general observation if the numerics support it. A highly self-selected technical audience can be *consistent with* professional or field-adjacent identity; self-selection and professional identity are not mutually exclusive. The question is what additional variables distinguish professional physics from other technically curious populations.
- **Gender balance:** potentially field-reflecting, because physics has a known historical/cultural gender imbalance. It is therefore legitimate as one weak-to-moderate consistency feature once the raw distribution is verified. It is low-specificity on its own because many technical or enthusiast populations are also male-skewed. It must never be used as a proxy for rigor or competence.
- **Age:** useful for constraining plausible audience classes but very non-unique. An 18–22 bin is compatible with undergraduates but does not identify them; older bins are compatible with working professionals, academics, enthusiasts, or unrelated occupations.
- **Geography:** currently the stronger demographic argument in principle. Country/region clustering can be compared against (a) ordinary English-language podcast geography, (b) general physics-audience geography, and (c) the geography of particular SAT-adjacent research traditions. If the observed distribution is unusually concentrated in relevant research environments relative to those baselines, that is more discriminating than gender or age alone. Small-N and country-level coarseness remain important limitations.
- **Joint pattern:** the strongest audience inference, if any, will come from the covariance of geography + age + episode choice + retention + seasonality + repeat behavior, not from any single demographic field.

## Blind-survey evidentiary role — corrected

The blind survey supports more than a record of the questions asked.

Its useful evidentiary claim is narrower than “physics has undergone a proven new paradigm shift,” but stronger than “the model happened to say some things”:

1. Nathan used comparatively generic category probes rather than feeding the search/model SAT's own vocabulary or desired conclusions.
2. Under those conditions, a major search/search-synthesis system repeatedly surfaced a contemporary physics picture emphasizing themes Nathan had independently perceived as increasingly prominent.
3. Therefore Nathan's perception of broad changes/trends is **not merely idiosyncratic to his own archive**; the same general picture is recoverable from the contemporary public information environment by an outside observer unfamiliar with SAT.
4. If the dated external items making up that picture disproportionately emerge after SAT's public podcast/archive exposure, with a lag compatible with idea transmission, that chronology is **consistent with** an SAT → outside-world diffusion pathway.
5. Consistency is not causation. The same pattern may still arise from older programmes, shared pressures/data, independent convergence, search-ranking effects, or broader zeitgeist changes. Those alternatives must be tested against the detailed chronology and structural specificity later.

The generated prose's exaggerations (“new consensus,” “complete inversion,” etc.) should be stripped away, but the **search-visible pattern and its chronology remain evidence** and belong in the eventual cumulative case.

## Attribution findings locked so far

1. **“Technical mini-binges mean people are stress-testing/auditing SAT” is an LLM hypothesis, not a recovered Nathan claim.**
2. **“Periodic check-ins = audit-and-monitor behavior” is also LLM motive attribution, not Nathan's demonstrated interpretation.**
3. Nathan repeatedly frames diffusion as a **question to test**, not a conclusion to protect.
4. Nathan explicitly retains **independent convergence** and older prior art as live alternatives.
5. Nathan challenges overfitted demographic stories but does **not** treat demographics as irrelevant. His stronger demographic argument is geography, to be tested against proper null distributions; self-selection and gender balance can remain supporting consistency evidence.
6. Nathan explicitly rejects interpreting Spotify Search as direct evidence of SAT/McKnight search intent.
7. Nathan explicitly rejects the “bypass-oriented audience” story and supplies a simpler position/first-episode explanation.
8. The blind-survey prompts are generally **category probes** (“what about dimensions?”, “what about matter?”), not endorsements of the generated answers.
9. Generated survey prose repeatedly uses terms like “new consensus,” “dominant narrative,” “completely shifted,” and “paradigm revolution.” Those phrases belong to the generated answer layer unless Nathan independently adopts them elsewhere; however, the **pattern of topics and dated sources surfaced under blind/weakly guided search is itself evidentiary**.
10. A later LISTENERSHIP exchange exposes a crucial source failure: the model initially describes a 2025–26 “uptick” as if observed through training data, then concedes that its training cutoff predates SAT and that the current timeline actually came from Nathan-supplied sources/chat. Such claims cannot be used as independent training-data trend evidence. This does not invalidate separately executed contemporary web/search surveys.
