# Nathan Voice Packet 001 — Humor

Status: seed packet; intentionally small first bite.
Purpose: preserve representative Nathan humor mechanisms without flattening them into a generic "funny" tag.

## Core mechanism map

### 1. Mock-formal / institutional deadpan
Nathan often lets an official, scholarly, bureaucratic, or procedural register carry the joke instead of stepping outside it to signal comedy.

Anchor examples already identified in the current reading pass:
- *Save the Kildans* — charity / administrative apparatus continues operating while the underlying crisis exceeds the categories and procedures available to it.
- *A Tealeaf* — scholarly provenance, textual criticism, and researcher irritation culminate in very ordinary student complaint.

### 2. Elevated register → petty landing
A sentence, paragraph, or scene begins in a historical, sacred, scholarly, or mythic register and lands in annoyance, bodily reality, profanity, gossip, or mundane complaint without announcing the register shift.

Anchor examples:
- *A Tealeaf*: vast historical apparatus built around ancient student grumbling.
- *The Gilgamesh Golem*: mythic / sacred / historical material repeatedly collides with money, property, vanity, argument, bodily danger, and ordinary social friction.

### 3. Excessive specificity
Compression is often refused past the point where a normal writer would summarize. The accumulating exactness becomes comic pressure.

Anchor examples:
- *A Tealeaf*: chain-of-custody / archival movement carried through warehouse, exchange, liquidation, auction, transfer, reception, etc.
- *Save the Kildans*: alien physiology and applicant classification rendered through increasingly specific forms and administrative categories.

### 4. Researcher snark
The observer or researcher is allowed to become a person through restrained irritation toward the object of study.

Anchor example:
- *A Tealeaf*: "rather less than stellar" record-keeping ethos — mild scholarly contempt, professional restraint, and an astronomical pun in the same move.

### 5. Artifact comedy
The joke can live in the medium itself: ad, form, letter, scholarly note, provenance record, page furniture, or institutional boilerplate.

Anchor examples:
- *Save the Kildans*: the story substantially exists as administrative debris.
- *Floating Liars’ Club* layout: fake ads and magazine furniture can extend the joke outside the nominal story body.

### 6. Tonal collision without release valve
The comedy does not cancel seriousness. Grotesque, sacred, tragic, bureaucratic, and silly material can coexist in the same passage without a clean switch telling the reader which mode now governs.

Anchor examples:
- *The Gilgamesh Golem*
- *Eye of the Angry God*
- *Save the Kildans*

## Collection rule
When adding samples, preserve a context envelope:
- exact excerpt
- date / source / thread or publication
- immediate setup
- humor mechanism(s)
- spontaneous vs edited / published
- Nathan-direct confidence
- notes on performance dependence, if known

## Distinction to preserve
Do not treat "Nathanese" as synonymous with humor. Word mutation, clipped speech, voice-to-text play, deliberate malapropism, fake etymology, compressed grammar, and callback language may overlap with humor but deserve a separate packet or subcorpus.
