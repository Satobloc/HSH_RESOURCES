# Glass Sausage Factory — design recommendations for the rewritten voice

These recommendations are based on the attached site copy and the rewritten voice pass. They are presentation recommendations, not a factual or technical audit of the live site.

## 1. Make status a visual grammar, not repeated prose

The existing copy has to keep stopping to explain that a historical source is not current authority, an analogy is not equivalence, a rendered document is not the source, and an outside result is not evidence for SAT/H(s)H. Those distinctions are important; repeating them paragraph by paragraph makes the page sound more defensive than precise.

Build a small, consistent status system that appears beside titles and cards:

- **CURRENT** — active construction or current premise state
- **HISTORICAL** — dated source retained for provenance
- **RECONSTRUCTED** — recovered or re-derived from source material
- **AI-ASSISTED** — machine contribution is materially involved
- **AUDITED** — has passed the stated internal audit, not peer review
- **EXTERNAL** — outside literature / comparison material
- **TENTATIVE** — live hypothesis or unresolved bridge
- **SOURCE** — primary artifact rather than synopsis

Do not rely on color alone. The word itself should remain visible.

## 2. Let the top of the page answer three questions immediately

The first screen should tell a new reader:

1. **What is this?** — an open development record of a geometry-first research programme.
2. **Where do I start?** — idea-first or status-first.
3. **What is its standing?** — active, public, not peer reviewed; source and status exposed.

The Conversation Viewer is important, but the current page introduces it twice before the reader has fully acquired the project frame. Keep one strong Viewer CTA in the hero, then let the fuller Viewer explanation appear slightly lower.

## 3. Promote “Two ways in” to the primary navigation decision

This is one of the strongest pieces of the information architecture because it models the reader rather than forcing every reader through the same funnel.

Make the two entrances visually unmistakable:

- **I want the idea first**
- **I want the status/evidence first**

Each should lead to a short, bounded sequence. Avoid making either card look like the “easy” or “real” route.

## 4. Keep FIE → SAT → H(s)H persistently visible

The ancestry is doing real conceptual work. Give it a thin persistent breadcrumb or timeline rail rather than repeating it only in prose.

When a reader opens a historical object, the rail can highlight where that object sits in the lineage. This would make “historical but still relevant” legible at a glance.

## 5. Give every document card a tiny provenance footer

For example:

> **Minkowski Proper**  
> HISTORICAL · PDF · SAT era · original source available

or:

> **BEDROCK**  
> CURRENT · premise register · H(s)H repo · direct-author + tracked edits

That is more useful than another paragraph explaining the general policy.

## 6. Put “What am I looking at?” beside the reader, not above it

The Presented edition / PDF viewer / Source-rendered / Mercer distinction is good. Make it a persistent help control or small expandable legend in the Reading Room.

The reader should be able to answer “am I looking at the original?” without scrolling back to the explanation.

## 7. Use the visual-era distinction, but do not let it imply epistemic rank

The existing SAT magenta versus H(s)H gray/white distinction is strong and memorable. Keep it.

But separate **era** from **authority**. A historical SAT item may still be foundational; a new gray H(s)H diagram may still be tentative. Use one visual channel for era and another for status.

## 8. Reduce link walls by giving each section one primary action

The source map can remain exhaustive. Elsewhere, prefer:

- one primary button;
- one or two secondary text links;
- “more sources” collapsed or deferred to the Reading Room.

This will make the page feel less like a directory while preserving the directory underneath it.

## 9. Make the News section structurally skeptical by design

Each item should have a visible relation badge such as:

- THEMATIC OVERLAP
- METHOD COMPARATOR
- MECHANISM COMPARISON
- POSSIBLE DISTINCTIVE MATCH
- DIFFUSION EVIDENCE (only if actually warranted)

Then show four compact fields:

**External result · Why it is here · What it does not show · Sources**

That makes the epistemic discipline part of the interface rather than a disclaimer appended afterward.

## 10. Turn the podcast from a list into an era map

The copy already says the feed changed function over time. Let the design show that.

A horizontal or vertical era strip could move through:

**consciousness/dialogue → SAT emergence → formalization/audit → H(s)H reconstruction/current method**

Then place the selected episodes on that strip. The full 321-episode index remains a separate browse/search surface.

## 11. Use first person where the site is actually Nathan speaking

The project sections can remain documentary and source-oriented. The About section should sound like a person, not a grant biography.

The rewrite moves this section to first person. I would keep that. It makes the site’s authorship clearer and creates a useful tonal shift without contaminating technical sections with personality performance.

## 12. Typography should reflect dependency structure

The prose often contains a strong claim followed by a local limitation. Design can help preserve that relation.

Recommended pattern:

- body measure around 65–75 characters;
- generous paragraph spacing;
- short landing sentences allowed to stand alone;
- status labels small but high-contrast;
- technical provenance in a condensed or monospaced secondary face;
- avoid turning every distinction into a card or pill.

The site should feel inspectable, not gamified.

## 13. Add glossary-on-demand, not glossary interruption

For terms such as *timesheet*, *readout*, *worldtube*, *Universal Indicatrix*, or *θ₄*, a hover/tap gloss with three layers would fit the project well:

**current definition · historical note · standard-physics comparison**

The full glossary page remains the authority. The popup is wayfinding.

## 14. Keep the “glass” idea operational

A very strong design principle for the whole site is:

> **Every polished surface should have an obvious route back to the machinery beneath it.**

For a claim: source.  
For a figure: generating code or provenance.  
For a synopsis: original document.  
For a current statement: status register.  
For a historical statement: date and era.  
For an external comparison: primary paper.

That is where the name *Glass Sausage Factory* stops being branding and becomes interface logic.
