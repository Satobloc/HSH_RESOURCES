LLM REVIEWER:

Yes. I think “stale” is the right *felt symptom*, but after going through the site I’d diagnose it more precisely as **runtime staleness / live-system ambiguity**, not stale content.

The actual editorial content is extremely current: the homepage says it was updated September 23, the update log has September 23 entries, the active-work language reflects the present paper push, and the roadmap is reconciled to September 22. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][1]) What makes it feel stale is that a site presenting itself as a living research instrument repeatedly exposes machinery that appears not to be alive.

And yes, your audio suspicion is basically right. The GitHub Pages Conversation Viewer has replay controls, but its documented modes are Cruise, Message replay, and simulated Typealong replay—not listen-along/audio. ([satobloc.github.io][2])  Meanwhile the Glass site says the featured conversation has “per-turn audio,” and the September 22 update says JSON conversations gained role voices and that readable pages gained browser-native audio controls. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][1]) So the public presentation is presently promising a more unified audio experience than the actual HsH viewer supplies.

I went through the main surface plus the dedicated Reading Room, Papers, manuscript display, Updates, Podcast Index, Living Glossary, Claims Explorer, Roadmap, Origin Story, and the HsH Conversation Viewer. I could inspect the live document structures, controls, content, routes, and repository-side viewer implementation. I did **not** get a trustworthy pixel-rendering browser for every page in this environment, so I’m not going to invent opinions about a five-pixel margin or a particular hover effect I couldn't actually see. The structural/presentation issues are much larger than that anyway.

My priority order would be:

1. **Fix the distinction between “live,” “loading,” “unavailable,” and “not built yet.”** This is the biggest thing. The homepage currently exposes “Loading work feed…,” “Loading current work…,” “Loading news feed…,” “Loading headlines…,” “Loading worker submissions…,” etc. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][1]) The Updates page likewise says these things are loaded from the public repository “on each visit” and then exposes three separate `Loading…` states. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][3]) Papers says “Checking current paper status…”. The manuscript says “Loading manuscript… / Verifying source text… / Loading contents…”. The Reading Room says “Loading reader…”. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][4]) Any one of these is normal. Collectively, they make the whole site feel like a demo whose JavaScript stopped halfway through booting.

   I would impose one sitewide state machine: **LIVE · snapshot timestamp**, **CACHED · last successful refresh**, **EXPERIMENTAL**, **OFFLINE**, or **NOT YET CONNECTED**. If a fetch fails, display the most recent cached payload with “snapshot from 18:42 EDT,” not an eternal loading message. The roadmap already demonstrates exactly the intellectual discipline I want here: it explicitly says public direct Mersearch querying waits on a stable API rather than pretending the endpoint exists. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][5]) Make the entire UI behave that way.

2. **Stop making the homepage carry the entire civilization at once.** I actually like a great deal of the homepage. “Start anywhere,” the visual/read/hear/trace division, the curious-reader/scientist-reader fork, epistemic labeling, direct source routes, and the mixture of current activity with historical record are all very appropriate to this project. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][1]) I would absolutely *not* sanitize this into a tasteful five-section academic portfolio.

   But the page is currently roughly 550 crawl lines and successively becomes: front door → research dashboard → featured conversation → audience router → memoir → FIE primer → podcast catalogue → operations board → science-news comparator → methodological statement → repository guide → history → translation desk → reading-room advert → image archive → papers → conversation archive → roadmap. It is almost a complete website vertically serialized into one document. The result isn't information overload so much as **hierarchy decay**. Once I am halfway down, everything has approximately the same rhetorical weight.

   I’d make the homepage a genuinely dynamic newspaper/front desk. Give the first screen and a half the best material **today**. Then provide strong portals into the deep systems instead of partially reproducing each system below.

3. **Let the “currentness” of the site be visible at a glance.** You already have the raw ingredients. I’d make the top portion feel almost like a strange scientific newspaper:

   `THE GLASS SAUSAGE FACTORY`
   current active edge / one-line state
   **one dominant current story or object**
   a compact scrolling strip of genuinely current headlines
   2–4 smaller current cards
   then the four big entrances: SEE / READ / HEAR / TRACE.

   The current “Updated 23 September 2026” is useful, but it is document metadata. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][1]) What I want is perceptual evidence that the *organism is breathing*: “3 worker packets since yesterday,” “paper R1 posted,” “active edge: …,” “latest conversation: …,” “321 podcast episodes,” “feed refreshed 7:14 PM.” Not all of those literally; enough to make life obvious.

4. **Treat the Conversation Viewer as an instrument deserving its own serious UX pass.** Its underlying archival design is actually quite good. It preserves raw source, follows conversation branches, progressively renders long conversations, supports search, deep links, timeline position, speaker colors, previous/next navigation, cross-repository discovery and provenance annotation.  That is not a toy.

   But the public first impression is currently a very utilitarian control panel: conversation filter, Development/Live toggles, hamburger, Prev/Next/Copy link, search, hit count, slider, replay controls, Mode, Speed, View, Show internals, status—before one has even begun reading. ([satobloc.github.io][2]) That makes sense for *us*. For somebody following “Conversation Viewer” from the public site, it is closer to opening a diagnostic console.

   I’d split it conceptually into **Reading** and **Instrument** modes. Reading mode: title, date/span, speakers, clean conversation, search, progress, previous/next, source, and—when actually operational—listen. Instrument mode reveals corpus selection, internals, typealong, provenance controls, dense timeline machinery, branches, etc. Nothing is lost; the complexity simply appears when it becomes useful.

5. **Then make listen-along genuinely first-class—or remove it from the promise until it is.** I would not just bolt speech buttons onto every bubble. The right version is a continuous reader: play/pause, previous/next turn, current speaker, speed, voice mapping, “skip internals,” and a persistent little transport that survives scrolling. Each turn can have its own play affordance, but the principal metaphor should be **listen to the conversation**, not 800 unrelated TTS buttons. The current archive's distinction among user/assistant/system/tool/developer makes intelligent skipping especially important. ([satobloc.github.io][2])

   Until that works end-to-end, I would change the Glass copy from “per-turn audio” to something truthful like “audio-enabled reading views are being integrated,” or hide that bit entirely. A missing feature is fine. A visible button or promise that appears broken makes the *whole product* seem older than it is.

6. **Keep the specialized pages. They are much better than making the homepage do their jobs.** The Podcast Index is one of the cleanest conceptual pages: it establishes scope, counts, eras, search/filter controls, provenance rules and what remains open in only 62 crawl lines. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][6]) The Claims Explorer is also particularly strong: each object has status, differentiator, current check state, dependencies and source routes. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][7]) The Living Glossary has an excellent information architecture—current control / active scaffold / historical translation / recovery target, plus “do not collapse into” and conventional comparison. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][8]) Those are models for the rest of the site.

   In other words: **trust your subpages more**. You don't need the homepage to prove that all this richness exists by reproducing huge chunks of it.

7. **Rationalize the metaphor vocabulary without killing it.** “Glass Sausage Factory,” “factory floor,” “research desk,” “Reading Room,” “paper desk,” “Index Desk,” “public notebook,” “active edge,” “signalbox,” “roadmap,” etc. are individually good. Collectively there are enough architectural metaphors that navigation can become a little Borges library. I would keep the names—they're part of the place—but routinely pair them with tiny functional descriptors:

   **Reading Room · documents & archive**
   **Index Desk · find material**
   **Factory Floor · current work**
   **Signalbox · active priorities**
   **Papers · manuscripts & review**.

   That gives newcomers semantic handrails without converting everything to “Resources / Blog / About.”

8. **The Origin Story should remain enormous, but should become more navigable rather than shorter.** It is 2,700+ crawl lines. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][9]) I don't think the answer is “edit Nathan down to 900 words.” It is a long-form piece; let it be one. But an unobtrusive progress rail, section landmarks, pull-quotes/images, “continue from last place,” and perhaps a contents overlay would make its scale pleasurable instead of merely large. It should feel like a feature essay, not a single HTML text extrusion.

9. **Bring the HsH viewer into the visual family without making it identical.** The Glass site should feel like the magazine/public lobby; the viewer should feel like the archive instrument behind a door in that lobby. At present they read as two separate products. Even just a common masthead treatment, typography variables, source/status badge vocabulary, and a clear “← Glass Sausage Factory” route would make the handoff feel intentional. Conversely, preserving some colder “instrument panel” quality in the viewer would be good. Don't skin it into a magazine article.

The strongest overall impression I got is that **the editorial thinking is ahead of the runtime integration**. This is actually a much better problem than having a polished shell with nothing to say. The Claims page, Glossary, Podcast Index, epistemic labeling, source discipline, historical/current distinctions, and the weird-but-coherent voice are already unusually substantial. ([The Glass Sausage Factory][6])

What is hurting it is that the site currently says, in effect, *“watch the machine work”*—and then several gauges read **LOADING…**

That is why it feels stale even though it was substantially updated **today**.

If I were taking the next design pass, I would therefore **not** start by making things prettier. I would first kill every ambiguous loading state, get the current-work data path robust, reconcile the claimed audio/listen feature with the actual Conversation Viewer, and turn the homepage into a tighter live front page that delegates depth to the genuinely good specialist pages. After that, I’d do the richer visual/ergonomic pass—more asymmetrical modular layout, deliberate scrollers, variable story prominence, long-form reading affordances, and stronger continuity between Glass and the archive instrument.

That would, I think, change the perceived state from **“very ambitious site that may have partly stopped working”** to **“alarmingly large living thing whose unfinished parts are intentionally labeled.”**
