**02 / Generative kernel**

# Fundamental Intuitions, extended

The optimism here is specific, not devotional.

The foundational intuitions were set up as import-and-constraint rules: preserve the physics that already works; translate it into a deliberately small geometric vocabulary; expose the places where the translation fails or becomes ambiguous; and then let repeated pressure from different domains decide what survives.

That does not guarantee that the theory is right. It gives the programme something more useful than a guarantee: a way to generate structure that can be compared, broken, repaired, or rejected.

### World history

A particle-like object is modeled as an extended four-dimensional history, not merely as a succession of isolated local states.

### Readout

What appears locally is treated as a resolution or intersection of the larger geometric object.

### Recursion

Coiling, curvature, torsion, bundling, and braiding recur across scale and complexity.

### Inheritance

Established physical success is something the model must recover or explain. It is not permission to overwrite a disagreement and call the translation complete.

**05 / Translation desk**

# Translation without smuggling equivalence

SAT developed a compact native vocabulary. Compact is useful internally; outside the archive, it can become opaque very quickly.

The translation desk places SAT terms beside standard-physics language without quietly declaring the two to be the same thing. A serious bridge should say what the source object is, what function is being compared, what is preserved, what is lost, what H(s)H counterpart is proposed, and whether the claimed relation is exact, approximate, analogous, or still unresolved.

Same word does not establish the same object. Similar mathematics does not, by itself, establish the same physics.

# Results, constraints, and curious touchpoints

Resemblance is cheap. Diffusion is expensive.

This section tracks developments in physics that touch problems, mechanisms, or mathematical habits also present in SAT/H(s)H. The useful question is not “does this sound familiar?” but *what kind of relation is actually present?*

### 1 · Establish the external result

What was measured, proved, or proposed? With what uncertainty, population, assumptions, or model dependence?

### 2 · Type the overlap

Shared topic? Shared mathematics? A genuinely similar mechanism? A distinctive prediction? Those are increasingly strong—and importantly different—relations.

### 3 · Test chronology and contact

Public availability creates a possible channel. It does not establish uptake. A diffusion claim needs more.

# About Nathan

I’m Nathan Bellomy-McKnight: an independent researcher, writer, artist, podcaster, and longtime informal science communicator with a background in geological sciences and paleontology.

The method running through this project is basically experimentalist and cartographic. Start with observations and theories that already work. Speculate widely enough to find something new. Then cross-check, revise, and use the geometry as a map of where to look—not as a substitute for finding out what is actually there.

### Science in public

Years in informal science education, including COSI and the American Museum of Natural History, left me with a strong preference for explanations that can be inspected, pictured, challenged, taken apart, and rebuilt.

### Other work

The larger creative record includes fiction, visual art, consciousness and epistemology experiments, historical cookery, public conversations with AI, and the autobiographical *Great Moments in Personal Scientific History*.
