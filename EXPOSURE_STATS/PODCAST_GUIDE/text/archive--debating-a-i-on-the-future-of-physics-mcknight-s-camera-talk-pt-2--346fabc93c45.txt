Mcknight's work is this massive neon sign reminding us that
perfection is frequently the enemy of the interesting.
A perfectly crisp, mathematically flawless
digitally autofocus photo of your lunch is forgotten in a
second. But a blurry, lopsided sepia
tone photo of a raccoon taken through a scratched garage lens
attached to a cookie tin with a Hot Topic belt?
That is a triumph of human stubbornness.
And I want to leave you with a challenge.
Today. In our modern lives, we've
surrendered the how of almost everything to corporations.
We treat our technology like a magic box.
We tap the smooth glass. Magic happens, and when it
breaks, we throw our hands up in helplessness.
So consider this What black box in your life, whether it's your
smartphone, your car engine, your coffee maker, or even your
own creative process, could benefit from being broken open?
What would happen if you stopped relying on smooth precision
tools to guarantee a perfect result and instead allowed
yourself to fail messily in the pursuit of genuine
understanding? What would you build if you
gathered the metaphorical cookie tins and silver napkin rings
lying discarded in your life and just negotiated with the physics
of the universe? The next time you see a piece of
junk, remember, with enough curiosity, A willingness to
fail, and maybe a little bit of bathtub chemistry, it might just
be a mechanism waiting to capture the world in a way no
one else has ever seen.
