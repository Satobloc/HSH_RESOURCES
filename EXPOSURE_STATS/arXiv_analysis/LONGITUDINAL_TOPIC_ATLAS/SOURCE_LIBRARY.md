# Source Library — arXiv taxonomy, moderation, classification, and scientometrics

This source list supports the **Longitudinal arXiv Topic Atlas**. It is external-literature infrastructure only; it does not define SAT/H(s)H or classify SAT-like content.

## Official arXiv institutional sources

### arXiv Roadmap 2014

**Citation-ready note:** arXiv. *2014 arXiv Roadmap*. Cornell University Library, 2014.

**URL:** https://info.arxiv.org/about/reports/2014_roadmap.pdf

**Why retained:** Documents work with the Physics Advisory Committee on public subject-category descriptions, explicitly noting that category scope/boundaries needed clearer definition; also records moderation/governance work. Useful for reconstructing institutional intent behind taxonomy and category boundaries.

**Evidence type:** official institutional / taxonomy-definition / moderation / governance

---

### arXiv Roadmap 2018

**Citation-ready note:** arXiv. *2018 arXiv Roadmap*. Cornell University Library, 2018.

**URL:** https://info.arxiv.org/about/reports/2018_roadmap.pdf

**Why retained:** Records author-notification work for paper reclassification and notes that policy questions had been resolved. Useful for the history of category assignment, moderation, and reclassification practice.

**Evidence type:** official institutional / reclassification / moderation-policy

---

### arXiv Annual Report 2020

**Citation-ready note:** arXiv. *arXiv Annual Report 2020*. Cornell University Library, 2020.

**URL:** https://info.arxiv.org/about/reports/2020_arXiv_annual_report.pdf

**Why retained:** Describes the user-facing automated classifier introduced during submission, including alternate-category recommendations and moderator commentary that classification improved. Important for understanding how category assignment became partially classifier-mediated.

**Evidence type:** official institutional / automated classification / moderation workflow

## Independent bibliometrics / classification research

### Leydesdorff and Bornmann — operationalizing fields through subject categories

Loet Leydesdorff and Lutz Bornmann. “The Operationalization of ‘Fields’ as WoS Subject Categories (WCs) in Evaluative Bibliometrics: The Cases of ‘Library and Information Science’ and ‘Science & Technology Studies.’” arXiv:1407.7849, 2014.

**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7849

**Why retained:** Directly addresses the danger of treating administratively evolved subject categories as analytically clean scientific fields; discusses indexer effects and classification limitations. Not arXiv-specific taxonomy history, but methodologically central to longitudinal category analysis.

**Evidence type:** scientometrics / field-classification validity / indexer effects

---

### Kandimalla et al. — large-scale subject classification from abstracts

Bharath Kandimalla, Shaurya Rohatgi, Jian Wu, and C. Lee Giles. “Large Scale Subject Category Classification of Scholarly Papers with Deep Attentive Neural Networks.” arXiv:2007.13826, 2020.

**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13826

**Why retained:** Uses millions of abstracts for subject-category classification and explicitly addresses overlapping and imbalanced categories. Useful methodological prior art for content-derived topic reconstruction independent of administrative labels.

**Evidence type:** automated subject classification / abstract-based classification / overlapping categories

---

### Haunschild et al. — algorithmic versus intellectual field classification

Robin Haunschild, Hermann Schier, Werner Marx, and Lutz Bornmann. “Algorithmically Generated Subject Categories Based on Citation Relations: An Empirical Micro Study Using Papers on Overall Water Splitting.” arXiv:1709.02955, 2017.

**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.02955

**Why retained:** Compares algorithmic paper-level clustering with intellectual and journal-based classification, and questions discriminatory reliability in a concrete topical case. Useful for validating any future clustering/topic-modeling layer rather than treating generated clusters as ground truth.

**Evidence type:** bibliometrics / algorithmic classification / validation

---

### Scientometric engineering using arXiv eprints

“Scientometric Engineering: Exploring Citation Dynamics via arXiv Eprints.” *Quantitative Science Studies* (MIT Press), 2022.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1162/qss_a_00174

**Why retained:** Large-scale scientometric work using arXiv metadata; includes an explicit aggregation of arXiv subject categories into broader discipline families. Useful as an external comparison for constructing a stable longitudinal category crosswalk.

**Evidence type:** arXiv scientometrics / discipline aggregation / longitudinal metadata analysis

## Library-use rules

1. Official arXiv documents are primary sources for arXiv’s own policies, category descriptions, moderation, and classification systems.
2. Scientometric papers are secondary methodological sources; they do not override arXiv’s own institutional record.
3. Native historical categories must always be preserved alongside any reconstructed/stable taxonomy.
4. Content-derived clusters and machine classifications are analysis aids, not field ontology.
5. Any claim of category creation, split, merge, alias, or scope change should point to an official source where one can be located.
6. Any “first appearance” or “reappearance” result must be scoped to the corpus/search procedure and must not be promoted into absolute historical priority without broader literature review.
