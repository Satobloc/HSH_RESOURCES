## 2. Working reconstruction of the user's model

**Core proposal:** a physically continuous, branching, four-dimensional biological structure is viewed through ordinary three-dimensional sections. What appear in a 3D slice as separate people/organisms are, in the proposal, spatially and temporally extended appendages or regions of a larger histological body.

**Histological architecture:** a neural/brain core is surrounded by layered tissue sheaths. The user's first colour key distinguishes neural, bone, muscle and skin. These are not merely decorative labels: the user says the layers are simplified sheaths that also have structural and functional roles. The coaxial-cable image is an architectural analogy for this tissue arrangement, not yet a formal anatomical specification.

**Temporal/branching relation:** the user's family-tree drawing is not, at this stage, a genetic inheritance model. It is a proposed physical connection through time: people on the tree are interpreted as branches/extensions of one 4D organism, with junctions simplified in the drawing. Do not collapse this into ordinary genealogical relatedness; the claimed physical continuity is the distinctive 4D hypothesis and remains to be specified.

**Distal geometry and coordinated action:** the user suggests that analogues of symmetrical appendages may be separated across the 4D organism and need not be adjacent in the 3D section. The possibility of coordinated action at the 4-space level is a conjectural consequence within the model. Keep it separate from established neurophysiology, social coordination, or measured nonlocal causation.

**Scale transition:** a later diagram sequence extends the same visual grammar downward into cell/nucleus/chromosomal and nucleotide organization. It should be treated as a proposed cross-scale representational scheme, not as evidence that cellular genetics and the larger histological 4D structure share a demonstrated physical mechanism.

## 4. Conventional biology boundary: chromosome representation

The user's blue-chromosome/green-nucleotide distinction is a diagrammatic ontology: the blue form is a visual/organizational aid, not a separate material substance. Preserve that interpretation when describing the user's drawing.

For scientific comparison, however, do not repeat “nucleotide sequence is the only substrate” as a conventional molecular-biology fact. Standard eukaryotic chromosome descriptions treat each chromosome as a long DNA molecule associated with proteins; chromatin includes histones and non-histone proteins, which package and regulate access to DNA. A **gene** is a functional nucleotide sequence/region, not synonymous with the whole nucleotide complement of a chromosome. Useful standard references:

- Alberts et al., *Molecular Biology of the Cell*, “Chromosomal DNA and Its Packaging in the Chromatin Fiber”: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26834/
- Alberts et al., *Molecular Biology of the Cell*, Chapter 4 “DNA and Chromosomes”: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK21074/

The appropriate distinction is therefore: **the chromosome is not a second, non-nucleotide chemical substance**, but real chromosomes are DNA–protein structures, not merely an abstract connected nucleotide string. This conventional biology note does not rewrite the user's visual convention; it prevents the convention from being mistaken for a complete cytological description.
