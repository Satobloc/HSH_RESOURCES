# Research questions and evidence-acquisition plan

**Scope:** physical/spatial higher-dimensional thought in physics and adjacent sciences, c. 1870–1945; the Zöllner–Slade affair; the public and professional association of higher space with spiritualism; and possible effects on academic careers, publication, institutional affiliation, and research choices.

**Operating rule:** no inference of suppression, causation, or professional penalty from chronology or search-result frequency alone. Preserve the distinction between explicit contemporaneous testimony, independently corroborated outcomes, temporal association, and later interpretation.

## A. What was actually being investigated?

1. Which named researchers proposed or used an extra spatial dimension as a physical hypothesis, rather than as a mathematical coordinate, spacetime coordinate, analogy, or spiritualist setting?
2. What physical problems did each model address: ether, electrical current, magnetism, gravitation, atomic structure, mechanics, perception, or something else?
3. Which proposals were published in mainstream scientific journals, proceedings, books, lectures, correspondence, or specialist periodicals?
4. Did any shared vocabulary, model lineage, or professional network connect these researchers, or were the projects mostly independent?
5. Which proposals were criticised on physical or mathematical grounds independently of spiritualism?

## B. What did the Zöllner–Slade controversy do?

1. What exactly did Zöllner claim to observe, under what experimental conditions, and what did each witness say at the time?
2. How did Zöllner describe Weber, Scheibner, Fechner, Wundt, and other participants in print? How did each later qualify or dispute that description?
3. What was the full sequence of Wundt's public interventions, Ulrici's replies, Fechner's private objections, and later retrospective accounts?
4. Which contemporary newspapers and scientific journals repeated, ridiculed, defended, or reframed the fourth-dimensional interpretation?
5. When did the Slade affair become shorthand for the fourth dimension itself, rather than a controversy about Slade or one investigator?

## C. Did academics suffer identifiable professional consequences?

For every candidate, create a dated career record from independent institutional sources:

- appointment, promotion, habilitation, chair, salary, leave, dismissal, retirement, and succession;
- academy membership, committee service, editorial role, invited lectures, society offices, and funding;
- journal submissions, rejections, reviews, publication venue changes, and correspondence with editors;
- collaboration starts/stops, recommendation letters, withdrawn endorsements, and changes in professional correspondents;
- obituary, memoir, diary, and retrospective biography accounts.

For each claimed consequence, record:

1. exact date and institutional event;
2. primary document and repository locator;
3. person's own explanation, if any;
4. independent corroboration;
5. contemporaneous alternative explanations;
6. whether spiritualism, higher space, or reputational concern is explicitly mentioned;
7. a confidence label: **documented causal link**, **documented event / cause unknown**, **corroborated pattern**, **temporal association only**, or **unverified lead**.

## D. Candidate people and comparison groups

### Direct physical/spatial higher-space candidates

Friedrich Zöllner; Charles Howard Hinton; Karl Pearson; W. W. Rouse Ball; Gustav Richter; Melchior Palágyi; Théophile Moreux; Cora Lenore Williams; Claude Fayette Bragdon; and other names recovered from journals, citations, and correspondence.

### Researchers caught in the reputational spillover

Hermann von Helmholtz; Gustav Theodor Fechner; Wilhelm Wundt; Wilhelm Weber; Wilhelm Scheibner; Wilhelm Foerster; Hermann Carl Vogel; and the Leipzig/Berlin academic network.

### Boundary-setters and institutional actors

Ulrici; Hugo Münsterberg; Max Dessoir; Albert von Schrenck-Notzing; Carl du Prel; university rectors/deans; academy officers; journal editors; and appointment committees.

### Comparison cases

William Crookes; Alfred Russel Wallace; Oliver Lodge; and other scientists who remained institutionally successful despite visible psychical-research associations. Compare status, wealth, institutional sponsorship, disciplinary home, and type of claim before inferring that outcomes were uniform.

## E. Core archive acquisition sequence

1. Leipzig University Library and University Archive: Wundt and Fechner correspondence, diaries, institute records, appointment records, and the Wundt-Ulrici-Fechner sequence.
2. Kalliope and repositories named in its records: Zöllner, Foerster, Helmholtz, and other correspondents. Record the holding institution and exact item identifier, not just the catalogue search page.
3. Berlin-Brandenburg Academy and related observatory archives: Helmholtz papers, Academy minutes, election/committee records, and the Leipzig-Berlin network.
4. Scientific journals: *Annalen der Physik*, *Astronomische Nachrichten*, *Nature*, *Philosophical Magazine*, *Mind*, *Zeitschrift für Philosophie und philosophische Kritik*, and *Vierteljahrsschrift für wissenschaftliche Philosophie*.
5. Newspapers: German Newspaper Portal, ANNO, British newspaper collections, Chronicling America, and French newspaper archives. Search both exact phrases and people/institutions; preserve the issue/page image where available.
6. SPR and Lodge records: investigations, letters, press cuttings, private reactions, and comparisons with people whose professional careers remained strong.
7. Biographical and institutional chronology: appointment registers, university calendars, academy lists, obituaries, memoirs, and biographies of lesser-known assistants, lecturers, and correspondents.

## F. Search and sampling cautions

- OCR is noisy, especially for Fraktur and historical typography. Search alternate spellings and inspect page images for decisive hits.
- Search-result counts are not article counts, unique documents, or relevance counts.
- Relevance-ranked result order is not chronology. Use date restrictions and full date sweeps where possible.
- Newspaper snippets are discovery leads. Read the complete article and surrounding issue context.
- Catalogue metadata is not the manuscript. Obtain the item, repository, shelfmark, and folio/page references.
- A failure to locate a source is a coverage statement about the search conducted, not proof that no source exists.
- Preserve false positives and rejected leads with reasons; do not silently delete them from the dataset.
