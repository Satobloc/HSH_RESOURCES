Abstract
This article establishes a functional and spectral framework for the canonical scalar Laplacian
on the standard Menger sponge and examines its use as an internal sector of a free scalar
field associated with the TCA-F5D program. The construction starts from the self-similar
metric measure space(M,ρ,µ ) and the canonical class of regular, symmetric, strongly local, and
irreducible Dirichlet forms, unique up to a positive multiplicative constant. The global energy
scale is fixed by an explicit normalization convention based on the effective resistance between
two opposite exterior faces.
The normalized form determines two nonnegative self-adjoint realizations: the conservative
realization LN
M and the exterior Dirichlet realizationLD
M. Their semigroups are trace class
at every positive time, their resolvents are compact, and their spectra are discrete. Spectral
positivity is expressed through the positive scalar measures induced by their spectral resolutions.
The short-time heat trace admits an expansion governed by the walk dimensiondw, the
spectral dimension ds = 2dH/dw, and coefficients periodic in logarithmic time, with period
lnτM, whereτM = 3dw. Mellin transformation yields meromorphic continuations of the spectral
zeta functions and locates candidate pole families; nonreal poles are effective only when the
corresponding Fourier coefficients do not vanish.
The internal operator is finally incorporated into a free scalar field onR1,3×M in an
operator-theoretic sense. The modal decomposition produces masses

adjusted to reproduce predetermined phenomenological values.

1. Introduction, Scope, and Fundamental Question
1.1. Context and Objective
Spectral analysis on fractal spaces replaces part of the classical differential apparatus with a
formulation based on metric measure spaces, Dirichlet forms, and self-adjoint operators. A
self-similar fractal generally lacks an ordinary differentiable structure on which one could directly
introduce a smooth metric tensor, a Levi-Civita connection, or a Laplace–Beltrami operator. It
may nevertheless support a regular Dirichlet form that determines a diffusion, a semigroup, and
a Laplacian in the functional-analytic sense [3, Theorem 1.3.1][2, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3].
This article studies that architecture for the standard Menger spongeM⊂ [0, 1]3, equipped
with the restricted Euclidean metricρ and the uniform self-similar measureµ. The work continues
and revises the TCA-F5D corpus deposited on Zenodo [14]; that deposit is cited as an academic
antecedent and development record, whereas the mathematical justification of the present results
relies exclusively on the primary sources and internal derivations indicated in each section. The
construction follows the chain

The objective is to determine the functional, spectral, and thermal structure generated by this
chain and the conditions under which it may be incorporated into a free scalar field.
1.2. Geometric Hypothesis and Methodological Principle
The Menger sponge provides an explicit three-dimensional self-similar compact set defined by
twenty similarities of ratio1/3. It also belongs to the class of generalized Sierpiński carpets for
which results are available on Dirichlet forms, diffusion, walk dimension, heat kernels, and heat
traces [5, Definition 2.2][2, Theorem 1.2][6, Theorems 4.7–4.10]. Its faces, edges, and vertices
further make it possible to distinguish the strata entering the subleading terms of the heat trace.
The choice ofM is a concrete hypothesis of the model, not a physical conclusion. This work
does not prove that the sponge is a unique internal geometry, that it is dynamically selected, or
that it is physically realized in nature. The conditional statement used throughout Paper A is:
If the internal sector is modeled byM, its scalar operator must be constructed from the
corresponding canonical Dirichlet form.
The methodology is summarized by
geometry−→energy−→operator−→spectrum. (1.2)
Accordingly, the spectrum is not introduced to reproduce a constant, a dimension, or a mass
ratio selected in advance. The mathematical normalization of the energy will be fixed through
effective resistance; the physical scale will be introduced separately throughΛM > 0:
mathematical normalization̸= physical determination of scale. (1.3)

1.4. Epistemic Status and Exclusions
Three levels are distinguished: adopted external results, consequences derived in this work,
and open physical hypotheses. The first level includes existence and uniqueness up to scale of
the canonical form, energy self-similarity,dw > 2, sub-Gaussian estimates, and the heat-trace
expansion. The second includes the boundary realizations, compactness of the resolvents, positive
spectral measures, the reduced Neumann zeta function, and the modal tower. The physical
interpretation ofM, the determination ofΛM, and any correspondence with particles remain
open.
The analysis is restricted to the scalar Laplacian and a free quadratic theory. It does
not construct Dirac operators, fermionic fields, chiral structures, gauge sectors, interactions,
dynamical gravity, renormalization, or anS-matrix. Nor does it postulaterM = 3/5, ds≃ 1.72,
or a thermal periodln 3; it does not derive a universal physical constant fromζ′(0); and it does
not claim ultraviolet finiteness or interacting unitarity.
1.5. Organization and Closing Statement
Sections 2 and 3 establish the geometry, measure, canonical form, and its normalization. Sections 4
and 5 construct the operators and their spectral structure. Sections 6 and 7 develop the heat trace
and the zeta functions. Section 8 formulates the free scalar field. Section 9 discusses physical
scope and falsifiability criteria, and Section 10 presents the conclusions and the subsequent
program.
Before physical meaning is assigned to a fractal spectrum, the geometry and measure
must be defined, the energy must be constructed and normalized, the operator must be
identified, and the convergence of the procedures used to compute its spectrum must be
established. Paper A provides the rigorous architecture corresponding to the geometry,
measure, energy form, and operator. The construction of convergent approximants and
the numerical discovery of the spectrum will be the subject of Paper B.

8.9. Limits of the Scalar Model
The constructed Laplacian acts on functions and does not generate spinors, chirality, gauge
charges, or fermionic statistics. The action is quadratic and contains no interactions. AΦ3 or Φ4
interaction would require modal tensors built from products of eigenfunctions, which are not
computed here. Therefore, the scalar tower is not a leptonic spectrum.
9. Discussion, Physical Scope, and Falsifiability
9.1. Status of the Results
The architecture developed here contains three levels that must not be conflated. The first
comprises external results: existence and uniqueness up to scale of the canonical class, energy
self-similarity, the inequalitydw > 2, sub-Gaussian estimates, and the heat-trace expansion. The
second comprises consequences derived in this work: theN/D realizations, compactness of the
resolvents, discrete spectra, positive diagonal spectral measures, continuation of the reduced
Neumann zeta function, and modal decomposition. The third contains physical hypotheses: the
choice ofM as an internal sector, the scaleΛM, and any correspondence between modes and
observables.
The validity of the first two levels does not imply the validity of the third. Conversely, a
future refutation of the physical interpretation would not invalidate the theorems concerning the
canonical Laplacian.
9.2. Dependence on Boundary Conditions, Normalization, and Geometry
The conservative and exterior Dirichlet realizations differ in their zero mode, gap, low eigenvalues,
modal masses, and boundary heat terms. The comparison(5.25) shows that they are not two
notations for the same model. The boundary condition must be fixed before any phenomenological
comparison.

9.3. Scope of the Scalar Model and Ultraviolet Behavior
The internal operator acts onfunctions. It does notconstruct spinors, chirality, fermionicstatistics,
gauge charges, or dynamical gravity. The action considered is quadratic; it contains no vertices,
renormalization, scattering amplitudes, orS-matrix. Internal self-adjointness and reflection
positivity of finite truncations do not by themselves imply unitarity of a future interacting theory.

9.5. Falsifiability Criteria and the Entry Gate to Paper B
Future evaluation must distinguish the continuous theorem, the discretization, and the physical
hypothesis. A numerical discrepancy may reveal an incorrect implementation, lack of convergence,
or approximation to a different operator; it does not automatically refute a theorem. Conversely,
a convergent construction may be mathematically correct and still lack physical correspondence.
For varying Hilbert spaces, the Kuwae–Shioya framework relates Mosco convergence, re-
solvents, semigroups, spectral measures, and compact convergence [11, Definition 2.11 and

Any physical interpretation must be halted if limits depend on subsequences or discretizations, if
persistent spurious eigenvalues appear, if signals are smaller than the error, or if normalization
introduces the value that was supposed to be discovered. A subsequent phenomenological
correspondence will require independently determined parameters, compatible symmetries, and
out-of-sample predictions.

The requested rigorous architecture consists of a self-similar metric measure space, a normalized
