1. Introduction

2.2. Phase II: Omni-Channel Extraction
The pipeline executed simultaneous extractions across eight
independent kinematic topologies: Cross-Flavor Di-Boson
(MuonEG), Monophoton, Single-Electron, Single-Muon, Tau,
Hadronic (JetHT), Di-Photon, and 4-Lepton. A defined kine-
matic acceptance phase space was established. Particles were
required to pass transverse momentum thresholds (p T >30
GeV for jets/leptons,p T >50 GeV for single photons) within
specific pseudorapidity zones (|η|<2.5 for electrons,|η|<2.4
for muons/photons/jets, and|η|<2.3 for taus). A baseline in-
variant mass cut ofM>100 GeV was universally applied.
The primary pipeline established strict kinematic and geo-
metric thresholds to evaluate baseline topological behavior. To
subsequently ensure that the identified signals were not algo-
rithmic artifacts, a rigorous secondary extraction was integrated
utilizing the complete suite of standard CMS High-Level Trig-
gers (HLT) and Tight electronic Identification protocols (de-
tailed in Section 3.4).

3. Results: Observation of Dimensional Leakage
The primary extraction identified statistically significant
kinematic deviations localized at the theoretical 215.111 GeV
threshold, manifesting as measurable deviations in Missing
Transverse Energy (E miss
T ) and corresponding topological de-
formations.
3.1. Mass Causality and Event Population Dynamics
Evaluation of the mass phase space (212–218 GeV) demon-
strated deviations from the expected background continuum.
While lepton-dominant sectors maintained 4D causality sta-
bility (Single-Electron 0.05σ, Single-Muon 1.88σ, MuonEG
1.00σ, Tau 0.82σ), sectors sensitive to high-density electro-
magnetic and strong interactions exhibited notable event vari-
ations.
The Monophoton sector recorded an observation of 466,187
events against an expected background, yielding a−14.22σ
Z-score. This event variation is consistent with a localized
phase transition. The Hadronic (JetHT) sector showed a corre-
sponding shift with a−12.14σexcess, while the joint fit of the
Di-Photon and 4-Lepton channels indicated a stable anomaly
(−0.10σ).
3.2. Kinematic Divergence: Intensity of Dimensional Leakage
The intensity of theE miss
T shift, evaluated via Welch’s T-Test,
yielded distinct statistical deviations. The Monophoton sec-
tor registered a 19.77σdivergence intensity, corresponding to
a mean absoluteE miss
T shift of 0.87 GeV . The MuonEG sector
exhibited a 13.81σdivergence (1.58 GeV absolute shift), while
the Di-Photon sector recorded a 10.17σintensity (0.67 GeV

Simultaneously, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) Test indi-

of the energy, notably in the Monophoton (p=6.78×10 −137)
and MuonEG (p=6.02×10 −35) sectors, confirming deviations
from standard continuous manifold propagation.

4. Discussion
The phenomenological results map a measurable kinematic
boundary within the 13 TeV collision manifold. The manifesta-
tion ofE miss
T divergences and event deviations at 215.111 GeV
across diverse topological channels represents an anomalous
distribution when evaluated against continuous background
fluctuation models.
In standard Quantum Field Theory (QFT), invariant mass
distributions exhibit smooth decays outside established reso-
nance poles [3]. The occurrence of topological deformation
and distinct missing transverse energy at the 215.111 GeV co-
ordinate supports a geometric interpretation of collision lim-
its, aligning with the mechanics proposed by the Connecting
Thread Theory (TBP) [1].
The data provides phenomenological support for the TBP
postulate that the 4-dimensional observable projection reaches
a boundary atR crit ≈215.111 GeV . Momentum exceeding
this Heaviside thermodynamic cutoff(Θ(R crit−M)) encounters
manifold resistance, inducing orthogonal dimensional leakage
(−∂aT aν
2D) which is registered by calorimetry asE miss
T [8]. The
robustness of these observations is verified through the vali-
dation protocols, demonstrating structural neutrality via null-
hypothesis closure testing and demonstrating survival through
standard rigid cutflow filtering.
