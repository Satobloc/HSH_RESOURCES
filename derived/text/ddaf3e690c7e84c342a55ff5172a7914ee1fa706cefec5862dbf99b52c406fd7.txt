About this model
Test-particle motion in the Schwarzschild metric is integrated in the φ -form d²u/d φ ² + u = 1/L² + 3u² with G = c = M = 1
and u = 1/r, using RK4. The Newtonian closed ellipse becomes an orange precessing rosette; the apsidal advance per
radial period is Δφ  ≈  6 π M/[a(1 − e²)], the same GR effect behind Mercury’s historic 43″/century perihelion shift. Horizon r
= 2M and ISCO r = 6M are annotated. Assumptions: geodesic motion of a test mass, Schwarzschild (non-spinning)
spacetime, and geometric units—no radiation reaction. Vary orbital parameters to compare nearly Newtonian ellipses
with strongly precessing rosettes near the hole.
Who it's for:Advanced undergraduate and graduate general relativity and relativistic astrophysics courses.

How it works
Schwarzschild orbit equation d²u/d φ ² + u = 1/L² + 3u² integrated by RK4 (G = c = M = 1).
The orange GR geodesic precesses by Δφ  ≈  6 π M/[a(1−e²)] per orbit — the same
mechanism that produces the famous 43″/century perihelion advance of Mercury. A
gray Newtonian ellipse is overplotted to isolate the GR correction; ISCO (r = 6M) and
horizon (r = 2M) are marked.
Frequently asked questions
Why does the ellipse fail to close?
The extra 3u² term in the orbit equation is a GR correction absent from Newtonian 1/r potentials. It advances the periapsis each radial period so the tip of the ellipse slowly rotates, drawing
a rosette. This is spacetime curvature, not a classical inverse-cube ‘extra force’ in flat space—though the math looks similar in the u( φ ) equation.

How is this related to Mercury’s 43″/century?
The weak-field limit of Δφ  ≈  6 π M/[a(1 − e²)] per orbit, accumulated over a century for Mercury’s a and e, yields about 43 arcseconds after other Newtonian perturbations are removed. The
simulator shows the strong-field version of that same mechanism in geometric units.
What do r = 2M and r = 6M mark?
r = 2M is the Schwarzschild event horizon; timelike circular orbits do not exist stably down to the horizon. The innermost stable circular orbit sits at r = 6M for a non-spinning hole—inside
that, circular orbits are unstable. The annotations orient the rosette relative to those scales.
