Abstract
A Spectral-Geometric Tensor (SGT) framework is developed as an operator-based
reduced description of selected atomic ionisation observables. The construction starts
from channel-resolved regular families of self-adjoint operators, isolated finite-rank
spectral sectors, and their Riesz projectors. The parameter manifold is mapped by the
projector into a Grassmannian of reduced quantum-state subspaces. On this image,
the quantum geometric tensor defines the metric and Berry curvature, and the metric
generates the Levi–Civita, Riemann, Ricci, and scalar-curvature hierarchy. This
geometry is distinguished explicitly from the leading energetic closure: for ordinary
channels, the numerical benchmark retains an anchor-normalised reduced-gap law,
whereas the near-degenerate 4s/d sector is represented by a reduced 2 × 2 mixing
Hamiltonian. The gap is a spectral scalar and becomes geometrically informative only
together with derivative couplings; curvature invariants enter the first omitted
correction layer rather than the reported leading-order values. Benchmarking on H,
He, Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F, Ne, Cr, Cu, and Pd yields ,

Electronic-structure theory has long been organised around wavefunctions, orbitals,
and densities as primary objects [4], [5], [6]. That architecture has been remarkably
successful, but it also fixes the mode of closure at the foundation of the theory:
Hartree–Fock through orbital mean-field structure, and Kohn–Sham through density-
based effective closure [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12]. In both cases, the observable
architecture is subordinated to density- or orbital-mediated reconstruction. A
different route is developed here. The primitive datum is neither orbital nor density,
but a regular family of self-adjoint operators together with an isolated spectral sector
stable under admissible deformation [1], [2], [3]. Observable structure is then
generated through projector reduction, bundle organisation, and tensorial response
[13], [16], [24].
This programme must be placed within the established nonadiabatic and quantum-
geometric literature. Tully’s surface-hopping construction made nonadiabatic
coupling vectors operational in molecular dynamics, and subsequent reviews have
systematised their use in mixed quantum–classical propagation [17], [18].
Independently, the quantum geometric tensor and its Riemannian content were
established by Provost and Vallée [16]. Finite-rank Abelian and non-Abelian versions
were later formulated for degenerate sectors [19]. Recent work has related transition
matrix elements to Riemannian response, developed covariance-based descriptions
of quantum-state manifolds, and extended the construction toward Cartan-type
connections, torsion, and curvature [20], [21], [22]. Accordingly, neither the quantum
geometric tensor nor the emergence of Riemannian or Cartan geometry is claimed
here as new.
The specific contribution of the present work is the observable layer placed after this
established geometry. A Riesz projector first selects an isolated atomic channel; the
compressed spectral quantities  then define a channel-resolved energetic
descriptor, and an explicit map Φ  sends that descriptor to the first ionisation
observable I . Thus the present proposal is not another derivation of quantum-state
geometry. It is a test of whether projector-defined spectral sectors can support an
anchor-normalised, regression-free observable closure, while the metric and gauge
curvatures identify the invariant structure omitted by that leading closure.
The terminology “reduced spectral manifold” is used in a precise sense. For each
channel g, M  is the parameter manifold of a family of quantum operators and states.
The projector defines a smooth map
and the reduced spectral manifold is the regular image of this map, locally identified
with M  when the differential of P  has constant rank. The projector does not create
the external control variables. It converts them into a family of physical quantum
subspaces. Conversely, projected expectation values
provide local coordinates whenever the Jacobian  has full rank. Atomic elements
are discrete samples λ (Z) ∈  M , not differentiable coordinates by themselves.

Three objectives are therefore separated. First, the paper states the operator-
theoretic hypotheses under which isolated finite-rank sectors define smooth bundles.
Secondly, it writes the complete geometric hierarchy ,
including its finite-rank interpretation and its relation to nonadiabatic coupling
vectors. Thirdly, it implements a leading atomic closure through the compressed gap
Δ  or the mixing matrix , and states explicitly which geometric invariants are
retained only at the next correction order. This separation is essential: the gap is
spectrally intrinsic, but it is not a geometric tensor by itself.
First ionisation energies provide the test domain. In ordinary channels the leading
observable depends on a compressed-gap ratio defined after projector reduction. In
the near-degenerate 4s/d sector it is generated by a reduced 2 × 2 spectral mixing
problem. The benchmark therefore tests a reduced closure principle, not a claim that
scalar curvature has already been measured for each atom. Geometry organises the
state manifold and constrains systematic corrections; the reported leading numerical
values use only the explicitly identified spectral descriptors.

Conclusion
A reduced spectral–geometric framework has been established for selected atomic
first-ionisation observables. The construction starts from regular self-adjoint operator
families and isolated spectral sectors, proceeds through Riesz-projector reduction
and bundle geometry, and culminates in a local tensorial structure induced by the
quantum geometric tensor [1], [13], [24]. The decisive conceptual point is that the
observable is not built from orbitals or densities taken as primitive objects. …
