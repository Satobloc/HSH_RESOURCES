2.3 Operational Definition of the Precursor Cascade

===== PAGE 5 =====
5
A precursor cascade is defined as: For two adjacent regime centers C1 and C2, if the fluctuation
transformation point (T₂) of C1 is precisely the external precursor boundary of C2, then C1 and C2
constitute a first-order precursor cascade. The operational test criterion is: |T₂(C1)−T_precursor(C2)| <
0.01GeV.
The threshold of 0.01 GeV is chosen based on the precision of the current data. The Chow scan step
size is typically 0.01–0.05 GeV, and the Bai-Perron search step size is 3–4 data points (corresponding to a
mass interval of approximately 0.05–0.1 GeV). At 0.01 GeV, roughly one-fifth to one-tenth of these step
sizes,anytwoboundarieswhosedeviationfallswithinthisrangecanbeconsideredstatisticallycoincident.
Cascade asymmetry is defined as the difference between the deviations of two adjacent cascades:
Asymmetry=|dev₁−dev₂|,wheredev₁=T₂(C1)−T_precursor(C2),anddev₂=T₂(C2)−T_precursor(C3).
Cascadeasymmetryservesasacandidateregimefactor,testedviainteractioneffecttestingtoverifywhether
itmodulatestheexposurecoefficientsofexecutivefactors.
