1. Introduction
Replacing the Hilbert–Einstein Lagrangian by a nonlinear
function of the Ricci scalar is one of the simplest extensions of
general relativity. The review by Capozziello and De Lauren-
tis summarizes metric and Palatini f (R) theories, their scalar–
tensor representations, cosmology, perturbations, and weak-
field limits [1]. In the Palatini variational principle the metric
and torsion-free connection are independent. The field equa-
tions therefore di ffer from metric f (R) gravity, although both
reduce to general relativity for a linear Lagrangian [2, 3].

4.2. How nested sampling works
Nested sampling rewrites the evidence Z =
R
L(θ)π(θ)dθ as
a one-dimensional integral over remaining prior mass X. A set
of live points is initially drawn from the prior. At each iteration
the point with the smallest likelihood is removed, assigned a
quadrature weight associated with the contracted prior mass,
and replaced by a new point drawn from the prior subject to a
higher likelihood threshold. Repeating this process moves the
live set through nested likelihood contours. The accumulated
weighted dead and live points estimate both Z and posterior
expectations.
We use staticdynesty 3.1.0 with 1024 live points. The bound
is multi-ellipsoidal and proposals are uniform within the con-
strained prior. The SH0ES and Planck seeds are 2301 and 2401,
and the stopping threshold is ∆ ln Z = 0.01 [13]. The likeli-
hood calls the exact Palatini roots and full covariance terms.
Equal-weight posterior samples are obtained by importance re-
sampling; reported intervals are the 16th, 50th, and 84th per-
centiles. The evidence values from the two runs are not a Bayes
factor because the runs condition on different data likelihoods.

6. Verification and limitations
We reconstructed the physical branch for every one of the
24739 retained raw nested points (12394 SH0ES and 12345
Planck) on 234 redshifts spaced by ∆z = 0.01 over 0 ≤ z≤
2.33, which covers the Pantheon + interval 0 ≤ z ≤ 2.26.
There were zero failures. The smallest g′, F, and ξ val-
ues were respectively (0.01599, 0.01638, 0.75381) for SH0ES
and (0.03214, 0.05916, 0.75280) for Planck; the largest rela-
tive trace residual was 1.01× 10−12 and the largest|E2(0)− 1|
was 1.28× 10−12. These retained points include the low-weight
prior-edge trajectory, not only posterior-resampled draws. The
proof following Eq. (7) applies throughout the full open prior,
and numerical evaluations that fail a physical-domain condi-
tion are excluded rather than reassigned to a different algebraic
branch.
Independent numerical checks cover the exact n = 0 limit,
E(0) = 1 on multiple branches, regular evaluations arbitrar-
ily close to the prior endpoints, luminosity and BAO distances
against adaptive high-accuracy quadrature, the observational
selections and covariance matrices, synthetic likelihood and
acoustic-scale recovery, analytical nuisance conditionals, and
the exact one-dimensional IOI/QDM relation. At the two DESI

these values are sampler diagnostics and are not used to com-

The with- and without-DESI comparisons use the same non-
BAO observations, priors, and numerical settings. The BAO-
only goodness-of-fit calculation uses 13 measurements minus
three fitted parameters ( n, k, ABAO). It is a focused shape diag-
nostic, whereas the displayed global χ2/ν is only conventional
because correlated Pantheon+ light curves are not independent
scalar observations.
Several limitations remain. Chronometer systematics con-
tinue to depend on stellar-population modeling. New LEGA-C
analyses cannot simply be added when they reuse galaxies al-
ready represented in the 32-point compilation. Pantheon+ light
curves are correlated and can include repeat observations, so
a naive data-count degree of freedom is only a conventional
goodness-of-fit summary. DESI constrains only late-time dis-
tance ratios here: the free ABAO scale avoids an inconsistent
ΛCDM calibration but does not replace a Palatini calculation
of pre-recombination physics or rd. The analysis omits a self-
consistent Palatini CMB likelihood, radiation, growth, and per-
turbation or local-gravity viability tests. Finally, the Planck-

7. Conclusion
We have updated the Palatini calculation to use Pantheon+, a
32-point cosmic-chronometer likelihood with available system-
atic covariance, and the 13-element DESI DR2 BAO likelihood.
DESI is included as an uncalibrated late-time ruler by marginal-
izing ABAO = c/(H0rd) rather than importing a ΛCDM sound
horizon. The observable matter parameter is k = κρm0/(3H2
0),
while E(0) = 1 derives ¯β from (n, k). Analytical marginaliza-
tion over the conditional H0, supernova-intercept, and BAO-
scale directions makes full-covariance nested sampling practi-
cal and yields bounded, asymmetric posterior intervals.
DESI contracts the positive-slope (n, k) degeneracy substan-
tially: relative to matched no-DESI runs, the central widths of
n shrink by factors of 4.6–5.0 and those of k by about 7. Both
central intervals lie below n = 0, but the one-sided probabil-
ities of 93.0 and 95.8 percent are not strong background-level
evidence against ΛCDM. The acceptable BAO-only probabil-
ity, 0.453, shows that the tightening is not accompanied by an
obvious distance-shape misfit. The conditional rd values must
not be interpreted as early-Universe Palatini predictions. The
H0 anchors themselves have QDM = 23.888, IOI = 11.944,
and p≃ 10−6. Reporting those standard inconsistency statistics
quantifies the discrepancy between the published anchor sum-
maries. The next scientific step is a matched Palatini-versus-
ΛCDM analysis with a theory-consistent sound horizon and
CMB likelihood, alternative prior measures, and perturbation
viability.
