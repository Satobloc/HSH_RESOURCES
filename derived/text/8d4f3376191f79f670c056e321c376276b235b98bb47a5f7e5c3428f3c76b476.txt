Abstract  
The Kerr metric provides the standard description of astrophysical black holes, while singularity -free regular 
black-hole models offer alternative spacetime geometries that can be tested through horizon -scale observations. 
Here, we quantify the shadow devi ations produced by the non -rotating Bardeen regular black -hole metric and 
assess their observational scale for Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) targets. A lightweight and fully reproducible 
computational framework is implemented in a single Google Colab noteb ook, using the photon-sphere condition 
and critical impact parameter to determine the Bardeen shadow boundary and its dependence on the regularisation 
parameter g. The calculation recovers the Schwarzschild shadow diameter of 10.3923M at g/M=0 and shows a 
smooth reduction to 10.2319M, 9.6795M, and 8.8817M at g/M=0.3, 0.6, and 0.8, corresponding to fractional 
deviations of approximately 1.54%, 6.86%, and 14.53%, respectively. For Sagittarius A*, the corresponding 
angular diameter decreases from 53.94μas at g/M=0 to 53.11μas at g/M=0.3 and 50.24μas at g/M=0.6; for M87*, 
it decreases from 39.69μas to 39.08μas and 36.97μas, respectively. Across the tested range, both targets therefore 
remain above the adopted EHT angular -resolution benchmark, indicating that geo metric resolvability is not 
equivalent to parameter distinguishability. The results provide a quantitative mapping between the Bardeen 
regularisation parameter and observable shadow -size deviations and establish a reproducible computational 
baseline for fu ture comparisons incorporating black -hole spin, plasma effects, scattering, and full EHT image 
modelling. The complete workflow is designed for open reproduction and extension using standard scientific 
Python tools in a browser-based environment. 
Keywords: black hole shadow; regular black holes; Bardeen metric; Event Horizon Telescope; null geodesics; 
ray tracing; Sagittarius A*; M87*; reproducible computational astrophysics  
1. Introduction 
General relativity describes astrophysical black holes through the Kerr metric, but regular black -hole models 
provide an alternative in which the central singularity is replaced by a nonsingular  geometry. The Bardeen 
solution, originally proposed by Bardeen (1968), is one of the earliest and most widely studied examples. Ayón -
Beato and García (1998, 1999, 2000) later demonstrated that the Bardeen geometry can arise as an exact solution 
of Einstein gravity coupled to nonlinear electrodynamics. Subsequent studies extended regular black holes to 
rotating configurations and invest igated their photon trajectories and shadow properties (Bambi and Modesto, 
2013; Li and Bambi, 2014; Abdujabbarov et al., 2016; Stuchlík and Schee, 2019; He et al., 2022).   The 
development of horizon -scale very -long-baseline interferometry has made these theoretical differences 
increasingly relevant to observations. The Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) produced the first resolved image of 
M87* (Event Horizon Telescope Collaborat ion, 2019) and subsequently imaged Sagittarius A* (Sgr  A*) at the 
centre of the Milky Way (Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, 2022a). EHT observations have also been used 
to test the consistency of the observed image structure with the Kerr metric (Event Horizon Telescope 
Collaboration, 2022b). These results provide an observational basis for examining deviations from standard black-
hole geometries through their shadow properties.  Previous studies have established that regularisation can modify 
the photon sphere and shadow and that these effects can, in principle, be distinguished from Kerr spin (Li and 
Bambi, 2014; Abdujabbarov et al., 2016; Stuchlík and Schee, 2019). The influence  of accretion and surrounding 
matter on Bardeen shadow appearance has also been investigated (He et al., 2022). However, an important 
practical question remains: how does the Bardeen regularisation parameter translate into a measurable 
shadow-size deviation, and what angular scale does this deviation correspond to for current EHT targets?   
This distinction is important because shadow resolvability does not necessarily imply metric distinguishability. A 
shadow may remain larger than the nominal instrumental resolution while the difference between competing 
spacetime geometries remains too sma ll to identify reliably. A useful assessment therefore requires a direct

5.Discussion 
The results highlight an important distinction between shadow resolvability and shadow distinguishability. Both 
Sagittarius A∗ and M87∗ remain above the adopted EHT angular-resolution scale throughout the parameter range 
considered, including the largest value of 𝑔/𝑀. Thus, the presence of a regularisation -induced change in the 
predicted angular scale does not, by itself, prevent the corresponding structure from being geometrically resolved. 
The more relevant question is whether the predicted change in shadow size is sufficiently large to be distinguished 
from observational, calibration, reconstruction, and astrophysical uncertainties. The fractional deviation from the 
Schwarzschild reference therefore provides a useful first-order measure of the magnitude of the geometric effect. 
The Bardeen prediction differs from the Schwarzschild reference by approximately 1.54%, 6.86%, and 14.53% 
for 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.3, 0.6, and 0.8, respectively. These values show that the effect is relatively modest at moderate 
regularisation but becomes progressively more pronounced as 𝑔/𝑀 increases. The resulting trend is therefore 
useful for identifying the parameter regime in which regularisation -induced changes become potentially relevant 
to horizon-scale observations. However, these percentages should be interpreted as geometric deviat ions of the 
model, rather than as direct observational exclusion or detection thresholds.  The comparison with EHT 
observations should consequently be regarded as an order-of-magnitude benchmark rather than as a substitute for 
a full metric -testing analysis. In particular, the EHT analysis of Sagittarius A∗ incorporates calibration, image 
reconstruction, astrophysical modelling, and associated uncertainties when evaluating consistency with Kerr 
predictions (Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, 2022b). A direct comparison between a calculated fractional 
shadow-size change and a single adopted uncertainty scale cannot reproduce this inference procedure. The present 
results therefore do not establish an observational constraint on the Bardeen parameter. Instead, they identify the 
range in which the predicted geometric effect becomes comparable to percent-level observational systematics and 
thereby motivate a more complete image-level analysis. 
Horizon structure and the role of the 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 endpoint 
A specific qualification is required for the upper end of the parameter range. The standard Bardeen solution 
possesses an extremal configuration at 𝑔ext/𝑀 ≃ 0.7698, beyond which the event horizon disappears. 
Accordingly, 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 lies slightly beyond the horizon -bearing black-hole branch. Its inclusion in the present 
study is deliberate rather than accidental: it provides a controlled extension of the geometric parameter sweep 
across the extremal boundary and allows the response o f the photon-orbit and optical critical-scale calculation to 
be examined immediately beyond the extremal limit.   The 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 result should therefore not be interpreted 
as an ordinary horizon-bearing Bardeen black hole. More precisely, the value 8.8817𝑀 obtained at this endpoint 
represents the characteristic optical critical scale associated with the calculated unstable photon orbit under the 
adopted null-geodesic prescription. In the horizonless regime, it is preferable to regard this quantity as a form al 
shadow-like critical -curve scale rather than automatically assigning it the same physical interpretation as the 
black-hole shadow on the sub -extremal branch. This distinction preserves the continuity of the geometric 
calculation while avoiding the assumption that the over-extremal configuration possesses an event horizon.   The 
choice of 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 is nevertheless informative because it probes the immediate neighbourhood beyond the 
extremal boundary rather than extending arbitrarily deep into the horizonless regime. The resulting continuation 
of the calculated optical scale provides a sensitivity te st of how strongly the observable responds to increasing 
regularisation near and slightly beyond extremality. The approximately 14.53% reduction relative to the 
Schwarzschild reference should consequently be regarded as a formal upper sensitivity marker for the selected 
parameter sweep, rather than as an astrophysical prediction for a conventional Bardeen black hole. This distinction 
is particularly important when interpreting the endpoint in the context of EHT observations. The 𝑔/𝑀 < 𝑔ext/𝑀 
cases correspond to the horizon -bearing branch of the Bardeen solution, whereas 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 is included as an 
over-extremal extension of the same metric prescription. The comparison therefore separates the physical black -
hole regime from the formal geometric continuation and prevents the largest parameter value from being 
misinterpreted as evidence for an astrophysically realised horizonless object.

Limitations 
Several additional limitations should be considered when interpreting the results:  
• Non-rotating assumption:  The analysis uses a spherically symmetric, non -rotating Bardeen metric. 
Astrophysical black holes are expected to possess spin, and a more complete treatment would require a 
rotating regular-black-hole geometry, such as the construction discussed by Bambi and Modesto (2013). 
A joint exploration of spin and regularisation would also allow possible degeneracies between rotation -
induced shadow asymmetry and regularisation-induced size changes to be examined. 
• Vacuum geodesic optics:  The optical boundary is obtained from idealised vacuum null geodesics and 
does not include an accretion flow, plasma effects, or interstellar scattering. These environmental effects 
can modify the apparent size and morphology of black-hole images and have been considered in previous 
studies of regular black -hole shadows (Abdujabbarov et al., 2016; He et al., 2022). The present results 
should therefore be interpreted as geometric predictions of the underlying spacetime rather than direct 
predictions of reco nstructed EHT images. This limitation is particularly relevant for the 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 
extension, where the calculated optical scale should not automatically be equated with a conventional 
black-hole shadow. 
• Simplified observational baseline:  The comparison with EHT angular resolution and systematic 
uncertainty is intentionally simplified. It does not reproduce the EHT image -reconstruction process, 
calibration pipeline, astrophysical modelling, or complete uncertainty budget. Consequently, the reported 
fractional deviations should not be interpreted as observational constraints on the Bardeen parameter. 
Instead, they provide a quantitative scale against which future image-level analyses can be designed. 
• Extremality and parameter interpretation:  The inclusion of 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.8 extends the calculation 
slightly beyond the extremal black -hole threshold. This endpoint is retained specifically to test the 
behaviour of the geometric observable across the extremal transition. It is not used to expand the 
physically interpreted black -hole parameter space. The distinction between the horizon -bearing and 
horizonless branches is therefore maintained throughout the interpretation.  
Future Work 
These limitations also define the most direct directions for further work. A natural extension would combine spin 
and regularisation in a rotating regular -black-hole metric and evaluate the resulting shadow morphology over a 
two-parameter space. The geomet ric predictions could then be coupled to realistic accretion -flow and plasma 
models and compared with EHT image simulations and published metric -testing frameworks (Event Horizon 
Telescope Collaboration, 2022b). Such an approach would allow the size, asymmetry, brightness distribution, and 
other image -level observables to be considered simultaneously rather than relying primarily on the shadow 
diameter.  Because the present workflow is lightweight and browser -based, it provides a practical starting point 
for these extensions without requiring specialised numerical-relativity infrastructure. Overall, the results establish 
a clear quantitative relationship b etween the Bardeen regularisation parameter and the predicted optical critical 
scale while demonstrating that geometric resolvability alone is insufficient to establish observational 
distinguishability. The sub-extremal results provide the physically relev ant black-hole branch, while the 𝑔/𝑀 =
0.8 calculation serves as a controlled over-extremal sensitivity test of the same geometric framework. The analysis 
therefore provides a reproducible baseline for more complete tests of regular black-hole geometries using horizon-
scale observations. 
 
6. Conclusion 
This study presents a lightweight and fully reproducible computational assessment of Bardeen regular black-hole 
shadow deviations using a single Google Colab notebook. Across the tested range 𝑔/𝑀 = 0 − 0.8, the photon-
sphere radius decreases from 3.000𝑀 to 2.196𝑀, while the shadow diameter decreases from the Schwarzschild 
value of 10.3923𝑀 to 8.8817𝑀. The corresponding absolute diameter change reaches 1.5106𝑀, giving a 
maximum fractional deviation of approximately 14.5%.  When converted to angular scales, the predicted shadow 
diameter decreases from 53.94𝜇as to approximately 46.1𝜇as for Sagittarius A* and from 39.69𝜇as to 
approximately 34.2𝜇as for M87* across the tested range. Both remain above the adopted EHT angular-resolution

benchmark of approximately 22.5𝜇as. Thus, the calculations indicate that Bardeen regularisation does not prevent 
geometric shadow resolution for either target, while the increasing fractional deviation provides a quantitative 
measure for assessing potential metric distinguishability.   The results also show that the deviation is modest at 
intermediate regularisation values, reaching approximately 1.5% at 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.3 and 6.9% at 𝑔/𝑀 = 0.6. These 
values should be regarded as geometric benchmarks rather than direct observational constraints, since a rigorous 
EHT test requires image -level modelling that incorporates calibration, reconstruction, scattering, plasma, and 
accretion-flow effects. The analysis therefore identifies a quantitative parameter range for future observational 
studies rather than establishing a definitive detection threshold.  Beyond the physical results, the complete 
workflow provides a transparent and reproducible computational baseline that can be executed in a free, browser-
based environment. The framework can be extended to rotating regular black holes, joint spin –regularisation 
studies, realistic plasma and accretion models, and full EHT forward modelling, providing a practical starting 
point for more rigorous tests of regular black-hole geometries.
