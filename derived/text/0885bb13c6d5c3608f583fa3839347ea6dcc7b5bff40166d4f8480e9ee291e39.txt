1 Introduction
Quantum electrodynamics describes the electron as a point-like Dirac field. This descrip-
tion has been verified with extraordinary precision and forms one of the cornerstones of
modern particle physics. In perturbative calculations, the point-like description requires
the renormalization procedure to obtain finite observable quantities. Renormalization
provides a mathematically complete and experimentally successful framework for cal-
culating physical predictions. The present work explores whether the experimentally
determined intrinsic properties of the electron may also admit an underlying geometric
and topological description.
Neutrino physics provides another important motivation for exploring possible underlying
structures. The observation of neutrino oscillations [7] establishes that neutrinos possess
non-zero masses, whose values are many orders of magnitude smaller than those of the
charged leptons. Extension models, including the seesaw mechanism [2, 3, 4], provide
elegant frameworks for generating naturally small neutrino masses. The origin of the
corresponding mass scales, however, remains an active subject of theoretical investigation.
These observations motivate the exploration of whether the empirical parameters admit
a deeper geometric or topological interpretation. Just as the structure of atoms provided
the foundation for understanding the periodic table, it is natural to ask whether the
observed properties of elementary particles originate from an underlying organization of
more primitive vacuum structures.
In this paper, we investigate one possible realization based on the hypothesis of charge
quantisation and pair production [1]. In this work electric charges are assumed to be
generated as a pair of primitive charge quanta, (ξ, ¯ξ), carrying fractional charges±e/3
of opposite sign. The up and down quarks are represented as stable topological assem-
blies of these charge quanta, with their physical properties determined by the geometry,
symmetry, and vacuum-energy distribution of the corresponding configurations.
The primary objectives of this work are:
i. To establish the minimal stable charge-quantum topologies for the electron and the
neutrino.
ii. To derive the electron radius, rest mass, intrinsic spin, and magnetic moment from
the proposed geometric construction without introducing adjustable parameters.
iii. To derive the neutrino radius, the heavy Majorana mass, and the light neutrino mass
through the same geometric framework together with the seesaw mechanism.
iv. To investigate whether these geometric constructions provide a structural interpreta-

2.3.3 Charge Topology and Relative Mass of Electron and Neutrino
The absence of the central charge quantum in the neutrino results in a weaker confinement
mechanism. Although the opposite charge pairs provide a closed neutral configuration,
the structure possesses a lower binding energy and a larger tendency toward delocalization
compared with the electron five-quantum state. Therefore, the neutrino corresponds to
a lower-energy and lower-mass excitation of the vacuum charge topology.
The mass hierarchy between the electron and neutrino follows from their different topo-
logical structures. The electron contains five charge quanta with a central stabilizing
component, whereas the neutrino consists only of two balanced charge pairs. Hence the
neutrino has a smaller confined vacuum-energy content and therefore a much smaller
rest mass. The electrically neutral character of the neutrino arises from exact charge
cancellation, while its small mass originates from the reduced topological confinement
energy.

configuration remain spatially localized while preserving its net electric charge.
A physically admissible charge–quantum configuration must satisfy four fundamental
conditions:
i. exact electric charge quantization;
ii. static mechanical equilibrium under the internal vacuum interactions;
iii. geometric stability against diffusion of the constituent charge quanta;
iv. sufficient symmetry to support the localized circulating vacuum modes responsible for

3.4.3 Consistency with the Uncertainty Principle
The distinction between charge-quantum localization and electron localization is directly
relevant to the Heisenberg uncertainty principle. The uncertainty relation applies to
conjugate quantum observables of a specified degree of freedom. In the present model,
Re specifies the localization scale of theinternalcharge quanta; it is not the spatial
uncertainty ∆xof the electron as a whole. Accordingly, one should not identify
∆x=R e (34)
for the complete electron state and conclude that a large momentum uncertainty follows.
Such an identification conflates the internal localization of the constituent charge quanta
with the quantum localization of the composite electron.
The uncertainty principle is fully respected at the level of the internal degrees of freedom:
a finite localization scale of a constituent charge quantum corresponds to a finite uncer-
tainty in its conjugate variables, and no divergence results. In particular, no “energy
explosion” follows from the existence of a finiteR e, since
Re finite =⇒finite internal quantum uncertainties,(35)
and the localization scale is an internal structural property, not an additional rest-energy
contribution. The present construction therefore establishes three mutually consistent
features: the charge quanta possess a finite maximum internal localization scaleR e; the
electron as a whole need not possess a classical bounding radius equal toR e; and the
finiteR e introduces no uncertainty-principle divergence. The resulting picture is one
of a quantum fermion with a finite internal charge-quantum structure but without the
necessity of a classical geometrical boundary.
