1. Introduction
Cluster algebras can be realized in terms of various important monoidal categories, 
such as ﬁnite-dimensional representations of quantum aﬃne algebras, perverse sheaves on 
quiver varieties, representations of quiver-Hecke algebras, equivariant perverse coherent 
sheaves on the aﬃne Grassmannian... (see the very recent [6]a n d [28]f o r a review).
In this paper, inspired by this general framework of monoidal categoriﬁcations, we get 
new results on the representation theory of quantum aﬃne algebras and on the positivity 
of certain corresponding Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.
Let Uq(ˆg)b e  an untwisted quantum aﬃne algebra of quantum parameter q ∈ C×
which is not a root of unity. Consider C to be the monoidal category of ﬁnite-dimensional 
representations of the algebra Uq(ˆg) (those representations are also regarded as represen-
tations of a quantum loop algebra Uq(Lg)). This category has been studied from several 
geometric, algebraic, combinatorial perspectives in connections to various ﬁelds, see [51,
38,17]f o r important recent developments and [11]f o r a very recent point of view in the 
context of physics.
The category C is non-semisimple, and non-braided. It has a very intricate structure 
and many basic questions are still open. The simple objects in C have been classiﬁed by 
Chari-Pressley in terms of Drinfeld polynomials [8], but the dimensions and characters 
of these simple objects are not known in general.
The fundamental modules in C are distinguished simple modules whose classes gen-
erate its Grothendieck ring K(C). (We do not distinguish the term “module” from 
“representation” in this paper.) For simply-laced types, Nakajima established in [54]
a remarkable Kazhdan-Lusztig algorithm to compute the multiplicity Pm,m′ of a simple 
module L(m′)i n a standard module M(m), that is, a tensor product of fundamental 
modules. Here m, m′ belong to an ordered set of monomials M which parametrizes both 
simple and standard objects. This allows one to calculate the classes of simple modules 
[L(m)] ∈ K(C)i n terms of the classes of fundamental modules. As the dimensions and 
characters of the latter are known, this gives an answer to the problem of calculating
