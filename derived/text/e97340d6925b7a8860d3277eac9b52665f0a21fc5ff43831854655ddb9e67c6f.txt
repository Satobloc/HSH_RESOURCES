Topology-driven tuning of nonlinear opto-electronic properties in spiral-distorted
quantum rings under magnetic field
A. Naifar  Show more

Highlights
• First combined study of spiral dislocation ( β ), confinement radius
(R ) and magnetic field (B) effects in 2D quantum rings.
• Magnetic field and spiral dislocation show antagonistic influence
on nonlinear optical responses.
• Structural geometry enhances oscillator strength, while dislocation
gradients reduce it.
• Coupled ( β –R –B) tuning enables optimization of refractive-
index changes for QR switching devices.
Abstract
This work unlocks the joint effects of radial spiral dislocation ( β ), the radial position
of the confinement minimum (R ) and an external applied magnetic field (B) on the
electron nonlinear optical properties of a charged spinless particle confined in a 2D
quantum ring (QR). The modified Schrödinger equation has been solved through a
self-consistent technique. Within the framework of the density matrix formalism, the
linear and nonlinear optical absorption coefficients as well as refractive index
changes are evaluated. Our calculations reveal that the electron wave function, dipole
matrix elements, oscillator strength, and optical responses are highly responsive to
the tested parameters. In particular, both the magnetic field and spiral dislocation act
in an antagonist way, while the radial minimum position seems to mask the torsional
deformation consequences when enlarged. The oscillator strength is strongly
enhanced by the structural geometry, while a radial dislocation gradient decreases its
magnitude. Two-dimensional colormaps have deciphered the complex relationship
between the combination ( β -R -B) on refractive index changes, suggesting the
possibility of nonlinear spectrum optimization for converter switching future QR
devices.

Introduction
For physical systems, topological defects must be investigated by condensed matter
and nanoscience physicists [1]. Among the defects that may arise are dislocations and
deformations caused by torsion, bending and many other constraints [2]. A structure
that undergoes curvature exhibits physicochemical properties different from those of
an ideally nonperturbated system. Such defects strongly impact the aspects of
electronic wave functions, whose perception of the new potential landscape
(quantum barriers and trapping zones) becomes different [3]. On the experimental
level, dislocation defects induced by applied constraints lead to consequences that
can shift numerical predictions. One phenomenon that is closely related to these
structures is electronic transport from one region to another in optoelectronic
devices involving semiconductors, junctions, and semiconductor–insulator interfaces
[4].
Spiral deformations (torsional defects) constitute a particular class of topological
defects characterized by a local torsion of the crystalline lattice without macroscopic
curvature of space [5]. Unlike ordinary elastic deformations, that relies on
displacement gradients, such specific structures introduce a continuous rotation of
local reference frames around a given axis or a topological core. Within the geometric
framework of analog gravity, reporters proved that defects are described by a nonzero
torsion tensor 𝑇𝜇𝜈𝜆  while the curvature tensor remains zero [6]. This fundamentally
distinguishes them from disclinations associated with effective curvature. This non-
Riemannian geometry profoundly modifies the effective metric experienced by
charge carriers and acts as a source of geometric fields influencing their wave
functions [7].
Spiral deformations may emerge in nanostructures subjected to constraints that are
incompatible with simple elastic relaxation. In QD-types like core/shell or
core/multishell systems [8]. Lattice parameter mismatch between the different layers
induces internal strain that, beyond a critical threshold, relaxes through the
formation of screw dislocations and helical torsion fields. Similarly, in graphene and
two-dimensional materials, thermal gradients, localized doping, charged impurities,
or transverse electric fields can generate a local symmetry breaking that leads to a
rotation of the crystallographic axes [9]. These mechanisms produce an effective
chiral geometry that acts as a pseudo-magnetic field and imposes a long-lasting
topological constraint on the system [10]. In a space endowed with torsion, the
Schrödinger equation acquires a geometric connection term equivalent to an effective
vector potential 𝐴torsion. This geometric field modifies the phase of the wave function
and induces a helical quantization of the eigenstates [11]. As a result, the degeneracy
of energy levels is lifted, charge carriers become directionally localized, and
geometric phases analogous to the Aharonov–Bohm effect appear. In confined
systems, such as quantum dots or cylindrical heterostructures, these effects lead to a

measurable shift of electronic transitions and a direct modification of the density of
states [12].
Researchers in recent years have paid much attention to studying the nonlinear
optical properties of quantum dots, quantum rings, and quantum wells [13,14]. Spiral
(torsional) distortions in quantum rings represent a particular case where geometric
and topological effects directly influence the confinement landscape. In such systems,
the presence of a screw dislocation leads to a coupling between radial and angular
coordinates, resulting in a non-Euclidean geometry. This modification alters the
electronic structure, transition energies, and optical response of the system [15]. In
particular, torsion-induced effects can significantly impact dipole transitions,
oscillator strengths, and nonlinear optical coefficients [16].
From a broader perspective, similar geometry-driven effects have been extensively
investigated in nonlinear optical media and structured photonic systems, where
symmetry breaking and spatial modulation play a crucial role in enhancing light–
matter interactions [17,18]. In particular, the control of nonlinear responses through
structural ordering and modulation has been demonstrated in advanced optical
platforms [19]. Likewise, engineered photonic systems exhibiting high-quality
resonances highlight the importance of geometry in tailoring spectral properties and
field localization [20]. In ring-like and toroidal configurations, confinement and
boundary conditions further amplify interference effects, leading to enhanced
sensitivity of optical responses [21]. Moreover, nonlinear responses in circular
structures underline the general impact of ring geometries on wave dynamics and
energy transfer processes [22]. As a consequence, spiral distortions provide an
additional degree of freedom to engineer and tune nonlinear optical responses. These
properties make spiral-distorted quantum rings promising platforms for next-
generation optoelectronic and photonic devices, with potential implications for
nanoscale sensing, bio-photonic systems, and advanced optical diagnostics [23,24].
Depending on the degrees of freedom, the electronic wave functions are manipulated
at the nanoscale to satisfy industrial applications. A spatial or partial overlap is the
starting point for acquiring the orders of magnitude of nonlinear attributes [25]. As
an immediate consequence of geometric restriction, quantum confinement becomes
more significant, and second harmonic and third harmonic generation become more
appreciated both from the point of view of spectral position and the optimization of
certain wavelengths that are otherwise inaccessible to obtain [26].
Choubani et al. [27] have reported the effects of transverse electric field, pressure,
and temperature on the nonlinear optical properties of lens-shaped core/shell
quantum dots coupled with a wetting layer. They proved that manipulating
geometrical metric could enhance the resonance frequencies. Karim et al. [28] have
deciphered the strain effects on the band gap and diameter of CdSe core and
CdSe/ZnS core/shell quantum dots at any temperature. They indicated that
temperature and dimension should be studied in paralel in order to obtain the
desired nonlinear optical properties. Fakkahi et al. [29] reported that temperature
and pressure represent effective tools to achieve tunable second harmonic generation
in GaAs/AlGaAs multilayered quantum dots. They also demonstrate that enlarging the
inner core radius redsfifted the computed coefficients with slight incrementation in
their amplitudes.
In this innovative work, we aim to clarify and examine the effect of the presence of a
radial distortion at the confinement trapping well on the electronic wave functions,

the dipole matrix element, the oscillator strength and the absorption/refraction
spectra. The originality of this work lies in the fact that most theoretical studies
assume a homogeneous confinement environment, although experimental results
show quite the opposite. Through this study, we aim to investigate the parallel effect
of spiral distortion, the magnetic field and the minimum of the confinement region.

observables is grouped in Section (II). The interpretation of the major outcomes are
presented in Section (III). Our investigation concludes with Section (IV) where we
summarize all the concluding points regarding our research.
