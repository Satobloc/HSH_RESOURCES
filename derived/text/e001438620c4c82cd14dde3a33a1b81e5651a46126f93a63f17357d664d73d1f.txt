Abstract 
Green functions and Feynman diagrams constitute two of the most successful 
mathematical frameworks in modern theoretical physics. Green functions describe 
propagation and response, while Feynman diagrams organize perturbative interactions 
through propagators, vertices, and loop corrections. Although these formalisms have 
proven extraordinarily successful, they describe physical processes primarily through 
analytical expressions and graphical expansions rather than through an explicit theory of 
process architecture. In this work, we introduce the Architectural Factorization 
Principle, according to which every perturbative process possesses an 
underlying Process Green-Function Architecture characterized by four primitive 
architectural sectors: propagation, interaction, holonomy, and recursion. We develop a 
graph-theoretical mapping that assigns every finite Feynman diagram a unique 
architectural tuple (𝑎,𝑏,𝑐,𝑑), from which a unique Architectural Multiplicity 
Π=2!3"(3𝜋)#𝐴$/&, 
 
is constructed. We prove that this multiplicity is a graph invariant and derive its 
exponential structure from fundamental architectural composition principles. The 
constants 2, 3, 3𝜋, and 𝐴 are interpreted as the primitive bases associated with binary 
transport, minimal interaction branching, triple holonomy, and recursive closure, 
respectively, within the axiomatic framework of Green-Function Architecture Theory. 
We further formulate the Architectural Factorization Principle, according to which 
every perturbative Feynman amplitude admits a decomposition into an architectural 
component and a dynamical component. This framework provides a structural 
interpretation of Green functions and Feynman diagrams while preserving their 
established computational role. Preliminary comparisons with particle masses, reaction 
energies, and decay widths suggest that Green-Function Architecture Theory may 
provide a unified structural language for both static particle architectures and dynamic 
process architectures. 
Keywords: Green functions; Feynman diagrams; graph theory; quantum field theory; 
particle reactions; particle decays; process architecture; architectural multiplicity; 
holonomy; recursion.

1. Introduction 
Green functions occupy a central position throughout theoretical physics. Since the 
pioneering work of George Green in the nineteenth century, Green functions have 
become indispensable for the solution of differential equations, boundary-value 
problems, quantum mechanics, electrodynamics, condensed matter physics, statistical 
mechanics, and quantum field theory [1–10]. In all these applications, Green functions 
describe the propagation of information, fields, or particles through an underlying 
mathematical structure. 
The second major development was introduced by Richard Feynman, who reformulated 
perturbative quantum field theory in terms of diagrammatic expansions [11]. Feynman 
diagrams provide a graphical representation of perturbative processes, transforming 
lengthy algebraic calculations into a combinatorial language based on propagators, 
interaction vertices, loops, and diagrammatic expansions. Together with the Dyson 
formalism [12], renormalization theory [13–16], and modern gauge field theory [17–20], 
they constitute one of the foundations of contemporary particle physics. 
Although Green functions and Feynman diagrams are mathematically connected, they 
describe different aspects of physical processes. Green functions primarily characterize 
propagation and response, whereas Feynman diagrams organize perturbative interactions 
and transition amplitudes. Neither framework, however, explicitly introduces the notion 
of a primitive process architecture. The elementary graphical objects of Feynman 
theory—propagators, vertices, loops, and recursive insertions—appear naturally within 
calculations, yet they are not usually regarded as manifestations of four independent 
architectural sectors describing an entire physical process. 
The present work develops such a structural description. 
Our starting point is the hypothesis that every physical process possesses an 
underlying Process Green-Function Architecture whose primitive constituents are 
• propagation, 
• interaction, 
• holonomy, 
• recursion. 
These four sectors are proposed to constitute the primitive architectural operations from 
which complete reaction and decay architectures are assembled. 
The present manuscript extends previous work on Green-Function Architecture Theory 
(GFAT), in which the primitive object was introduced as a Green-Function Architecture 
and particle energies were associated with an Architectural Multiplicity [21]. Here we

3. The Feynman Diagram Method 
3.1 Historical Background 
The diagrammatic formulation of quantum field theory introduced by Richard P. 
Feynman revolutionized perturbative calculations by replacing lengthy algebraic 
expressions with graphical representations of elementary interactions [11]. Combined 
with Dyson's perturbation expansion [12], Wick's theorem [22], and subsequent 
developments in renormalization theory [13–16], Feynman diagrams have become one of 
the principal computational tools of modern particle physics.

uniquely to a mathematical expression obtained from the Feynman rules.
