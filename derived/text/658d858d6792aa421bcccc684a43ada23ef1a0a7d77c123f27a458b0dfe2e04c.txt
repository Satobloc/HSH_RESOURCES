ABSTRACT
Mach’s principle suggests that inertial response should not be regarded as an entirely intrinsic property
ofalocalbody,butasaconsequenceofitsrelationtothelargerphysicalsysteminwhichitisembedded.
We formulate this possibility through variational openness: the local variational balance retains a
contribution from external degrees of freedom across an active interface. After causal reduction to a
retained collective coordinate, this contribution appears as a temporal response whose low-frequency
curvature contains a local acceleration sector. When the corresponding second temporal moment
exists, the induced inertial coefficient equals one half of that moment. A passive harmonic realization
yields a nonnegative induced contribution, while an independent Schur-complement condition controls
stability. Within a fixed reduced description, the observed inertia is decomposed into residual local
and openness-induced sectors, while the stronger claim that the residual sector vanishes is treated as
an additional falsifiable hypothesis.
1. Introduction
In Newtonian mechanics, inertial mass is introduced
operationally as the coefficient relating force to acceleration.
This specifies how inertia is measured, but not why a local
body should possess such a coefficient. Mach questioned
the attribution of inertial properties to a body considered
in complete isolation, arguing instead that they should
reflect its relation to the larger physical system [1]. This
relational intuition influenced Einstein’s early discussion of
gravitationandinertia[ 2],althoughitdidnotleadtoaunique
mathematical formulation of Mach’s principle.
Later programmes implemented different aspects of this
intuition.Sciamaproposedgravitationalinductionasamech-

mechanics replaced absolute inertial structure by relations
