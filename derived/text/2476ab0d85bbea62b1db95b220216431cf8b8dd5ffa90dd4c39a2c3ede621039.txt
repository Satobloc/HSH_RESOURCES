Abstract
Relaxation-Driven Cyclic Cosmology (RDCC) is a minimal and nonsingular cosmological
framework in which the Universe undergoes an inﬁnite sequence of smooth contraction and
expansion phases. The theory is formulated entirely within standard General Relativity
and a single complex scalar ﬁeld, supplemented only by the leading Planck-suppressed
interaction between two CPT-related sectors that share the same spacetime metric. This two-
sector structure replaces the assumption of a globally directed time with a CPT-symmetric
description in which thermodynamic arrows, entropy production and matter–antimatter
asymmetries emerge as sector-dependent phenomena. The global system remains entropy-
conserving, thereby resolving the Tolman entropy problem without invoking inﬂation, modiﬁed
gravity or additional ﬁelds.
The scalar potential consists of a radiatively tilted double-well structure and an ekpyrotic
term that dominates during contraction. Together with the bounded effective Friedmann
correction implied by the EFT expansion, these ingredients generate a smooth, ghost-free
bounce with ﬁnite curvature invariants. The ekpyrotic phase produces a nearly scale-invariant
spectrum of curvature perturbations, while the Planck-suppressed cross-sector interaction
damps isocurvature modes as a−α, driving the Universe toward adiabaticity. The resulting
spectral index and amplitude are consistent with current CMB observations.
A key phenomenological consequence of the relaxation mechanism is the emergence of a
temperature asymmetry between the two sectors, which naturally generates the observed
dark-matter-to-baryon ratio through an exponential sensitivity to the relaxation parameter
α. The same parameter controls small, correlated deviations from ΛCDM in the growth
rate and matter power spectrum, as well as a modest contribution to the effective number
of relativistic species. These signatures provide a clear and falsiﬁable observational window
into the two-sector structure.
RDCC thus offers a uniﬁed and minimal explanation for nonsingular cosmological evolu-
tion, dark matter, the baryon asymmetry, the thermodynamic arrow and the near-adiabaticity
of primordial perturbations. Its predictions arise from a single infrared parameter and are
testable with upcoming large-scale-structure and CMB experiments, making RDCC a struc-
turally simple yet observationally rich alternative to standard cosmology.
1 Introduction
The standard ΛCDM model provides an excellent description of the expansion history and the
statistical properties of cosmological perturbations. Yet several foundational questions remain
unresolved: the nature of dark matter and dark energy, the origin of the baryon asymmetry, the
thermodynamic arrow of time, the near-adiabaticity of primordial perturbations and the presence
of an initial singularity. These puzzles are typically addressed by introducing new degrees of
freedom, modiﬁed gravity or an inﬂationary phase. While such extensions can resolve individual
issues, they do not explain why these problems appear together or why their proposed solutions
remain conceptually disconnected.
