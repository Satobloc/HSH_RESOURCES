Controlling surface reactions of hydrogen
atoms by the electron spin

Gas molecules possess various internal degrees of freedom. Whereas recent
advances in surface dynamics have clariﬁed the roles of translational, vibra-
tional and rotational motion in gas-surface reactions, the effect of its electron
spin on surface reactions remains poorly understood. Here, we demonstrate
the spin dependence of H adsorption and abstraction reactions on a Ni surface
using a spin-polarized atomic-hydrogen beam. The results indicate that H
adsorption is suppressed when the H electron spin is oriented perpendicular
to the magnetization direction of the Ni(111) surface. Furthermore, the same
spin preference is observed for the H abstraction reaction on a D-covered
surface. These ﬁndings suggest that controlling the spin orientation of mole-
cules and surfaces by applying an external magnetic ﬁeld provides a viable
means to modulate surface reaction rates.
An intriguing feature of a particle is its spin-degree of freedom. The
symmetry with respect to the permutation of identical particles
imposes rigorous restrictions on the wavefunction and energy level of
the relevant system. Whereas nuclear spins lead to the presence of
nuclear-spin isomers that are strongly coupled with the rotational
motion1, electron spins induce exchange interaction that plays an
important role in chemical processes including surface reactions. The
exchange interaction due to electron-nucleus Coulomb attraction
favors the spin-singlet state, which corresponds to chemical-bond
formation as described by the Heitler-London theory2. In contrast, the
exchange interaction originating from electron-electron repulsion
tends to stabilize a spin-triplet state in accordance with Hund’sr u l e2.
These two effects would compete in gas-surface interactions. Com-
pared with other degrees of freedom related to nuclear motion, such
as vibrational and rotational motion, the role of electron spin in the
gas-surface interaction has been less explored to date.
The effects of the electron spin on surface reactions have attrac-
ted considerable attention in recent years. Theoretical calculations
on ammonia synthesis on magnetic surfaces revealed that the spin-
split d-band plays an important role in the reactions3– 5.W i t hr e s p e c tt o
reactant gas molecules, the in ﬂuence of the spin state of oxygen
molecules and atoms on the adsorption were examined in detail
experimentally. The ground states of both O 2 and O are spin-triplet
with electronic terms of O2 (3Σ/C0
g )a n dO (3P). It has been reported that
the adsorption probability of O 2 molecules depends on the relative
orientation of the O2 spin and substrate magnetization of Ni and other
magnetic surfaces6,7. Moreover, the adsorption probability of O(1D) is
substantially higher than that of O(3P) on a graphite surface8.
A hydrogen atom consists of an electron and a proton, each
having spin 1/2. As the simplest molecule, the adsorption of hydrogen
on surfaces is regarded as a prototype reaction and is of fundamental
importance in many ﬁelds, ranging from heterogeneous catalysis,
interstellar chemistry to plasma catalysis, which has been extensively
studied on various surfaces to date9– 11. In the dissociative adsorption of
H2,t h eb r e a k i n go ft h eH−H bond is a key process, which is promoted
by vibrational excitation as well as translational motion12.F u r t h e r m o r e ,
because the potential energy surface depends on the molecular
orientation, the inﬂuence of the rotational motion on adsorption has
been reported for several metal surfaces13– 18. For atomic H adsorption,
energy dissipation has been a central topic over the past decade.

Recent advanced experimental and theoretical studies using a well-
deﬁned atomic H beam have clari ﬁed that electron-hole pair excita-
tions play a decisive role in the H adsorption on metal surfaces19,20.O n
the other hand, the behavior of the H spin was theoretically analyzed
during scattering at a Cu surface21. Although spin effects in H adsorp-
tion can be anticipated, experimental evidence about the spin effect
has yet to be established.
In this study, we investigate spin effects in H adsorption on the
ferromagnetic Ni(111) surface. By controlling the spin direction of H
atoms relative to the Ni surface, whose magnetization lies in the sur-
face plane, using a recently developed spin-polarized H beam 22,w e
compare the adsorption behavior for cases in which the H spin is either
parallel or perpendicular to the Ni majority spin. As a result, we ﬁnd
that H adsorption is substantially suppressed when the H spin is
oriented perpendicular to the Ni surface. Furthermore, the H
abstraction reaction, which is well known as a prototypical non-
thermal reaction proceeding via the Rideal-Eley / hot-atom
mechanism23– 25, is also suppressed when the H spin is oriented per-
pendicular to the Ni surface. The magnitude of the spin effect depends
o nt h es t r e n g t ho ft h ea p p l i e dm a g n e t i cﬁeld which controls the
electron spin direction with respect to the surface. The present results
strongly suggest controlling the spin direction provides a means to
modulate the rates of surface reactions.

We discuss the origin of the dependence of the H reactivity on the
magnetic-ﬁeld direction with respect to the surface. We ﬁrst address
the magnetic properties of the Ni surface. The magnetism of the bare
Ni(111) surface has been investigated by magnetic imaging techniques
in detail 31. It was reported that the magnetization at the surface is
entirely in-plane in the absence of an external magneticﬁeld. Based on
the study by Dzemiantsova et al.31, the magnetization at the surface is
expected to remain in-plane regardless of the direction of the applied
magneticﬁeld, either parallel or perpendicular to the surface, although
domain walls may move depending on the magnitude and direction of
the magnetic ﬁeld applied in the present study. In addition, the mag-
netization of Ni gives rise to a magnetic ﬁeld extending into the
vacuum region above the surface. Since the saturation magnetization
of Ni is about 600 mT 32 and the magnetic permeability of Ni is
100– 60033,34,t h em a g n e t i cﬂux density outside the Ni surface of each
magnetic domain is estimated to be 1-6 mT with its direction in-plane
because of the boundary condition that the parallel component of the
magnetic ﬁeld must be continuous across the surface.

When a high external magnetic ﬁeld is applied parallel to the surface,
the surface magnetization forms a single domain aligned with the
external ﬁeld, and the H spin is also aligned in the direction of the
external magneticﬁeld. In contrast, when the external magneticﬁeld is
applied perpendicular to the surface, the Ni magnetization remains in-
plane and the H spin is aligned in the direction of the sum of the
external ﬁeld and the magnetic ﬁeld generated by Ni.
The present experimental results shown in Fig. 2c indicate the
adsorption probability of H with its spin oriented parallel to the Ni spin
is higher than that with the perpendicular orientation. This observa-
tion is counterintuitive, because a spin-parallel con ﬁguration is gen-
erally unfavorable for chemical-bond formation between H and Ni
atoms. As discussed in Supplementary Note 1, the preference for the
parallel spin conﬁguration suggests that exchange interactions arising
from the electron-electron Coulomb repulsion between the electron in
the H atom and those in the surface Ni atoms play an important role in
the observed spin dependence. One possible interpretation of the
spin-dependent sticking is that an additional repulsive interaction may
give rise to an energy barrier in the potential-energy curve (PEC) for
the perpendicular spin conﬁguration, thereby effectively reducing the
adsorption probability.
We further consider the possible magnitude of the energy barrier
for the perpendicular spin conﬁguration. Since the mean translational
energy of the H beam in the present study is as low as 66 meV (the
normal component of the incidence energy is 33 meV at an incidence
angle of 45˚, Supplementary Fig. 1)22, if a spin-dependent barrier exists,
its height is expected to be on the order of several tens of meV, roughly
in the range of 10– 100 meV. Previous studies have reported very high
adsorption probabilities for non-spin-polarized H atoms on Ni(111). A
sticking probability of approximately 0.9 was observed for H atoms
with an incidence energy of 0.99 eV produced by photolysis19,a n da n
initial sticking probability close to unity was reported for H atoms
produced by a heated W tube (2000 K) 30,35. Although the incidence
energy was not reported in the latter study, it is expected to be sub-
stantially higher than that of the present H beam (66 meV). These
previous reports are therefore not inconsistent with the present
interpretation, because H atoms with such high kinetic energies would
readily overcome an energy barrier of the magnitude proposed here.
As in the case of H adsorption, the abstraction reaction was also
suppressed when an external magnetic ﬁeld is applied perpendicular
to the surface, although the effect was weaker as shown in Fig.3b. This
suggests that the surface magnetization remains in-plane for a
D-covered Ni(111) surface, and that there is an energy barrier for inci-
dent H atoms with a perpendicular spin orientation. According to a
previous theoretical study36 and our density-functional theory (DFT)
calculations (see Supplementary Fig. 2 and 4), the magnetic moment
per surface Ni atom is reduced by about 16 – 30% upon H adsorption.
We therefore speculate that the barrier height for the D-covered sur-
face is lower than that for a clean surface as a result of the reduced
magnetic moment. Such an in ﬂuence of adsorbed H may also be
relevant to H adsorption on Ni(111) as evidenced by the decrease in the
intensity ratio between the parallel-H and perpendicular-H conﬁgura-
tions with increasing H coverage as shown in Fig.2c. Moreover, as the
magnetic-ﬁeld strength decreases, the ratio of the HD yield decreases
as shown in Fig. 3b. This is most likely because the barrier height is
reduced in the perpendicular-H conﬁguration; the spin moment of H
becomes increasingly inclined toward the in-plane direction as the
resultant magneticﬁeld given by the sum of the external ﬁeld and the
ﬁeld due to Ni tilts toward the in-plane direction.
To gain insight into the mechanism of the energy barrier, we
performed preliminary DFT calculations for the spin-dependent PECs.
Although these calculations do not show an explicit adsorption barrier,
they indicate that the PECs depend on the relative spin orientation
between H and Ni (see SupplementaryFig. 2 and 4). Further theoretical
investigations are required to establish an atomic-level understanding
of the observed spin-dependent adsorption. In particular, it will be
necessary to determine spin-dependent potential-energy curves while
preserving the spin orientation and moment of the H atom. Such cal-
culations will clarify whether a spin-dependent adsorption barrier
exists on the potential-energy curves.
Even if a spin-dependent barrier is found on these PECs, this
would not necessarily explain the experimental observations. During
the approach of the H atom to the surface, the H spin orientation might
change, which corresponds to transitions between spin-dependent
PECs. The role of transition between spin-dependent PECs has been
discussed theoretically in the context of O2 adsorption on Si37 and Al38
surfaces; the incident O2 molecule is a spin-triplet state whereas the
ﬁnal adsorption state is a low-spin state. In those studies, non-adiabatic
transitions between spin states were proposed to in ﬂuence the
adsorption process, due to the absence of a barrier in the calculated
spin-adiabatic PECs available at that time. We note, however, that
subsequent studies employing screened hybrid approaches 39– 41 sug-
gested that such non-adiabatic spin-state transitions may not be
essential for describing O2 adsorption on Al surfaces. Likewise, whe-
ther transitions between spin-dependent PECs play an important role
in the present H/Ni(111) system remains an open question and warrants
further theoretical investigation.

Methods
Experimental methods
The experiments were carried out in an ultrahigh vacuum chamber at a
base pressure of 3.0 × 10−8 Pa equipped with a spin-polarized H beam
source22. The spin-polarized H beam was prepared with a combination
of a microwave discharge source consisting of a quartz tube with a
diameter of 10 mm and a subsequent beam collimation by a skimmer
with a diameter of 1 mm and a hexapole magnet. As a feed for the
hydrogen discharge, high purity H 2 (7 N) was used. To assist and
enhance the discharge, water of about 1% was mixed with the H 2 gas.
After reporting ref.22, a center stop (2 mm in diameter) was installed at
the entrance of the hexapole magnet to block non-dissociated H2 and
H2O and the down-spin component of H at the central region of the
hexapole magnet. In this setup, the main component of the H beam is
the up-spin state of an H atom. With trajectory simulations, the spin
polarization at the sample position taking account of the velocity
distribution and velocity-dependent focusing is estimated to be 51% as
(Iu-Id)/(Iu + Id), where Iu and Id denote the intensities of the up- and
down-spin states. The diameter of the up-spin H beam spot at the
sample surface is ≤8 mm at normal incidence.
The clean Ni(111) surface was obtained by Ar + sputtering with an
energy of 1 keV at room temperature, annealing at 1100 K, followed by
oxidation in 1.3 × 10−5 Pa of O2 and reduction in 1.3 × 10−4 Pa of H2.T h e

lowed by annealing to 750 K. The surface cleanliness and crystal-
lographic order were veriﬁed by TD spectra of H2

electron diffraction, respectively. A permanent magnet was positioned

whereas it was positioned perpendicular to the surface, corresponding
to the ½111/C138 direction, for the perpendicular- H conﬁguration. The
magnetic-ﬁeld strength at the sample position was controlled by

Theoretical calculation
DFT calculations were carried out with the VASP-code version
6.3.247– 51. We adopted the projector-augmented wave formalism 52,53,
with the Perdew-Burke-Enzerhoff generalized gradient approxima-
tion exchange correlation functional 54 and the electronic con-
vergence of 10 −7 eV and 450 eV for the cut off energy of the plane
wave basis set. We adopted the Monkhorst-Pack method55 to perform
the Brillouin zone integrations with 5 × 5 × 1 k-points. The 3 d and 4 s
electrons of Ni and the 1 s electron of H were treated as valence
electrons, while the remaining electrons were accounted for as core
electrons by using pseudo-potentials. We modeled the clean Ni(111)
surface as a periodic slab with a size of 3 × 3 × 3 separated by a
vacuum region of 20.0 A ̊.W ec o nﬁrmed that the results are essen-
tially unchanged for 4-layer and 5-layer slabs. We corrected the
interaction between adjacent Ni surfaces in the periodic boundary
condition by putting the dipole moment at the middle of the super-
cell with the dipole correction method 56. We considered the lattice
constant 3.524 Åfor fcc-Ni, and the atomic structure of Ni(111) sur-
faces was optimized by minimizing the atomic force within 0.001 eV/
Å. For PEC, the total energy relative to the energy at a suf ﬁciently
distant place (5 A ̊) from Ni(111) is calculated as a function of the
distance of H from the surface at fcc-hollow, hcp-hollow, bridge and
on-top sites by including the spin-orbit interaction. In the calcula-
tions, the magnetization direction of the Ni(111) surface was ﬁxed to
the ½2/C221/C221/C138 direction (in-plane direction of the surface). Then, we con-
sidered three directions of H spins, which are the [ /C221/C221/C221] direction
(perpendicular to the surface) and two in-plane directions on the
surface, the [2 /C221/C221] direction (parallel to the Ni spin) and the [ /C22211]
direction (antiparallel to the Ni spin). For the calculation of the spin-
dependent potential, the initial orientation of the H spin was set to be
perpendicular, parallel or antiparallel to the Ni majority spin, and the
electronic structure of the system was calculated in a self-consistent
manner. In the calculations, the spin directions and moments of all
atoms were fully relaxed. During the calculations, the orientation of
the H spin and the spin moments of H and Ni remained unchanged in
some cases, whereas in other cases they spontaneously changed. For
the calculation of local spin moments, the sphere sizes of H and Ni
are set to 0.37 Å and 1.286 Å, respectively.
