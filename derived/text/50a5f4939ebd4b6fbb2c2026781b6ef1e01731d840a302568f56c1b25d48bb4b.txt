Abstract:We study the pseudo-gauge transformation (PGT) freedom at freeze-out in
relativistic spin hydrodynamics. The Cooper–Frye map is shown to factor through the
quotient of freeze-out data by a universal stabilizer, yielding a stratified fibration over the
space of thermodynamic Lagrange multipliers. This classifies observables into base and
fiber types, bounds the number of independent PGT-sensitive observables by the family-
restricted fiber dimension, and implies cross-observable consistency relations. Applied to
heavy-ion polarization data, the fibration structure provides a structural interpretation
of the tension between Λ polarization andϕ-meson spin alignment as evidence that the
vorticity-dominated response sector may need to be enlarged with local field-correlation
data. We show that Weyl-anomaly-induced currents studied recently are classified as base
observables and recover the known Belinfante–canonical obstruction Ω ab̸=ϖ ab from the
stabilizer condition.

1 Introduction
The discovery of global Λ hyperon polarization in non-central heavy-ion collisions [1, 2] has
established a quantitative bridge between the bulk rotational dynamics of the quark-gluon
plasma (QGP) and the spin polarization of the produced hadrons. Subsequent observations
of vector-meson spin alignment [3] and of azimuthal-angle-dependent local spin polariza-
tion have stimulated an intense theoretical effort to understand spin transport in relativistic
fluids and the freeze-out conversion of fluid spin data into hadronic observables [4, 5]. Fol-
lowing the standard sharp-switching idealization of heavy-ion hydrodynamics, we model
the spin-hydrodynamic stage of the QGP as a four-dimensional domainM 4 whose future
boundary contains a three-dimensional freeze-out hypersurface Σ FO [4, 6]. On this hyper-
surface a Cooper–Frye prescription [7] converts surface hydrodynamic data into hadronic
spectra and polarizations [8–11].
A persistent obstruction to making this conversion precise is the pseudo-gauge trans-
formation (PGT) freedom of the spin tensor [12–17]. The decomposition of the conserved
angular momentum current into an orbital and a spin part is not unique: different pseudo-
gauges, related by a PGT generator Φλ,µν, give different stress-energy and spin tensors that
share the same total energy-momentum and angular momentum but differ at the level of
local densities. Buzzegoli’s explicit computation [18] establishes that the standard formulae

===== PAGE 3 =====
for Λ polarization and for the axial vortical conductivity are sensitive to this PGT choice.
In particular, the canonical–Belinfante shift of the spin-polarization vectorS µ(k) contains
the freeze-out integral of (ϖ µν−Ωµν), whereϖ µν is the thermal vorticity and Ω µν is the
spin potential.
The novelty of the present work is not the observation that local-equilibrium spin ob-
servables can depend on the pseudo-gauge choice, but the formulation of this dependence
as a universal stabilizer problem for the freeze-out map. In contrast to approaches that re-
move the ambiguity by imposing pseudo-gauge invariance directly on the local-equilibrium
density operator, we keep the fixed-multiplier Cooper–Frye construction and identify the
subgroupH univ of pseudo-gauge transformations that is invisible to all admissible readouts.
This leads to the quotient structureQ FO =R FO/Huniv, to a base/fiber classification of ob-
servables, and to a finite-family counting criterion for residual PGT-sensitive directions.
The key technical input is a Bogoliubov–Kubo–Mori (BKM) argument proving that
the c-number shift condition on ∆ Φ ˆKLE is both necessary and sufficient for observable
invariance. The quotientQ FO is naturally fibered over the spaceM phys of thermodynamic
Lagrange multipliers, and the framework predicts cross-observable consistency relations:
for several fiber observables measured in the same kinematic bin, their simultaneous values
must lie on the image of a common RSH and pseudo-gauge response slice. We apply this
to the joint Λ-hyperon-polarization andϕ-meson-spin-alignment sector and show that the
consistency condition implied by the fibration picture provides a structural explanation
of the local field-correlation mechanism proposed in [19]: the apparent tension with a
narrow vorticity-dominated description is resolved by enlarging the freeze-out response
space, with the added sector approximately invisible to Λ polarization but visible toϕ-
meson spin alignment. Finally, as an explicit check, the canonical-to-Belinfante PGT for a
Dirac spin fluid on a planar Σ FO recovers Buzzegoli’s obstruction Ωab̸=ϖ ab directly from
the universal stabilizer condition.
The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we set up the local-equilibrium chain,
derive the central insertion formula (2.10), and prove the universal stabilizer theorem. In
section 3 we establish that the universal stabilizer saturates the full pseudo-gauge group at
global thermodynamic equilibrium, and we carry out the canonical-to-Belinfante test as the
simplest one-parameter linearization away from this saturated endpoint. Section 4 develops
the fibration picture and the resulting classification of physical observables. Section 5
extracts the framework-level predictions. We conclude in section 6.

6 Discussion
We have given a geometric formulation of the pseudo-gauge dependence of Cooper–Frye
observables at freeze-out. The central object is the universal stabilizerH univ defined by the
condition ∆ Φ ˆKLE =c Φ1, which is necessary and sufficient for any observable computed
from ˆρLE to be pseudo-gauge invariant. For each chosen observableO, the Cooper–Frye
readoutF O factors exactly through the quotientQ FO =R FO/Huniv, while the residual
quotient is fibered over the spaceMphys of thermodynamic Lagrange multipliers with state-
dependent fiberG PGT/Huniv[β,Ω,ξ]. The non-trivial fiber is the obstruction underlying
the Buzzegoli effect; its dimension upper-bounds the number of independent PG-sensitive
observables and supplies a target for future searches beyond the canonical–Belinfante com-
parison.
The two-class classification of observables, base observables onM phys versus fiber
observables onQ FO, gives a sharp criterion for which polarization moments are intrinsi-
cally pseudo-gauge robust and which carry residual pseudo-gauge dependence. The cross-
observable consistency relations (5.15) provide multi-observable self-consistency tests that
are absent from standard analyses and could be tested by simultaneous measurement of
Λ hyperon polarization [1, 2] andϕ-meson spin alignment [3] in the same kinematic bins.
They also give a structural interpretation of the mechanism proposed in [19]: the large
ρϕ
00−1/3 signal need not contradict a narrow vorticity-based description of Λ polarization,
but rather points to additional freeze-out response data that are approximately invisible
to the Λ spin-vector observable and visible to theϕ-meson tensor-alignment observable.
