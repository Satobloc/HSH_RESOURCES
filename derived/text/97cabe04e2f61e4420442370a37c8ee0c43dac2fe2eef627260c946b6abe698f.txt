Negative energies states in TTBM
Giuseppe Ragun´ ı∗
Departamento de F´ ısica, Universidad de Murcia, E-30071 Murcia, Spain.
It is shown that within TTBM — a recent deterministic theory in which the apparently paradoxical
aspects of Quantum Mechanics are the effect of the existence of an extra unobservable time —
negative energy states represent particles that do not respect the uncertainty principle and have
therefore never been observed. This property could be interpreted as detrimental to the theory, but
it is nevertheless compatible with the symmetric theory proposed by Majorana in 1937 and still
having to be tested by experience.
Keywords: Quantum Mechanics Foundations; Negative energies; Antimatter; Majorana’s sym-
metric theory.
I. INTRODUCTION
TTBM [1–3] arises as a result of literally taking Feyn-
man’s sum over history interpretation [4–8]. Determin-
ism is restored through the introduction of extra hidden
dimension of time, calledτ. Movements inτ, caused by
a Bohmian-type quantum potential, are instantaneous
with respect to the usual time and, while not directly
observable, are supposed to generate the quantum uncer-
tainties of the observables. For a particle withpositive
energy, the famousZitterbewegungmotion [9, 10] is de-
duced and a more general law of it, free from the call of
antimatter, is achieved. However, the case of a particle
withnegativeenergy has not yet been studied: what mo-
tion does the quantum potential generate in that case?
The following section presents the fundamental con-
cepts and basic equations of the TTBM theory and the
next one studies the case of negative-energy free states. A
final section discusses the results, comparing them with
the standard theory and with the one proposed by Majo-
rana in 1937, which still awaits experimental verification.

In conclusion, in TTBM, a sufficiently negative energy
state can be associated with a positive-mass particle with
rapidly vanishingτ-motions; this means that, according

uncertainty principle.
IV. DISCUSSION
Particles of this type, if they exist, have not yet been
observed: all known particles (including antimatter [12–
14]) obey the principles of quantum mechanics. But the
real flaw for TTBM would be that it is incapable to de-
scribe the negative-energy states that standard Quantum
Field Theory (QFT) associates with antiparticle creation
operators [15, 16]. However, this interpretation has not
yet been proven to be necessary. A symmetric theory pro-
posed by Majorana in 1937 reinterprets these states as
positive-energy states with opposite charges [17]. Only
by a curious fate, linked to an unfortunate neglect of
Majorana’s work, this theory is still considered an exotic
hypothesis today, when in fact it is a simpler model with
several advantages that are certainly worth reiterating
[18–20]:
1. By losing the need for antimatter to be something
intrinsically different from matter, observed reality
is halved.
2. The Dirac equation can be derived by exploiting
the variational principle starting from a suitable
Lagrangian.
3. The previous deduction also yields the correct
fermionic statistics; while in QFT it must be added
”by hand”.
4. The above advantages do not detract the modus
operandi of QFT, which continues to function nor-
mally.
Even considering only the first point, it seems that
Occam’s razor has not yet been applied to the standard
viewpoint; although, certainly, the experiments must
have the last word. If Majorana’s theory is correct, then
antimatter is an ultimately useless concept, since an an-
tiparticle can be considered a normal particle with op-
posite charges; in particular, neutrinos and antineutrinos
would coincide. TTBM would be perfectly integrated
into this model. As for its prediction of particles that
have not yet been observed, this is certainly not an in-
consistency, since nothing forces everything mathemati-
cally possible to exist. And, however, experiments could
reveal surprises.
