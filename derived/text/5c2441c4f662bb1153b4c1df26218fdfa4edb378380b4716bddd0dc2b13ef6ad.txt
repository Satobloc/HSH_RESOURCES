Abstract
We report a discovery-style official-data spectroscopy result under the MMA-DMF one-
scale hadronic closure. The result is not the discovery of a new experimental particle by this
author and not collaboration endorsement. The discovery claim is an organizing no-retuning
injected-scale law: public toponium-like threshold morphology and doubly-charmed partner-
family spectroscopy remain classifiable under one MMA-DMF composite-spectroscopy ledger
without manual Yukawa assignment, state-by-state retuning, or independent ontology for
every newly resolved peak. The public products used are CMS and LHCb papers, HEPData
records, PDG listings, CMS Open Data metadata, luminosity/normtag/certification files,
and an archived public NANOAOD acquisition scaffold with verified ZIP integrity.
The nontrivial result is not that the retained states lie below 100 TeV; it is that their
threshold, spin-parity, flavor-family and public-status structure can be organized by a
common no-retuning ledger. The CMS near-threshold pseudoscalar excess in pp→t¯t +X at√s = 13 TeV and 138 fb−1 is retained as the main scale-law branch: injected energy resolves
a composite response mode whose public line-shape and parity morphology are retained
without assigning a manual Yukawa knob. The HEPData ηt cross section and parity scan
are therefore official morphology inputs, not a new measurement by this paper. A separate
CMS heavy scalar/pseudoscalar search is used as an anti-overclaim control, distinguishing a
threshold/quasi-bound reading from a new-elementary-boson ontology claim.

1 Introduction
Collider physics often discovers structure by increasing energy, luminosity, acceptance, recon-
struction quality, and flavor reach. That operational fact can be read in two very different
ways. The first reading is ontological multiplication: every newly resolved threshold, resonance,
excess, or spectroscopy entry is treated as a candidate for a new fundamental object. The second
reading is spectroscopic deepening: higher reach resolves more internal modes, flavor partners,
threshold structures, quasi-bound states, angular correlations, and rare channels of an already
existing composite system.
The present paper develops the second reading inside the MMA-DMF one-scale hadronic
closure. This paper challenges ontology inflation, not the experimental observations. More
collider energy does not automatically mean more fundamental ontology; it may simply reveal
more internal spectroscopy of the same underlying system. In the balloon analogy used in the
author’s public explanation, adding air corresponds to increasing injected energy, luminosity and
detector reach; the larger balloon corresponds to more resolved states, thresholds, resonances
and line shapes; a new balloon would correspond to a new fundamental ontology. The claim
here is that the public heavy-composite spectrum is first tested as the same balloon with more
resolved internal structure, not as a new balloon every time a feature appears.
The organizing claim is ontological and scale-theoretic. Peaks are not treated as independent
fundamental objects by default. They are the experimental face of modes. A collider threshold
excess, an X-ray spectral-polarimetric feature, a QPO peak, a heavy-ion medium-response
correlation, or a toponium-like line-shape component can all be detector-resolved signatures of
the same deeper type of phenomenon: injected energy or environmental stress excites a structured
vacuum/confinement response, and the instrument resolves that response as a peak, threshold,
line shape, mode, or correlation. This paper applies that rule only to the heavy-composite
collider branch. It is motivated by two public collider facts. First, CMS reports a near-threshold
pseudoscalar excess in t¯t production at √s = 13 TeV using 138 fb−1, with an interpretation
consistent with a color-singlet pseudoscalar 1S[1]
0 quasi-bound toponium state and an observed
excess cross section of 8.8+1.2
−1.4 pb [1, 2]. Second, LHCb has observed doubly-charmed baryons,
beginning with Ξ ++
cc in the Λ +
cK−π+π+ channel at 13 TeV [5], followed by additional decay and
lifetime measurements [ 6, 7], and a Run-3 observation of Ξ +
cc [8]. The public data therefore
already contain both a heavy t¯t threshold branch and a double-heavy baryon branch.
The question asked here is not whether these observations are surprising in conventional
QCD or NRQCD. The paper does not attempt to replace the experimental publications and
does not perform a detector-level reanalysis. The question is instead a bounded framework
question: can these public observables be organized under a one-scale composite-spectroscopy
closure with no new per-state tuning and no automatic promotion of every threshold feature
into a new fundamental ontology?
The answer is stronger than a compatibility statement, but narrower than an experimental
particle-discovery claim. The retained discovery is a no-retuning scale-law pattern: the toponium-
like threshold branch and the doubly-charmed partner-family branch are classifiable under
a common one-scale composite ledger without manual Yukawa assignment or state-by-state
parameter insertion. In ordinary language, the collider does not create a new ontological object
every time energy or luminosity resolves another structure; it reveals additional morphology of a
scale-controlled composite spectrum.
The toponium-like state is retained as aq¯q threshold-spectroscopy datum belowM = 100 TeV.
The doubly-charmed states are retained as qqq heavy-flavor spectroscopy below the same scale.
The paper also identifies what is not claimed. It does not claim collaboration endorsement,
independent detector-level reproduction, raw-event reprocessing, new cross-section extraction,
direct validation of state-by-state binding energies, or a proof that the Standard Model labels
are fundamental objects inside MMA-DMF.

2 Claim Boundary and Evidence Classes
The paper uses bounded claim language. A public-observable claim is a claim made from
values, tables, plots, likelihood scans, masses, lifetimes, channels, or status labels published by
experimental collaborations, PDG, HEPData, or arXiv papers. A framework-retention claim says
that a public observable can be organized under the local equation spine without contradiction
and without retuning. A symbolic gate is a formula whose state-specific numerical value is not
claimed because a transition operator, binding term, or spin correction has not been locked for
that state. A non-claim is an analysis layer explicitly not performed.

3 Data, Provenance, and Public-File Policy
The analysis uses an official public-data chain rather than a narrative-only source list. The
first class is public experimental papers: CMS TOP and HIG papers for the t¯t threshold
and scalar/pseudoscalar control; LHCb papers for doubly-charmed baryon observations and
decays. The second class is HEPData: the CMS TOP record for the ηt cross section and
parity scan, and the CMS HIG record for limits and likelihood grids. The third class is
PDG: quark masses, top-quark properties, and doubly-charmed baryon listings. The fourth
class is a CMS Open Data acquisition scaffold: public NANOAOD-derived ROOT files, CMS
luminosity/normtag/certification metadata, file indexes, and a four-part ZIP integrity manifest.
This fourth class upgrades the paper from a purely interpretive note to a claim-bounded
official-data audit, while still not making a detector-level reanalysis claim.
The operational rule is that high-level official observables and official public files may enter
the source ledger, while detector-internal reconstruction, raw-event reprocessing, and new cross-
section extraction remain outside scope. A large public-data volume is not used as a rhetorical
substitute for analysis. It is documented through explicit file families, manifests, ZIP checksums,
and the claim-boundary table; the claim-bearing numerical values remain those reproduced in
the HEPData/PDG/LHCb/CMS layers and mapped to local equations below.

3.1 What is added beyond a conventional QCD/NRQCD catalogue
This manuscript does not claim to replace the CMS or LHCb analyses, nor does it deny the
conventional QCD/NRQCD language used by those collaborations. The added MMA-DMF
content is not submission to a conventional catalogue but a no-retuning ledger discipline. Previous
MMA-DMF papers define the one-scale vacuum-rigidity setting, topological-friction mass reading,
geometric-charge replacement of continuous Yukawa inputs, and the interpretation of the 125GeV
scalar as an effective breathing mode of vacuum rigidity rather than a final ontological mass
source [21, 22, 23, 24]. The same one-scale gate, mass/width/status rule, spin-parity guard,
production-language guard and no-retuning constraint are applied to both the t¯t threshold
branch and the ccq baryon branch. A conventional catalogue can say that these are different
hadron-spectroscopy entries; the present paper asks whether they remain classifiable in one
composite ledger without adding independent ad hoc parameters for each newly resolved state.
This is the nontrivial part of the evolutionary prediction in Section 10: future entries should
thicken structured partner families, thresholds and splittings, not force independent ontology for
each peak.

13 Discussion
The strongest part of the paper is not a large new numerical fit. It is a disciplined reclassification
of a collider pattern. Increasing energy does not automatically create a separate ontology at
every new feature. It opens phase space and detector reach. That reach thickens spectroscopy.
The toponium-like branch is a clean example because the public CMS interpretation already
points toward quasi-bound t¯t threshold structure. The MMA-DMF contribution is to place
that structure inside a one-scale closure and protect the interpretation against both extremes:
denying the public threshold result, or overclaiming it as a new fundamental object.

I Detailed Future Falsification and Upgrade Path
The paper is designed to be upgradeable without rewriting its core claim. The upgrade conditions
are intentionally strict.
I.1 Toponium branch upgrade
The toponium branch would strengthen if future public analyses supply finer threshold scans,
improved angular correlations, public covariance matrices, and public likelihood surfaces that
continue to prefer a quasi-bound threshold contribution over smooth continuum-only backgrounds.
The branch would weaken if the excess disappears under improved modeling, if the parity-sensitive
observables cease to favor the pseudoscalar threshold component, or if a high-mass new-particle
interpretation becomes decisively favored by a public likelihood comparison. The present paper
does not need that future outcome; it only states the current retained public-observable branch.

The double-charm branch strengthens with more Ξ ++

upgrade would be a public table of ccq ground and excited states with masses, widths, lifetimes,
channels and spin-parity assignments.
I.3 Energy-evolution prediction upgrade
The energy-evolution prediction strengthens if future higher-luminosity or higher-energy runs
resolve additional threshold states and heavy-flavor partners in a structured pattern. It weakens
if additional features remain isolated and require unrelated explanation for each new resonance.
This is the collider form of the balloon analogy: more energy increases the accessible internal
volume of the same system; it does not by itself create a new balloon at every increment.

K Long-Form Interpretation of the Final Claim Set
The final claim set should be read as a narrow spectroscopy result. The paper does not ask
whether all of particle physics should be rewritten from collider observables alone. It asks
whether two public heavy-sector signals - one t¯t threshold branch and one double-charm baryon
branch - can be organized under one composite-spectroscopy closure without adding a new ad
hoc degree of freedom for each observed feature.
The answer is affirmative because the two branches occupy complementary regions of the
same argument. Toponium tests the high-mass threshold side: a near-2 mt line-shape structure
at hundreds of GeV. Double charm tests the heavy-flavor baryon side: ccq states at a few GeV.
Both are far below 100 TeV and both are naturally composite in public experimental language.
That is exactly the domain in which a one-scale composite closure should operate.
The paper is also deliberately conservative. It does not try to convert every public label into
a direct MMA-DMF proof. The mass closure marks state-specific residuals symbolic unless the
topological binding and spin terms are locked. The width equations mark channel transitions
symbolic unless the transition operator is locked. The production equation accepts public cross
sections but does not claim to reproduce detector-level selections. This conservative discipline is
why the result is defensible.
