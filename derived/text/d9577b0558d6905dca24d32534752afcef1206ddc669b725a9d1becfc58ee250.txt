Abstract
The Active Vacuum Framework (AVF) treats the vacuum as an active energy dis-
tribution possessing a uniform Poincar´ e invariant ambient energy densityA. Matter
produces a local depletion of this energy density, giving rise to a deformation field that
accounts for the physical effects ordinarily attributed to gravitation. This paper de-
velops the relativistic extension of the AVF through two more postulates: (i) first, the
local vacuum response delay is inversely proportional to the available vacuum-energy
density; (ii) second, the response delay mirrors the relativistic time-dilation behaviour
established by Special Relativity (SR). These two postulates are used to define the gen-
eral non-linear deformation law uniquely, which reproduces the temporal and spatial
structure of the Schwarzschild solution exactly, with the horizon following as a con-
sequence rather than being imposed. The framework’s own requirement that a scalar
cannot carry the deformation once the source is in motion is then made explicit and
extended to a symmetric rank-two tensor field, of which the scalar law is the static,
spherically symmetric component. That tensor is required as a structural necessity; it
is not identified with the spacetime metric, and no field equation of general relativity
is imposed upon it.
1 Introduction
The Active Vacuum Framework (AVF) treats the vacuum as a real, omnipresent energy
distribution of densityA, identified with the measured dark-energy density, and treats mat-
ter as a localised confinement of that energy. In the companion paper that establishes
the framework’s foundations [1], five postulates fix the vacuum as omnipresent, matter as
confined vacuum energy, the ambient background as uniform and Poincar´ e invariant, the
vacuum as the sole active agent, and the gradient of the vacuum-energy deformation as the
origin of force. From these, the Newtonian deformation profileD G(r) =AGM/(c 2r) and
Newton’s law of gravitation follow directly, with the deformation deepest near the source
and decreasing as 1/r.
Those five postulates are stated entirely in the language of static configurations: mass,
spatial gradient, and the minimisation of total energy. None of them refers to motion, to

This paper develops the relativistic extension of the AVF through two more postulates: (i)
first, a property of vacuum energy: the local vacuum response delay is inversely proportional
to the available vacuum-energy density; (ii) second, the response delay behaviour mirrors
the relativistic time-dilation behaviour established by Special Relativity (SR) [2, 3]. These
postulates together relate the deformation profile at radiusrto the Lorentz factor of a
free-fall trajectory.
The framework’s own internal requirement, that deformation in the relativistic regime
cannot be represented by a single scalar once the source is in motion, is then made explicit
and extended to a symmetric rank-2 tensor field, of which the scalar law derived here is the
static, spherically symmetric component. It does not identify that tensor with the spacetime
metric, and it imposes no field equation upon it.
2 Foundational Postulates of A VF
The AVF begins with five foundational postulates that define the nature of the vacuum,
matter, and force.
Postulate 1(The Vacuum Is Omnipresent).The vacuum permeates all physical space at
every scale. It is present wherever matter is not.
Postulate 2(Matter Is Localised Vacuum Energy).Matter is a localised confinement of
vacuum energy. The formation of any material object confines a portion of vacuum energy
within a finite region of space.
Postulate 3(Ambient Vacuum Energy Is Uniform and Active).Ambient vacuum energy
is distributed uniformly throughout space with energy densityA. This ambient energy is
dynamically active: it generates forces and governs the acceleration of material objects.
Postulate 4(The Vacuum Is the Sole Active Agent).Only the vacuum generates force and
produces acceleration. Material objects are passive. They do not directly exert forces on one
another. All observed forces arise from the dynamics of the ambient vacuum energy acting
on passive material objects.
Postulate 5(Deformation of Ambient Energy Generates Force).Material entities locally
deform the ambient vacuum-energy density. The force on a material object at any point is
proportional to the local spatial gradient of the vacuum-energy deformation. Physical systems
evolve toward configurations that minimise total energy.
These five postulates are the foundation of AVF. The next section introduces a sixth and
a seventh postulate that extend the framework to the relativistic regime.

4 Structural Axioms for Full Relativistic Extension
The five basis postulates of AVF provide the linear deformation law for a single static, spher-
ically symmetric source. Two things separate that result from a full relativistic theory: the
source may move, and the field must respond to changes causally rather than instantaneously.
The presence of several sources at once is already handled by the linear superposition of the
foundational deformation law. The two new postulates extend the linear deformation law
to a non-linear relativistic regime. We need two more axioms, as stated below, to provide
exactly the structure needed for the full relativistic extension of AVF.
Axiom 1(Lorentz Covariance).The laws governing vacuum deformation are invariant un-
der local Lorentz transformations, and changes in the deformation field propagate causally
at the speed of lightc. This is the covariance requirement of SR. No requirement of invari-
ance under general coordinate transformations, and no commitment to a dynamical, curved
spacetime, is made here.
Basis.SR is independently confirmed physics; a framework whose laws are not invariant un-
der Lorentz transformations would predict different physics for observers in uniform relative
motion, contrary to every relevant experimental test.
Implication.This axiom is what licenses the appearance of the Lorentz factorγ(r) in Postu-
late 6 at all: without a prior commitment to Lorentz covariance, there would be no reason
to expect a relativistic correction built fromγ, rather than a Galilean correction, or no
velocity-dependent correction at all, to be the relevant one. Axiom 1 is therefore the struc-
tural justification for the specific form Postulate 6 takes, not an independent, unrelated
requirement.
Relevance to the relativistic extension.This axiom is the engine of the entire extension, and
it does more than license the Lorentz factor already used. Applied to the static, spherically
symmetric solution, Lorentz covariance fixes the field of a moving source from the field of a
stationary one: the field seen when the source moves is obtained by boosting the static result.
That boosted field is no longer spherically symmetric. It is compressed in the directions
transverse to the motion and acquires components that depend on the velocity of the source,
in the same way that the electric field of a moving charge does. Lorentz covariance therefore
does not merely permit a relativistic correction to be made; applied to the static scalar
result, it actively generates directional, motion-dependent structure that no scalar function
of position can hold. This is the precise point at which the scalar description fails, and it is
what makes the tensor of Axiom 2 unavoidable rather than optional.
Axiom 2(Tensorial Structure of the Relativistic Deformation).In the relativistic regime,
a single scalar field is insufficient to describe vacuum deformation: the deformation must,

in general, depend on direction relative to the source’s motion, requiring a symmetric rank-

Basis.A scalar field can depend only on distance from its source, not on direction. Once the
source is in motion, or once the deformation is examined by an observer in relative motion,
the deformation it produces is no longer isotropic: it is stronger ahead of the motion than
behind it, a directional asymmetry no scalar field can represent, in direct analogy with the
boosted and gravitomagnetic fields of a moving mass already recognised as real effects.
Implication.This rules out, in principle, any attempt to extend Postulate 6 to a moving
source using only the scalarH G(r)/Aevaluated at a displaced position; the genuinely rela-
tivistic deformation of a moving source requires components beyond what a scalar field can
carry. It does not, by itself, specify what those components are, or what equation governs
them, only that scalar deformation cannot be the whole story once motion is admitted.
Relevance to the relativistic extension.This axiom records the direct consequence of the
preceding one. Because boosting the static field produces anisotropy and velocity-dependent
components, the relativistic deformation must be carried by an object capable of representing
both direction and motion, and the minimal such object is a symmetric rank-2 tensor whose
static, spherically symmetric, rest-frame component is the scalar law derived above. The
gravitomagnetic field of a moving mass, a real and measured effect, is the concrete physical
instance of structure that a scalar potential cannot describe and a tensor can. What this
axiom deliberately does not do is fix the tensor’s dynamics. It establishes that a tensor is
required and identifies the scalar law as one of its components, while leaving entirely open
what equation governs that tensor, and in particular whether that equation is, or is not, the
one of general relativity. That open question, and the restraint behind it, are the subject of
the closing remark.
Remark 4(What is deliberately not assumed).Axiom 2 requires that the relativistic defor-
mation be tensor-valued; it does not identify this tensor with the spacetime metricg µν, and
no equation of the formR µν = 0,G µν = 8πGTµν/c4, or any other relation specific to general
relativity is imposed on it anywhere in this paper. The tensorial requirement is a structural
necessity, independent of whether that tensor turns out to coincide with, or differ from, the
metric of general relativity; that question is left open.
5 Conclusion
The relativistic extension of the Active Vacuum Framework requires two new postulates
beyond the original five. The vacuum’s response delay at a point is the relativistic time-
dilation factor of a free-fall trajectory there, and the local vacuum-energy fraction is its
inverse. Built from Newtonian energy conservation and SR alone, these postulates fix the
nonlinear deformation law uniquely, with no free exponent, and reproduce the temporal and
spatial structure of the Schwarzschild solution, with the horizon following as a consequence
rather than an imported result. Time dilation and spatial stretching emerge as two aspects
of the same function.
