The concept of the irreducible mass ( Mirr) has led to the mass-energy ( M) formula of a Kerr-
Newman black hole (BH), in turn leading to an expression for its surface area S = 16πM 2
irr. This
allowed as well the coeval identification of the reversible and irreversible transformations. The
concepts of extracted and extractable energy soon followed. This new conceptual framework avoids
inconsistencies recently evidenced in a repetitive Penrose process. We consider repetitive decays in
the ergosphere of an initially extreme Kerr BH and show that these processes are highly irreversible.
For each decay, including the BH capture of the particle falling into the horizon, the increase of
the irreducible mass is much larger than the extracted energy. Correspondingly, the horizon radius
increases. The energy extraction process saturates and stops when the horizon reaches the decay
radius. Thus, reaching a final non-rotating Schwarzschild BH state by this accretion process is
impossible. We have assessed such processes for three selected decay radii, r = 1.2M, 1.5M, and
1.9M. The energy extracted until saturation is only 2 .75%, 4.54%, and 2.45% of the BH initial
mass, while the extractable energy is reduced by 25%, 56%, and 92%. The BH rotational energy
is mostly converted into irreducible mass, which increases to 75%, 83%, and 95% of the initial BH
mass. Therefore, evaluating the effect of the irreducible mass increase in the energy extraction
processes in the Kerr BH ergosphere is now mandatory.
INTRODUCTION
In a recent article [1] (hereafter Paper I), we have re-
visited the equations describing a single Penrose process
of a test particle µ0 that decays into two particles µ1
and µ2 in the ergosphere of an extreme Kerr black hole
(BH) with mass M and spin ˆa =a/M = 1. We assumed
µ0 = 10−2M, fulfilling the condition of being a test parti-
cle. We addressed the decay process in three radii inside
the BH ergosphere: r = 1.2M, 1.5M, and 1.9M. The
basic assumption to describe the process was that the
decay of the test particles would occur at the turning
points of their trajectories. We have further shown that
to identify a specific process, we could adopt two addi-
tional conditions: one that the angular momentum of the
particleµ1 entering the BH could be assumed in all three
decay processes to be equal to pϕ1 =−19.434µ1M and
that the ratio of the two particlesµ2/µ1 = 0.78345. This
choice has the advantage, shown in Paper I, of leading
to a working solution of the decay equations (see Sec-
tion II therein). Still, in Paper I, we also showed that
if one turns from a single Penrose process, which is lin-
ear in the BH mass and angular momentum, an attempt
to apply this linear process into a sequence of repetitive
events leads to a violation of energy conservation. Thus,
a nonlinear treatment of the mass and angular momen-
tum becomes necessary. The topic of this new paper is
considering the nonlinear relation between the BH mass,
angular momentum, and the irreducible mass [2]. The
latter separates the BH transformations into reversible
ones that keep constant the irreducible mass and irre-
versible ones in which it monotonically increases.
To approach this problem in the most general formu-
lation, we express in Section the general equations for
the mass-energy of a Kerr Newman metric characterized
by mass, charge, angular momentum, and the irreducible
mass [3, 4]. We also give the upper limits to the charge
and spin parameters and the expression of the horizon
surface area. In addition to these classic results, we
also express the inverse relation relating the irreducible
mass to the BH mass, spin, and charge. In total gener-
ality, fulfilling the above limitations, we also introduce
the extracted energies presented in Paper I and the novel
concept of extractable energy. We recall how the maxi-

CONCLUSIONS
We have considered self-consistently the change in the
BH mass and angular momentum at every step in a repet-
itive sequence of Penrose processes in the ergosphere of

===== PAGE 6 =====
6
an initially extreme Kerr BH. We have shown that this
leads to a nonlinear increase of the BH irreducible mass
and, hence, of the horizon radius. The above effect, con-
sistent with the BH mass-energy formula, leads to the end
of the repetitive process once the horizon reaches the de-
cay radius, leading to major consequences for repetitive
Penrose processes:
1. The energy extracted by the end of the processes is
only a small fraction of the initial Kerr BH mass.
2. For decays near the BH horizon (e.g., r = 1.2M),
the horizon reaches the decay radius after a few
tens of decays. It leads to small energy extracted
(≲ 3%), so the final BH mass, spin, and extractable
energy remain close to their initial values.
3. The larger the decay radius, the larger the decay
number until the process stops (e.g., for r = 1.9M,
after 205 decays). The final spin and the ex-
tractable energy are largely reduced, while the en-
ergy extracted remains little, ≲ 3% of the initial
BH mass. Therefore, in these cases, the BH rota-
tional energy has been spent mostly on increasing
the irreducible mass, as shown by direct calculation
of the latter (see Fig. 2).
From the above points, we conclude that the repet-
itive Penrose processes either change very little the BH
spin and the rotational energy, leaving most of it to be ex-
tracted, or it can approach a final state of a Schwarzschild
BH but by converting the BH rotational energy into irre-
ducible mass, without real energy extraction. Therefore,
it is mandatory to consider self-consistently for the effect
of the irreducible mass increase in the energy extraction
processes in the Kerr BH ergosphere.
As a major consequence, we are brought to the quest
for alternative BH energy extraction processes that could
approach reversibility as much as possible, i.e., keeping
the increase of the irreducible mass as small as possible.
Electrodynamical processes in the vicinity of a Kerr BH
embedded in a magnetic field and surrounded by ionized
