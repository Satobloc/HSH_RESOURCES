FRACTIONAL HARDY INEQUALITIES AND CAPACITY DENSITY
LIZAVETA IHNATSYEVA, KAUSHIK MOHANTA, AND ANTTI V. V¨AH¨AKANGAS
Abstract. We prove that a pointwise fractional Hardy inequality implies a fractional Hardy
inequality, deﬁned via a Gagliardo-type seminorm. The proof consist s of two main parts. The
ﬁrst one is to characterize the pointwise fractional Hardy inequalit y in terms of a fractional
capacity density condition. The second part is to show the deep ope n-endedness or self-
improvement property of the fractional capacity density, which w e accomplish in the setting
of a complete geodesic space equipped with a doubling measure. These results are new already
in the standard Euclidean setting.

[39, 7, 6, 36]. The motivation for our nonlocal problems comes from their local an alogues.

A more speciﬁc outline for the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the standing
assumptions and notation, and recall some concepts that are nee ded later on. For instance,
the fractional Poincar´ e inequalities are recalled from [10]. An important notion of a fractional
relative capacity is deﬁned in Section 3, where we also give estimates for relative capacities of
balls and lower bounds for relative capacities of closed sets in terms of Hausdorﬀ contents. The
fractional (β,p,q )-capacity density condition is introduced in Section 4, where its equivalence
with the pointwise (β,p,q )-Hardy inequality (3) is also shown. In Section 5 we deﬁne certain
Haj/suppress lasz–Triebel–Lizorkin seminorms and we show in Section6 that these seminorms are often
comparable to Gagliardo-type seminorms that are used to deﬁne th e fractional capacities. A
Sobolev–Poincar´ e type inequality is a key tool here, see Lemma 5.3. The comparability of
fractional and Haj/suppress lasz–Triebel–Lizorkin relative capacities followsfrom the equivalence of the
corresponding seminorms, as it is shown in Section 7. Using the capacity comparison and
results from [ 36], we obtain a q-independence property for the fractional relative capacity in
Theorem 7.6; we refer to [2, Sections 3.6 and 4.4] for analogous properties of Triebel–Lizorkin
capacities in Euclidean spaces. Using the capacity comparison result s from Section 7 and [36,
Theorem 11.4], in Section 8 we show that the fractional ( β,p,q )-capacity density condition
has three ways of self-improvement, with respect to parameters β, p and q, see Theorem 8.5.
Our main results are proved in the last Section 9, including Theorem 9.3 that follows from
the self-improvement of the pointwise Hardy inequality in Theorem 9.2.
Finally, we make some remarks on the line of research developed in [ 39], [7], [6], [36]. The
origins of our results can be traced to the seminal work by Lewis [ 39], who proved that a
Riesz (β,p )-capacity density condition in Euclidean spaces is doubly self-improv ing, that is,
self-improving in both parameters β and p. More recently, a Haj/suppress lasz (β,p )-capacity density
condition in metric spaces was introduced in [7] by using Haj/suppress lasz gradients of order 0<β ≤ 1.
It was also shown that this condition is doubly self-improving, if X is a complete geodesic
space. The comparability of Riesz and Haj/suppress lasz (β,p )-capacities was shown in [6], where Lewis’
result was also transferred to complete geodesic spaces. A ﬂexible and a priori more general
Haj/suppress lasz–Triebel–Lizorkin (β,p,q )-capacity density condition was introduced in [ 36] by using
fractional Haj/suppress lasz gradients and Haj/suppress lasz–Triebel–Lizorkin seminorms, see Deﬁnition 8.1. The
Riesz (β,p )-capacity density condition for 0 < β < 1 is its special case, corresponding to
the indices (β,p, ∞). It was shown in [ 36] that the Haj/suppress lasz–Triebel–Lizorkin (β,p,q )-capacity
density condition is self-improving in all three parameters β, p, q and, in fact, independent
of the q-parameter, under suitable assumptions. Consequently, the a priori strongest Riesz
(β,p )-capacity density condition is equivalent to a Haj/suppress lasz–Triebel–Lizorkin (β,p,q )-capacity
density condition, for all 1 <q ≤ ∞. Theorems 7.6 and 8.5 complement this line of research,
the former proving the q-independence of the fractional capacity, and the latter proving the
self-improvement of a fractional capacity density condition in all th ree parameters.

The following theorem characterizes the fractional capacity dens ity condition in connected
metric spaces. This result is a nonlocal or fractional analogue of [ 32, Theorem 2]. To our
knowledge, Theorem 4.6 is new in this generality.
Theorem 4.6. Let 1 ≤p< ∞, 1 ≤q <∞ and 0<β < 1. Assume that X is connected. Let
E ⊂X be a closed set. Then the following conditions are equivalen t:
(i) E satisﬁes the fractional (β,p,q )-capacity density condition (19).
(ii) E supports the boundary (β,p,p,q )-Poincar´ e inequality(20).
(iii) E supports the pointwise (β,p,q )-Hardy inequality (21).
Proof. Since the metric space X is connected, the measure µ satisﬁes the quantitative reverse
doubling condition ( 10), with constants σ > 0 and cσ > 0. By Lemma 2.5, we see that X
supports the ( β,p,p,q )-Poincar´ e inequality. Hence, the implication (i) to (ii) follows from
Lemma 4.3. Lemma 4.4 shows that (ii) implies (iii). The last implication from (iii) to (i)
follows from Lemma 4.5. □
