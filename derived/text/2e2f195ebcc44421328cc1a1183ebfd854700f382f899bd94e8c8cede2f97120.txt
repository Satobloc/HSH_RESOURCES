We show that the existence of a maximal arc in a finite projective plane 
implies the existence of certain block designs and partial geometries. The block 
designs obtained have interesting resolvability properties. 
We shall show that the existence of various combinatorial configurations 
is a consequence of the existence of a maximal arc in a finite projective 
plane. We shall start by defining our terms. 
Suppose B is a finite projective plane of order s (that is, there are s + 1 
points on each line). By a maximal arc d of 9 of parameter n 
(0 < n < s + 1) or maximal n-arc, we shall mean a set ZI of a points 
of 9’ such that each line of 9’ is incident either with n points or with no 
points of z?. 
A balanced incomplete block design is a method of selecting b k-sets 
(blocks) from a set u objects (treatments) such that each treatment belongs 
to r blocks and each pair of distinct treatments occur together in X blocks. 
A balanced incomplete block design is called resolvable if its blocks can 
be partitioned into r disjoint sets of br-I blocks in such a way that every 
treatment belongs to precisely one block in each set. Such a set, in which 
all the blocks are disjoint, is called a parallel class. 
A partial geometry (r, k, t) is a set ‘9 of points and a set n(S) of lines 
such that 
(i) any two points lie on at most one line, 
(ii) any point lies on r lines,
