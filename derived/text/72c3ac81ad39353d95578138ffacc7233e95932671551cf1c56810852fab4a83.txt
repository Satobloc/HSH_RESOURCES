ABSTRACT
Gravitational waves are now routinely detected with ground-based observatories, and, through a process known as
Bayesian inference, their source properties are inferred. However, terrestrial noise artifacts, often referred to asglitches,
commonly overlap astrophysical signals. This invalidates a fundamental assumption of gravitational wave analyses:
the noise is no longer stationary and Gaussian. As a result, traditional techniques can provide biased inferences in
realistic data. One method for mitigating the effect of glitches is to jointly analyse both the signal and noise in
a single framework. In this work, we introducebilby-antiglitchto infer the astrophysical signal properties in
non-Gaussian noise. By additionally including a quasi-physical glitch model to describe short duration non-Gaussian
noise transients, we show that unlike traditional techniques, we infer the true source properties of simulated signals
contaminated with loud glitches. We also show thatbilby-antiglitchprevents false violation claims of General
Relativity, and validates the exceptional nature of gravitational wave signals in spurious data.
1 INTRODUCTION
The Advanced LIGO (Aasi et al. 2015), Virgo (Acernese et al.
2015) and KAGRA (Akutsu et al. 2021) gravitational wave
(GW) detectors have observed∼400 signals originating from
compact binary coalescences (CBCs, The LIGO Scientific &
collaboration 2026a). The peak sensitivity of these detectors
falls within the 20−1000 Hz frequency band, corresponding to
the minimum of the noise power spectral density (PSD). Over
short timescales, the detector noise is assumed to be Gaussian
and stationary, allowing for the PSD to be estimated (see e.g.
Cornish & Littenberg 2015; Littenberg et al. 2016). However,
over longer durations (≳1 minute, Chatziioannou et al. 2019)
the detector sensitivity is time dependent, meaning that the
stationary assumption no longer holds (Mozzon et al. 2020;
Edy et al. 2021). In addition, non-Gaussian noise artifacts of
terrestrial origin, hereafter referred to asglitches, contami-
nate the GW strain data at a rate of 0.5−1.28 per minute
in each detector (Soni et al. 2025; Ashton et al. 2026). When
glitches overlap an astrophysical signal in both time and fre-
quency, they can cause significant biases when inferring the
source properties through Bayesian inference (see e.g. Ghonge
et al. 2024). The clearest and most famous event that was sig-
nificantly affected by a glitch was GW170817 (Abbott et al.
2017). In the third GW observing run, 20% of signals were
polluted by glitches (Davis et al. 2022).
Glitches are classified by their distinct morphologies in
time-frequency spectrograms. By leveraging these distinct
shapes, machine learning frameworks like Gravity Spy (Zevin
et al. 2017, 2024) enable rapid, low-latency glitch classifica-
tion. Examples of different glitch types include blips (nar-
row, tear-drop shapes lasting∼5−10 ms, Cabero et al.
2019), tomtes citep[wider, low-frequency triangular pro-
files,][]Glanzer:2022avx, and koi fish (transients similar to
blips but with excess noise on either side, Bahaadini et al.
2018). Since glitches are typically caused by local effects, they
are statistically independent between detectors. This lack of
correlation distinguishes glitches from true astrophysical sig-
nals, as only the latter is coincident in multiple detectors.
Glitch mitigation is vital for ensuring unbiased astrophysi-
cal inference. For example, Macas et al. (2022) demonstrated
that glitches can have a drastic effect on the sky localisation
of the GW source where, in extreme circumstances, the true
position can lie outside of the inferred 90% credible interval.
Lecoeuche et al. (2026) also recently highlighted that short
duration GW signals in particular are sensitive to blips, thun-
der, and fast-scattering, with parameter biases more severe
when the glitch is near the merger time of the astrophys-
ical signal. Several methods exist to subtract glitches from
the data, includingBayesW ave, which models the glitch
using a sum of sine-Gaussian wavelets (Cornish & Litten-
berg 2015; Littenberg et al. 2016; Cornish et al. 2021; Gupta
& Cornish 2024; Hourihane et al. 2022), andgwsubtract,
which subtracts glitches based on data in auxiliary witness
channels (Allen et al. 1999; Davis et al. 2019). Both these
methods are preferred by the LIGO–Virgo-KAGRA collab-
oration (LVK, The LIGO Scientific & collaboration 2026a),
withBayesW avebeing consistently used since the second
observing run (Abbott et al. 2017; Pankow et al. 2018) and
gwsubtractsince the third observing run (Abbott et al.
2023).

In this work, we combine the core concept of jointly infer-
ring the signal and noise inBayesW avewithbilby;bilby
has been used by the LVK to extract the source properties for
all GW signals since the third observing run (Abbott et al.
2023). Unlike previous (Ashton 2023; Malz & Veitch 2025;
Udall et al. 2026) and also future in-preparation implemen-
tations (Yin Cheung et al. 2026), we adopt a quasi-physical
parameterised glitch model to describe short duration non-
Gaussian noise transients, and interact with traditional off-
the-shelf nested samplers. This removes the possibility of
overfitting the data and provides improved interpretability
with known failure modes. By implementing our methodol-
ogy intobilby, our algorithm can trivially interface with the
latest and most accurate waveform models and, thanks to its
modular design, new glitch models can be added when avail-
able. To verify our framework, namedbilby-antiglitch, we
analyse a simulated signal and show that we recover the true
source properties of the astrophysical signal injected into non-
Gaussian data. We further highlight that, unlike traditional
methods,bilby-antiglitchresolves possible false deviations
of General Relativity that arise from spurious data. We fi-
nally applybilby-antiglitchto GW250114 082203 (Abac
et al. 2025a) and GW200129 065458 (Abbott et al. 2023).
For GW250114 082203, we obtain consistent results with the
LVK and no significant evidence for glitches in the data. For
GW200129 065458 we show that the precession measurement
reported in Hannam et al. (2022) is robust to possible short
duration non-Gaussian noise transients.
In Section 2 we discuss the methodology behind the joint
signal plus noise inference inbilby-antiglitch. In Sec-
tion 3 we verify thebilby-antiglitchalgorithm with a
simulated GW signal, and discuss the implications of using
standardbilby. In Section 4 we applybilby-antiglitch
to GW250114 082203 and GW200129 065458, and finally we
conclude in Section 5.

2.2 The signal plus noise hypothesis
When the noise is non-Gaussian, for example when a glitchg
overlaps the astrophysical signal in both time and frequency,

In reality, the LVK does not perform a signal-only anal-
ysis on the data shown in Fig. 1. Rather, glitch mitiga-
tion techniques are applied before Bayesian inference is per-
formed (see e.g. The LIGO Scientific & collaboration 2026a).
Typically, a preliminary analysis is performed on the glitch
withBayesW ave(Cornish & Littenberg 2015; Littenberg
et al. 2016; Cornish et al. 2021; Gupta & Cornish 2024) and
the median glitch realisation is subtracted from the data,
leaving behind the underlying astrophysical signal for anal-
ysis. Whilebilby-antiglitchis designed to perform joint
inference, we can reproduce this process, and subtract only
the non-Gaussian noise artifact for subsequent analyses to be
undertaken withbilby.
In Fig. 4 we show the inferred component masses when
analysing our simulated signal withbilby, after subtracting
the median glitch realisation inferred bybilby-antiglitch.
For comparison, we also show the inferred posterior when
subtracting different realisations of the glitch (rather than the
median), and when jointly modeling the signal plus noise. In
Appendix A, we also show the spectrogram after subtract-
ing the median glitch and signal realisations. We see that
all methods recover the true parameters of the binary. How-
ever, we see a large variation in the inferred binary prop-
erties when subtracting different glitch realisations, beyond
those expected from the stochastic nature of sampling (see
e.g. Romero-Shaw et al. 2020). This highlights one of the
main disadvantages with removing a single glitch realisation.
We also see small differences between the joint and signal-
only inferences, implying that some of the astrophysical sig-
nal is being removed by the glitch subtraction. This is the
reason why, although possible, we recommend performing a
joint signal plus noise inference where possible to marginalize
over different glitch realisations.

this section, we use the same sampler settings, PSDs, calibra-
tion envelopes etc. as the original LVK analyses. We also use
theAntiGlitchmodel to describe any non-Gaussian noise

4.1 GW250114 082203
GW250114 082203 (Abac et al. 2025a) was observed in the
second half of the fourth GW observing run (The LIGO Sci-
entific & collaboration 2026a). It is the loudest GW signal
detected to date, and was inferred to have source proper-
ties similar to GW150914 (Abbott et al. 2016b,d). At the
time of this event, there were no data quality issues re-
ported in LIGO-Hanford, but there was some excess power
in LIGO-Livingston at low frequencies. This is therefore an
ideal candidate to testbilby-antiglitchin the high SNR
regime. In our re-analysis, we use the NRSur7dq4 waveform
model (Varma et al. 2019) due to its improved accuracy.
In general, we obtain consistent results with the LVK: for
the astrophysical signal, we infer an equal mass ratio binary
withm 2/m1 = 0.95+0.05
−0.09 and a chirp massM= 31.2 +0.4
−0.6M⊙.
In Fig. 5, we show the reconstructed GW signals obtained
withbilby-antiglitch. We see remarkable agreement be-
tween the CBC signal and the underlying whitened data. In-
terestingly, we see small, but non-zero, vibrations inferred by
the glitch model in LIGO-Livingston. This correlates with the
model trying to capture the reported excess low-frequency
power. As expected, we obtain no evidence for non-Gaussian
noise artifacts in LIGO-Hanford.
Regarding computational cost, we found thatbilby-
antiglitchused 1.5×more computational resources than
bilby. This is expected given the larger parameter space.
Unlike in Section 3, this is because there is no strong evi-
dence for a glitch in the data, meaning that the likelihood
surface is not made more difficult to sample when using the
signal-only hypothesis.
From this analysis we see thatbilby-antiglitchis ro-
bust to analysing real GW signals, even when there are no
significant non-Gaussian noise transients reported. Although
further testing is needed, this implies that it can generally be
used for the majority of GW signals reported by the LVK.
The only downside is an increase in the computational cost
due to the larger parameter space explored.
4.2 GW200129 065458
Next, we consider GW200129 065458 (Abbott et al. 2023).
GW200129 065458 was observed in the second half of the
third GW observing run, and this signal sparked interest
within the community as it was the first signal to show signifi-
cant evidence for spin-precession (Hannam et al. 2022); initial
analyses conducted by the LVK obtained different conclusions
regarding the evidence for spin-precession in the signal, with
one model showing evidence for spin-precession, and another
showing no evidence (Abbott et al. 2023). However, the ex-
ceptional nature of GW200129 065458 was later questioned,
since it coincided with excess noise caused by the 45 MHz
electro-optic modulator system (Abbott et al. 2016a). There-
fore, the question was asked whether non-Gaussian noise ar-
tifacts were causing biased inferences (Payne et al. 2022;
Macas et al. 2024). Here, we re-analyse GW200129 065458
withbilby-antiglitch. Unlike the original LVK analysis, we

5 CONCLUSION
In this work, we combined the core joint signal plus noise
inference ideology presented inBayesW avewith the LVK
flagship Bayesian inference pipelinebilbyto provide an al-
ternative algorithm for mitigating the effect of short duration
non-Gaussian noise transients on Bayesian parameter estima-
tion. We demonstrated that, unlike traditional techniques,
our algorithm successfully recovered the true source prop-
erties of an astrophysical signal in non-Gaussian noise. We
further demonstrated that it can help to prevent false viola-
tion claims of General Relativity due to non-Gaussianities in
the data. Finally, although not recommended, we also demon-
strated thatbilby-antiglitchcan be used within the cur-
rent infrastructure by producing glitch subtracted data for
traditional techniques to analyse.
In this work, we only implemented a quasi-physical pa-
rameterised glitch model, suitable for short-duration non-

APPENDIX A: GLITCH SUBTRACTED DA T A
In Section 3, we highlighted that in reality, the LVK does
not perform a signal-only analysis on data containing non-
Gaussian noise transients. Instead, a signal-only analysis is
performed on data where the median glitch realisation is
subtracted. In Fig. A1, we show the spectrograms of the
data before, and after the median glitch and signal real-
isations have been subtracted. We see that whenbilby-
antiglitchsubtracts the median glitch and signal joint re-
alisation inferred from the original non-Gaussian data, the
resulting background is Gaussian as expected. This implies
that the fundamental assumption of the Whittle likelihood
is valid. Similarly, we see that whenbilby-antiglitchsub-
tracts the median glitch realisation inferred from the original
non-Gaussian data, we obtain a clean astrophysical signal for
bilbyto analyse. As expected, we see that whenbilbysub-
tracts the median signal realisation inferred from the glitch
subtracted data, we obtain the expected Gaussian assump-
tion.
