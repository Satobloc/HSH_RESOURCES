Abstract  
 
Contemporary metaphysics of physics is still largely committed to a 
spatiotemporal substantialism inherited from general relativity in which 
spacetime is hypostatized as a primitive fundamental entity. This paper proposes 
an ontological shift to informational monism, based on the scheme of Ontic 
Structural Realism (OSR). We argue that relational information is the irreducible 
ontic substrate of reality, and that space and time are macroscopic epiphenomena, 
the foundational principles of which can be epistemologically deconstructed. In 
this context, the phenomenological flow of time is rephrased as the s equential 
actualization of states in the informational network, and gravitation loses its role 
as an efficient cause to become a syntactic constraint on the bandwidth of these 
exchanges. The global informational volume is strictly conserved. We show that 
the antinomy between the fundamental reversibility of the quantum evolution and 
the irreversibility of the arrow of time is resolved by replacing the classical 
concept of "entropic degradation" with the concept of  "topological 
delocalization". 
 
Keywords: Informational Monism, Ontic Structural Realism, Emergent Gravity, 
Thermal Time, Philosophy of Physics, Entropy.

Prologue: A Brief Genealogy of the Substantialism-Relationalism 
Controversy 
 
Before we make our positive proposal it is useful to recall the conceptual 
landscape we inherit: The opposition of Newton's absolute space (duration as a 
'sensorium of God ') and Leibniz's relationalism (space as 'order of coexistences') 
was never conclusively resolved - only displaced. Kant attempted to do this 
synthesis through his transcendental idealism (space and time as a priori forms of 
intuition). Ironically, it was Einstein's general relativity that revived the 
substantialist reading: the metric field is a dynamical entity with it s own degrees 
of freedom. 
Since then, this consensus has been tested by three major crises: 
 
- The problem of time in quantum gravity (Wheeler-DeWitt equation). 
The Hamiltonian constraint leads to a static wavefunction of the universe, 
frozen dynamics. 
- Black hole thermodynamics: The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy indicates 
information is proportional to area, not volume  – a n on-spatiotemporal 
locus. 
- Holographic principle ('t Hooft, Susskind): the content of a volume can 
be described by degrees of freedom on its boundary, aga inst substantialist 
intuition. 
 
Our informational monism is not some exotic speculation, but the sober a ttempt 
to take these three crises at their ontological word. 
 
1. Introduction: The Impasse of Geometric Substantialism  
 
(Terminological warning to the reader: “information” in this essay is not to be 
taken in the New Age or popular spiritual sense. It is not  “consciousness” or 
“vibration” or “energy” or any mystical substance. Information is strictly defined 
as relational difference — a formal, mathematical, and physically grounded 
concept. Reading panpsychism, spiritualism, or quantum woo into this framework 
is a category error.) 
 
Modern physics was a debate between Leibniz’s relationalism (space as order of 
coexistences) and Newton’s substantialism (absolute space). In the three centuries 
of physics, the operational triumphs of Newtonian mechanics reigned; a nd, even 
though Einstein’s general relativity united space and time in a dynamic fabric, it 
also preserved a substantialist ontology: spacetime acts on matter, and is acted 
upon by matter.

But the stubborn inability of gravity quantization and the paradoxes of black hole 
thermodynamics (notably the information paradox) indicate that spacetime is not 
the ultimate substrate of reality. To get round this, it is necessary to suspend the 
usual geometric analogies, and to go back to first principles. This paper d raws 
from Wheeler's 'It from bit' (1990) and subsequent work in the philosophy of 
information to promote a radical metaphysical thesis: that the basic building 
blocks of nature are not point particles, continuous fields, or the spatiotemporal 
continuum its elf, but pure informational relations. Space and time are the 
macroscopic illusion of the density of underlying quantum entanglements . 
Objects do not inhabit space and time . 
 
2. A Distinction That Matters: Shannon Inf ormation vs. Ontic 
Information 
 
We must clear up a common equivocation before going any further. Shannon 
information ( H = -∑pi log pi ) is an epistemic measure: it measures an observer's 
uncertainty about the state of a system, conditioned on the probability distribution. 
That is a matter of knowledge. 
Ontic information (what we call "informational substance") is different: it is the 
absolute relational difference that constitutes a physical state, independent of any 
observer. When realized as , say , a spin up or down , classical bit ( 0 or 1 )  is 
ontic information . The same bit has one Shannon bit of uncertainty for a person 
who knows the value of the same bit. 
 
Our thesis is one of ontic information alone. Ontic information is subject to the 
conservation principle (axiom 3). In particular, Sh annon entropy can locally 
increase (i.e. ignorance can increase) while ontic information is strictly conserved 
- precisely because delocalization makes ontically structured information 
epistemically inaccessible correlations. This, in particular, dispels a  common 
misconception: we are not saying that "information is physical" in a trivial sense 
(Landauer), but that physical reality is the struct ure of ontic differences.  
 
3. Axiomatic of Informational Monism 
 
In order to isolate the core conflict in modern physics (spatiotemporal continuity 
versus quantum discretization) our framework is based on five metaphysical 
postulates that re -define the nature of substance and neutralize the classical 
ontological division between matter and space: 
 
- Ontic Primacy of Infor mation. In the spirit of Ontic Structural Realism 
(Ladyman & Ross, 2007), information is not an epistemological category 
measuring our ignorance, but the ultimate ontological substance. 
Information here is not understood as a property of some pre -existing

substratum (matter or field) but as pure relational difference that constitutes 
the very texture of reality. There is no information of something; there are 
differences that make a difference and these differences are all there is. 
Support of information is information in its relational form. 
- Interaction as a Logical Operator: Classical Efficient Causality Replaced 
by Formal and Computational Causality. A physical interaction is simply 
the evaluation of a relational Hamiltonian changing the state of two 
subsystems. This is not an externalized computation (by a Laplacean 
demon or a cosmic computer), but an immanent self -evaluation of the 
informational network. A physical interaction is a local realization of a 
logical constraint inscribed in the relational structure. By “computation” in 
this context we simply mean that one relational state entails another 
according to determinate rules —without presup posing any external 
processor. 
- Absolute Conservation of Being (as a Regulative Principle) : The global 
informational content of the Universe is strictly conserved, in accordance 
with the unitary evolution of quantum mechanics. We assume as a 
metaphysical principle the conservation of total information. In the unitary 
framework of quantum mechanics this means taking the whole universe as 
a pure state (S(ρ_universe) = 0). This assumption is, however, empirically 
undecidable for any internal observer. It is a regulator of ontological 
discourse: any local increase of entropy must be compensated by an 
informational depletion elsewhere or by a delocalization. 
- Temporal Emergentism :  Time is not a fundamental dimension It is the 
statistical epiphenomenon of the logical sequence of local informational 
transformations, which results from the incompleteness of the observer. 
- Gravitation as Entropic Syntax : Gravity is not a fundamental attraction 
force but an organizational law that governs the routing of exchanges over 
the relational graph.

5. The State of Time: From Absolute Fl ow to Relational Actualization  
 
The phenomenological experience of the passage of time is at odds with the static 
block-universe of relativity. The conflict is solved by our relational ontology with 
the notion of thermal time (Rovelli, 1993).  
 
If the global information is invariant, then time does not have an objective 
existence at the level of the fundamental graph of the Universe. "Becoming" can 
only come out of the partial point of view of a subsystem in interaction with its 
environment. The perceived speed of time is the hard metric of how often this 
subsystem updates its relative data. Relativistic time dil ation thus becomes 
formally legible: a system deprived of the ability to interact (either by virtue of its 
speed approaching that of light or by virtue of its submersion in a field of high 
informational density) drains its computational power. Mechanically , it runs out 
of its relational bandwidth to “generate” local time.  
 
A classical objection is to say that a “logical sequencing” already presupposes a 
temporal order. This objection confuses the phenomenal order with the 
metaphysical order. Logical order (for example: a formal cause comes before its

subsystem is incomplete, it must iterate its informational updates, and this is how

local structure, of the global constraint of  coherence of the network.

7. The Great Observer (God) as a logical limit 
 
Our approach, in which time is an epistemic consequence of incomplete 
information, naturally leads to the question of what an omniscient observer, e.g. 
God, or a Laplacean demon, who knew the exact state of the enti re universe, 
would experience. 
Three possible answers must be distinguished, with different theological  or 
metaphysical implications. 
First reply (orthodox relationalism): the Great Observer has no tim e If time is 
simply the measure of local informational incompleteness, the n a being 
possessing complete information about the universal state, all nodes, all edges, all 
assignments φ: E → Σ, experiences no succession. For such a being the universe

is a static, eternal structure. All logical steps exist simultaneously in the orde r of 
definition, not in the order of duration. This entity does not "wait" for anything. It 
doesn't "remember." It doesn't "anticipate." It just sees. 
It is the classical block universe from the perspective of God, timeless, complete, 
and perfectly transpa rent. In this respect our metaphysics is compatible with a 
neo-Einsteinian theology: God does not exist in time, God is the name we give to 
the total informational structure itself. Second answer ( process theology ) : The 
Great Observer observes . This creates time . 
An alternative, more radical interpretation: omniscience is not a static snapshot 
but an infinite act of simultaneous comprehension. If God must preserve the 
coherence of the informational network — if divine knowledge is not a passive 
reflection but an active synthesis — then the Great Observer is not external to the 
network but its maximal node. This node contains all relations and thus has the 
maximum possible relational bandwidth. And such a node, far from being 
timeless, would experience a ll possible temporal perspectives simultaneously – 
not a single linear time, but a superposition of all local times.  
 
This resonates with certain process theologies (Whitehead, Hartshorne) where 
God is not the unmoved mover but the fellow sufferer who und erstands. In our 
language: God is the node with local time equal to the integral over all local times 
- an experience we cannot represent but can describe formally.  
 
Third answer (skeptical): the Great Observer is a meaningless notion. 
 
A more deflationary response: the concept of "omniscience" is incoherent in our 
framework. Information is relationally defined. There is no view from nowhere 
(Nagel). The observer is always (never outside) a node of the network. A being 
who knew the whole network would have to be the whole network itself -- but the 
network knows nothing. A knower is a subsystem . A k nower is required for 
knowledge. The “God as observer” is therefore a grammatical il lusion, not a 
possible being. 
 
Our position : We accept the first answer as the  logical consequence of the 
formalism, but we observe that the second and third are not incoherent, they 
simply choose different interpretations of "omniscience". What is important for 
our metaphysics is that none of these answers reintroduces a Newtonian absolute 
time. Not even the Great Observer, if it exists, lives in the time of physics 
textbooks. It is either nothing (static structure) or everything (superposition of 
local times) or the concept crumbles. Theological consequence If one wants to 
say anyt hing about God, the only formulation that is consis tent with this

8. Gravity as a Syntactic Constraint on Organization 
 
What is the metaphysical status of gravity if it is not a mechanical interaction in 
the sense of the Standard Model? Our monism, by philosophically transposing the 
model of entropic gravity (Verlinde, 2011), claims that gravity belongs to a formal 
cause only.  
A macroscopic mass must be reconceptualized as a zone of ontological saturation: 
a hyper-dense node of the informational graph, exhibiting a massive gradient of 
density of states. Gravity is just the statistical effect that pushes subsystems to 
maximize t heir entanglement entropy by moving to such hyper -connected 
regions. The geometric curvature phenomena described by Einstein are the large-
scale projected shadows of this algorithmic constraint which seeks to optimize the 
processing of fundamental information. 
 
9. Cosmological Implications The Direction of Cosmic Time 
 
Our framework predicts explicitly for the large scale arrow of time: the universe 
as a whole has no arrow. Time is local only. If the entanglement structures of 
different regions allow, they can have statistically opposite thermal arrows. 
This is not a bug, but a feature. It forecasts: 
 
- Early universe: low entropy (high local information structure, low 
delocalization)  
- Cosmic expansion: not a metric expansion -in-time, but the increase in the 
diameter of the gr aph as new nodes and edges are "folded in " from 
previously inaccessible correlations.  
- Dark energy: possible artefact of the information cost of maintaining global 
coherence as local subsystems become increasingly delocalised relative to 
each other. The accelerated expansion corresponds to satur ation of 
relational bandwidth. 
 
These are speculative but empirically accessible: they predict specific corrections 
to the Friedmann equations at high redshift (a potential signature in future surveys 
like Euclid or Roman). This we present not as a resu lt but as a research 
programme.

10. An Aside on Hidden Variables: Bohm and 't Hooft 
 
Our framework is naturally suited to comparison with hidden variable approaches, 
in particular Bohmian mechanics and ``t Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation. 
 
Similarities with t’Hooft (2016). Gerard 't Hooft has proposed that quantum 
mechanics is a coarse grained description of an underlying deterministic cellular 
automaton. Our informational graph is structurally similar. The rewriting rules R 
are deterministic at the basic level, and quantum indeterminacy arises from 
relational incompleteness. The main difference is that 't Hooft retains a primitive 
notion of time (the automaton step) while we eliminate it altoge ther, replacing it 
with logical succession. 
 
Bohm (1952) divergences. Bohmian mechanics introduces a non-local pilot wave 
and real particle positions. Our framework does not have particles, does not have 
a wave function as a separate entity, and does not h ave a preferred foliation of 
spacetime. Bohm's ontology is dual (particles + wave); ours is monist 
(information only). Bohm's non-locality is kinematic, ours is structural. 
 
What we borrow: The idea (from 't Hooft) that quantum mechanics and 
determinism can co-exist. From Bohm the insight that non -locality is not a bug 
but a feature of relational ontologies. But we go further. Particles are not 
fundamental, nor is time. 
 
A testable difference: We predict in our framework that any hidden variable 
theory needing a primitive temporal parameter cannot be successful (Bohm's time 
is Newtonian). 't Hooft's cellular time might be compatible but only if 
reinterpreted as logical succession - which is empirically indistinguishable from 
ordinary time in present experiments. The divergence appears only at the Planck 
scale, where our framework predicts discrete, but not  temporal graph dynamics. 
 
11. Topological Delocalization: The Resolution of the Entropic Conflict 
 
One of the main epistemological issues is to reconcile the strict conservation of 
quantum information with the irreversibility of the Second Law of 
Thermodynamics. How can disorder increase without loss of global information? 
 
Our framework offers a purely topological interpretation, with no theoretical 
compromises. If information can not be destroyed , then the increase in entropy is 
just a growing relational delocalization . When a structured system (low entropy) 
dissipates its initial information is not erased, but rather migrates from 
macroscopic degrees of freedom to inaccessible microscopic degrees of freedom, 
becoming entangled with the environment. The universe doesn't decay, it

entangles itself. Local order becomes epistemic opacity, but global ontologica l 
transparency is preserved. 
 
11. Objections and Response 
 
Objection 1 (testability). Gravity as a syntactic constraint is unfalsifiable. This 
removes the framework from science. 
 
Reply: This objection applies a Popperian criterion to a scientifically -oriented 
metaphysics. We do not want to replace general relativity, but to propose an 
ontological interpretation that dissolves its internal aporias (singularities, the 
information paradox). Indirect testability is by coherence with existing theories 
and the ability to make new qualitative predictions (e.g. about black hole 
thermodynamics). 
 
Objection 2 (support of information) Relational information presupposes relata 
different from relations. A node in a graph is not reducible to its edges.  
 
Reply: This is exactly the substantialist assumption, which we deny. In a pure and 
simple relational framework (radical OSR) relata are simply nodes defined solely 
by their relations. There is no substr atum underneath: the network is all there is. 
The distinction between node and edge is a convenient abstra ction, not an 
ontological one. 
 
Objection 3 (the Great Observer objection). You say time is the consequence of 
the observer's incompleteness. But if an omniscient observer, God or a Laplacian 
demon, had perfect knowledge of the whole informational network, would it have 
no experience of time at all? And if so, does not that make time an illusio n and 
your framework idealist? 
 
Answer This objection confuses two different claims: (a) that time is an illusion 
only; (b) that time is a genuine relational property for finite systems. We support 
(b) not (a). The Great Observer, if it existed, would indeed have no temporal 
experience -- but this is because the Gr eat Observer is not a subsystem, and 
therefore not an observer in the informational sense. By definition, an observer is 
a node that has incomplete access to the total network. The fact that a hypothetical 
complete knower would not see time does not imply that time is illusory for us. It 
just implies that time is perspectival. This is no more problematic than saying that 
color does not exist for a being with no photoreceptors, but color is real for 
trichromatic primates. Finite observers have real time. The  Great Observer is a 
limiting case, not a contradiction.

Objection 4 (unitarity of the universe): The hypothesis S(ρ_universe)=0 is strong 
and arguably undecidable. Can you build a metaphysics on an undecidable 
proposition? 
 
Answer: Yes, if the propositio n is a regulative principle (in the Kantian sense), 
not an empirical hypothesis. It poses the question: any local increase of entropy 
should be interpreted as delocalization, not destruction. The alternative, a 
fundamentally mixed state, would mean that information can be truly lost, which 
is in contradiction with the unitary core of quantum mechanics. Therefore, our 
choice is not arbitrary but theoretically driven. 
 
12. How does Informational Monism Change Things? Beyond Physics 
 
If the preceding arguments are  right, the metaphysical picture of the world is 
radically changed. But metaphysics is not physics. It also influences how we 
position ourselves in reality. 
Our framework yields three existential corollaries (tenta tively proposed, not 
deduced):  
The self as a local information center. ‘Consciousness’ or ‘self’ is a metastable 
pattern of informational differences that preserve coherence in the face of 
delocalization. Death is not annihilation but relational saturation, where the 
differences of the pattern become indistinguishable from the background of the 
environment. 
Value as difference, opposing delocalisation. If "good" had an ontological 
correlate (a very big claim), it might be the preservation of structured 
informational differences against e ntropic flattening. Art, memory, knowledge, 
love are all dense local informational structures that resist dissolution. 
No cosmic telos, but local significance . At the most basic level, the universe has 
no “point” – information just gets re -arranged. But f or nodes like us, in the 
network, the experience of building, maintaining and transmitting informational 
structures is the source of meaning. 
 
These are not arguments but attitudes which our metaphysics makes consistent. 
We mention them only to show that, unlike some eliminative physicalisms, 
informational monism does not make room for a humanly meaningful world as a 
concession but as a consequence of taking emergence seriously. 
 
13. The Problem of Free Will 
 
There is no metaphysical scheme that can avoid the problem of free will. The view 
of informational monism is non-standard and unique. The deterministic menace. 
So if the universe is a pure network with fixed rewriting rules R, does this not 
imply hard determinism? Yes and no. The global evolution is in fact deterministic

===== PAGE 13 =====
13 
 
in the sense that if you know the entire state of the network at one logical step, 
there is a unique next step determined by R. 
The compatibilist escape hatch. However, fundamental determinism does not 
imply the non-existence of free will for local subsystems. Free will, to us, is not 
the ability to break the laws of physics (an incoherent concept), but the 
experienced ability of a high-density informational node to update its internal state 
in the face of multiple possible futures, limited by its memory and goals. 
Our framework allows this in three ways:  
 
- Informational lack of completeness. No node has ever had access to the full 
network. So it takes several possible futures (branching) even though only 
one actually materializes globally. This node really branches. 
- Self-modification Unlike classical billiard b alls, informational nodes can 
change their own internal rewriting rules (meta-rules) with time. This is not 
indeterminism but self -determination — the node becomes part of the 
causal chain that determines its future states 
- Topological freedoms. High -relational-bandwidth nodes have a greater 
freedom in how they respond to inputs. In this sense, freedom is not the 
absence of causality but the abundance of responses. 
 
Free will conclusion:  informational monism is compatible with a strong 
compatibilism. Free will is not an illusion , but the experience of being a node of 
high information density with multiple viable pathways . Just because a Laplacean 
demon (if such a thing existed) could pr edict your choice, does not make your 
deliberation any less real. It just means that the demon has a perspective that you 
do not share.

15. Summary 
 
Informational monism dissolves the aporias of geometric substantialism by 
refusing to hypostatize space and time. The realization that reality is a mere graph 
of mathematical relations opens up the possibility to re -unify the 
phenomenological experience of temporal irreversibility with the causa l rigidity 
of quantum gravity. 
 
Informational monism is not just a re -description of the world, but a real 
ontological economy: no more substantial spacetime, no more absolute time, no 
more primitive matter. What is left is a pure network of actualizing diff erences. 
Our job is not to fill in this framework, but to learn to think without the categories 
classical physics has bequeathed to us. ¹ 
 
One final note: this essay proposes a metaphysics, not a physics. It does not aim 
at empirical prediction (although i t does some), but at conceptual coherence. In 
an age when physics is multiplying entities (strings, branes, multiverses) there is 
intellectual virtue in asking: what is the minimal ontology needed to explain all 
phenomena? Our answer: pure informational relation. That might be wrong. But 
it's a question worth asking.
