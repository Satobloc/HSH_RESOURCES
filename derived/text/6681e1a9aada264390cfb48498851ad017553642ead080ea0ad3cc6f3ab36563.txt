framework, a dimensionless comparative index for dissipative processes defined as:

different scales. Limitations include the reliance on fitted parameters for the oscillator, 
the assumed constant thermospheric temperature (T = 1000 K) for the satellite, and the 
exclusion of conservative systems, massless particles, and relativistic effects.

 Introduction 
The description of dissipative (irreversible) processes in physics conventionally 
relies on time as an independent parameter. However, time itself is operationally 
defined through periodic motion – rotations, oscillations, atomic transitions. This 
circularity suggests that motion cycles might serve as a more primitive 
observable. 
The Ja Model (Khawaldeh, Jalal Y . A. 2026e) proposes a descriptive framework 
where the conventional time parameter t is replaced by a cycle count N = t/ τ₀, 
with τ₀ the period of a chosen reference cycle. The central quantity is the 
dimensionless comparative index: 
𝑴𝑼𝒋𝒂 =  (𝑭 · 𝒅) / (𝒎 · 𝑬) ·  𝒎₀ ·  (𝜟𝑺 / 𝒌_𝑩) 
where F is force, d displacement, m mass, E energy, ΔS entropy change, m₀ a 
reference mass, and k_B Boltzmann's constant. 
For dissipative processes where the dissipated energy ΔE_diss equals F·d (e.g., 
viscous damping, atmospheric drag), and where the dissipation occurs 
isothermally, the entropy change of the environment is ΔS_env = ΔE_diss / T. 
Consequently, ΔS/k_B = ΔE_diss/(k_B T). The expression then simplifies to: 
𝑴𝑼𝒋𝒂 =  (𝒎₀/𝒎) ·  (𝜟𝑬_𝒅𝒊𝒔𝒔) / (𝒌_𝑩 𝑻) 
with T the absolute temperature of the environment. The general form with 
ΔS/k_B is retained for future extensions to non -isothermal or 
information-theoretic contexts. 
The Ja Model is a reparametrisation of non-relativistic physics, not a new physical 
law. It does not eliminate time but reinterprets it as a derived count. The present 
paper provides the complete methodology and empirical demonstration of the 
MUja framewor k on two radically different dissipative systems: a 
laboratory-scale damped harmonic oscillator (reconstructed from published 
data) and a space-based satellite (GRACE-FO 1) experiencing atmospheric drag. 
The objective is to test whether MUja can be compute d consistently, remains 
finite and interpretable, and allows a common cycle -based description across 
scales. 
This paper is self-contained and does not require prior reading of the theoretical 
Ja Model paper. All necessary definitions, equations, data sources, and 
computational steps are provided herein.

1. Methodology 
1.1 Objective of the Empirical Study 
The present study constitutes the first empirical evaluation of the MUja 
framework using a real experimentally characterized dissipative physical system. 
The objective of this investigation is not to establish a new law of physics, nor to 
replace existing dynamical theories. Instead, the purpose is to determine 
whether the MUja descriptor can function as a meaningful dimensionless 
measure of dissipative moti on when applied to experimentally observed 
systems. 
The central research question is: 
Can a single dimensionless quantity provide a consistent characterization of 
dissipative motion independently of the physical scale of the system? 
To address this question, the damped harmonic oscillator was selected as the 
first test system because it is one of the most extensively studied dissipative 
systems in classical physics and possesses a well -established theoretical and 
experimental description. 
The present analysis should be regarded as a proof-of-concept evaluation of the 
MUja framework. Its objective is not to validate a new physical law, but to 
determine whether MUja behaves consistently when applied to a real dissipative 
system whose dynamics are already well understood. 
 
1.2 Central Hypothesis 
The empirical analysis is based on the following operational hypothesis: 
Dissipative systems may possess a common dimensionless structure that can be 
quantified through the MUja descriptor. 
More specifically: 
If MUja captures a physically meaningful aspect of dissipative motion, its 
evolution should systematically reflect changes in dissipative energy loss within 
a dynamical system.

Failure to observe such behavior would indicate that MUja does not provide a 
useful descriptor of dissipative evolution for the system under consideration. 
The present hypothesis does not claim that MUja constitutes a new physical law. 
Rather, it tests whether MUja can serve as a stable and operationally meaningful 
dimensionless descriptor of dissipative processes.

The damped harmonic oscillator was selected for four primary reasons. 
Reason 1: Physical Simplicity 
The oscillator is governed by the classical equation of motion: 
m d²x/dt² + b dx/dt + Kx = 0 
where: 
• m is the oscillating mass 
• b is the viscous damping coefficient 
• K is the spring constant

Reason 2: Controlled Dissipation 
The rate of energy loss is analytically known and experimentally measurable. 
This allows direct estimation of: 
ΔE_diss

Reason 3: Availability of Experimental Data 
The work of Sebastião (2014) provides experimentally fitted parameters derived 
from a real laboratory spring-mass system. 
The published study reports: 
• mass

• damping coefficient

These quantities are sufficient to reconstruct the dissipative dynamics required 
for the MUja analysis. 
 
Reason 4: Reproducibility 
The experiment is sufficiently simple to be independently replicated. 
Consequently, any conclusions obtained from the MUja analysis can be verified 
through future experimental studies.

4. MUja Evaluation Procedure 
4.1 Energy Dissipated per Oscillation Cycle 
For a viscously damped oscillator, the energy dissipated during one oscillation 
cycle is: 
ΔE_diss = π b ω A² 
where: 
• b is the damping coefficient 
• ω is the angular frequency 
• A is the instantaneous oscillation amplitude 
This expression represents the mechanical energy converted into dissipative 
losses during one oscillation cycle. 
 
4.2 Temperature Assumption 
The original experiment does not explicitly report laboratory temperature. 
For consistency, room temperature is assumed to be: 
T = 293 K 
A variation of ±5 K changes the absolute value of MUja by less than 
approximately 2%, because: 
MUja ∝ 1/T 
Such a variation does not affect the qualitative behavior of MUja or the 
conclusions of the present study. 
 
4.3 Simplified MUja Form 
For the present analysis: 
MUja = (m₀/m) × (ΔE_diss/(kB T)) 
where:

kB = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J·K⁻¹ 
T = 293 K 
Substituting the dissipated energy expression: 
MUja = (m₀/m) × (π b ω A²)/(kB T) 
 
5. Predicted MUja Behavior 
The amplitude decay law implies: 
A(t) ∝ e^(−λt)

A²(t) ∝ e^(−2λt)

MUja(t) ∝ e^(−2λt) 
This relationship should not be interpreted as an independent physical 
prediction. Rather, it is an internal consistency expectation arising from the 
adopted MUja definition and the experimentally fitted amplitude-decay law. 
Thus, the purpose of the oscillator analysis is to verify whether the reconstructed 
MUja evolution remains mathematically well -defined, monotonic, and 
consistent with the known dissipative behavior of the system.

• the functional behavior of MUja is more robust than its absolute 
numerical magnitude,  
• the analysis does not provide an independent experimental test of the 
fitted oscillator dynamics.

mass: 
m₀

convention. 
However, the reconstructed evolution law: 
MUja(N) ∝ exp(−0.173N)

Because the dataset is reconstructed from fitted functions, no experimental 
scatter is present.

7.10 Interim Conclusion 
The damped harmonic oscillator constitutes the first system to which the MUja 
framework has been applied using experimentally derived physical parameters. 
The reconstructed analysis demonstrates that: 
• MUja can be computed directly from experimentally derived quantities,  
• MUja remains finite and mathematically well -defined throughout the 
observed evolution,  
• the cycle-based representation remains consistent with the conventional 
time-based description,  
• the computed MUja evolution remains consistent with the dissipative 
behavior encoded in the fitted oscillator model.  
Within the assumptions and limitations of the reconstructed analysis, the MUja 
evolution follows: 
MUja(N) ∝ exp(−0.173N) 
This result does not establish a new physical law, nor does it demonstrate the 
universality of the MUja framework. 
Rather, it shows that the framework can be implemented consistently in a real 
dissipative system using experimentally derived parameters and a transparent 
calculation procedure. 
The present oscillator analysis therefore establishes computational feasibility, 
internal consistency, and operational transparency, but not independent 
empirical validation. 
The broader question of whether MUja provides useful cross -domain 
comparisons between fundamentally different dissipative systems remains open 
and requires independent analyses of additional systems beyond the oscillator 
considered here.

Before extending the MUja framework to additional dissipative systems, the

9. Cross-Domain Application of MUja: Atmospheric Drag Dissipation in 
the GRACE-FO 1 Satellite 
9.1 Objective and Scientific Scope 
Sections 7 and 8 demonstrated that the MUja framework can be applied 
consistently to a classical dissipative system represented by a damped harmonic 
oscillator. The present section extends the analysis to a fundamentally different 
physical domain in order to determine whether the same cycle -based 
methodology remains operational when applied to a real orbital system subject 
to atmospheric drag. 
The objective of this investigation is not to replace conventional orbital 
mechanics, nor to establish a new physical law. Rather, the purpose is to evaluate 
whether a dimensionless cycle -based representation of dissipation can be 
constructed from real spa cecraft observations using the same conceptual 
procedure previously applied to the oscillator. 
The GRACE-FO 1 satellite was selected because its orbital evolution is strongly 
influenced by atmospheric drag and because high -quality orbital records are 
publicly available over a sufficiently long time interval to permit a meaningful 
dissipative analysis. 
The central question addressed in this section is: 
Can the MUja framework be implemented consistently in a real orbital system 
using observed orbital decay as a measure of dissipative evolution? 
The present analysis constitutes the first space -based application of the MUja 
framework and the first cross-domain comparison between laboratory-scale and 
orbital-scale dissipative systems.

9.10 Interpretation, Limitations, and Scientific Significance 
The present analysis shows that real orbital -decay observations can be 
transformed into a cycle-based dissipation sequence and a corresponding MUja 
representation. 
Oscillator reconstruction limitations.  The oscillator analysis uses published 
fitted parameters, not raw experimental data. The numerical MUja values are 
illustrative estimates, and the exponential decay is mathematically forced by the 
fitted amplitude envelope. It serves as a proof -of-concept consistency test, not 
an independent validation. 
Satellite analysis limitations. Thermospheric temperature is assumed constant 
at T = 1000 K. In reality, temperature varies with altitude, solar activity, and 
geomagnetic storms. The 31.8 km altitude decrease implies non -negligible 
temperature variation. Section 9.8 shows that a range o f 800 –1200 K scales 
MUja by approximately ±20 –25% without altering the qualitative distribution, 
but the constant-T assumption remains a simplification. A rigorous model (e.g., 
NRLMSISE-00) is beyond this proof -of-concept sco pe. Results are 
order-of-magnitude estimates demonstrating operational feasibility, not precise 
physical constants.

Page44 
The analysis does not include: 
• direct atmospheric density reconstruction, 
• drag-coefficient estimation (C_D implicitly absorbed into ΔE_diss), 
• full uncertainty propagation from orbital elements, 
• independent drag-force reconstruction (energy loss used directly). 
MUja distribution interpretation.  Positive skewness (2.425) is consistent with 
intermittent high-dissipation events (e.g., geomagnetic storms), but may also be 
influenced by boundedness ( ΔE_diss ≥ 0) and numerical differentiation. The 
bootstrap CI accounts for non -normality and confirms s tatistical stability, but 
causal attribution is qualitative. 
What this study does not claim.  MUja does not predict new physics, replace 
conventional dimensionless descriptors (Q -factor, ballistic coefficient), or 
constitute a universal law. The reparametrisation t = N τ₀ is mathematically 
equivalent to standard non -relativistic physics. No falsifi cation of standard 
theories is claimed. 
What this study does demonstrate.  A dimensionless, cycle -based dissipative 
index can be constructed from real spacecraft observations (and a reconstructed 
oscillator) using a transparent, reproducible procedure. MUja remains finite, 
statistically stable over thousands of cycles, and enables a common descriptive 
language across systems differing by ten orders of magnitude in energy scale. 
The methodology is operational and audited—methodological, not ontological. 
Future studies should apply MUja to additional dissipative systems (e.g., other 
satellites with independent density modelling, collective cell migration, or 
mechanical oscillators with raw time -series data) to further test cross -domain 
consistency and asse ss whether the observed scaling has broader physical 
significance.

cycle-based dissipation analysis to real orbital systems. The framework does not 
offer new predictive power but provides a dimensionless, motion -centred 
language for comparing dissipative processes across scales. Future work should 
address the limitations noted above and extend the analysis to reversible 
systems and relativistic regimes. 
 
10. Discussion 
The two analysed systems differ by over ten orders of magnitude in mass, energy 
scale, and dissipation mechanism: the oscillator loses energy through viscous 
damping; the satellite through atmospheric drag. Despite these differences, both 
admit the same cycle-based MUja formulation. 
Oscillator. MUja decreases exponentially with cycle count (decay constant 0.173 
cycle⁻¹), as expected from A² dependence. This serves as a proof-of-concept that 
MUja can be computed from a well-understood dissipative system. 
Relation to Q-factor. For a damped oscillator, Q = ω₀/(2λ) ≈ 36.3. The MUja decay 
constant satisfies 2λT_osc = 2π/Q ≈ 0.173, so MUja(N) ∝ exp(−2πN/Q). MUja 
encodes the same dissipative information as Q but with additional thermal 
normalisation (k_B T) and reference mass m₀, making it sensitive to environmental 
temperature. 
Satellite. MUja remains finite and statistically stable over thousands of orbital 
revolutions. Mean MUja ≈ 7×10²⁰ (one to two orders above the oscillator’s initial 
value), reflecting larger dissipated energy per orbit. The distribution shows moderate 
variability (CV=0.5) and positive skewness (2.425), consistent with episodic 
atmospheric density enhancements (e.g., geomagnetic storms).

===== PAGE 48 =====
Page47 
Relation to ballistic coefficient. The ballistic coefficient B = C_D A/m governs drag 
efficiency. MUja does not replace B; it offers a complementary thermally normalised 
measure per orbit. For GRACE-FO 1, mean MUja = 6.97×10²⁰ corresponds to mean 
ΔE_diss ≈ 4.16×10³ J per orbit. The two are related via ΔE_diss per orbit ≈ 2π B ρ v² a. 
MUja captures integrated drag per cycle; B describes instantaneous efficiency. 
Absolute normalisation. Values are normalised to m₀ = 1 kg. Changing m₀ rescales 
all MUja proportionally. For cross-study comparisons, only relative changes or 
functional form (e.g., decay constant) are meaningful unless a common m₀ 
convention is adopted. 
What the analysis does not claim. MUja is not a fundamental constant, does not 
replace Q or B, does not constitute a universal law, and does not predict new physics. 
The reparametrisation t = N τ₀ is mathematically equivalent to standard 
non-relativistic physics. 
Limitations (as detailed in Section 9.10): 
• Oscillator: fitted parameters, not raw data; exponential decay is forced by the 
model. 
• Satellite: constant T = 1000 K assumption; no atmospheric density model; no 
full uncertainty propagation. 
• Both assume F·d = ΔE_diss (holds for viscous damping and orbital drag). 
• Framework excludes conservative systems, massless particles, and relativistic 
effects. 
Future work. Apply MUja to additional dissipative systems (e.g., other satellites with 
independent density modelling, collective cell migration, mechanical oscillators with 
raw time series) to further test cross-domain consistency.

11. Conclusion 
This paper provides the full methodology and empirical demonstration of the 
MUja framework on two physically distinct dissipative systems. The key findings 
are: 
• The reconstructed damped oscillator yields MUja(N) ∝ exp(−0.173N), 
with a dimensionless decay constant of 0.173 cycle⁻¹. 
• The GRACE-FO 1 satellite under atmospheric drag yields a mean MUja of 
6.972 × 10²⁰, a median of 6.421 × 10²⁰, and a 95% confidence interval 
[6.99 × 10²⁰, 7.26 × 10²⁰], based on 2608 orbital intervals. 
• The distribution of satellite MUja values is right-skewed (skewness 2.425), 
consistent with episodic atmospheric disturbances. 
• The same cycle-based computational procedure applies to both systems 
despite their different scales and dissipation mechanisms. 
The MUja framework does not replace conventional physics but offers a 
dimensionless, motion -centred language for comparing dissipative 
processes. The novelty of the MUja framework is not in the underlying physics 
but in the operational procedure that allow s dissipative processes to be 
compared using a common dimensionless, cycle -based language. This is a 
descriptive tool, not a replacement for existing theories.  The present work 
establishes its operational feasibility and internal consistency. Future extensions 
should address reversible systems (using action instead of entropy) and 
relativistic formulations.

The purpose of the analysis was to determine whether the MUja framework

The procedure converts orbital decay measurements into orbital mechanical

and an orbit -decaying satellite. The following research directions emerge 
naturally from the conceptual stru cture of the framework but lie beyond the 
scope of the present investigation. They are included solely to identify possible 
avenues for future work and should not be interpreted as established results, 
validated extensions, or claims arising from the present study. 
D.1 Critical-Transition Indicators 
Future investigations may examine whether cycle -based derivatives of MUja, 
such as: 
d(MUja)/dN 
and 
d²(MUja)/dN² 
contain information relevant to the detection of critical transitions or abrupt 
changes in dissipative systems. 
 
D.2 Information-Dissipative Systems 
The present work considers only physical energy dissipation. Future studies may 
explore whether analogous constructions can be formulated for information -
processing systems, complex networks, or adaptive computational systems in 
which information loss or i nformation flow plays a role analogous to physical 
dissipation.

D.3 Biological Dissipative Systems 
Potential applications may include cyclic biological processes such as cardiac 
dynamics, respiration, cell division, and other non -equilibrium biological 
systems. Whether the cycle-based MUja representation remains meaningful in 
such contexts remains an open question. 
D.4 Relative Normalization Schemes 
Alternative normalization procedures based on relative or reference -system 
scaling may be investigated in order to improve comparability between studies 
and reduce dependence on specific normalization choices. For example, future 
work may examine relative measures based on ratios between system -specific 
and reference-state MUja values. 
D.5 Geometric Representations of Dissipative Evolution 
Future studies may explore representations of dissipative systems within higher-
dimensional state spaces involving MUja and its cycle -based derivatives. Such 
representations may provide additional insight into similarities and differences 
among dissipative processes occurring in distinct physical domains. 
D.6 Variational Principles 
Whether cycle -based descriptions of dissipative evolution admit useful 
variational or extremal formulations remains unknown. The possible existence 
of such principles would require independent theoretical development and 
empirical investigation. 
The topics listed above are prospective research directions motivated by the 
conceptual structure of the MUja framework. None are examined, tested, or 
claimed as results of the present study. Their inclusion should not be interpreted 
as evidence for their validity, and each would require independent theoretical 
development, empirical evaluation, and critical assessment before any scientific 
conclusions could be drawn. 
The future directions listed in this appendix are not intended to constitute an 
exhaustive catalogue of possible extensions. As with any emerging conceptual 
framework, additional theoretical, computational, and empirical developments 
may arise that are not presently foreseeable.
