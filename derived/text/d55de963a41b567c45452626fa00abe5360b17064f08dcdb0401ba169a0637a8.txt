The Observer and the Vacuum:  
Zero Detection and the Ontological Loading of Absence 
 
Juliet Zhong 
Independent Researcher | London, United Kingdom | August 2026 
ORCID: 0009-0006-5099-3671 
 
Abstract 
A region is called empty when nothing is detected in it. This paper examines what such a designation 
establishes and what it does not. Four companion papers established that the observer is the existential 
precondition of physical theory, that discrimination is realised electromagnetic ally, that observing 
systems sharing a radiative expression constitute a rendering domain individuated by mechanism rather 
than position, and that no domain -internal operation resolves a cosmological datum into an observer -
independent remainder. Those results concerned what observation registers. This paper turns to what it 
fails to register, and takes the vacuum as the case where a null result has been carried furthest. 
Two criteria share the name. The empirical vacuum is a region in which no particles are detected 
under a given mechanism, sensitivity and interval; the vacuum state |0⟩ is the lowest-energy eigenstate 
of a specified Hamiltonian. They are not identical and serve different formal roles, and the continuity 
of the word between them permits an ontological intuition to travel from one context to the other 
unremarked. Neither criterion defines absence. The first is a domain-conditioned null result, from which 
ontological absence does not follow. The second presupposes the fields whose state it specifies, and is 
in addition not observer-independent: it varies with the observer's motion and admits no unique global 
form in a general spacetime. The concluding analysis traces how a term introduced to mark undetected 
content acquired the standing of a state, an energy, and a gravitational source. Falsification conditions 
are stated. 
 
Keywords: vacuum; zero detection; ontological absence; observer dependence; rendering domain; 
vacuum state; Unruh effect; zero-point energy; cosmological constant; conceptual transfer

1. The Question Torricelli's Tube Left Open 
1.1 A space above the mercury 
In 1644, in Florence, Evangelista Torricelli sealed a glass tube at one end, filled it with mercury, 
inverted it into a basin, and watched the column fall until it stood at about seventy -six centimetres. 
Above the mercury there was a space. The tube had been full; now part of it was not. Nothing had been 
let in, and nothing had been taken out except the mercury that had descended. 
What was in that space became one of the most consequential disputes of the century, and its structure 
is worth recovering, because the dispute was not about the experiment. Everyone agreed on what the 
apparatus did. The disagreement concerned what could be inferred from it. Torricelli's supporters held 
that the space contained nothing. Their opponents held that it contained a subtle matter too fine to be 
weighed, sensed, or otherwise registered by any means then available, and that the appearance of 
emptiness was a limitation of the instruments rather than a property of the space. 
The opponents were, on the physics, wrong about the particular substance they proposed. They were 
not wrong about the logic of the objection. The apparatus could establish that no air was present, because 
the presence of air had detectable consequences that were absent. It could not establish that nothing was 
present, because no arrangement of that apparatus produced a signature of nothing. It produced only the 
absence of the signature of something. What the tube demonstrated was the removability of air. Th at 
the residue was empty was an inference, and the inference required a premise the experiment did not 
supply: that air was the only thing there was to remove. 
Three and a half centuries later, the space above Torricelli's mercury is known to contain mercury 
vapour, and beyond that, the tube sits in a universe permeated by a microwave background, by neutrinos, 
and by whatever else has not yet been catalogued. The subtle-matter theorists lost their theory and won 
their objection. The objection has never been answered, because it is not the kind of objection that 
improved instruments answer. Every improvement in sensitivity lowers a threshold. No improvement 
in sensitivity converts a threshold into a floor. 
This paper is about what happened to the word that Torricelli's contemporaries were arguing over, 
and about what physics has done with a designation whose original content was operational. 
1.2 What the companion papers establish 
Four companion papers precede this one. Their results are taken as established and are not re-derived; 
each is stated below in the form in which it will be used. 
The first established that the observer is not a peripheral element of physical theory but its existential 
precondition (Zhong, 2026d). A reference frame cannot be physically instantiated without a system 
capable of discriminating between physical states a nd retaining a record. The Lorentz transformation,

deprived of such systems at both ends, is a map from the empty set to the empty set: formally valid, 
physically vacuous. And the Minkowski metric, whose invariance is conventionally taken as evidence 
of an observer -independent reality, presupposes the obse rver it is held to transcend. Physics has 
operated throughout with an existential presupposition it has not examined. 
The second established the ground of the observer's discriminative capacity (Zhong, 2026e). Every 
known act of discrimination and registration, biological or instrumental or computational, is realised 
through electromagnetic interaction, and the convergenc e is not an accident of terrestrial technology: 
of the four interactions, only electromagnetism operates across the full range of scales from atomic to 
macroscopic, builds stable intermediate structures, supports cascade amplification, and yields 
recoverable records. What is registered is registered electromagnetically or it is not registered. 
The third established that observing systems sharing a common physical expression of the radiative 
substrate constitute a rendering domain, within which mutual rendering is coherent and across which it 
is not (Zhong, 2026f). The criterion of domain members hip is the mechanism through which 
discrimination is realised, not the position of the observer or the range of inputs its instruments register. 
Domains are therefore individuated by mechanism, and are not spatial regions. The boundary between 
them is not a wall that a sufficiently long journey or a sufficiently powerful telescope crosses. 
The fourth carried these results into cosmology and asked what status attaches to data obtained by 
observers who cannot leave the domain in which they were generated (Zhong, 2026g). A cosmological 
datum has the form D(O, I, G): a quantity conditioned on observer state, on instrument and procedure, 
and on the domain within which observation is possible at all. The question was whether any operation 
F available within the domain yields from D a unique remainder independent of O. The answer was 
negative, established by exhausting the three ways an exit might be attempted. Movement changes 
position and carries the domain along. Construction multiplies channels within the domain, since every 
instrument is built from its materials, calibrated against its standards , and read by its procedures. 
Inference establishes a decomposition only relative to a model built and validated within the domain, 
and no benchmark independent of the observer-domain coupling exists against which the residual could 
be checked. Two consequ ences were drawn. Reliability and observer -independence are distinct 
properties, and cosmology possesses the first in full measure without having established the second. 
And the Copernican principle is a condition of cosmological inference rather than one of its findings, 
since testing it would require comparing observations from positions outside the domain, and every 
available position is inside. 
1.3 The question this paper asks 
The fourth paper analysed what observation registers: temperatures, expansion rates, fluctuation 
amplitudes, all of them conditioned in the manner described. It did not ask about the other outcome. 
Every measurement has two possible results, and only one of them was examined.

When an instrument registers nothing, a claim is made about the world on the strength of it. The 
claim is not usually noticed as a claim, because a null result feels like the absence of a claim rather than 
the presence of one. But the inference from "this instrument registered nothing" to "there is nothing 
there" is a substantive step, and it is a step of exactly the kind the companion papers have been 
examining: it moves from a domain -conditioned outcome to a statement about what is the case 
independently of the domain. 
The vacuum is the case in which this step has been taken furthest and with the least examination. 
What began as a designation for a region from which air had been removed became a region containing 
no matter, then a region containing no particles, then a p hysical state, then a state possessing energy, 
then a source term in the field equations of gravity, and finally a candidate explanation for the 
accelerating expansion of the universe. At no point in this progression was the original inference 
revisited. T he term was carried forward, and each new theoretical role attached to it inherited the 
ontological standing the previous role had accumulated. 
Two things must be said at the outset about what follows, because the argument is open to two 
misreadings and both are refused here. 
The first is that this paper does not claim that physics is mistaken about the vacuum, that the 
measurements are unreliable, or that the theoretical treatment of vacuum states is defective. The 
treatment is extraordinarily successful, and nothing in what f ollows requires any calculation to be 
revised. What is examined is the relation between a term's operational content and the ontological load 
it has come to carry, which is a different question from whether the physics works. 
The second is that this paper does not argue that the universe contains no vacuum, nor that vacuum 
is a fiction. Both would be stronger claims than the argument supports and neither is needed. The claim 
is narrower and, being narrower, is harder to defeat:  an empirical vacuum designation is a domain -
conditioned null result from which ontological absence does not follow, and the vacuum state of 
quantum field theory, which is not a null result but a mathematical definition, defines something other 
than absence. Neither route arrives at nothingness. What has been called vacuum, in every sense physics 
gives the word, is not nothing. 
2. The Empirical Vacuum 
2.1 What the designation records 
Consider what is actually done when a region is certified as vacuum. 
In a laboratory, gas is evacuated from a chamber and the residual pressure is measured, which is to 
say that a gauge responds to the flux of molecules striking a surface, and the response falls below some 
value. In astronomy, a line of sight is examined for absorption features, emission features, or scattering, 
and where these fall below the noise the column density is reported as an upper limit. In particle physics, 
a detector is operated over an interval and no events above threshold are recorded. In each  case the

procedure has the same shape. An apparatus with a characterised response is exposed to a region for an 
interval, and its output does not exceed a stated value. 
This is a measurement, and it is often an extraordinarily precise one. Modern ultra -high-vacuum 
systems reach pressures at which the residual number density is of order a hundred thousand molecules 
per cubic centimetre, which is a reduction of some fourtee n orders of magnitude from atmospheric. 
Astronomical limits on the density of the intergalactic medium are similarly impressive as limits. 
Nothing in this analysis disputes the quality of the work. 
What must be stated is the form of what has been established. The output of the procedure is: under 
this mechanism of interaction, at this sensitivity, over this interval, no signal above threshold. The 
designation "vacuum" is then applied to the region. T he designation is a name for the outcome of the 
procedure, and the procedure has three parameters built into it. It has a mechanism, since the gauge 
responds to molecular impacts and the telescope responds to photons and neither responds to what does 
not couple to it. It has a sensitivity, since every threshold is finite. And it has an interval, since every 
observation is bounded in time. 
An empirical vacuum, then, is a region in which no entity of a specified kind is detected, by a 
specified mechanism, above a specified threshold, within a specified interval. That is the complete 
content of the designation as the procedure delivers it. 
2.2 Two propositions that are not the same 
The step from that content to a claim about the world is a step from one proposition to another, and 
the two are not equivalent. 
The first proposition is: no signal was registered. This is a fact about the outcome of a procedure, and 
it is as reliable as the procedure is. 
The second proposition is: nothing is there. This is a claim about the region, and it does not follow 
from the first without an additional premise, namely that everything that could be there would have 
produced a signal. 
That premise is never available in full. It could only be established by an enumeration of everything 
that might be present together with a demonstration that each member of the enumeration couples to 
the mechanism in use. The enumeration cannot be complet ed, because it would have to include what 
has not been catalogued, and the demonstration cannot be given for the uncatalogued portion, because 
there is nothing to demonstrate it about. What can be established, and routinely is, is the narrower and 
perfectly respectable claim that entities of specified kinds are absent above specified limits. Physics 
states this correctly in its technical literature, where upper limits are reported as upper limits. The 
slippage occurs in the transition to ontological language, where "no detected particles" becomes "empty 
space." 
This is not a subtle point and it is not new. It is the elementary observation that a negative existential 
claim is not established by a failed search, and it holds for any negative existential claim whatever.

Stated at that level of generality it is a remark from the philosophy of science and not a result about 
physics, and if the argument stopped here it would deserve the response that everyone already knows 
this. 
The argument does not stop here, and the next two moves are what give the observation purchase on 
the specific case. 
2.3 The null result as a domain-conditioned datum 
The first move is to locate the null result within the analysis the companion papers have already 
established. 
A null result is a datum. It has the form D(O, I, G) exactly as a positive measurement does: it is 
conditioned on the observer's state, on the instrument and its procedure, and on the domain within which 
observation occurs. The vacuum designation is the value of that datum when the value is zero, and being 
zero does not exempt it from the conditioning. If anything the conditioning is more consequential in the 
null case, because a positive result carries information about what produced it, some of which constrains 
the conditions of its own production, whereas a null result carries none. 
The second companion paper's result now enters, and it sharpens the mechanism parameter into 
something specific. Discrimination in this domain is realised electromagnetically. Every instrument that 
has ever certified a region as empty did so through an electromagnetic mechanism: the pressure gauge 
through molecular impacts mediated by electromagnetic interaction, the telescope through photons, the 
particle detector through ionisation, scintillation, or charge collection, each of them electromagnetic at 
the point where the registration occurs. The convergence established in that paper was total across 
biological, instrumental and computational registration, and it holds here without exception. 
So the content of a vacuum designation, stated with the mechanism made explicit, is: no 
electromagnetically coupled entity was registered here, above this threshold, within this interval. That 
is a considerably narrower claim than emptiness, and the narrowing is not a philosophical caveat added 
from outside. It is what the second companion paper established about the only mechanism by which 
any registration occurs. 
The third move is the one that closes the escape route, and it is the fourth companion paper's theorem 
applied to the null case. 
It might be held that although a single null measurement is conditioned, the conditioning can be 
removed by combining measurements: different instruments, different mechanisms in the loose sense 
of different detector technologies, different positions, diff erent epochs, cross -validated against one 
another until the residual is the region itself. This is a reasonable proposal and it is what the practice 
does. The fourth companion paper answered it in general terms and the answer applies here without 
modification. Consistency among many determinations establishes that no single instrument or pipeline 
is responsible for the result. It does not establish that the class of determinations is free of a contribution 
common to all of them, because a common contributio n is precisely what cross -validation among

members of the class cannot detect. Every member of the class registers electromagnetically. Every 
member is built, calibrated and read within the domain. The class has a common condition, and no 
operation performed among its members exhibits that condition as a separable term. 
The conclusion for the null case follows in the same form as the general result. There is no operation 
F, available within the domain, that converts a domain -conditioned null result into an observer -
independent statement of absence. Zero detections, howeve r many instruments produce them and 
however far apart those instruments are placed, remain zero detections by the domain's mechanism. 
They do not accumulate into ontological absence, for the same reason that access does not accumulate 
into exit. 
This is the paper's first result, and it is worth stating in the flattest available language. The reliability 
of a vacuum designation and the emptiness of the region designated are two different properties, and 
physics has established the first with great care while treating the second as though it followed. 
3. The Distance Between the Vacuum and Nothingness 
The result of the previous section is structural: it holds whatever the region actually contains. It is 
worth setting beside it what is known, on entirely conventional grounds, about what regions called 
empty do contain, because the gap between the designa tion and the ontology is not merely a logical 
possibility. It is a measured quantity, and it is large. 
Three layers must be distinguished, and they differ in evidential standing. Conflating them would 
strengthen the appearance of the argument while weakening the argument itself. 
3.1 What cannot be pumped out 
The first layer is residual matter, and it is straightforwardly empirical. 
No terrestrial procedure evacuates a region completely. Ultra -high-vacuum systems are limited by 
outgassing from the chamber walls, and as the pumping improves this source comes to dominate; the 
residual density falls but does not approach zero, and the practical limits are set by materials rather than 
by pumps. This is a technological fact and carries no ontological weight by itself. 
The astronomical case is more instructive because the technological limits do not apply. The 
interstellar medium of the galaxy holds of order one atom per cubic centimetre. The intergalactic 
medium is far more dilute. Cosmic voids, the emptiest large structures known, have densities well below 
the cosmic mean, and in their interiors the mean atomic density falls to something of order one atom 
per cubic metre. At that density the mean separation between atoms is of order a metre, so that most 
individual cubic metres contain no atom at all: the figure is an average over a large volume and does 
not describe any particular one. Voids are genuinely and deeply empty of atoms, and there is no reason 
to soften this.

But atoms are not the only matter present. Cosmic rays traverse every region of the galaxy. The 
cosmic neutrino background, a relic of the first second, has a predicted number density of order a few 
hundred per cubic centimetre and is present everywhere; i t has not been directly detected, and its 
existence rests on the same nucleosynthetic and cosmological reasoning that supports the rest of the 
standard cosmological model, but no volume is excluded from it. If the standard cosmological model is 
approximately correct, dark matter is distributed throughout the cosmic web at densities of order a 
fraction of a GeV per cubic centimetre in the solar neighbourhood, and is less concentrated than 
luminous matter, so that a region containing no atoms need not be free of it. 
The last of these is the sharpest illustration of the section's point, and it deserves to be stated as such. 
Dark matter is inferred from gravitational effects and has never been registered by any electromagnetic 
instrument, which is what its name records: it does not couple to the mechanism by which every direct 
detection is performed. Whatever else it is, it is on this account a component of the universe that can be 
present in regions certified as empty and that the certifying instruments cannot see. Physics has therefore 
already encountered, within its own standard model of cosmology, an instance of the exact situation the 
previous section described in general terms: a region can be electromagnetically null and materially 
occupied, and the null designation records the mechanism rather than the region. 
3.2 What cannot be shielded 
The second layer is background radiation, and it is the best-measured item in the entire discussion. 
The cosmic microwave background fills the universe. Its temperature is close to 2.725 K and its 
photon number density is of order four hundred per cubic centimetre, and it is not a signal arriving from 
a direction but a bath present at every point (Planck Collaboration, 2020a). No shielding excludes it, 
because any shield is at a temperature of its own and radiates accordingly; no region has been found 
without it, and the standard cosmological model gives no reason to expect one at the present epoch. 
One qualification belongs here and it should be made in the paper rather than left for a referee to 
make. The microwave background is thermal radiation, and thermal radiation dilutes. The universe 
continues to expand, the background continues to cool, and its density continues to fall. So the statement 
that no region is free of it is a statement about the present epoch, and someone may reasonably respond 
that this reflects the current temperature rather than a structural impossibility. That response is corr ect 
as far as it goes, and it is the reason the argument of this paper does not rest on the microwave 
background. What the background establishes is an existential fact about the present universe: there is 
at present no region, anywhere, in which radiation  is absent. It does not establish that such a region is 
impossible, and the argument does not need it to. 
The contrast between the two layers is what earns the section its place. Matter is distributed in lumps 
with gaps between them, and regions containing no atoms are easy to find. Radiation is not distributed 
that way. It has no gaps. The observational asymmetry between the two is a fact of the present universe, 
whatever one concludes from it.

3.3 What cannot be removed 
The third layer is theoretical, and its standing must be marked carefully, because it is where the 
largest claims in the literature are made and where the largest confusions occur. 
In quantum field theory, the state of a field with no particles present is not a state in which the field 
is absent. The field is defined throughout the region; what is specified is which of its states obtains. In 
the lowest-energy state, the expectation v alue of the field vanishes while the expectation value of its 
square does not: ⟨0| φ |0⟩ = 0 and ⟨0| φ² |0⟩ ≠ 0. The variance is non -zero. This is not a description of 
the field oscillating in time. It is a statement about the distribution of values that m easurements of the 
field would yield, and about the fact that the distribution has width. 
The origin of this width is the uncertainty relation between a field and its conjugate momentum, 
which forbids the simultaneous specification of both. It is therefore not a thermal effect and does not 
vanish with temperature, and it is not a dilution effect and does not vanish with expansion. 
The empirical standing of this layer requires care. The variance itself is not directly observed; what 
are observed are effects that the standard treatment attributes to it. The Lamb shift in atomic hydrogen 
was measured in 1947 and is calculated with reference to the coupling of the electron to the quantised 
electromagnetic field (Lamb & Retherford, 1947). The Casimir force between conducting plates was 
predicted in 1948 and measured in the modern era to good precision (Casimir, 1948; Lamoreaux, 1997). 
The dynamical Casimir effect, in which a boundary condition modulated at relativistic speeds produces 
real photons, was observed in a superconducting circuit in 2011 (Wilson et al., 2011). These are among 
the best-tested predictions in physics. 
They do not, however, license the inference that a definite quantity of energy resides in every cubic 
centimetre of space, and one prominent argument holds that they do not license even the weaker 
inference usually drawn. The Casimir force, on Jaffe's anal ysis, can be computed entirely in terms of 
the interaction between the charges and currents of the plates, with the coupling constant appearing 
explicitly and the zero -point energy of the free field never invoked; on that treatment the effect 
demonstrates the physics of quantum electrodynamics without demonstrating the reality of a vacuum 
energy density (Jaffe, 2005). The point is not that the standard account is wrong. It is that the effects 
establish that the theory works, and the further claim that the t heory's vacuum is a reservoir of energy 
is a claim about interpretation which those effects do not by themselves settle. That distinction becomes 
the subject of Chapter 6. 
What this layer does establish, and it is enough for the present purpose, is that the theory's own 
account of a region with no particles is not an account of a region with nothing in it. Whatever else the 
vacuum state is, it is a state of fields the theory has already specified. In the standard formulations of 
quantum field theory, the vacuum is not the absence of fields, and the theory contains no procedure that 
removes them.

3.4 What the three layers show together 
Taken together the three layers give the measure of the gap. A region certified as empty contains, on 
entirely orthodox grounds, residual atoms in most cases and dark matter in all, a bath of relic photons 
and relic neutrinos everywhere, and fields in a st ate whose variance no operation reduces. The 
designation records that an electromagnetic instrument found nothing above threshold. The inventory 
is what is there. 
None of this shows that a region of genuine absence is impossible. That is not the claim and it is not 
needed. What it shows is that in every case where the question has actually been examined, the gap 
between the designation and the contents has been larg e, and it has been closed in one direction only: 
each new investigation has added to the inventory. The subtle -matter theorists of the seventeenth 
century were wrong about mercury vapour and right about the structure of the inference, and the 
structure of the inference has not changed. 
4. Two Criteria Under One Name 
4.1 The two criteria 
Physics uses the word "vacuum" under two distinct criteria, and the purpose of setting them side by 
side here is to analyse a definitional discontinuity. It is not to claim that the two are in contradiction, 
and it is not to claim that they have been reconciled. Neither claim would be correct. 
The first criterion is the one inherited from classical physics and still in continuous use. A vacuum is 
a region containing no material medium. This is the sense carried by the constants of classical 
electrodynamics, where the speed of light in vacuum, th e vacuum permittivity ε₀ and the vacuum 
permeability μ₀ characterise propagation in the absence of a material medium; it is the sense in which 
Maxwell's equations in vacuum are written; and it is the sense operative in every laboratory and every 
vacuum chamber. On this criterion, light passing through a region does not disqualify it. Radiation is 
what propagates through the vacuum; it is not what fills it. 
The second criterion belongs to quantum field theory. The vacuum state |0 ⟩ is the lowest -energy 
eigenstate of the Hamiltonian, equivalently the state annihilated by every annihilation operator, so that 
a_k |0⟩ = 0 for all modes k (Peskin & Schroeder, 1995; Weinberg, 1995). On this criterion the presence 
of a single photon disqualifies a state from being the vacuum, since a photon is an excitation of the 
electromagnetic field and a state containing one is not the lowest. The criterion is not about the absence 
of a medium but about the excitation level of the fields. 
These two do not cut in the same place, and the divergence is easy to exhibit. Take a chamber 
evacuated of gas with sunlight passing through it. On the classical criterion this region is a vacuum, and 
it is precisely the configuration in which the vacuum s peed of light is defined. On the quantum field 
theoretic criterion, the electromagnetic field in that region is manifestly not in its lowest state, so that

the same region satisfies the classical criterion while the field state within it is not the vacuum state. 
The two criteria take different logical subjects, a region in the first case and a state in the second, and 
the verdicts they return do not coincide. 
4.2 Non-identical and differently employed 
It would be a mistake to present this as an inconsistency in physics, and the mistake is worth naming 
so that the analysis is not misread as making it. 
The two criteria answer different questions and serve different formal roles. The classical criterion 
answers a question about media: whether propagation through this region is modified by matter within 
it, which is what determines whether the free-space equations apply. The quantum criterion answers a 
question about states: which state of the field obtains, which is what determines what calculations of 
transition amplitudes take as their reference. Each is well defined within its own setting, each is used 
correctly by practitioners, and there is no situation in which a physicist is confused about which is meant. 
The retention of one word for two criteria has cost the practice nothing. 
What it has permitted is something else, and it belongs to a different level of analysis. When a term 
migrates between theoretical settings while retaining its name, the ontological intuition attached to the 
name migrates with it, and does so without being  restated or defended in the new setting, because 
nothing in the migration signals that a restatement is required. The reader of a text on classical 
electrodynamics acquires from "vacuum" the sense of a region with nothing in it. That sense is 
approximately harmless there, because on the classical criterion the claim being made is about the 
absence of a material medium and can be verified as such. When the same reader encounters the vacuum 
state of quantum field theory, the acquired sense travels intact, an d it is now attached to an object it 
does not fit: a state of fields that are present, whose variance is non -zero, and whose energy is a 
contested quantity. Nothing in the notation warns of the transfer, because the notation is the same word. 
This is the method of an earlier paper in this series applied to a second term. The examination of the 
boundary between matter and radiation found that two classification systems coexist in physics, the 
fermion/boson taxonomy of the Standard Model and the inherited matter/radiation vocabulary of the 
nineteenth century, each internally serviceable, and that their coexistence conceals an unreconciled 
classificatory inconsistency in which "radiation" spans both a fermion flux and a boson flux (Zhong, 
2026a). The analysis there was not that physicists are confused, and it is not that here. It is that a term 
carrying an unstated ontological commitment can move between classification systems without the 
commitment being examined at the point of transfer, and that the examination is worth performing. 
The point of performing it in the present case is that the term does not stop at two settings. It acquires 
a third role, and a fourth, and the load it carries at the end is not the load it was given at the start.

5. What |0⟩ Defines 
5.1 The definition escapes the argument of Chapter 2 
The argument of Chapter 2 applies to the empirical criterion. It does not apply to the vacuum state, 
and this must be conceded without qualification before anything further is said, because an argument 
that appeared to defeat |0⟩ by observational considerations would be defeating something other than |0⟩. 
The vacuum state is not defined by a failed detection. It is defined mathematically, as the lowest -
energy eigenstate of a specified Hamiltonian, and its properties are derived rather than measured. No 
instrument certifies a state as |0 ⟩, and no threshold enters the definition. Whatever is wrong with 
inferring absence from a null result, nothing of that kind is wrong here. The definition is exact, and it is 
one of the most productive definitions in the history of physics. 
Two features of it, however, are consequential for the question this paper is asking, and both are 
internal to the definition rather than imposed on it from outside. 
5.2 What the definition presupposes 
The first is a matter of logical order. 
To speak of the lowest-energy eigenstate, a Hamiltonian is required. To write a Hamiltonian, a field 
content is required: which fields exist, with what couplings, on what manifold. The vacuum state is 
therefore specified relative to a prior specification of the fields whose state it is. It is the answer to the 
question "which state of these fields is lowest?", and that question cannot be asked before the fields 
have been given. 
The consequence is that quantum field theory has never defined a state of nothingness and has never 
claimed to. It defines the state of minimum excitation of a given field content. The fields are the input 
to the definition, not its output. This is not a l imitation that the theory conceals; it is stated plainly in 
every textbook treatment, where the construction begins from a Lagrangian and proceeds to the vacuum, 
never the reverse. But the consequence is rarely drawn in the ontological register, and the consequence 
is this: the object that physics calls the vacuum is, by construction, a state of a specified field content. 
It is not ontologically prior to the fields it presupposes, since those fields had to be supplied before the 
state could be identified. 
The word covers the excitation level and says nothing about the substrate. Whatever the fields are, 
and whatever their existence consists in, the vacuum state does not address it. The question of where 
the fields come from is not answered by quantum field theory because it is not asked; the theory takes 
them as given and computes. This is the boundary of the theory's competence, not a defect in it, and it 
is a boundary the theory itself draws.

5.3 The identification of a vacuum is not observer-independent 
The second feature is more consequential still, and it is the point at which the results of the companion 
papers reappear from an unexpected direction. The identification of a state as the vacuum, although 
mathematical and untouched by every observational argument of Chapter 2, is nevertheless not 
observer-independent, and neither is the particle content with respect to which that identification is 
made. This is not a claim advanced by this paper. It is a standard result of quantum field theory, 
established in the 1970s and treated as elementary in the specialist literature ever since. 
The result has two parts. 
The first concerns accelerated observers in flat spacetime. Fulling showed that the canonical 
quantisation of a field in Minkowski space does not yield a unique particle notion: the quantisation 
carried out with respect to the time coordinate of a uniformly accelerated frame is unitarily inequivalent 
to the standard one (Fulling, 1973). Davies and Unruh drew out the physical content: a uniformly 
accelerated detector moving through the standard Minkowski vacuum responds as though immersed in 
a thermal bath, with temperature proportional to its proper acceleration (Davies, 1975; Unruh, 1976). 
The state is one state. The inertial observer characterises it as containing no particles; the accelerated 
observer's detector clicks. The disagreement is not a disagreem ent about the state, nor a matter of one 
observer being mistaken. Particle content, on this result, is not an attribute the state possesses 
independently of the observer with respect to whom it is defined. 
It should be stated that the Unruh effect has not been directly observed. The temperature associated 
with attainable accelerations is minute, and the experimental proposals that exist are demanding. What 
the effect has is a secure theoretical standing with in the standard formalism, derived rather than 
postulated, and a place in the textbook treatments of quantum field theory in curved spacetime (Birrell 
& Davies, 1982; Wald, 1994). 
The second part concerns curved spacetime, and it is structural rather than dynamical. The privileged 
status of the Minkowski vacuum rests on the Poincaré symmetry of flat spacetime, which supplies a 
preferred timelike Killing vector and hence a preferred decomposition of field modes into positive and 
negative frequency. A general spacetime has no such symmetry and no such Killing vector. In its 
absence the decomposition is not unique, different choices yield unitarily inequivalent quantisations, 
and there is no preferred vacuum state to be found (Birrell & Davies, 1982; Wald, 1994). The vacuum 
of quantum field theory in curved spacetime is not a state the spacetime possesses. It is a state relative 
to a choice, and the spacetime of cosmology is not Minkowski space. 
The bearing of this on the argument of the companion papers is direct, and it is worth stating carefully 
because it is the strongest single result the present paper has to offer. 
The fourth companion paper established that a cosmological datum is conditioned on the observing 
domain and that no domain-internal operation resolves it into an observer-independent remainder. That 
argument proceeded from the conditions of observation. The result just described reaches a structurally

===== PAGE 14 =====
14 
 
parallel conclusion from inside the formalism, without any premise about observers, rendering, or 
domains: what counts as the vacuum depends on the state of motion of the observer with respect to 
whom the field is quantised, and in a general spacetime the dependence cannot be removed by any 
choice that is not itself a choice. Two independent routes, one from the conditions of observation and 
one from the mathematics of quantisation, arrive at the same conclusion about the same object. 
The convergence is not evidence for the companion papers' account, and it is not offered as such; a 
result of standard quantum field theory does not support an ontology that it neither states nor requires. 
What it does is remove a line of objection. The observer-dependence of the particle notion, and the non-
uniqueness of vacuum selection in a general spacetime, are not exotic theses that this series of papers 
has introduced. It is a property of the vacuum that quantum field theory derived for itself, half a century 
ago, and has taught ever since. 
5.4 What the two routes have in common 
The empirical criterion and the mathematical criterion fail to deliver nothingness for different reasons, 
and the difference is worth marking because it prevents the two arguments from being run together. 
The empirical criterion fails to deliver it because a null result is a domain-conditioned datum, and no 
operation within the domain converts it into a statement of absence. The failure is epistemic in form: it 
concerns what can be established. 
The mathematical criterion fails to deliver it because the state it defines is the minimum -excitation 
state of a field content that had to be given in advance, and because the identification of that state is in 
addition relative to an observer's motion and , in general spacetimes, to a choice of quantisation. The 
failure is definitional in form: it concerns what has been defined. 
What they have in common is the conclusion. Under neither criterion does physics possess a term for 
nothing. Under the first it possesses a term for what its instruments did not register. Under the second 
it possesses a term for the quietest available state of things that are there. The word "vacuum" has never 
named an absence in physics. It has named a threshold, and then a state. 
6. From Absence of Detection to Physical Property: The Ontological Loading of Vacuum 
The preceding chapters have established what the two criteria deliver. This chapter examines how a 
term whose original content was operational came to carry ontological weight, and what happened at 
each point where the weight increased. 
The status of this chapter must be stated before it begins. It does not establish the claims of Chapters 
2 and 5, which stand or fall on their own grounds. It does not offer the difficulties encountered at the 
end of the sequence as evidence that the seque nce was mistaken. What it does is display the transfers 
themselves, so that the point at which a term acquired a commitment it had not previously carried can 
be located rather than assumed.

===== PAGE 15 =====
15 
 
6.1 The first transfer: from designation to state 
The starting point is the operational designation described in Chapter 2: a region in which the relevant 
entities are not detected. This is a classification applied to a region on the basis of a measurement 
outcome, and it carries no commitment about what the region contains beyond the negative result. 
Torricelli's space, the evacuated chamber and the interstellar sightline are all designations of this kind. 
The first transfer occurs with the quantisation of fields. The vacuum ceases to be a classification 
applied to a region and becomes a state of a system: a vector in a Hilbert space, an eigenstate of an 
operator, an object with a definite mathematical characterisation. This is a substantial change in the type 
of thing being spoken of. A designation is a description of a region relative to a procedure; a state is an 
element of the theory's ontology, on the same footing as any other state the system can occupy. 
The transfer was not marked, because the word did not change. And the consequence of leaving it 
unmarked is a persistent ambiguity between the two, visible in the way the results of Chapter 4 are 
discussed: "the vacuum" refers sometimes to a region and som etimes to a state, and the properties of 
the second are routinely attributed to the first. A region of intergalactic space is described as having a 
vacuum energy density; what has an energy is a state, and whether that state obtains in that region is a 
further question which the description passes over. 
6.2 The second transfer: from state to energy 
The second transfer assigns the state an energy. 
The formal basis is direct. Each mode of a quantised field contributes ½ħω to the energy of the lowest 
state, and the sum over modes is the zero -point energy. That the sum diverges was recognised 
immediately, and the standard response is that in the absenc e of gravity only energy differences are 
physically significant, so that the divergent constant may be removed by normal ordering or absorbed 
by renormalisation without observable consequence. 
This response is entirely successful within its scope, and the scope is worth naming precisely: the 
standard non -gravitational observables invoked in this context do not require an independently 
measurable absolute vacuum-energy offset. Each of them is a difference. The Lamb shift is a difference 
between energy levels. The Casimir force is a difference between the configuration with plates and the 
configuration without them. The dynamical Casimir photons are produced by a time -dependent 
boundary condition, and what is calculated is a rate. The theory does not require the absolute value to 
be defined, and it takes no position on whether the divergent sum corresponds to anything. 
The ontological transfer occurs when the removed constant is nevertheless spoken of as a quantity 
that is there. This move is not part of the calculation and is not required by it. It is an interpretation of 
a term that the calculation discards, and it has  been contested within physics: on Jaffe's analysis the 
Casimir force can be derived without invoking the zero-point energy of the free field at all, which does 
not show the standard account to be wrong but does show that the effect fails to establish the reality of

===== PAGE 16 =====
16 
 
the discarded term (Jaffe, 2005). The philosophical literature has made the same point in more general 
form, arguing that the vacuum energy density of quantum field theory is not straightforwardly a physical 
quantity of the kind that the cosmological discussion assumes (Rugh & Zinkernagel, 2002). 
At this stage of the sequence, then, the term carries: a state, and an energy attributed to the state 
whose status as a physical quantity is not settled by the calculations that succeed. 
6.3 The third transfer: from energy to gravitational source 
The third transfer is the one that makes the difference visible, because gravity is the one place where 
the absolute value cannot be discarded. 
In general relativity every form of energy gravitates. If the vacuum possesses an energy density then 
it enters the stress-energy tensor, and a Lorentz-invariant vacuum energy has the equation of state of a 
cosmological constant: an energy density that doe s not dilute as space expands. Zel'dovich set out the 
identification, and the difficulty it produces has been the subject of a substantial literature since 
(Zel'dovich, 1968; Weinberg, 1989). 
The difficulty is that the magnitude estimated from the field-theoretic side and the magnitude inferred 
from cosmological observation do not agree, and the discrepancy is not marginal. It should be described 
carefully. The theoretical estimate is not a sin gle well-defined number: it depends on the cut-off scale 
adopted, on the regularisation scheme, and on which fields are included, and quoting a specific number 
of orders of magnitude gives the estimate a definiteness it does not have. What can be said with out 
depending on any of these choices is that natural estimates from the field -theoretic side exceed the 
observationally inferred value of the cosmological constant by an enormous margin under any of the 
standard treatments, and that no accepted mechanism accounts for the difference (Weinberg, 1989; 
Rugh & Zinkernagel, 2002). The observational side is by contrast well determined: the accelerating 
expansion was established from supernova distances (Riess et al., 1998; Perlmutter et al., 1999) and the 
cosmological parameters have been measured to high precision (Planck Collaboration, 2020b). 
The point of examining this here is not that the discrepancy proves anything about the vacuum's 
ontological status. It does not, and a substantial fraction of theoretical physics is directed at resolving it 
by means that leave the standard account intact. The point is that it is located exactly where the sequence 
predicts a problem would appear. The first two transfers took place in settings where the absolute value 
of the vacuum energy played no role and where nothing tested whether the term denoted a quantity. The 
third transfer placed the term in the one setting where the absolute value cannot be avoided, and the 
theory's largest unresolved quantitative discrepancy appeared at that step. That is not a proof. It is a 
diagnosis of where the load was placed. 
6.4 The accumulated load 
The sequence can now be stated in one line, and the statement is the substance of this chapter.

===== PAGE 17 =====
17 
 
A term introduced to record that a procedure detected nothing came to name a state; the state was 
assigned an energy that no non -gravitational measurement requires to be defined; and the energy was 
made a source term in the field equations of gravity and a  candidate explanation for the large -scale 
behaviour of the universe. 
Each step was individually reasonable, and each was taken by physicists doing careful work for good 
reasons. What was never done was to return to the beginning and ask what the term had been introduced 
to record. The designation recorded a threshold. Four steps later, the same word denoted a quantity of 
energy in every cubic centimetre of space, driving cosmic acceleration. 
This is what the ontological loading of a term consists in, and it is why the analysis of Chapter 4 was 
not a pedantic point about vocabulary. A word that migrates between theoretical settings carries its 
accumulated commitments forward and acquires new ones at each stage, and the commitments are never 
audited, because auditing them would require noticing that a migration had occurred, and nothing in the 
notation records it. 
7. Consequences and Falsification 
7.1 What follows 
Two consequences require statement, and both are narrower than the discussion that produced them. 
The first concerns how null results should be classified. In current practice a null result is reported 
as an upper limit in the technical literature and translated into an emptiness claim in the interpretive one, 
and the translation is invisible because the same word does both jobs. On the analysis given here, a null 
result is a datum of the form D(O, I, G) whose value is zero, and it is conditioned on the observing 
domain exactly as a positive result is. What it establishes is that entities of a specified kind, coupling to 
a specified mechanism, are absent above a specified threshold. What it does not establish is absence. 
The distinction has practical content: it identifies which questions further sensitivity will settle, namely 
questions about entities t hat couple to the mechanism in use, and which it will not, namely questions 
about what is there. 
The second concerns the standing of the vacuum in cosmological reasoning. The vacuum energy is 
treated in the cosmological literature as a physical quantity whose value is to be determined, and the 
difficulty is understood as a discrepancy between two determinations of it. On the analysis given here, 
the prior question is whether the term denotes a quantity of that kind, and the answer is not established 
by the successes that made the term indispensable, since those successes are all differences and none of 
them tests the absolute value. This does not resolve the difficulty and is not offered as resolving it. It 
relocates it: the question is not only why the numbers disagree but whether the object whose numbers 
disagree has been shown to be an object. 
Beyond these, the general result of the paper connects to the series that precedes it. The four 
companion papers established that physical theory presupposes an observer, that discrimination is

===== PAGE 18 =====
18 
 
realised electromagnetically, that domains are individuated by mechanism, and that a datum is not 
resolvable within a domain into an observer -independent remainder. This paper adds the case of the 
null datum, and finds that it behaves as the general result  predicts: zero detections do not accumulate 
into absence, for the same reason that access does not accumulate into exit. And it finds, in the observer-
dependence of the vacuum state, that quantum field theory reached a structurally parallel conclusion 
about the same object by a route of its own. 
7.2 Falsification 
Each condition below is stated so that its satisfaction defeats a result of this paper rather than 
requiring its adjustment. 
First, an operational criterion for ontological absence that does not consist in a null detection. The 
argument of Chapter 2 holds that the designation of a region as empty is the outcome of a procedure 
with a mechanism, a threshold and an interval, and th at no such procedure establishes absence. A 
criterion by which a region could be certified as containing nothing, and which did not reduce to the 
failure of some instrument to register something, would defeat that argument directly. Criteria which 
reduce to null detection by a broader class of instruments do not satisfy this condition, and the condition 
is stated so as to exclude them. 
Second, a demonstration that combination of null results across mechanisms yields an unconditioned 
absence claim. The argument holds that consistency among null determinations establishes that no 
single instrument is responsible, and not that the class is free of a common condition. An explicit 
separation of the common condition, validated against a benchmark not itself constructed within the 
domain, would end the result. 
Third, a formulation of quantum field theory in which the vacuum state is defined without prior 
specification of a field content. The argument of Chapter 5.2 holds that the vacuum is the minimum -
excitation state of fields given in advance, and that the theory therefore defines no state of nothingness. 
A construction that derived the field content rather than assuming it, and identified a vacuum with 
respect to no antecedent specification, would defeat that argument. 
Fourth, a demonstration that the vacuum state is observer -independent after all. The argument of 
Chapter 5.3 rests on the non-uniqueness of the particle notion under inequivalent quantisations and on 
the absence of a preferred timelike Killing vector in a general spacetime. A demonstration that a 
preferred vacuum can be singled out in a general spacetime on physical grounds, without an additional 
choice, would remove the second route to the paper's conclusion, leaving the first standing but 
unsupported by it. 
Fifth, a measurement demonstrating sensitivity to an absolute vacuum -energy offset by non -
gravitational means. The analysis of Chapter 6.2 holds that the non -gravitational observables adduced 
in support of vacuum energy are differences, and that none of th em requires an absolute offset to be 
defined. A measurement sensitive to the absolute value, independent of gravitational effects, would

===== PAGE 19 =====
19 
 
show the term to denote a quantity in the way the third transfer assumed, and would defeat the diagnosis 
of Chapter 6. 
7.3 The position 
What remains is to state the position without qualification. 
A region is called empty when an instrument built within one rendering domain, responding through 
the mechanism by which that domain discriminates, registers nothing above a threshold within an 
interval. That outcome is reliable and it is conditioned, and no operation available within the domain 
converts it into a statement about what the region contains. 
Where physics has replaced the designation with a definition, the definition is not a definition of 
absence. The vacuum state is the minimum -excitation state of a field content specified in advance; it 
presupposes what it is a state of; and it is not the same state for observers in different states of motion, 
nor uniquely determined in a spacetime without the symmetry that flat space supplies. 
Between these two, the word acquired an energy and then a gravitational role, and the largest 
unresolved quantitative discrepancy in the subject appeared at the step where the absolute value could 
no longer be discarded. 
Physics has never had a term for nothing. It has had a term for what was not registered, and a term 
for the quietest state of what is there, and it has used one word for both. Torricelli's opponents were 
wrong about the substance and right about the infere nce, and the space above the mercury was never 
empty.
