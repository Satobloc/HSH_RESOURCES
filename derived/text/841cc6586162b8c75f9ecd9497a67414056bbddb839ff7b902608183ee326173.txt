Abstract
We present a cross-survey chemical decomposition of 468,000 Milky Way stars assembled by harmonising 
APOGEE DR17 and GALAH DR3 abundances and matching the resulting spectroscopic catalogue to Gaia 
DR3. The classifier is restricted to four abundance dimensions ([Fe/H], [Mg/Fe], [Si/Fe] and [Al/Fe]), so 
orbital actions and integrals of motion remain held out for validation. A Bayesian-information-criterion-
selected four-component Gaussian mixture model identifies the thin disc, thick disc, a chemically 
anomalous population (CAP) and the metal-poor halo; 91 per cent of the sample has posterior membership 
probability above 0.75. The chemically defined populations occupy distinct regions of action space and of 
the E–Lz Lindblad plane, providing an orthogonal dynamical test rather than a circular chemo-kinematic 
selection. Under explicit literature-motivated kinematic boundaries, the CAP is partitioned into Gaia-
Sausage/Enceladus candidates (34 ± 5 per cent), Sequoia candidates (8 ± 3 per cent) and a Splash-like 
component (58 ± 5 per cent), where the uncertainties include boundary sensitivity. The thin-disc radial 
metallicity gradient is −0.065 ± 0.004 dex kpc⁻¹, whereas the thick-disc gradient is flatter at −0.020 ± 0.006 
dex kpc⁻¹. These fractions are selection- and boundary-dependent properties of the analysed catalogue, not 
intrinsic Galactic mass fractions. The principal contribution is a transparent validation architecture, an 
explicit uncertainty budget and conservative claim boundaries.
Keywords: Milky Way; Galactic disc; Galactic halo; stellar abundances; stellar kinematics; Gaussian 
mixtures; survey selection

1. Introduction
The Milky Way is the only large galaxy for which chemical abundances, distances and three-
dimensional motions can be measured star by star across its principal structural components. Galactic 
archaeology exploits the long-lived fossil information encoded in stellar chemistry and phase space: 
abundances constrain birth environments and enrichment timescales, while orbital structure records 
subsequent heating, migration and accretion (Freeman and Bland-Hawthorn, 2002; Helmi, 2008; 
Bland-Hawthorn and Gerhard, 2016; Helmi, 2020). Because phase mixing erases configuration-space 
substructure more rapidly than structure in integrals of motion, combining chemically informative 
surveys with Gaia astrometry has become central to reconstructing the Galaxy's assembly history 
(Helmi and White, 1999).

The final APOGEE release provides high-resolution near-infrared spectroscopy across the Galactic 
disc and bulge (Majewski et al., 2017; Abdurro'uf et al., 2022), GALAH DR3 supplies complementary 
optical abundances (Buder et al., 2021), and Gaia DR3 provides astrometry and radial velocities on an 
unprecedented scale (Gaia Collaboration et al., 2023). These surveys make it possible to ask whether 
structure identified in chemical space has an independent dynamical counterpart. That separation of 
roles is essential: a population selected simultaneously by chemistry and kinematics cannot later use 
the same kinematic information as independent confirmation. It is equally important to distinguish 
astrophysical structure from survey structure, because APOGEE targeting, Gaia completeness and 
Gaia subsample selection vary with magnitude, colour, sky position and observing strategy (Zasowski 
et al., 2013, 2017; Cantat-Gaudin et al., 2023; Castro-Ginard et al., 2023).
Barua (2025) described the combined recovery of chemical and dynamical fossils as "Galactic 
palaeontology". A subsequent chemo-kinematic tomography study combined APOGEE, GALAH and 
Gaia DR3 to connect chemical-space classification with action and integral-of-motion structure 
(Barua, 2026). The present study converts that conceptual and empirical foundation into a stricter 
falsifiable design in which the dynamical variables are explicitly withheld from the classifier and 
reserved for validation. Barua (2022) developed a complementary full-spectrum stellar-population 
synthesis framework; it is cited here for methodological continuity rather than used as evidence for 
catalogue ages.
1.1. Closest prior work and scope of the contribution
The closest methodological comparator is Myeong et al. (2022), who used an unsupervised 
Gaussian mixture decomposition of APOGEE DR17 and GALAH DR3 to identify four dominant 
constituents of the local eccentric halo. That study sits within a broader literature that established 
chemically and dynamically distinct halo populations, including low- and high-alpha sequences, Gaia-
Sausage/Enceladus (GSE), Sequoia and in-situ heated populations (Nissen and Schuster, 2010; 
Hawkins et al., 2015; Belokurov et al., 2018; Helmi et al., 2018; Mackereth et al., 2019; Myeong et al., 
2019; Naidu et al., 2020; Horta et al., 2021). Accordingly, neither Gaussian mixtures nor the joint use 
of APOGEE and GALAH is claimed as novel. The present scope is different in three respects: it treats 
the dominant disc and halo populations in a common architecture; it withholds dynamics from the 
chemical classifier and uses actions and the E–Lz plane as an orthogonal validation layer; and it 
reports boundary, potential, model-family and radial-bin sensitivity alongside the component fractions. 
The central question is therefore not whether four clusters can be fitted, but whether a chemically 
defined decomposition survives physically independent dynamical tests. Barua (2026) provides the 
immediate empirical precursor; the present study narrows those claims, stress-tests the decomposition 
and reformulates the analysis around held-out validation and reproducibility.
The analysis addresses four questions. First, is the four-component chemical model statistically 
preferred over nearby model orders? Secondly, do the chemical components occupy distinct action-
space and integral-of-motion families? Thirdly, how strongly do operational GSE, Sequoia and Splash 
fractions depend on adopted boundaries? Finally, are the inferred radial metallicity gradients 
consistent with modern APOGEE and Gaia-ESO measurements? Fig. 1 summarises the analysis logic.

2. Data and sample construction
2.1. Spectroscopic and astrometric inputs
The primary spectroscopic input is APOGEE DR17, which provides H-band spectra at resolving 
power R approximately 22,500 and homogeneous ASPCAP estimates of [Fe/H], [Mg/Fe], [Si/Fe] and 
[Al/Fe] (Majewski et al., 2017; Abdurro'uf et al., 2022). GALAH DR3 provides optical spectra at R 
approximately 28,000 and a complementary abundance set (Buder et al., 2021). The merged 
spectroscopic catalogue uses stars observed by both surveys as abundance-calibration anchors; the 
resulting records are positionally matched to Gaia DR3 within 1 arcsec to obtain astrometry and 
phase-space information. Gaia astrometric systematics and covariance information are treated 
following the DR3 documentation and parallax-characterisation work (Lindegren et al., 2021; Gaia 
Collaboration et al., 2023). Bayesian distances are taken from StarHorse where available (Queiroz et 
al., 2018), and line-of-sight velocities are drawn from the spectroscopic pipelines.
Absolute abundance scales are not identical between surveys. Comparisons of APOGEE, GALAH 
and Gaia-ESO find broad consistency but residual element-dependent offsets that can exceed 0.1 dex 
in restricted stellar-parameter regimes (Hegedus et al., 2023). The merged catalogue therefore uses 
overlap stars as empirical calibration anchors, and residual survey dependence is carried as a 
systematic limitation. The scientific interpretation is restricted to broad population structure and does 
not rely on abundance differences of only a few hundredths of a dex. The reproducibility specification 
therefore includes overlap-star residuals as functions of effective temperature, surface gravity, 
metallicity and signal-to-noise ratio (Appendix B).
2.2. Quality cuts and final sample

parameter astrometric solution, fractional parallax uncertainty below 5 per cent, APOGEE 
STARFLAG = 0 where applicable, Galactic latitude |b| > 5 degrees, and fractional action uncertainty 
σ(J)/J < 0.2. The latter is evaluated after Monte Carlo propagation of the Gaia covariance matrix. The

3. Methods
3.1. Chemical-space Gaussian mixture model
Each star i is represented by the four-dimensional abundance vector qi = ([Fe/H], [Mg/Fe], [Si/Fe], 
[Al/Fe]). A k-component Gaussian mixture with weights πk, means μk and covariance matrices Σk is 
fitted by expectation maximisation. No velocity, action, orbital-energy or angular-momentum variable 
enters the fit. The baseline model treats the measured abundances as point estimates; unlike extreme-
deconvolution approaches, it does not explicitly convolve each component with star-by-star 
heteroscedastic abundance uncertainties (Bovy et al., 2011). This approximation is tested indirectly 
through quality cuts, survey-separated checks and bootstrap stability, and is retained as a stated 
limitation.
ln ℒ = ∑ᵢ ln [ ∑ₖ πₖ ￿(qᵢ | μₖ, Σₖ) ] (1)
Posterior membership is evaluated from Bayes' rule, and a hard label is assigned only when P(k | q i) 
> 0.75. Stars below that threshold remain in the soft catalogue and contribute through their posterior 
probabilities where appropriate. This distinction prevents uncertain objects from being forced into a 
physically over-interpreted class.
P(k | qᵢ) = πₖ ￿(qᵢ | μₖ, Σₖ) / ∑ⱼ πⱼ ￿(qᵢ | μⱼ, Σⱼ) (2)
3.2. Model order and non-Gaussian sensitivity
Model order is selected over k = 2,...,8 using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). For sample 
size n and pk free parameters, the criterion is:
BIC(k) = pₖ ln n − 2 ln ℒₘₐₓ(k) (3)
The analysis yields a unique minimum at k = 4, with ΔBIC greater than 1,200 relative to the nearest 
alternatives. The BIC follows the large-sample approximation of Schwarz (1978), and a difference of 
this magnitude is conventionally regarded as decisive within the tested model family (Kass and 
Raftery, 1995). Because Gaussian ellipsoids cannot perfectly trace curved abundance sequences, the 
same four-dimensional space is also checked with HDBSCAN (Campello et al., 2013). The purpose of 
the non-parametric run is not to force one-to-one labels, but to test whether the CAP disappears when 
ellipsoidal geometry is relaxed.

3.3. Actions, energy and the held-out validation layer
Orbital actions are computed with AGAMA using the Stäckel-fudge approximation in 
MWPotential2014 (Binney, 2012; Bovy, 2015; Sanders and Binney, 2016; Vasiliev, 2019). For each 
population, we report the trace of the action covariance matrix as an action-space dispersion statistic, 
DJ. This is a descriptive measure of orbital spread and is not a thermodynamic temperature or a 
calibrated stellar-age proxy.
DJ = Tr(CJ) = Var(JR) + Var(Jφ) + Var(Jz) (4)
Observational uncertainties are propagated with 500 Monte Carlo draws from the Gaia covariance 
matrix in proper motion, parallax and radial velocity, including the published astrometric correlations 
(Lindegren et al., 2021; Gaia Collaboration et al., 2023). A second Galactic potential based on 
McMillan (2017) is used as a sensitivity test; the resulting actions change at the approximately 5 per 
cent level. This test probes potential normalisation but not non-axisymmetric or time-dependent 
perturbations.
The independent integral-of-motion projection uses the specific orbital energy E and the vertical 
angular momentum Lz:
E = ½ |v|² + Φ(r),      Lz = (r × v)z (5)
Because neither E nor L z is used in the GMM, separation in the Lindblad plane is a held-out 
validation result. It does not by itself prove a unique formation channel, but it tests whether the 
chemically defined groups correspond to dynamically distinct families.
3.4. Operational decomposition of the CAP
The CAP is partitioned with literature-motivated boundaries that define, rather than discover, the 
sub-populations. GSE candidates satisfy Lz < 500 kpc km s⁻¹ and eccentricity e > 0.70, motivated by 
the strongly radial debris identified in Gaia-era studies (Belokurov et al., 2018; Helmi et al., 2018; 
Mackereth et al., 2019). Sequoia candidates satisfy Lz < -500 kpc km s⁻¹ and JR > 1,000 kpc km s⁻¹, 
following the retrograde structures discussed by Myeong et al. (2018, 2019) and their globular-cluster 
associations (Massari et al., 2019). The remaining CAP stars are labelled Splash-like, consistent with 
the operational language used for heated in-situ populations (Belokurov et al., 2020; Naidu et al., 
2020). These labels permit reproducible comparison but are not asserted to be unique physical 
memberships.
Fractions are estimated in 1,000 bootstrap iterations, each drawing 80 per cent of the CAP without 
replacement. Statistical uncertainty is combined in quadrature with the shift induced by moving the L z 
boundary by ±100 kpc km s⁻¹. The latter changes the Splash-like fraction by approximately 4-5 
percentage points and dominates the final error budget.
3.5. Geometric weighting and spatial gradients
A 1/Vmax weight based on the available APOGEE and Gaia footprint masks is used only as a 
geometric sensitivity correction. It is not a complete forward model of the combined APOGEE-
GALAH targeting and selection functions, and the distinct GALAH selection is not fully represented. 
APOGEE targeting changes across cohorts and fields (Zasowski et al., 2013, 2017), while Gaia 
completeness and subsample selection depend on observables and sky position (Cantat-Gaudin et al., 
2023; Castro-Ginard et al., 2023). The resulting fractions are therefore described throughout as 
weighted sample fractions rather than intrinsic Galactic mass fractions. Stability is assessed in four 
radial bins: R = 4-7, 7-9, 9-11 and 11-13 kpc.

4. Results
4.1. Chemical decomposition
The four-component model recovers the familiar low-[Mg/Fe] and high-[Mg/Fe] disc sequences 
together with a metal-poor halo and a lower-density CAP between the canonical thick-disc and halo 
loci (Fig. 2). Similar abundance-plane structure has been mapped extensively with APOGEE and 
complementary spectroscopic surveys (Hayden et al., 2015; Anders et al., 2017; Imig et al., 2023). The 
high-[Mg/Fe] component is centred near ([Fe/H], [Mg/Fe]) = (-0.35, +0.30), whereas the low-[Mg/Fe] 
component peaks near (-0.05, +0.05). The CAP is more metal poor, with median [Fe/H] = -0.75 and 
intermediate [Mg/Fe]. A density minimum between the main disc sequences is visible, but chemistry 
alone does not identify its cause; no claim of a unique quenching or two-infall timescale is made. This 
decomposition is consistent with the broad chemo-kinematic structure reported in the preceding 
tomography study (Barua, 2026), while the present analysis applies more conservative interpretation 
limits.

4.3. Lindblad-plane validation
The E–Lz projection provides the strongest orthogonal test of the chemical classification (Fig. 4). 
Thin-disc stars cluster at high prograde angular momentum and high binding energy; thick-disc stars 
occupy a broader intermediate locus. The CAP extends towards low and retrograde Lz, while the halo 
covers the broadest energy range, as expected for phase-mixed accreted and heated populations that 
retain structure in integrals of motion (Helmi and White, 1999; Helmi, 2008). Since these coordinates 
were withheld from the GMM, the separation is not a restatement of the selection function.

4.4. Operational CAP fractions
Under the adopted boundaries, the CAP contains 34 ± 3 per cent GSE candidates, 8 ± 2 per cent 
Sequoia candidates and 58 ± 3 per cent Splash-like stars from bootstrap sampling alone. After 
boundary sensitivity is included, the uncertainties expand to ±5, ±3 and ±5 percentage points, 
respectively (Table 3). The dominant Splash-like fraction is qualitatively consistent with studies 
arguing that a substantial share of the metal-poor eccentric inner-Galaxy population formed in situ and 
was heated (Belokurov et al., 2020; Myeong et al., 2022). The GSE-like and Sequoia-like tails are 
compared with the chemically and dynamically defined debris populations in Helmi et al. (2018), 
Mackereth et al. (2019), Myeong et al. (2019) and Naidu et al. (2020). However, the numerical 
fractions are definitions under the stated cuts, not unique progenitor masses or intrinsic halo fractions.

4.5. Radial variation of the weighted sample
The weighted sample fractions vary strongly with Galactocentric radius (Fig. 5). The thin-disc 
contribution rises from approximately 35 per cent in the 4-7 kpc bin to approximately 80 per cent at 
11-13 kpc, whereas the thick-disc contribution falls from approximately 45 to 15 per cent. The halo 
fraction also declines, from approximately 17 to 3 per cent; the CAP remains a small contribution of a 
few per cent. These trends describe the analysed selection-weighted catalogue. A full inference of 
Galactic density normalisations would require the targeting selection functions and a forward model of 
observability.

fractions without a full survey-selection model.
4.6. Metallicity gradients
The thin disc has a radial metallicity gradient of -0.065 ± 0.004 dex kpc⁻¹, while the thick-disc 
gradient is -0.020 ± 0.006 dex kpc⁻¹ (Fig. 6). The thin-disc value agrees with the -0.06 ± 0.001 dex 
kpc⁻¹ low-alpha gradient measured from the final APOGEE release by Imig et al. (2023) and lies 
within the broader range obtained from APOGEE, Gaia-ESO, red-giant and open-cluster tracers 
(Hayden et al., 2015; Anders et al., 2017; Cunha et al., 2016; Magrini et al., 2023). At R = 7-9 kpc, the 
thin-disc vertical gradient is -0.250 ± 0.020 dex kpc⁻¹. The factor-of-three difference in radial slope 
between the disc components is compatible with a more centrally concentrated and chemically mixed 
high-alpha population, but the gradients alone do not identify a unique assembly history. The 
numerical comparison with independent survey measurements is summarised in Table 4.

5. Robustness, interpretation and limitations
5.1. What the validation establishes
The agreement between the chemical classifier and the held-out dynamical projections establishes 
that the fitted components are not merely numerical partitions of abundance space. It does not, by 
itself, establish a unique progenitor for every star. In particular, chemical evolution can produce 
overlapping abundance tracks, and heated in-situ stars can enter the same low-L z region as accreted 
debris. The result is therefore strongest at the population level: the four chemical classes correspond to 
different orbital families.

5.3. Relation to recent decompositions
Myeong et al. (2022) found four dominant constituents within the local eccentric halo using 
APOGEE DR17 and GALAH DR3: Aurora, Splash, GSE and Eos. The present four-component model 
has a different scope and label set because it includes the dominant thin and thick discs and groups 
residual metal-poor structure into the CAP and halo. The two decompositions should therefore not be 
compared label by label. More generally, studies based on chemistry, actions, globular clusters and 
spatial structure do not always assign identical boundaries or nomenclature to the same overlapping 
debris field (Mackereth et al., 2019; Massari et al., 2019; Naidu et al., 2020; Horta et al., 2021). The 
common message is that the inner halo contains substantial in-situ structure in addition to GSE and 
that any chemically defined component requires independent dynamical and nucleosynthetic cross-
checks. Relative to Barua (2026), the present version also separates descriptive catalogue results from 
claims that require unreleased age products or a complete targeting-selection model.
A particularly important caveat is that a rectangular E–Lz or eccentricity selection is not a clean 
discovery operator. It is best treated as a reproducible comparison definition, because GSE, Sequoia, 
Splash-like and other inner-halo populations overlap in chemistry and orbital space (Helmi et al., 
2018; Myeong et al., 2019; Naidu et al., 2020; Myeong et al., 2022). Future work should add 
abundance combinations such as [Mg/Mn]-[Al/Fe], uncertainty-aware mixture models, probabilistic 
orbit families, and survey-separated posterior predictive checks. These additions would reduce the 
degree to which a small change in a hard boundary propagates into a physical narrative.
5.4. Limitations
Five limitations are decisive. First, the APOGEE and GALAH abundance scales are not perfectly 
homogeneous; the merged analysis requires release of the overlap-star calibration diagnostics. 
Secondly, a Gaussian mixture approximates curved and potentially non-Gaussian abundance 
manifolds and does not deconvolve star-by-star chemical uncertainties; extreme deconvolution or a 
hierarchical errors-in-variables model would be a stronger successor (Bovy et al., 2011). Thirdly, the 
geometric 1/Vmax weight does not substitute for a complete target-selection and observability model 
(Zasowski et al., 2013, 2017; Cantat-Gaudin et al., 2023; Castro-Ginard et al., 2023). Fourthly, the

6. Discussion
The combined chemical and dynamical picture supports a Milky Way in which the present inner 
halo is a mixture of accreted debris and stars formed in the early Galaxy, consistent with modern Gaia-
era reconstructions (Helmi, 2020). The CAP lies chemically between the canonical disc and halo 
sequences and dynamically between the thick disc and the most extreme halo orbits. Its dominant 
Splash-like operational fraction is qualitatively consistent with merger-driven heating of an early in-
situ population (Belokurov et al., 2020), while the GSE-like tail remains substantial and the Sequoia-
like contribution is smaller and more sensitive to the retrograde selection. This interpretation is 
population-level and deliberately does not assign a unique origin to every star. The result therefore 
refines, rather than merely repeats, the chemo-kinematic tomography interpretation advanced by Barua 
(2026).
The radial metallicity results provide an independent disc-scale consistency check. The thin-disc 
gradient closely matches the final-APOGEE low-alpha result of Imig et al. (2023), while the high-
alpha/thick-disc slope is much flatter. Such a contrast is compatible with inside-out growth, a more 
compact early disc and subsequent radial redistribution, as discussed in dynamical and chemo-
evolutionary studies (Sellwood and Binney, 2002; Minchev et al., 2013; Frankel et al., 2020; 
Mackereth and Bovy, 2020). The present analysis does not distinguish uniquely among these 
processes. A forward model connecting chemistry, ages, migration and the survey selection function 
would be required for that stronger inference.
Methodologically, the most transferable result is the held-out validation strategy. It provides a 
simple safeguard against circular discovery: define classes in one information domain and test them in 
another. In Galactic archaeology this can be extended beyond chemistry versus dynamics to include 
ages, nucleosynthetic clocks, asteroseismology and spatial density as separate validation layers. Barua 
(2025) used the term Galactic palaeontology for this broader reconstruction programme; the present 
study supplies one concrete implementation with explicit failure modes.
7. Conclusions
The principal conclusions are:
1. A four-component Gaussian mixture in [Fe/H], [Mg/Fe], [Si/Fe] and [Al/Fe] is strongly preferred 
within the tested model family and assigns 91 per cent of the 468,000-star sample with posterior 
probability above 0.75.
2. Because actions and integrals of motion are not used by the classifier, their separation provides an 
independent dynamical validation of the chemical decomposition.
3. The action-space dispersion increases by approximately a factor of 29 from the thin disc to the 
halo; this statistic measures orbital spread and is not an age proxy.

4. Under explicit operational boundaries, the CAP is partitioned into 34 ± 5 per cent GSE, 8 ± 3 per

Appendix A. Model-order diagnostic
Fig. A.1 shows the BIC values for k = 2,...,8. The global minimum occurs at k = 4. Models with 
fewer components merge the CAP into the broader halo or thick-disc structure, while higher-order 
models split one of the dominant disc sequences without a clear independent physical interpretation. 
The diagnostic supports four components within the specified feature space and covariance family; it 
does not imply that the Galaxy contains only four physically meaningful populations.
