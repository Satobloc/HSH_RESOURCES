1.4CorePropositionandNaming
This paper proposes that stars possess a universal "regime boundary" at temperatures of
approximately 4400–5800 K (the K/G transition zone) and a surface gravity of approximately log g =
4.64.This boundary corresponds to the complete physical process of the stellarouter convection zone
from its emergence to its full dominance.Among these, 4762 K is the precise spectral type boundary
betweenKandGtypesandconstitutesthemostrepresentativecriticalpoint.
We designate this boundary the "Stellar Main-Sequence Regime Boundary," abbreviated as
theTangBreak.
In the classical literature of stellar physics, Kraft (1967) discovered the sharp transition in rotation
velocity at approximately 6250 K (the Kraft Break), whose physical origin is the "emergence" of the
convection zone. The Tang Break is structurally isomorphic with this, but captures the five-dimensional
effects of the complete evolutionary process from "emergence" to "full dominance," far exceeding the
single-dimensional discovery of the Kraft Break. The Kraft Break corresponds to the threshold of the
convection zone "just emerging" (extremely thin convection zone, magnetic braking beginning to
strengthen), while the Tang Break corresponds to the complete process of the convection zone "from
emergence to full dominance"—the two are complementary, together depicting the complete physical
pictureofthestellarouterconvectionzone.

2.2UnifiedApplicationoftheTestabilityNormsAcrossAllDimensions
All five dimensions strictly follow the Testability Norms proposed by Tang (2026g), covering all six
functionaldimensions:
First Core Dimension: Search Completeness. Traverse all combinations of candidate executing
factors, rank by adjusted R² under the hard constraint of VIF < 5. The optimal combination wins
automatically,withoutrelyingonsubjectiveresearcherselection.Thecurrenttoolisexhaustivetesting.
Second Core Dimension: Modulation Relationship (Core Evidence). Test whether the candidate
regime factor modulates the exposure coefficient of the executing factors on the dependent variable. A
significant modulation relationship is a necessary condition for identifying a regime factor.The current tool
isinteractioneffecttesting.

grouping variable, test whether the entire equation exhibits a structural break between two sub-samples.

tools are the Chow test and an alternative tool suite (threshold regression, SupF test, Bai-Perron sequential
test,CUSUMtest,MOSUMtest).
