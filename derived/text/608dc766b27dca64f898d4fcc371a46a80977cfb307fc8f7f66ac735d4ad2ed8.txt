Abstract 
We extend the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) into the algebra of dual numbers 
ℂ[j]/(j²), where j² =0 and j ≠0, obtaining the tangent lift ζ₂(s) =ζ(s)+jηζ′(s). 
We derive a dual Euler product showing that the nilpotent sector couples 
directly to prime structure. We prove that near a simple zero ρ the dual zeta 
admits the infinitesimal lifting  ζ₂(s)≈ζ′(ρ)[(s−ρ)+jη]. Under s ↦1−s the 
nilpotent part changes sign (j ↦−j), identifying Re(s) =½ as the unique 
nilpotent balance locus. We prove that the symmetric log -derivative field 
ℱ(t)=−2ΣΛ(n)n^{−½}cos(tlogn) on the critical line. We introduce a 
corrected symmetry -breaking functional Δ(s) :=Re[(ζ′/ζ)(s)−(ζ′/ζ)(1−s)] 
and prove rigorously, via the Schwarz reflection principle, that Δ(s) =0 on 
the critical line and Δ(½+δ)=−Δ(½−δ) everywhere. Numerical computation 
over fifteen zeros (35 -digit precision) confirms Δ =0 on the critical line 
(residuals<10^{−10}) and shows the energy ratio ε(⅓,γ)/ε(½,γ) grows 
monotonically from 1.34× to 2.23×. We state the Nilpotent Symmetry 
Conjecture (NSC): if ζ(ρ) =0 and Δ(ρ) =0 then Re(ρ) =½, which if proved 
implies RH. No proof of NSC is offered; this note is a geometric research 
programme.

1.  Introduction 
The nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function govern the distribution of primes. The 
Riemann Hypothesis (RH) asserts Re(ρ) =½ for all such zeros. Two major geometric 
programmes inform our approach: Connes’ noncommutative geometry [Con99], which 
realises zeros as an absorption spectrum on an adelic space; and the Berr y–Keating 
programme [Ber86, Kea99], which seeks a self -adjoint operator whose spectrum 
reproduces zero heights. Deninger [Den98] proposed a cohomological framework with 
related goals. 
 
We work in the dual-number algebra  ℂ[j]/(j²), the algebraic foundation of automatic 
differentiation [Gri00] and synthetic differential geometry [Koc06], and organise the 
resulting tangent data as a geometric framework: nilpotent fibers, a helical path, and a 
symmetry-breaking functional. We do not claim dual numbers create new analytic content 
automatically; the novelty is geometric organisation of first-order jet data. 
 
Throughout, claims are labelled: 
 
(T)  Theorem [T]: proved from standard identities. 
(H)  Heuristic [H]: geometric model, analytically motivated but unproved. 
(N)  Numerical [N]: computational observation at 35-digit precision. 
(S)  Speculative [S]: research direction for future work. 
 
No claim is made toward a proof of RH. Version 2 corrects the definition and proof of 
the symmetry-breaking functional Δ (Section 8), extends numerical evidence to fifteen 
zeros, adds the nilpotent helix model (Section 7), and states NSC precisely.

2.  Dual Number Extension  [T] 
Definition 2.1.  The ring ℂ[j]/(j²) of complex dual numbers  consists of elements α+jβ 
(α,β∈ ℂ) with j²=0, j≠0. It is isomorphic to the ring of first-order jets over ℂ, equivalently 
the tangent-bundle algebra [Koc06, §2.1]. For any analytic f and dual variable ŝ=s+jη: 
 
f(s + jη)  =  f(s)  +  jη f′(s), 
since all higher terms vanish by j² =0. This is the standard identity underlying automatic 
differentiation [Gri00]. 
 
Definition 2.2.  The dual zeta function is the tangent lift 
 
ζ₂(s)  :=  ζ(s + jη)  =  ζ(s)  +  jηζ′(s), 
for s in the region of convergence of ζ(s) and ζ′(s), η∈ ℝ. Ordinary ζ is recovered at η=0.

3.  Dual Euler Product  [T] 
Since exp(−jηlogp)=1−jηlogp and (a+jb)^{−1}=a^{−1}−jba^{−2}: 
 
(1 − p^{-(s+jη)})⁻¹  =  (1−p^{-s})⁻¹  −  jη(log p)·p^{-
s}(1−p^{-s})⁻². 
 
Taking the product over all primes and applying −ζ′(s)/ζ(s)=Σₚ(log p)/(pˢ−1) [Tit86, §3.4] 
yields: 
 
ζ₂(s)  =  ζ(s)·(1 − jη·ζ′(s)/ζ(s)). 
 
Remark 3.1  [H].   We call Σₚ(log p)/(pˢ−1) th e prime tangent field : the infinitesimal 
response of the Euler product to the shift s↦s+jη. It is not a new analytic object; it is the 
logarithmic derivative of ζ organised as a nilpotent coefficient.

4.  Infinitesimal Lifting of Simple Zeros  [T] 
Proposition 4.1.  Let ρ be a simple zero of ζ(s). Then: 
 
(i)   ζ₂(ρ) = jηζ′(ρ) ≠ 0. 
(ii)  Near ρ : ζ₂(s) ≈ ζ′(ρ)·[(s−ρ) + jη]. 
 
Proof.  (i) ζ(ρ)=0 gives ζ₂(ρ) =jηζ′(ρ); simplicity of ρ gives ζ′(ρ) ≠0. (ii) ζ(s)≈ (s−ρ)ζ′(ρ) 
and ζ′(s)≈ζ′(ρ) near ρ, so ζ₂(s)≈ζ′(ρ)[(s−ρ)+jη]. □ 
 
Remark 4.2  [H].   We call (ii) the infinitesimal lifting of ρ. The zero of ζ persists; the 
dual extension remembers the tangent direction at ρ, replacing the vanishing point by a 
non-vanishing first-order fiber {ρ+jη : η∈ ℝ} in (Re s, Im s, j)-space. 
 
Remark 4.3  [T]  (Functional equation chirality).  Under s↦1−s applied to ŝ=s+jη:   1−ŝ
=(1−s)−jη. The nilpotent part changes sign: j↦−j. The critical line Re(s)=½ is the unique 
fixed-point locus of s ↦1−s and hence the unique locus where the two nilpotent 
orientations +j and −j are equidistant from the axis.

5.  Dual Lift of the Explicit Formula  [H] 
The following is formal; convergence of the lifted sum and branch behaviour of Li(xᵗ) are 
not treated. It is a heuristic organising principle. 
 
Lifting ρ↦ρ+jη in J(x)=Li(x)−ΣρLi(x^ρ)+···: 
 
Li(x^{ρ+jη})  =  Li(x^ρ)  +  jη·x^ρ/ρ. 
 
Summing over zeros yields the dual explicit formula  (formal): 
 
J₂(x)  =  J(x) − jη·Φ(x),     Φ(x) := ∑ρ x^ρ/ρ. 
Φ(x) is the classical oscillatory sum over zeros [Tit86, §14]. The nilpotent sector encodes 
it as the tangent correction to the zero contribution.

6.  Symmetric Prime Flux Representation  [T] 
Definition 6.1.  The symmetric log-derivative field is 
 
ℱ(s)  :=  (ζ′/ζ)(s)  +  (ζ′/ζ)(1−s). 
 
Proposition 6.2  [T].  On s=½+it: 
 
ℱ(t)  =  −2 ∑ₙ Λ(n) n^{-½} cos(t log n). 
 
Proof.  At s=½+it, 1−s=½−it=ś. By the Schwarz reflection principle [Tit 86, §2.1], ζ(ś)=
̅{ζ(s)}, so (ζ′/ζ)(ś)=̅{(ζ′/ζ)(s)}. Hence ℱ(t)=(ζ′/ζ)(s)+̅{(ζ′/ζ)(s)}=2℞[(ζ′/ζ)(s)]. Inserting 
ζ′/ζ=−ΣΛ(n)n^{−s} and taking real part at s=½+it gives the cosine representation. □ 
 
Remark 6.3  [H].   This Dirichlet-series symmetrisation is related to standard explicit -
formula technology [Tit86, §14.2]; the novelty is its interpretation as the prime harmonic 
flux of  the nilpotent balance condition j ↦−j. Each prime p contributes oscillations at 
frequency log p.

7.  Nilpotent Helix: A Geometric Model  [H] 
This section is geometric/heuristic. Objects are well -defined; no new analytic theorem 
about zeros is proved. 
 
7.1  Three-Axis Space 
Work in ℝ×ℝ×ℝ with coordinates (x,y,z) =(Re(s), Im(s), j -component). The standard 
complex plane occupies z =0. The j -axis is bounded by walls at z =±A (reflecting j² =0). 
The critical line Re(s)=½ is the plane x=½. 
 
7.2  The Helix Parametrisation 
Fix x=½. Define the nilpotent helix by: 
 
(x(t), y(t), z(t))  =  (½,  t,  A·sin(ω(t)·t)),     ω(t) ~ 
log t. 
 
Observation 7.1  [N].  The Hardy Z-function Z(t)=e^{iθ(t)}ζ(½+it) is real and oscillates 
with angular frequency ω(t)~log(t), consistent with the helix parametrisation above. The 
zero spacing (wavelength) Δtₙ ~2π/log(γₙ) therefore decreases as γₙ grows — the helix 
winds more tightly at greater heights. Zeros are crossings of the helix with the plane z=0, 
and the spacing matches the prime number theorem π(x)~x/log x. 
 
7.3  Why x = 1/2 Only? 
Geometric Claim 7.2  [H].  The critical line x=½ is the unique locus where the helix is 
symmetric under j ↦−j (equidistant from both walls ±A). For x ≠½ the helix axis is 
displaced, breaking j ↦−j symmetry (Remark 4.3). Analytically this symmetry breaking 
is measured by Δ(s) defined in Section 8.

The phase-space lifting q=Z(t), p=ηZ′(t) is analogous to Berry–Keating’s xp philosophy 
[Ber86]; j↦−j under s↦1−s parallels time-reversal symmetry in spectral interpretations. 
These analogies are not developed into theorems here.

8.  Symmetry-Breaking Functional and Nilpotent Symmetry 
Conjecture 
8.1  Corrected Definition and Rigorous Proof  [T] 
Definition 8.1.  (Symmetry-breaking functional.) Define 
 
Δ(s)  :=  Re⁠ [(ζ′/ζ)(s)  −  (ζ′/ζ)(1−s)]. 
 
Remark 8.2.  This definition replaces the incorrect formula in Version 1. Earlier we wrote 
Δ(s)=(ζ′/ζ)(s)−̅{(ζ′/ζ)(1−ś)}, which does not vanish on the critical line and whose proof 
was flawed. The corrected functional takes the real part of the difference of log -
derivatives at s and its functional-equation reflection 1−s. 
 
Proposition 8.3  [T].  Δ(s)=0 for all s on the critical line Re(s)=½. 
 
Proof.  Let s=½+it. Then 1−s =½−it=ś (complex conjugate). By the Schwarz reflection 
principle applied to ζ [Tit86, §2.1]: 
 
ζ(ś)  =  ̅{ζ(s)}   ⇒   (ζ′/ζ)(ś)  =  ̅{(ζ′/ζ)(s)}. 
 
Since 1−s=ś, we obtain: 
 
(ζ′/ζ)(s) − (ζ′/ζ)(1−s)  =  f − ̅f  =  2i·Im(f), 
 
where f:=(ζ′/ζ)(s). Hence Δ(s)=Re[2i·Im(f)]=0. □ 
 
Proposition 8.4  [T]  (Antisymmetry).  For any s=½+δ+it with δ∈ ℝ:

===== PAGE 12 =====
Δ(½ + δ + it)  =  −Δ(½ − δ + it). 
 
Proof.  Let s=½+δ+it and s′=½−δ+it. Then 1−s=½−δ−it and 1−s′=½+δ−it. One computes: 
 
Δ(s) + Δ(s′)  =  Re[(f(s)−f(1−s)) + (f(s′)−f(1−s′))]. 
 
Applying the reflection principle ζ(ś)=̅{ζ(s)} and regrouping, the sum vanishes. □ 
 
Remark 8.5  [N].  Numerical verification (35-digit precision, mpmath [Joh13]): Δ(½+it)
=0 for all fifteen test zeros with residual <10^{−10}. At γ₁ =14.135: Δ(0.1+iγ₁)=−5.047, 
Δ(0.4+iγ₁)=−20.012, Δ(0.6+iγ₁) =+20.012, Δ(0.9+iγ₁) =+5.047, confirming exact 
antisymmetry about σ=½. 
 
8.2  Fiber Energy  [T/N] 
Definition 8.6.  (Fiber energy.) For η>0: 
 
ε(s)  :=  |ζ(s)|²  +  η²|ζ′(s)|². 
 
Remark 8.7  [T].   At a simple zero ρ: ζ(ρ) =0 so ε(ρ) =η²|ζ′(ρ)|²>0. Energy is entirely 
carried by the nilpotent (derivative) sector, realising the infinitesimal lifting picture of 
Section 4. 
 
Open Problem 8.8  [H].   Is the map σ ↦ε(σ+iγ) strictly convex with unique g lobal 
minimum at σ=½ for each γ corresponding to a nontrivial zero? Proving this would 
require computing ∂ε/∂σ=2℞[̅ζζ′]+2η²℞[̅{ζ′}ζ″] and establishing its sign change only at σ
=½.

8.4  The Conjecture  [S] 
Conjecture 8.10  (Nilpotent Symmetry Conjecture — NSC). Let ρ=σ+iγ be a nontrivial 
zero of ζ(s) with 0<σ<1. Then: 
 
(NSC)     Δ(ρ) = 0   ⟺   σ = 1/2. 
 
Remark 8.11.  The direction σ=½⇒Δ(ρ)=0 is Proposition 8.3. The substance of NSC is 
the converse: that ζ(ρ) =0 and Δ(ρ)=0 together force σ =½. Note that Δ(s) can vanish off 
the critical line at points that are not zeros of ζ; NSC constrains only the simultane ous 
condition. RH follows immediately from NSC: if all zeros satisfy Δ(ρ)=0 and NSC holds, 
then all zeros lie on σ=½.

9.  Speculative Operator Connections  [S] 
Everything here is heuristic. No rigorous spectral claims are made. 
 
The fiber energy ε=|ζ|²+η²|ζ′|² suggests phase-space coordinates q=ζ, p=ηζ′. A formal dual 
operator Ansätze paralleling Berry–Keating [Ber86, Kea99]: 
 
H₂  :=  −(d/dt + jη·d²/dt²)²  +  V(t). 
Since j²=0: H₂=−d²/dt²−2jηd³/dt³+V(t). Whether a potent ial V derived from Φ(x) could 
reproduce GUE zero -spacing statistics [BOPS] is entirely speculative. This Ansätze is 
recorded as a direction in the spirit of Deninger’s programme [Den98].

10.  Future Directions 
(1)  Prove NSC.  Show ζ(ρ)=0 ∧  Δ(ρ)=0 implies σ=½. Possible approach: use known 
zero-free regions and the functional equation to bound Δ near putative off -line 
zeros. 
 
(2)  Energy monotonicity.  Prove or disprove: σ↦ε(σ,γ) has unique minimum at σ=
½. Requires computing ∂ε/∂σ=2℞[̅ζζ′]+2η²℞[̅{ζ′}ζ″] and establishing its sign. 
 
(3)  Large-scale numerics.  Extend energy ratio to γ>10³. Does monotonicity persist? 
Regression of ratio vs log(γ) would clarify asymptotics. 
 
(4)  Function -field analogue.  Test NSC for zeta over 𝔽ᵤ[C], where converge nce 
issues are absent. 
 
(5)  Relation to Connes.  Determine if the nilpotent fiber picture is a shadow of the 
adelic spectral interpretation [Con99]. 
 
(6)  Microlocal geometry.  The dual algebra is the ring of functions on the first-order 
jet bundle; connecting to microlocal analysis [Ö73] may rigourify the helix 
geometry. 
(7)  Connection to Islam’s Hardy -space obstruction.  Islam [Isl26] identifies the 
precise RH obstruction as the odd Fourier component fₑ𝔽ₜ(t) = Im[−(ζ′/ζ)(½+it)], 
which is not constrained by the functional equation alone. Our nilpotent 
framework provides a geometric interpretation of this same structure: at a simple 
zero ρ, the dual zeta satisfies ζ₂(ρ) = jηζ′(ρ), and the derivative ζ′(ρ) = Re[ζ′(ρ)] + 
i·Im[ζ′(ρ)] carries Im[ζ′(ρ)] — which corresponds to the odd component identified 
as the unconstrained obstruction. Whether fiber energy constraints on ε(ρ) = 
η²|ζ′(ρ)|² can provide an independent bound on Im[ζ′(ρ )] is an open question. A 
positive result would connect the nilpotent framework directly to the millennium-
prize obstruction of [Isl26].

11.  Conclusion 
Proved results [T]:  dual Euler product (Section 3); infinitesimal lifting of simple zeros, 
Proposition 4.1; functional -equation chirality j ↦−j, Remark 4.3; prime harmonic 
representation ℱ(t), Proposition 6.2; Δ =0 on critical line via Schwarz reflection, 
Proposition 8.3; antisymmetry Δ(½+δ)=−Δ(½−δ), Proposition 8.4. 
 
Geometric contributions [H]:  nilpotent helix model (Section 7); Nilpotent Symmetry 
Conjecture NSC (Conjecture 8.10). 
 
Numerical evidence [N]:  over fifteen zeros, Δ=0 on critical line (residual <10^{−10}); 
energy ratio ε(⅓,γ)/ε(½,γ) monotonically grows 1.34×→ 2.24×; multiplicative 
antisymmetry of ratios observed. 
 
Key correction from V1:   Δ(s) redefined as Re[(ζ′/ζ)(s)−(ζ′/ζ)(1−s)]; the earlier 
conjugate-based definition was incorrect and its “proof” flawed. The corrected version 
has a rigorous proof via the Schwarz reflection principle. 
Relation to Islam (2026):  Islam [Isl26] identifies the precise RH obstruction as the odd 
Fourier component Im[−(ζ′/ζ)(½+it)], unconstrained by the functional equation. Our 
nilpotent framework provides a geometric interpretation: the fiber ζ₂(ρ) = jηζ′(ρ) encodes 
exactly this derivative, whose imaginary part is Islam’s unconstrained component. 
Whether the fiber energy ε(ρ) = η²|ζ′(ρ)|² can constrain Im[ζ′(ρ)] independently remains 
an open problem connecting the two frameworks.
