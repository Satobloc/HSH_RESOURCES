About this model
This lab runs a Monte-Carlo CHSH Bell test on the singlet state | Ψ ⁻ ⟩ . Four measurement settings (a, a′, b, b′) define
correlators that combine into the CHSH parameter Ŝ . At the canonical Bell angles (0°, 90°, 45°, −45°) the running
estimate approaches the Tsirelson bound 2 √ 2 ≈  2.828, violating the local-realist limit |S| ≤  2. Each trial samples
projective outcomes ±1 according to quantum probabilities for the singlet. The simulator assumes ideal projective
measurements, perfect entanglement, and no detection loopholes or communication between parties. You choose
angles and accumulate shots to watch Ŝ  converge — entanglement made statistically visible against the classical
bound.
Who it's for:Quantum information and foundations courses introducing Bell inequalities, entanglement, and local
realism.

Any local-hidden-variable model that assigns predetermined outcomes independent of the distant setting cannot exceed |S| = 2. Quantum mechanics predicts up to 2 √ 2 for the singlet at
optimal angles. Observing Ŝ  > 2 (with enough statistics) rules out that classical picture for the prepared state — it does not by itself close experimental loopholes.
Why is 2 √ 2 the maximum, not 4?
Algebraically each correlator is at most 1, so |S| ≤  4 looks possible, but quantum measurements on two qubits cannot saturate that. Tsirelson’s bound 2 √ 2 is the tight quantum maximum
for CHSH; reaching 4 would require stronger-than-quantum correlations.
Do I need exactly the angles 0°, 90°, 45°, −45°?
Those angles maximize the singlet violation. Other choices can still violate |S| ≤  2, but with a smaller margin; some angles give S compatible with local realism even for an entangled state.
The lab lets you mis-set angles to see the violation shrink or disappear.
