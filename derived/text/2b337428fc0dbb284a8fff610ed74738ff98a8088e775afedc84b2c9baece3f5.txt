Abstract
The Copenhagen and Everett interpretations of quantum mechanics have been treated as  
competitors for nearly seven decades. Copenhagen posits wavefunction collapse producing one  
definite outcome. Everett posits branching producing all outcomes. The Born rule — the  
probability measure P(a) = | a|ψ |²⟨ ⟩  — is the wedge that keeps them apart: Copenhagen  
treats it as an unexplained postulate; Everett derives it from rationality axioms that remain  
contested.
This paper proposes that the wedge can be removed by a universal conservation law. The  
Information Conservation Principle (ICP) — which formalizes information conservation as a first-
class physical principle — constrains both interpretations to assign the same unique probability  
measure. The conserved quantity is identified as the squared-amplitude measure of the universal  
state, interpreted as information capacity (distinguishable state-space volume). By Busch's  
extension of Gleason's theorem, the Born rule is the unique probability measure on quantum state 
space consistent with this conserved measure.
Three theorems close the derivation: (1) the C*-algebra structure is derived from operational 
postulates via the ICP rather than assumed; (2) the 2-norm is shown to be the unique unitarily-
invariant, no-signaling-compatible conserved scalar on Hilbert space; and (3) the capacity  
interpretation is grounded in a uniqueness result showing that the 2-norm is the only permutation-
invariant, subdivision-additive, normalized functional of the branch distribution.
The result is a bridge rather than a competitor: Copenhagen preserves one outcome per branch  
locally, requiring no modification. Everett preserves the branch ensemble globally, requiring no  
modification. The ICP constrains them both, ensuring that the Born rule probabilities are  
physically necessary rather than interpretation-dependent. Probability-Weighted Branch Realism 
(PWBR) provides the physical interpretation: branches are real but probability-weighted, with the 
Born rule measure grounded in information conservation rather than postulated.

Axiom 4 (Continuous Reversibility). Between any two pure states there exists a continuous

These axioms are operationally testable and make no reference to Hilbert space or the Born rule.

3. The 2-Norm as Information Capacity
With the Hilbert space structure derived in Section 2, the 2-norm emerges as the unique conserved 
scalar. This section develops its interpretation as information capacity, connecting it to established 
information-theoretic quantities.
3.1 Related Work
The use of the squared-amplitude measure as a branch weight has extensive precedent. Everett  
(1957) defined the branch measure as the squared norm of the relative state. Brukner & Zeilinger 
(1999) identified the 2-norm Σ|aᵢ|² as an operationally invariant informational quantity. Deutsch  
(1999) and Wallace (2012) used squared-amplitude weights in decision-theoretic derivations.  
Carroll & Sebens (2014) developed self-locating uncertainty with squared-amplitude measure.
The present framework differs in three respects. First, the 2-norm is not postulated as conserved — 
it is derived as the unique unitarily-invariant scalar on the Hilbert space that emerges from  
operational axioms. Second, the capacity/content distinction clarifies that the 2-norm is conserved 
because it quantifies state-space volume (capacity), not observer knowledge (content). Third, the 
non-negativity and normalization conditions for Gleason's theorem are satisfied by the derived 2-
norm, not externally imposed.
3.2 Definition
Following Everett (1957), let the universal quantum state be expressed as |Ψ  = Σᵢ aᵢ|i⟩ ⟩ . 
Define the total informational capacity: C_total(Ψ) = Σᵢ |aᵢ|² , identically 1 for any  
normalized state. The capacity allocated to branch Bᵢ is Cᵢ = |aᵢ|², representing the fraction of 
the total informational capacity carried by that branch.
3.3 Conservation
Under any unitary transformation U, |Ψ'  = U|Ψ⟩ ⟩ , the total capacity is invariant: C_total(Ψ') 
= Σᵢ | i|U|Ψ |² = Ψ|U†U|Ψ  = Ψ|Ψ  = C_total(Ψ)⟨ ⟩ ⟨ ⟩ ⟨ ⟩ . No physical process — 
unitary evolution, decoherence, branching, or measurement — changes C_total. The capacity is  
only redistributed across degrees of freedom.
3.4 Operational Properties
The capacity measure satisfies three properties that jointly justify its interpretation as an  
informational resource:
• Subspace additivity. For any orthogonal decomposition H = H₁  H₂, the capacity ⊕
satisfies C(P₁Ψ) + C(P₂Ψ) = C(Ψ). This is the frame-function condition.
• Measurement independence. For any orthonormal basis {|eᵢ}, the capacity partitions as ⟩
Σᵢ |eᵢ|Ψ|² = 1. The total capacity is independent of basis.⟨ ⟩
• Asymptotic frequency correspondence. For an ensemble of N → ∞ identically prepared 
systems, the squared amplitude |aᵢ|² equals the limiting frequency of outcomes associated 
with branch Bᵢ.

3.5 Capacity vs. Content
Traditional information measures quantify content: Shannon entropy measures uncertainty; von  
Neumann entropy measures mixedness. Both change under measurement because the observer's  
knowledge changes. A conserved quantity cannot be a measure of content — content is what gets 
created and destroyed as observers learn and forget.
Capacity, by contrast, quantifies what the physical state can support, regardless of any observer's 
knowledge. The Born rule connects the two: an observer's probability for an outcome equals the  
capacity allocated to the branches yielding that outcome. Capacity is the conserved substrate;  
probability is the local experience of that substrate by an embedded observer.

• Systems: physical entities that can be prepared, transformed, and measured.
• Tests: collections of outcomes {i} with associated probabilities p(i|ω, T).

No Hilbert space, no C*-algebra, no inner product is assumed.

Theorem 2: The p-Norm No-Go Theorem (Uniqueness of the 2-Norm)
The Problem
Given a Hilbert space, many p-norms Σ|aᵢ|^p are mathematically definable. Why is the 2-norm the 
physically relevant conserved scalar? Without ruling out alternatives, the Born rule derivation is  
not forced.
The Fix
On a complex Hilbert space, any candidate conserved scalar C_p must satisfy: F1 (Frame 
function: ΣC_p(eᵢ) = 1 for any orthonormal basis), F2 (Unitary invariance: C_p(UΨ) = C_p(Ψ)), 
F3 (No-signaling compatibility: marginal states obtained via partial trace are independent of 
remote measurements).
Theorem 2 (Uniqueness of the 2-norm as informational measure)
Let H be a complex Hilbert space of dimension d ≥ 3. Suppose a physical theory assigns to 
each normalized state |Ψ = Σᵢ aᵢ|i a conserved capacity measure of the form C_p(Ψ) = (Σᵢ |⟩ ⟩
aᵢ|^p)^(1/p) for some p > 0. Then C_p satisfies all three of the following conditions 
simultaneously if and only if p = 2:
1. Frame-function property (F1): for every orthonormal basis {eᵢ}, Σᵢ C_p(P_{eᵢ}Ψ) = 
C_p(Ψ);
2. Basis independence (F2): C_p(Ψ) is invariant under all unitary transformations U;
3. No-signaling (F3): the marginal capacity assigned to subsystem A of a bipartite state |
Ψ_AB is independent of any measurement performed on B.⟩
Proof
F2 requires p = 2 directly. The unit sphere in H is homogeneous under U(d), so C_p must be a  
function of ‖Ψ‖ alone. For normalized states, ‖Ψ‖₂ = 1 always, but ‖Ψ‖_p for p ≠ 2 varies with basis 
components and is not unitarily invariant. Concretely: |Ψ = (1/√2)|0 + (1/√2)|1 and |Φ = |0⟩ ⟩ ⟩ ⟩ ⟩  
have ‖Ψ‖₄⁴ = 1/2 ≠ 1 = ‖Φ‖₄⁴, but a Hadamard maps |0 to |Ψ. The only unitarily-invariant p-norm⟩ ⟩  
on normalized vectors is p = 2.
F3 eliminates remaining candidates. No-signaling requires that the reduced state on A, obtained by 
partial trace, depends only on the global state. The partial trace uses the Hilbert-Schmidt inner  
product, which is sesquilinear — uniquely picking p = 2 among p-norms. Linearity of the trace  
requires this.
F1 follows from p = 2. The frame-function property Σ|eᵢ|Ψ|² = ‖Ψ‖² = 1 is the Parseval identity. For⟨ ⟩  
p ≠ 2, Σ|eᵢ|Ψ|^p is basis-dependent.⟨ ⟩
Corollary. The Born rule is the unique probability measure consistent with any conserved scalar on 
complex Hilbert space that is unitarily invariant and no-signaling-compatible. The 2-norm is not  
one choice among many — it is forced by the Hilbert space structure derived from operational  
axioms.

measurements is the Born rule. Busch (2003) extends this to POVMs in all dimensions including 
dimension 2.

8. Honest Limitations
8. Purification and ideal compression. The CDP reconstruction relies on operational axioms 
that are independently motivated but not themselves derived from a deeper principle. 
Purification (every mixed state has a pure-state extension) and ideal compression 
(redundant degrees of freedom can be removed reversibly) have direct experimental 
interpretations (no-hiding theorem, Schumacher compression), but closing this gap would 
require showing they follow from something more fundamental. This remains open work.
9. Quantum structure required. The argument applies to quantum measurements described 
by POVMs on the Hilbert space derived from the axioms. It does not derive probability 
measures for hypothetical post-quantum theories that violate the operational primitives 
(e.g., theories without purification or tomographic locality).
10.Dimension 1. In trivial one-dimensional Hilbert spaces, the normalized state is unique 
and all outcomes are deterministic. The derivation holds trivially but adds no physical 
content.
11.The quasi-probability objection. Wigner functions and Kirkwood-Dirac distributions are 
not frame functions over orthonormal bases and are not counterexamples to uniqueness. 
Theorem 2 reinforces this: quasi-probabilities violate no-signaling (F3) when treated as 
physical measures. The deeper question of why Hilbert space itself — and not a more 
general structure — is the arena of physics is addressed by the CDP axioms but not fully 
resolved at the level of physical first principles.
12.The bridge unifies rather than chooses. This paper does not argue that Copenhagen is 
correct or that Everett is correct. It argues that both are interpretations of a formalism in 
which the Born rule is a theorem of operational axioms. The choice between them 
remains a matter of which level of description is more useful for a given problem.
