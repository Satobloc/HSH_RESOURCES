Abstract 
The incompatibility between General Relativity (GR) and Quantum Mechanics (QM) is 
commonly regarded as one of the central problems in theoretical physics. Conventional 
approaches typically seek a unified framework capable of describing both theories within a 
common mathematical structure. In this work, an alternative possibility is explored: that the 
apparent conflict between GR and QM arises because both theories are interior descriptions of an 
emergent spacetime domain and therefore encounter complementary limits of applicability at a 
common underlying boundary. 
The proposed framework begins with a simple premise: spacetime remains physically 
meaningful only when both spatial extension and temporal progression are simultaneously  
defined. From this perspective, the loss of either component is sufficient to undermine the 
conditions required for a spacetime-based description. General Relativity encounters such a limit 
at the contraction boundary, where spatial extension approaches a limiting condition and 
geometric structure becomes undefined. Quantum Mechanics encounters a corresponding limit at 
the propagation boundary associated with the speed of light, where proper time approaches zero 
and the temporal structure required for evolution, measurement, and state definition becomes 
unavailable. 
Although these limits appear different within conventional formulations, they are shown to 
exhibit a common structural pattern. In both cases, a defining component of spacetime becomes 
unavailable, resulting in the breakdown of the framework upon which the respective theory 
depends. From this viewpoint, singularities, divergences, vanishing proper time, and 
measurement-related limitations may be interpreted not as independent pathologies, but as 
indicators that the spacetime description itself has approached its domain of validity. 
This interpretation suggests that the longstanding tension between GR and QM may not arise 
from incompatibility within spacetime, but from the fact that both theories are anchored to 
opposite approaches toward the same underlying boundary of emergent spacetime. The 
framework developed here provides a unified conceptual interpretation of these complementary 
limits and proposes a new perspective on the relationship between gravitation, quantum theory, 
and the emergence of spacetime itself.

1. Introduction: The GR/QM Boundary Problem 
General Relativity (GR) and Quantum Mechanics (QM) are among the most successful theories 
in the history of physics. General Relativity provides an accurate description of gravitation, 
spacetime geometry, black holes, and cosmological evolution, while Quantum Mechanics 
successfully describes atomic, subatomic, and field-theoretic phenomena across an enormous 
range of scales [1–18]. Despite their empirical success, the relationship between the two theories 
remains unresolved. 
Traditionally, the incompatibility between GR and QM has been attributed to differences in their 
mathematical structure. General Relativity is formulated as a classical geometric theory of 
spacetime, whereas Quantum Mechanics is formulated through probabilistic state evolution and 
operator-based dynamics. Numerous approaches have therefore sought a deeper framework 
capable of reconciling these descriptions, including quantum gravity, string theory, loop quantum 
gravity, holographic dualities, and emergent spacetime models [19–30]. 
A common feature of these approaches is the assumption that the primary problem lies in the 
relationship between the theories themselves. The present work explores an alternative 
possibility. Rather than asking why General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics fail to agree, we 
ask whether the apparent conflict may arise because both theories depend upon a deeper structure 
whose limits have not been fully recognized. 
The starting point is a simple proposition: spacetime may not be fundamental. If spacetime is 
instead an emergent structure, then every theory formulated within spacetime necessarily 
possesses a limited domain of applicability. The breakdown of a theory near a boundary 
condition would therefore not necessarily imply an error in the theory itself. It may instead 
indicate that the underlying spacetime framework required by the theory is no longer available. 
Within this perspective, the limits encountered by General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics 
take on a different significance. General Relativity encounters well-known difficulties near 
singularities, where spatial extension, curvature, causal structure, and geometric description 
become increasingly problematic [4–9]. Quantum Mechanics encounters corresponding 
conceptual difficulties near the null limit associated with propagation at the speed of light, where 
proper time approaches zero and the temporal structure required for evolution, measurement, and 
state definition becomes increasingly difficult to maintain [10–18]. 
The central hypothesis of this paper is that these limitations are not independent phenomena. 
Rather, they represent complementary approaches to a common boundary condition of emergent 
spacetime. General Relativity approaches this boundary through the progressive loss of spatial

structure. Although these processes appear different within conventional formulations, both 
ultimately involve the loss of a defining component required for a spacetime description. 
This interpretation leads to a reformulation of the GR/QM problem. Instead of viewing the two 
theories as fundamentally incompatible descriptions of reality, they may be understood as 
complementary interior descriptions of a bounded spacetime domain. The apparent conflict 
arises not because either theory is incorrect within its domain of validity, but because both

mathematical formalism, but at the level of the conditions required for each theory to remain

Quantum Mechanics is fundamentally a theory of states, evolution, and measurement. Whether 
expressed through wavefunctions, operators, path integrals, or quantum fields, the theory

accumulate, measurements occur, and observables change through time [10–18]. The central 
argument of this section is that as proper time approaches a limiting condition, the structures 
required for quantum description become progressively unavailable. Under these circumstances, 
Quantum Mechanics encounters a limit of applicability analogous to the geometric limitations 
encountered by General Relativity near singularities.

requiring unification. The framework proposed here offers a different interpretation. Both 
theories are viewed as interior descriptions of an emergent spacetime domain. General Relativity 
approaches the boundary of that domain through the loss of spatial structure, while Quantum 
Mechanics approaches it through the loss of temporal structure. 
The resulting limitations are therefore interpreted not as independent failures, but as 
complementary manifestations of a common boundary condition. Under this view, the 
longstanding GR/QM divide may reflect not a conflict within spacetime, but the fact that both 
theories encounter the limits of the same underlying emergent structure. 
6. Implications for Quantum Gravity and Emergent 
Spacetime 
The interpretation developed in the preceding sections suggests that the longstanding tension 
between General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics may originate from a common underlying 
assumption: that spacetime remains available as a valid framework under all physical conditions. 
If spacetime is instead emergent, then the limitations encountered by both theories may provide 
information about the conditions required for spacetime itself to exist. 
This perspective has implications for quantum gravity, emergent spacetime research, and the 
interpretation of singularities, measurement, and fundamental physical observables. 
6.1 Quantum Gravity as a Boundary Problem 
Most approaches to quantum gravity seek a mathematical framework capable of incorporating 
both General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics into a single description. 
Such approaches typically begin with one of two assumptions: 
• Gravity must be quantized. 
• Quantum theory must be geometrized. 
The framework proposed here suggests a different possibility. The central difficulty may arise 
because both theories are formulated within spacetime, while the domain of interest involves the 
conditions under which spacetime itself becomes unavailable. Under this interpretation, quantum 
gravity is not merely a problem of combining two mathematical formalisms. It is fundamentally 
a problem concerning the boundary of emergent spacetime.

===== PAGE 20 =====
The primary question therefore becomes: 
What structures remain meaningful when one of the defining components of spacetime is 
no longer available? 
6.2 Relationship to Emergent Spacetime Programs 
A growing number of theoretical approaches have explored the possibility that spacetime is not 
fundamental but emergent from deeper structures [19–30]. 
Examples include: 
• Holographic approaches, 
• Entanglement-based spacetime emergence, 
• Quantum informational descriptions, 
• Relational formulations, 
• Background-independent quantum gravity models. 
The present framework shares with these approaches the assumption that spacetime may arise 
from a more fundamental level of organization. However, the emphasis here is different. Rather 
than focusing primarily on the mechanism by which spacetime emerges, attention is directed 
toward the conditions under which spacetime ceases to remain meaningful. In this sense, the 
proposed framework complements rather than competes with existing emergent spacetime 
programs. 
6.3 Singularities Revisited 
Within conventional General Relativity, singularities are frequently interpreted as regions where 
curvature becomes infinite and classical physics breaks down. 
The present framework suggests an alternative interpretation. The essential feature of a 
singularity may not be the existence of infinite quantities. Rather, it may be the disappearance of 
the spatial distinctions required to define geometry. 
Under this view: 
• Infinite curvature is not necessarily a physical prediction. 
• It may instead function as an indicator that geometric description has exceeded its 
domain of applicability. 
This interpretation is consistent with the broader proposal that singularities represent boundary 
conditions of emergent spacetime rather than physical locations within spacetime.

6.4 Quantum Measurement Revisited 
A similar reinterpretation applies to quantum measurement. 
Many discussions of the measurement problem focus on: 
• Wavefunction collapse, 
• Decoherence, 
• Many-worlds descriptions, 
• Hidden variables, 
• Observer-dependent interpretations. 
The framework proposed here does not attempt to resolve these debates directly. 
Instead, it highlights a more basic observation: Measurement requires temporal structure. 
Preparation, evolution, interaction, and outcome registration all depend upon the existence of 
temporal distinction. Consequently, some difficulties traditionally associated with quantum 
measurement may reflect the dependence of measurement itself upon the temporal component of 
spacetime. From this perspective, the measurement problem may be connected to the broader 
question of how temporal structure emerges and is maintained. 
6.5 Observables as Interior Quantities 
Both General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics define observables using quantities that 
presuppose spacetime. 
Examples include: 
• Distance, 
• Duration, 
• Momentum, 
• Energy, 
• Curvature, 
• Position. 
If spacetime is emergent, then these observables may themselves be interior quantities rather 
than fundamental entities. This interpretation suggests that divergences and pathologies 
appearing in observable quantities near boundary conditions should not necessarily be regarded 
as evidence for physical infinities. Instead, they may indicate that the framework used to define 
those quantities is approaching its limits of applicability.

across multiple scales.

Globally:

6.7 Conceptual Predictions 
Although the present work is primarily interpretive, it leads to several conceptual expectations. 
1. The breakdowns encountered by General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics should 
exhibit deeper structural similarities than currently recognized. 
2. Divergences and singularities should increasingly be interpreted as indicators of 
boundary conditions rather than physically realized infinities. 
3. Measurement-related limitations and singularity-related limitations should prove to be 
connected through their shared dependence on spacetime structure. 
4. Future approaches to quantum gravity may benefit from focusing on the emergence and 
maintenance of spacetime rather than solely on the reconciliation of existing formalisms. 
These expectations do not constitute a completed theory, but they provide a framework for future 
theoretical development. 
6.8 Summary 
The interpretation developed in this paper suggests that the limitations encountered by General 
Relativity and Quantum Mechanics may provide information about the structure of emergent 
spacetime itself. Rather than viewing singularities, divergences, and measurement-related 
difficulties as isolated theoretical problems, they may be understood as complementary 
indicators of a common boundary condition. 
This perspective reframes the quantum gravity problem as a question concerning the emergence, 
maintenance, and eventual breakdown of spacetime structure. Under this view, General 
Relativity and Quantum Mechanics remain extraordinarily successful interior theories while 
simultaneously revealing, through their limitations, the conditions under which spacetime ceases 
to remain physically meaningful.

7. Conclusion 
The incompatibility between General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics is widely regarded as 
one of the central unresolved problems in theoretical physics. Conventional approaches typically 
seek a mathematical framework capable of reconciling the geometric description of gravitation 
with the dynamical and probabilistic description of quantum phenomena. The present work has 
explored a different possibility. 
Rather than treating the conflict as originating within the theories themselves, this paper has 
examined whether the apparent incompatibility may arise because both theories depend upon a 
deeper structure whose limits have not been fully recognized. The central proposal is that 
spacetime is not fundamental, but emergent, and that both General Relativity and Quantum 
Mechanics are interior theories whose domains of applicability are bounded by the conditions 
required for spacetime itself to remain physically meaningful. 
The analysis began with a simple premise: spacetime requires both spatial extension and 
temporal progression. The loss of either component is sufficient to undermine the conditions 
required for a spacetime-based description. From this perspective, the limitations encountered by 
General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics acquire a common interpretation. 
General Relativity encounters a limit of applicability as spatial structure approaches a limiting 
condition. Geometry, curvature, causal relationships, and the observables derived from them 
become increasingly difficult to define as the distinctions required for spatial description are 
progressively lost. Quantum Mechanics encounters a corresponding limit as proper time 
approaches a limiting condition. Evolution, measurement, phase accumulation, and observer-
system distinctions become increasingly difficult to define as the temporal structure required for 
dynamical description becomes unavailable. Although these limitations appear different within 
conventional formulations, the framework developed here suggests that they are structurally 
related. In both cases, a defining component of spacetime is lost. The resulting breakdowns 
therefore need not be interpreted as independent theoretical failures. Instead, they may be 
understood as complementary manifestations of a common boundary condition associated with 
emergent spacetime itself. 
This interpretation leads to a reformulation of the GR/QM problem. The central issue may not be 
the incompatibility of geometry and quantum dynamics, but rather the assumption that spacetime 
remains a valid framework under all physical conditions. If spacetime is emergent, then 
singularities, divergences, measurement-related limitations, and null-limit phenomena may all 
provide insight into the conditions required for spacetime to exist. 
The framework proposed here does not constitute a completed theory of quantum gravity, nor 
does it attempt to replace the mathematical formalisms of General Relativity or Quantum 
Mechanics. Both theories remain extraordinarily successful within their established domains. 
Instead, the present work offers a conceptual interpretation of why both theories encounter 
limitations and why those limitations may be related. The principal claim may therefore be 
summarized simply: General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics approach the same underlying 
boundary of emergent spacetime from opposite directions. General Relativity approaches that

===== PAGE 24 =====
boundary through the loss of spatial structure, while Quantum Mechanics approaches it through 
the loss of temporal structure. Their apparent conflict may therefore reflect complementary 
approaches to the limits of spacetime rather than a fundamental contradiction between the 
theories themselves. 
If correct, this perspective suggests that progress toward quantum gravity may depend not only 
on unifying existing theories, but also on understanding the conditions under which spacetime 
emerges, persists, and ultimately ceases to remain physically meaningful. In that sense, the 
limitations of General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics may be viewed not merely as 
obstacles to unification, but as clues to the deeper structure from which spacetime itself arises.
