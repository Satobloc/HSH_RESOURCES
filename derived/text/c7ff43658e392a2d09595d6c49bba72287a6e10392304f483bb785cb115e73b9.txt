Repeated black-hole mergers in dense stellar clusters are a plausible mechanism to populate the
predicted gap in black hole masses due to the pair-instability supernova process. These hierarchical mergers
carry distinct spin characteristics relative to first-generation black holes. We introduce an astrophysically
motivated model in the joint space of effective inspiral and precessing spins, which captures the dominant
spin dynamics expected for hierarchical mergers. We find decisive evidence for a transition at
m1 ¼ 46.2þ12.6
−7.2 M⊙, above which the population is nearly entirely hierarchical, a location consistent with
the anticipated onset of the pair-instability gap. We also infer a global peak in the hierarchical merger rate at
m1 ¼ 15.7þ3.2
−1.1 M⊙. The existence of low- and high-mass subpopulations of higher-generation black holes
suggests the contribution of both near-solar-metallicity and metal-poor star clusters to the hierarchical
merger population. Our results reinforce the growing evidence for detailed, mass-dependent substructure in
the spin distribution of the binary black hole population.

Introduction—Theoretical models for binary black hole
(BBH) formation can be broadly divided into two classes:
isolated evolution of field binary stars and dynamical
channels (see, e.g., Refs. [1–3] for reviews). Identifying
the formation history of an individual merger can be
difficult since multiple channels may be consistent with
a given set of black-hole parameters. The latest catalog of
gravitational-wave (GW) events [4,5] released by the
LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) Collaboration [6–10] allows
for the most detailed investigation yet of the constitution of
the BBH population.
It is increasingly clear, both from individual events and
population analyses, that massive black holes exist in the
range 45 − 120M⊙ [11–14]. This is in tension with the
notion of pair-instability supernovae, which stellar evolu-
tion theory predicts should leave no black holes in this mass
interval [15–23]. These observations have spurred further
investigation into mechanisms that can populate this gap
[24–29].
Most observed BBHs are thought to contain two first-
generation black holes formed from stellar collapse, creat-
ing a second-generation remnant [30,31]. In dense stellar
environments, remnants may undergo subsequent coales-
cences, creating hierarchical mergers: those containing at
least one component that is the product of one or more
previous mergers [32–37]. Hierarchical mergers are char-
acterized by higher primary masses, isotropic spin orienta-
tions [32], and dimensionless spin magnitudes χ ≈ 0.7,
arising from the orbital angular momenta of their progeni-
tors [38–42], with some spread about χ ≈ 0.7 due to their
mass ratio and spins [43,44]. These spin predictions are
robust across binary star evolution assumptions [45]. Since
hierarchical assembly produces more massive black holes,
it is a natural candidate for filling the pair-instability
supernova (PISN) gap.

To disentangle hierarchical mergers from first-generation
counterparts, it is useful to look in parameters that are both
well-measured and in which we expect distinct behavior.
The rapid spins, unequal mass ratios, and/or high masses of
several observed BBH signals — namely, GW190521 [11],
GW231123 [14], and GW241011 [46]— have led to their
interpretation as hierarchical mergers [26,47–51], though
nonhierarchical interpretations have also been put forward,
e.g., [24,52–58]. However, data quality issues and wave-
form inaccuracies can introduce systematic biases in
measurements of individual systems, including their spins
[7,14,58–63]. When performing and interpreting popula-
tion analyses, we therefore turn to effective parameters ,
which are typically more reliably measured than compo-
nent spin magnitudes and tilts [64–66]. Effective inspiral
spin [67,68] is typically the best-measured combination of
spin parameters [69], defined as the mass-weighted pro-
jection of the spin vectors onto the orbital angular momen-
tum: χeff ¼ð χ1 cos τ1 þ qχ2 cos τ2Þ=ð1 þ qÞ, where q
denotes the mass ratio (defined on 0 <q ≤ 1), χ1 and χ2
are the respective spin magnitudes of the primary (heavier)
and secondary (lighter) black holes, and τ1;2 are the angles
between the spin vectors and the Newtonian orbital angular
momentum at a chosen reference frequency.
The natal spins of black holes are theoretically uncertain
but often expected to be small [70–74], in which case first-
generation BBHs ( 1G þ 1G) will have a narrow χeff
distribution centered near zero. Isotropically oriented
dynamical mergers create a distribution symmetric around
zero, while preferentially aligned isolated binaries would
induce a positive centroid [68,75]. In contrast, hierarchical
mergers between one first-generation and one second-
generation black hole ( 1G þ 2G) will have a uniform
χeff distribution centered at 0 with a width of ∼1 [25].
As higher-generation merger rates are likely an order of
magnitude below that of 1G þ 2G mergers [76–78],w e
specialize to the latter.
As spin parameters are useful differentiators between
formation channels, many studies have searched for
population-level signatures in χeff , spin magnitudes,
and/or spin orientations, often in conjunction with mass
dependence [79–102]. Recently, Refs. [93,94,99] found
indication of a transition to higher spin magnitudes in the
population at primary masses above m1 ∼ 40 − 50M⊙, and
Refs. [95–98,100] presented strong evidence for a tran-
sition in χeff from narrow to broad above m1 ∼ 45M⊙.
References [97,100] furthermore demonstrated the χeff
transition to be coincident with a truncation in the secon-
dary mass distribution, remarkably in line with the theo-
retical expectation for the lower edge of the PISN gap.
These studies suggest that at m1 ≳ 45M⊙ the population is
dominated by second-generation black holes.
While effective spin has been a successful population-
level diagnostic of hierarchical mergers, it is a lossy
compression of the six-dimensional spin space to a single
parameter. Hierarchical mergers are distinguished from
1G þ 1G mergers not only in χeff , but cleanly in the joint
space of χeff and effective precessing spin χp, which is a
linearization of the in-plane spin dynamics [103–105]:
χp ¼ max½χ1 sin τ1; ð3 þ 4qÞ=ð4 þ 3qÞqχ2 sin τ2/C138 . The
joint χeff –χp distribution for 1G þ 1G mergers is expected
to cluster near χeff ; χp ∼ 0 [106], while that of 1G þ 2G
mergers scatters around the boundary of an ellipse centered
on 0 with major axis χp ≈ 0.7 and minor axis χeff ≈
0.47 [51,95].
In this Letter, we introduce an astrophysically motivated
population model in the joint space of effective inspiral and
precessing spin. We include a two-dimensional ellipse-
boundary component to capture 1G þ 2G mergers along-
side an uncorrelated Gaussian to describe 1G þ 1G bina-
ries. We explore the mixing fraction between these
components as a function of mass and recover strong
evidence for a subpopulation of hierarchical mergers at
mpeak ¼ 15.7þ3.2
−1.1 M⊙ and a transition at m/C3 ¼ 46.2þ12.6
−7.2 M⊙
above which the majority of mergers are hierarchical (here
and elsewhere we quote the medians and 90% symmetric
credible intervals). Our results are consistent with and
bolster studies that model only in χeff by capturing the
astrophysical expectation with higher fidelity in the multi-
dimensional spin space. We reveal correlated structure in
the χp distribution that increases credence both for the
robustness of spin-mass features and their association with
hierarchical mergers.
Models—We analyze BBHs in the Fourth Gravitational-
Wave Transient Catalog (GWTC-4) [4] with false-alarm
rates < 1 yr−1 alongside GW241011 and GW241110, two
events from the second part of the fourth observing run
(O4b) whose spin properties imply a hierarchical origin
[46]. We model the population χeff –χp distribution as a
mixture of two components: independent truncated
Gaussians for the 1G þ 1G distribution and a probability
density concentrated on an ellipse boundary for the 1G þ
2G distribution. (Throughout, we associate the Gaussian
and ellipse-boundary components to 1G þ 1G and 1G þ
2G mergers, respectively, and refer to the components with
their associated astrophysical interpretations interchange-
ably.) Figure 1 schematically depicts the model compo-
nents. The Supplemental Material [107] contains further
details of our models and hierarchical inference settings,
including sensitivity injections and waveform models for
single-event parameter estimation.

We model the probability density for 1G þ 2G mergers

boundary with a filter along χp to account for the declining
density. The characteristic spin χc, characteristic mass ratio

χeff and χp distributions of the 1G þ 1G component are
modeled as uncorrelated Gaussians, though we consider
other models in Supplemental Material [107]. We allow the
mixing fraction between the Gaussian ( 1G þ 1G) and
ellipse-boundary ( 1G þ 2G) components to vary with
primary mass, similar to the models employed in
Refs. [98,100]. We use a cubic spline to flexibly model
the branching ratio without imposing strong priors on the
existence or location of transitions; Supplemental Material
[107] contains specifics of our node choices. While this is a
strong model, we do not enforce the 1G þ 2G component’s
existence; further, it is built on simple, robust astrophysical
grounds.
For primary mass, we make the simplifying assumption
that the population is described by a single distribution, for
which we use the default model of the LVK for GWTC-4: a
broken power law with two Gaussian peaks and low-mass
smoothing [5]. While we employ the standard power-law
mass ratio model for the 1G þ 1G component, for the 1G þ
2G mergers we instead use a truncated normal, as hierar-
chical mergers may have a mass ratio peak away from
unity. Evolution over cosmic time for both components is
modeled with a single power law.

The astrophysically informed ellipse-boundary χeff –χp
model for the hierarchical component induces, by design, a
quasiuniform marginal χeff akin to the one-dimensional
model employed by Refs. [98,100]. Hence, our comparable
identification of spin-mass features is not unexpected. The
inclusion of χp does not significantly improve constraints
(see Supplemental Material [107]), but crucially corrobo-
rates the hierarchical hypothesis in the precessing spin
dimension. Further, χp enables richer inference of the
population spin structure, such as the inferred spin magni-
tude peak at χ ≈ 0.7, which strengthens the emerging
picture of a high-spin, isotropic subpopulation consistent
with hierarchical assembly.
The inclusion of the 1G þ 2G population significantly
impacts the inferred spin distribution of the bulk BBH
population (also see Supplemental Material [107] Fig. 3).

The model including the hierarchical component, with
the branching ratio an evolving function of mass, is
preferred over a pure Gaussian model with a natural log
Bayes factor of ln B ¼ 14.5, which accounts for model
complexity. It is favored over a model where the branching
ratio is constant over mass with ln B ¼ 6.8. These Bayes
factors indicate significant evidence for structure in the
effective inspiral and precessing spin distribution consistent
with hierarchical mergers, which, moreover, is nonlinearly
correlated with primary mass.
Discussion and conclusion —Using an astrophysically
informed mixture model in the joint χeff –χp space, we find
strong evidence for a subpopulation of binary black hole
mergers that occupy the boundary of an ellipse with a
characteristic spin near χc ∼ 0.7, consistent with the expect-
ation for hierarchical mergers. Allowing the mixing frac-
tion between the components to vary with primary mass,
we identify a transition to primarily high-spin and quasiuni-
form χeff at m/C3 ¼ 46.2þ12.6
−7.2 M⊙. Interpreting the elliptical
arc component as hierarchical mergers, the data indicate
most black holes above 46M⊙ arise from repeated mergers
in dense stellar clusters, aligned with the predicted onset of
the PISN gap.
Moreover, we measure a peak in the fraction of hierar-
chical mergers at mpeak ¼ 15.7þ3.2
−1.1 M⊙. Hierarchical
assembly is frequently discussed as a method to produce
black holes in and above the PISN gap. Indeed, the
transition near 46M⊙ corroborates this interpretation.
However, simulations of dense stellar clusters suggest
the 2G mass distribution declines with primary mass and
peaks near 15M⊙, driven by an abundance of low-mass
first-generation black holes produced by solar-metallicity
stars [34,76,78,120]. As such, the observation of this lower
peak in BBH data is unsurprising and may constitute the
maximum of the 2G mass spectrum. At the same time, 2G
black holes above 46M⊙ are uncommon in near-solar-
metallicity clusters, potentially implying the high-mass

component arises from clusters with different initial con-

population. Coupling the features in the data with
predictions from stellar evolution may enable constraints
on the metallicity distribution of the progenitor stars of

While the low-mass peak meshes with theoretical
expectation, the ostensible lack of hierarchical mergers
between 20 and 40M⊙ is perhaps more surprising. The
dominance of hierarchical mergers in the 10 − 20M⊙ range
followed by the dearth in 20 − 40M⊙ may imply either a
dip in the first-generation mass spectrum in the former
range, or a pileup in the latter. A drop in progenitor core
compactness may drive a dip in this lower regime [121], for
which several studies have searched and some have
tentatively identified [122–124]. If the observed feature
at ∼35M⊙ primarily contains first-generation mergers, it
may also contribute to the apparent lack of second-gen-
eration black holes at moderate masses. Untangling what
the inferred branching fraction across primary mass indi-
cates for both the first- and second-generation mass spectra
— and the properties of their stellar progenitors — is a key
avenue for future study.
Although past work has yielded similar results, no
studies have thus far considered the influence of precessing
degrees of freedom. Neglecting to model a parameter
(which, in practice, means assuming it follows a fixed
distribution— here, the single-event parameter estimation
prior) can be a source of systematic bias. While we find χp
does not substantially further constrain the mass-dependent
fraction of hierarchical mergers obtained with χeff only, the
consistency between results bolsters confidence in the
hierarchical interpretation of effective spin structure while
extending into a deeper observable space.
Taken at face value, the fraction of hierarchical mergers
inferred from GW data is consistent with a population of
entirely dynamical mergers. This meshes with Ref. [125],
who note consistency between the BBH merger rate found
by the L VK and from cluster simulations. We find the total
1G þ 2G fraction to be ξ ∼ 14%, while 1G þ 2G mergers
comprise ∼17% of mergers in the Cluster Monte Carlo
catalog [76,77], and the astrophysical hierarchical fraction is
likely closer to ∼10% [120]. The 1G þ 1G χeff distribution
has support at negative values, which may signify a large
contribution of 1G dynamical mergers, as expected given the
apparent observation of 2G dynamical mergers. However, its
confidently positive centroid is instead indicative of pref-
erentially aligned isolated binaries. We caution against
overinterpreting the similarity in population fraction, as
both rely on uncertain assumptions: the former on associat-
ing the ellipse-boundary component to 1G þ 2G mergers,
and the latter on prescriptions that control the merger rate
such as the escape velocity distribution.
Sequential mergers may also occur in active galactic
nuclei disks, preferentially creating aligned mergers
[26,27,126,127]. A symmetric ellipse arc is favored (see
Supplemental Material [107]), suggesting active-galactic-
nuclei-driven mergers are not dominant constituents of the
inferred χeff –χp distribution. Further observations might
allow us to elucidate hierarchical subpopulations corre-
sponding to different mechanisms that prompt the repeated
mergers.
Linking the black-hole mass spectrum to features in the
spin distribution is a promising avenue to more precisely
identify hallmarks of stellar evolution that may otherwise
be washed out, like the PISN mass gap, by combining
information across multiple dimensions. Improved con-
straints on the PISN gap will sharpen bounds on nuclear
processes inaccessible at laboratory temperatures, in par-
ticular the cross section of the 12Cðα; γÞ16O reaction, pivotal
to energy production in massive stars [22,128,129]. As the
catalog of binary black hole mergers continues to grow
alongside advancements in stellar theory and simulation,
the statistical significance and associated astrophysical
interpretation of spin-mass features in the population will
be tested with increasing rigor, emphasizing the power of
astrophysically informed population studies to uncover the
evolutionary history of black holes.
