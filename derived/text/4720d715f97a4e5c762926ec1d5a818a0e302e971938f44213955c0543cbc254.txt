Abstract
We study the Ollivier–Ricci curvature and its modiﬁcation introduced by Lin, Lu,
and Y au on regular graphs. We derive precise formulas for the Ollivier–Ricci curva-
ture and the Lin–Lu–Y au curvature on regular graphs, expressed in terms of graph
parameters and an optimal assignment between neighborhoods. Using these precise
formulas, we provide an efﬁcient implementation for computing the Ollivier–Ricci
curvature and the Lin–Lu–Y au curvature on regular graphs, enabling us to explore the
distribution of regular graphs with positive Lin–Lu–Y au curvature. Our main contri-
bution is establishing a lower bound on the degree of a regular graph that guarantees
positive Lin–Lu–Y au curvature and non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature, along with
an analysis of the sharpness of this bound. Additionally, we study the maximal number
of vertices that an d-regular graph with positive Lin–Lu–Y au curvature can have.

1 Introduction and statement of results
Since Gauss and Riemann introduced the geometric notion of curvature over 150
years ago, it played a central role in differential geometry. Among the various types of
curvature, Ricci curvature is of particular importance, serving as a fundamental tool
in the study of Riemannian manifolds. Given the important role of Ricci curvature
in differential geometry, there is a natural interest in extending this concept to metric
spaces more general than Riemannian manifolds. This has led to the proposal of
various generalized notions of curvature for non-smooth or discrete structures (see,
e.g., [1–4]). Each approach captures a different aspect of the Riemannian notion, and
each is thus suited for a different purpose.
V on Renesse and Sturm [5] established a connection between Ricci curvature and
optimal transport on smooth Riemannian manifolds. Building on their results, Ollivier
[6] introduced a discrete notion of Ricci curvature on metric spaces equipped with
Markov chains or a measure. His approach leverages optimal transport theory, as his

A Comparative analysis of implementations
In this section, we present a comparative analysis of our implementation for computing
the Lin–Lu–Y au curvature against existing implementations. We compare our program
with those by Ni et al., presented in [ 17], and by Cushing et al., presented in [ 20]. For
this comparison, we generate random d-regular graphs on n vertices, employing the
algorithm proposed by Steger and Wormald in [18]. We then calculate the Lin–Lu–Y au
curvature of these graphs using the different implementations. To ensure robustness,
computation times are averaged over 100 graphs for each combination of parameters
n and d. The computations are performed on an Apple M2 chip (8-core CPU at up to
3.5 GHz) with 16 GB RAM. Due to the limited efﬁciency of the implementation by
Cushing et al., we exclude it from the analysis on larger graphs, as the computation
time is prohibitively high. For smaller graphs (n ≤ 20) our implementation achieves a
speedup of 20–50 times over the other implementations. The results of our comparative
analysis are presented in Fig. 6.
