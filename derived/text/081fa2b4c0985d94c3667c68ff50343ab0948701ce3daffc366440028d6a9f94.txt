1. Introduction 
This paper adopts a cosmological picture in which the universe was formed by a phase transition and the spatial 
scale does not subsequently expand. The analysis does not depend on the microscopic properties of the pre -
transition phase, the phase-transition front, energy redistribution, or the details of matter generation. It requires 
only that the dynamic Φ field became established as the cosmic substrate after the transition and that its response 
rate changed with the elapsed time since the transition. Cosmic history is therefore described as the evolution of 
the response rate of the dynamic Φ field rather than as a history of spatial expansion. 
The kinematic model of phase-transition-front cosmology that provides the cosmogenic background for this study 
has been published on SSRN as an earlier paper [11]. Against that background, the present paper formulates a 
phenomenological model that describes cosmological redshift, the time dilation observed in Type Ia supernova 
light curves, and the distance-duality relation within a common framework. 
The dynamic Φ field is a continuous substrate present throughout the universe. Representative effective properties 
adopted in KUT are a bulk modulus KΦ≈10³⁷–10³⁸ Pa and a mass-equivalent density ρΦ≈10²⁰–10²¹ kg m⁻³. The 
wave speed cΦ≈√(KΦ/ρΦ) determined by their ratio is of the order of the speed of light. In the dynamic phase, 
the effective viscosity is taken to be nearly zero and the elasticity extremely large, and light is treated as a wave 
propagating through this substrate. These are effective material  properties in KUT and provide the physical 
starting point for describing gravity, light, and matter in terms of the same substrate. 
In the post-transition dynamic Φ field, a common Φ-response rate governing atomic oscillations, material motion, 
chemical reactions, radiative processes, and other local physical processes increased with the elapsed time since 
the phase transition. Past cl ocks are therefore slow when compared with the present standard, and all local 
physical processes accelerate by the same ratio toward the present. We denote this epoch -dependent response 
rate by RΦ(t) and adopt RΦ(t)/RΦ,0=t/t₀ as its minimal form. 
In standard FLRW cosmology, cosmological redshift is described by a change in the cosmic scale factor. Type 
Ia supernovae are not only standardizable light sources but also standard clocks whose light-curve widths can be 
compared. Goldhaber et al. showed that the light curves of distant Type Ia supernovae are stretched by 1+z [1]. 
An analysis of 1,504 Type Ia supernovae from the Dark Energy Survey reported b=1.003±0.005 (statistical) 
±0.010 (systematic) for Δt_obs=Δt_em(1+z)^b [2]. Any alternative model of redshift must therefore reproduce 
not only the frequency decrease of spectral lines but also this time dilation by the same factor. 
Many historically proposed non- expanding redshift hypotheses reduce only the energy or frequency of light 
during propagation and therefore do not stretch an entire supernova light curve by 1+z. This observation has 
provided a decisive test that strongly excludes simple tired-light models [1,2]. KUT does not append an auxiliary 
mechanism merely to evade this test. Instead, it assumes that the same Φ-response-rate ratio that produces redshift 
maps the temporal structure of both a finite wave train and a light curve by the factor 1+z.

2. Model Definition and Physical Assumptions 
2.1 Φ-Response Rate 
Let t be the physical cosmic time defined by connecting the local clocks at successive epochs from the origin of 
the universe, and let t₀ denote the present. We write the response rate of local physical processes—such as atomic 
oscillations, material motion, and radiative processes —to the dynamic Φ field as RΦ(t). The present value is 
RΦ,0=RΦ(t₀), and the value at emission time t_e is RΦ,e=RΦ(t_e). We assume that RΦ(t) is smaller in the past 
and increases toward the present. 
The temporal response rate RΦ(t) used here is the relative rate at which clocks and physical processes proceed at 
each epoch when compared with present local physical processes. Put simply, it represents the rate of physical 
change—the rate at which clocks advance. It is not a rate relative to an external absolute time, but a relative rate 
obtained by comparing different epochs with the present standard. An observer at a given epoch does not perceive 
time as slow because the observer's clock, body, and measuring instruments all obey the same response rate.

8.4 Phenomenological Test of the Distance Curve with Pantheon+ (z≤1.6) 
At the high-redshift end, the number of observations is small, and the effects of calibration, selection, and 
standardization corrections may become relatively more important. We therefore restrict the primary Pantheon+ 
comparison in this paper to z≤1.6. The parameter-free form of Equation (24) is the γ=1 reference prediction 
corresponding to isotropic phase transport. To measure the curvature required by Pantheon+, we 
phenomenologically generalize the luminosity distance to d_L^KUT(z)=c₀t₀z(1+z)^γ and fix t₀=13.8 Gyr. The 
exponent γ and the brightness zero point ΔM are fitted simultaneously. 
From the public Pantheon+ file Pantheon+SH0ES.dat, we selected 1,575 uncalibrated distance-modulus entries 
satisfying IS_CALIBRATOR=0, z_HD>0.01, and z≤1.6 and used the corresponding statistical-plus-systematic 
covariance submatrix from Pantheon+SH0ES_STAT+SYS.cov. Refitting the generalized KUT law gives 
γ=0.63086, ΔM=−0.05216 mag, and χ²_KUT=1391.70. Refitting flat-ΛCDM under the same conditions to the 
same 1,575 entries gives Ω_m=0.33043 and χ²_ΛCDM=1384.80. 
Thus Δχ²=χ²_KUT−χ²_ΛCDM=6.90. By comparison, using all 1,580 entries over z≤2.26137 gives Δχ²=15.01; 
restricting the analysis to z≤1.6 reduces the difference to 6.90. The five excluded entries at z>1.6 and their 
covariance make a large contribution to the full-range model difference. Accordingly, this paper uses z≤1.6 as 
the primary analysis and treats the full-range result as a sensitivity comparison. Over the primary range, 
generalized KUT has a χ² larger by 6.90 than flat-ΛCDM, but the distance-modulus curves are visually very 
close on a conventional plot, and the generalized law reproduces the observed curve with an accuracy close to 
that of standard cosmology. 
The value γ=0.63086 is fitted from the primary z≤1.6 Pantheon+ analysis; it is not a theoretical prediction 
independently derived from a Φ-field wave equation. The result shows that the generalized KUT law, with a 
single curvature exponent, can closely approach the supernova distance–redshift curve; it does not mean that 
the parameter-free KUT reference law γ=1 is supported. 
Furthermore, if d_A=r and Equation (15) are retained while the generalized distance law is used, then 
d_L=(1+z)^(γ+1)d_A. At the best-fit γ=0.63086, the exponent is 1.63086, inconsistent with the exponent 2 
required by the distance-duality relation in Equation (20). Under the same definitions, the corresponding 
bolometric surface-brightness exponent is −2(γ+1)=−3.26172, also inconsistent with −4 in Equation (21). 
Therefore, making the generalized supernova fit, distance duality, and the Tolman law hold simultaneously 
requires at least one of RΦ(t), the angular-diameter distance, or the transverse light-bundle transport law to be 
reformulated on the basis of additional observations. Related nonstandard fits to Pantheon+ include the 
covarying-coupling-constants (CCC) and CCC+TL hybrid models proposed by Gupta [12]. Their redshift 
decomposition and distance law differ from the response-rate and isotropic-transport construction used here.

11. Discussion 
A strength of the model is that it does not divide redshift and Type Ia supernova time dilation between separate 
mechanisms; both are described by the same response -rate ratio and the transport condition for one wave train. 
By holding the locally measured speed of light at c₀ at every epoch while producing differences only in 
observables that connect the past and present, the construction avoids a direct contradiction with local 
measurements of the speed of light. 
The five basic relations of this paper —the response-rate definition of redshift, mapping of temporal structure, 
conservation of phase-front number, constancy of the local speed of light, and isotropic phase transport—form a 
closed phenomenological system l inking frequency, wavelength, supernova time width, light -bundle cross -
sectional area, surface brightness, and the two cosmological distances. In particular, the reductio argument that 
an unstretched wavelength would increase the phase -front number by 1+z clearly shows why wavelength 
stretching is required by continuity of the same wave train.

angular-diameter distances, and surface brightness. 
12. Conclusions 
The KUT redshift model assumes that the universe was formed by a phase transition, that its spatial scale did not 
subsequently expand, and that the Φ-response rate increased with the elapsed time since the transition. Under this 
minimal physical picture, c ombining 1+z=RΦ,0/RΦ,e, constancy of the local speed of light, conservation of 
phase-front number in the same finite wave train, and the time mapping Δt_obs=(1+z)Δt_e gives f_obs=f_e/(1+z) 
and λ_obs=(1+z)λ_e. The model thereby shows that spectral redshift and the 1+z time dilation of Type Ia 
supernovae can be reproduced within the same phenomenological framework based on a single physical 
mechanism. 
Extending the isotropy of the dynamic Φ field to the transverse direction of freely propagating light multiplies 
the transverse linear scale by 1+z and the light-bundle cross-sectional area by (1+z)². Together with the reductions 
in photon energy and arrival rate, this gives F=L/[4πr²(1+z)⁴], d_L=(1+z)²d_A, and S_obs=S_e/(1+z)⁴ 
simultaneously. Through this single isotropic transport law, the model links supernova time dilation, the distance-
duality relation, and the Tolman surface-brightness law. 
In the linear-response model RΦ(t)=RΦ,0t/t₀, t_e=t₀/(1+z). For t₀=13.8 Gyr and z=14, this gives a physical cosmic 
age of 0.92 Gyr. Applying the time transformation consistently to both speed and time interval gives a light-travel 
distance of 12.88 Gly under the same conditions and a luminosity distance d_L=c₀t₀z(1+z). At low z the model 
gives H_eff≈70.9 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹; the high-z curvature can be tested clearly with supernovae and BAO. 
A principal result of the model is that it reproduces the Type Ia supernova time dilation not explained by simple 
tired-light hypotheses using the same factor as redshift, and further derives the observed distance-duality relation 
and Tolman law from the s ame transport mechanism. Simultaneous fits to Pantheon+, DESI BAO, and high-
redshift galaxies can quantitatively test the KUT response-rate cosmology without spatial expansion. 
In the full-covariance comparison over the primary Pantheon+ range z≤1.6, generalized KUT gives γ=0.63086 
and χ²=1391.70, whereas flat-ΛCDM gives Ω_m=0.33043 and χ²=1384.80, a difference of Δχ²=6.90. The 
difference is smaller than the full-range value Δχ²=15.01, and the two curves are visually very close in a 
conventional distance-modulus plot. On the other hand, the best-fit value γ=0.63086 does not match the 
reference value γ=1, which gives distance duality and the Tolman law simultaneously. The generalized formula 
is a useful phenomenological description, but it does not mean that the isotropic phase-transport law of KUT is 
observationally complete.
