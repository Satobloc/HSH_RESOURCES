Abstract
Near contact binary (NCB) systems are astrophysically quite interesting; the
light curve (LC) of ST Aqr, a member of this class, was obtained from the Tran-
siting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) database and analyzed in detail in this
article. A q-search was undertaken to determine the mass ratio ( q = M2/M1)
for ST Aqr, and the q was found to be 0.472 ± 0.009. It was showed that each
body in the ST Aqr system is a NCB with a lling factor of approximately 99
percent. As accurate absolute parameters are essential for investigating the evo-
lutionary properties of such systems, the body masses of ST Aqr were estimated
as M1 = 1.81 ± 0.10M⊙ and M2 = 0.85 ± 0.06M⊙, while their corresponding
radii were found to be R1 = 2.27±0.05R⊙ and R2 = 1.67±0.04R⊙. In the anal-
ysis of ST Aqr's orbital period (OP), a decrease in the period was detected, and
this value was calculated to be 2.08 seconds per century. Since the bodies do not
ll their Roche lobes, we believe that the decrease in OP cannot be explained
by a conserved mass transfer between the bodies. For this reason, the observed
orbital period decrease may be attributed to angular momentum loss (AML)
from the system through magnetic braking associated with magnetic activity.
The non-conservative mass transfer rate was calculated to be 1.64 × 10−7M⊙
per year. To better understand the nature of ST Aqr, it was plotted on the log
Mtotlog J and Hertzsprung-Russell (HR) diagrams.

1. Introduction
Near contact binaries (NCBs) were dened by Shaw (1990) as a class of close
binaries. Shaw (1994) reported that NCBs, which have orbital periods (OPs)
shorter than one day (though there are rare exceptions that slightly exceed this
limit), exhibit strong tidal interactions between their bodies. Although their5
facing surfaces are close, they are binary stars that are not in physical contact,
such as W UMa-type systems (Shaw, 1994). In these systems, one component
is typically a hot star of spectral type A or F, while the cool component is 1-2
spectral classes later (cooler) than the hot component (Shaw, 1994). In such
systems, at least one of the bodies has lled or is about to ll its Roche lobe.10
Regarding NCBs, it has been proposed that they are the progenitors of A-type W
UMa stars; Shaw et al. (1996) cited as reasons that NCBs are in the early stages
of mass transfer and that their X-ray luminosity functions are similar to those of
A-type W UMa stars. Since these systems are not in contact and therefore have
not reached thermal equilibrium, the temperature dierence is greater than in15
contact binaries. Shaw et al. (1996) examined 58 NCB systems using ROSAT
data and found that their X-ray luminosity functions were similar to those of
A-type W UMa systems, thereby supporting the scenario that NCBs could be
the evolutionary precursors of A-type W UMa systems. Furthermore, Shaw et
al. (1996) noted that the 14 identied NCB examples suggest that the single,20
apparent A-F-type stars observed in RASS may actually be binary systems with
cold, X-ray-emitting companions, and proposed photometric methods for the
discovery of new NCB candidates. Since determining the physical parameters of
NCB candidate systems and reexamining them with precise data would provide
valuable insights into our understanding of such systems, the ST Aqr system25
was selected and subjected to a detailed photometric analysis.
ST Aqr (TIC 38378877, TYC 5232-397-1, HD 211965, Gaia DR3 2622626156150704896),
was discovered by Leavitt at Harvard in 1908 (Pickering, 1908) and classied as

LC analysis allows for the determination of the stars' orbital inclination, radius85
ratios, temperature ratios, luminosity contributions, and geometric structures.
In current studies, LC analysis is performed using the Wilson-Devinney (WD)
model and similar techniques. Thus, observational data are compared with
theoretical models to calculate the most appropriate physical parameters of
the system. In this study, the WD (Wilson and Devinney, 1971; van Hamme90
and Wilson, 2003) technique was used for LC analysis of data obtained from
TESS. In the LC analysis performed using the WD technique, MOD 2 was
selected for ST Aqr in accordance with the system's geometric structure. The
results of the photometric solution particularly the initial parameters such as
the eective temperature of the primary body ( T1) and the system's q are crucial95
for arriving at a solution. The T1 was determined by considering the system's
spectral type, and the value ( T1 = 7745 K) corresponding to spectral type A7
was taken from the tables in the study by Eker et al. (2020). This value was used
as a xed parameter in the LC analysis, while the temperature of the second
body ( T2) was left as a free parameter during the solution and determined100
to provide the best t between the observational LC and the theoretical LC.
To determine the system's q, radial velocity analysis is preferred when radial
velocity data are available; however, when a reliable spectral q is not available for
the system, the photometric q-search method can be employed. In this context,
a q-search was performed, and the q corresponding to the point where the value105
of P(O −C)2 is minimal was accepted as the system's photometric q. During
the photometric solution, the atmospheric properties of the stars were taken
into account; for components with convective atmospheres, the gravitational
darkening coecient ( g = 0.32) proposed by Lucy (1967) and Rucinski (1969)
were used for bodies with convective atmospheres, while values of ( g = 1.0)110
and ( A = 1.0) were used for bodies with radiative atmospheres. The nonlinear
edge darkening coecients were taken from the tables provided by van Hamme
(1993). During the solution, appropriate parameters were left free to obtain the
theoretical LC that best represents the observational LC (see Figure 1), thereby
determining the system's fundamental geometric and physical parameters. In115

4. Orbital Period Analysis
OP analysis is a method that examines changes in the OP of multiple systems120
over time. The purpose of OP analysis is to identify the physical processes that
inuence the dynamic evolution of the systems and their OPs. The analysis
is typically conducted using MTs. The dierences between the observed MTs
and the calculated MTs are plotted on an Observed-Calculated (O-C) diagram.
Parabolic variations in the O-C diagram may indicate mass transfer or AML125
in the system, while periodic oscillations may suggest the presence of a third
body or be caused by magnetic activity. This method is widely used in the
study of the long-term dynamic behavior of eclipsing multiple systems. When
high-precision minimum time data spanning multiple observation periods are
used, even very small changes in the OP can be reliably determined. The MTs130
for ST Aqr, selected for this study, were prepared for analysis using data from
the literature (O-C Gateway (Paschke and Brat, 2006)) and those determined
in this study. The initial minimum time ( T0) and orbital period ( P ) values
were obtained from the O-C Gateway (Paschke and Brat, 2006) and Atlas O-C
(Kreiner, 2004). A decreasing parabolic variation was observed in the ST Aqr135
system and is expressed by Equation 1.
