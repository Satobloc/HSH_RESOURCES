Abstract 
This manuscript presents the full theoretical development, 
experimental prediction, mathematical formulation, 
comparisons with Maxwell optics, Fourier-domain analysis, 
and interpretive framework behind the Furner Projection 
Duality Theorem. The model proposes that wave-like and 
particle-like signatures of light emerge from distinct two-
dimensional projections of a single three-dimensional helical 
photon trajectory. A cosine-theta–dependent axial fringe 
translation is proposed as a falsifiable signature 
distinguishing this theory from conventional Maxwell 
diffraction. This offers a deterministic, non-collapse-based 
account of measurement signatures in quantum optics, 
reframing duality as a geometric projection phenomenon. 
1. Introduction

===== PAGE 3 =====
Wave–particle duality is a central challenge in quantum 
physics, historically explained through probabilistic wave-
function collapse and Bohr’s complementarity principle. Yet 
no geometric structure underpins this dichotomy in 
conventional theory. 
Here, wave and particle behaviors are resolved 
geometrically: light propagates as a real helical photon 
trajectory, with observed behavior determined by how the 
trajectory is projected onto the detection plane. This 
produces a prediction for cosine-theta fringe translation 
under detector rotation — a robust, low-cost, falsifiable 
experimental test separating this geometric model from 
Maxwell optics. 
2. Mathematical Helix Model 
A photon is modeled as a real three-dimensional helical 
trajectory: 
x(t) = r cos(omega t)

===== PAGE 4 =====
y(t) = r sin(omega t) 
z(t) = c t 
Where: 
•r is a microscopic rotational radius (10^-12 to 10^-10 
meters) 
•omega = 2 pi c divided by wavelength 
•detector orientation modifies the observed projection 
Projection onto a detection plane defined by angle theta 
yields different measurable behaviors: 
•As theta approaches 90 degrees, the full transverse 
sinusoid appears (wave). 
•As theta approaches 0 degrees, transverse amplitude 
vanishes (particle). 
Duality emerges purely from geometry.

3. Statement of the Furner Projection Duality 
Theorem 
Theorem: 
Wave-like and particle-like signatures of light arise from 
different two-dimensional projections of a single three-
dimensional helical photon trajectory. Rotating only the 
detection screen about the propagation axis induces a 
measurable axial phase translation proportional to 
cosine(theta), which cannot occur in Maxwell optics. 
Corollary (Fringe Drift): 
Delta phi(theta) = (2 pi r / wavelength) times cos(theta) 
This prediction is independent of polarization, slit geometry, 
and beam shaping.

4. Experimental Prediction: Cosine-Theta 
Fringe Drift 
Rotating the detector by an angle theta yields: 
Delta phi(theta) = constant times cos(theta) 
Standard Maxwell optics predicts: 
Derivative of phi with respect to theta = 0 
Maxwell allows fringe rotation but not axial translation. 
Any non-zero fringe drift is direct evidence of a helical 
photon trajectory. 
5. Maxwell Optics Baseline

===== PAGE 7 =====
Standard scalar diffraction gives sinusoidal fringes 
determined by slit spacing, wavelength, and distance. Under 
rotation of the screen: 
•Only the coordinate grid rotates 
•Fringes do not shift axially 
•No phase term depends on theta 
Spin–orbit coupling requires physical rotation of optical 
elements inside the beam path, not just the detector plane. 
6. Structured Light and Spin–Orbit Contrast 
Laguerre-Gaussian beams exhibit helical phase but travel 
linearly. 
Spin–orbit coupling involves geometric phases but operates 
through polarization and field manipulation.

The Furner theorem differs fundamentally:

not phase-front shaping. 
7. Fourier-Space Interpretation 
Projected transverse motion: 
x_theta(t) = r sin(omega t) sin(theta) 
Its Fourier spectrum compresses as theta approaches zero. 
When transverse spatial frequencies fall below detector 
resolution, the signal becomes discrete and particle-like. This 
reproduction of particle signatures does not require wave-
function collapse; it arises from projection and sampling.

Collapse 
Detectors record photons as discrete events because they

follow from projected intersections, aligning with the Born 
rule without invoking a physical collapse.
