Abstract
Physical singularities—such as those in general relativity and quantum field theory have long
been taken as indicators of theoretical breakdowns. This paper examines analytic contin-
uation and zeta regularization as mathematical techniques that stabilize such divergences
by extending physical quantities into domains of well-defined meaning. It argues that this
process exemplifies a deeper ontological stabilization of physical theory: where structure,
rather than substance, carries the explanatory weight. Drawing on the framework of Ontic
Structural Realism (OSR), the paper contends that zeta-regularized quantities, including the
analytically continued spectral zeta functionζA(s) of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, model
an ontology in which mathematical continuation restores coherence to otherwise ill-defined
regions of theory. Thus, mathematical regularization is not merely a calculational device
but a structural act revealing how the formal architecture of mathematics stabilizes physical
ontology at the limits of knowledge.
Keywords: analytic continuation, zeta regularization, singularities, Ontic Structural
Realism, spectral geometry, mathematical ontology
1. Introduction
The use of analytic continuation and zeta regularization in modern physics extends far
beyond technical renormalization. At its core lies a metaphysical insight: singularities may
mark the limits of description, not the limits of reality. When a divergent quantity is contin-
ued analytically, the mathematics does not erase the singularity but re-expresses it within a
broader structural framework. This paper argues that such stabilization through structure
embodies the central tenet of Ontic Structural Realism (OSR): that the world’s fundamental
ontology consists in relational structure rather than in individual entities.

3. Ontic Structural Realism and Analytic Ontology
Ontic Structural Realism (OSR) asserts that structure is ontologically primary. Ladyman
(1998) and Rickles (2008) advanced this position within the philosophy of physics, arguing
that entities are merely nodes in a web of relations described by mathematical form. Within
thisview, analyticcontinuationexemplifieshowmathematicalrelations, notintrinsicobjects,
determine the persistence of ontology through theoretical transformation.
Regularizationthus becomesametaphysicalact ofstructuralpreservation: itensures that

an expression of relational persistence across formal discontinuities. In this sense, analytic
continuation embodies the OSR principle that reality is defined by invariant structure rather
than by the specific numerical values of divergent quantities.

5. Conclusion and Philosophical Synthesis
This study has argued that analytic continuation and zeta regularization embody a meta-
physical principle of ontological stabilization. The mathematical act of continuing a diver-
gent function mirrors the philosophical act of extending ontology beyond apparent limits.
Within the OSR framework, zeta regularization becomes a paradigm of structural realism:
mathematics reveals the continuity of reality through its own analytic coherence.
Three contributions follow. First, the framework provides a structural resolution of sin-
gularities, treating them as analytic boundaries. Second, it reinterprets vacuum energy and
the cosmological constant as finite structural invariants. Third, it unites the metaphysical
stance of OSR with the formal machinery of regularization, showing that structural relations
constitute the enduring core of physical ontology.
Future work may extend this analytic-ontological framework to black-hole singularities,
quantum-gravity regularization, or other dualities where analytic structure mediates between
divergent regimes. The broader implication is that the mathematics of continuation reveals
a deep principle of reality’s resilience: existence is secured not by substance, but by the
analytic unity of structure.
