ABSTRACT
The precise determination of the fundamental parameters of stars is crucial for understanding stellar structure and evolution.
In this regard, the asteroseismic parameters of solar-like oscillating stars obtained from space telescopes are very useful for
determiningthemass(𝑀)andradius(𝑅)ofsinglestarsusingclassicalscalingrelations.Theserelationsstillneedtobeimproved.
Inthisstudy,wedevelopalternativescalingrelationsbasedonreferencefrequencies(𝜈 min)duetoheliumionizationzoneglitches,
specificallyforsubgiant(SG)stars.Wecompilemain-sequenceandSGMESAmodelsfromtheliteratureandderivenewscaling
relations for stellar𝑅and surface gravity (𝑔) of evolved stars. The expressions for𝑅and𝑔are obtained simultaneously as
functions of the large frequency separation,𝜈min, and metallicity. These new relations allow𝑀and𝑅to be reliably determined
only from observational parameters, without the need for detailed stellar modelling. The resulting𝜈min-based relations are
applied to a sample of 31 SG stars observed by theKeplermission, with masses in the range 0.85–1.74 M⊙ and radii between
1.03 and 2.82 R⊙. Using simultaneous solutions, we estimate new mass and radius ranges of 0.89–1.62 M⊙ and 1.04–2.95 R⊙,
respectively. The results demonstrate significant improvements in the determination of𝑀and𝑅for evolvedKeplertarget stars,
highlighting the potential of the new scaling relations in asteroseismic analyses.
Key words:
asteroseismology – stars: evolution – stars: fundamental parameters – stars: interiors – stars: oscillations – stars: solar-type.

5.3 Comparison with detailed seismic modelling
To more thoroughly evaluate the reliability of the reference
frequency-based approach, the results obtained in this study are
compared with previous studies in which masses and radii were
calculated. In Li et al. (2020) and Silva Aguirre et al. (2017), stel-
lar masses and radii were derived using the detailed asteroseismic
modelling approach, in which individual oscillation frequencies are
fitted to theoretical models. Of the subgiant stars analysed in Li et
al. (2020), nine are also investigated in the present study. Fig. 10
compares the masses and radii derived here with those reported by
Li et al. (2020) for these nine stars, while the calculated masses and
radii are listed in Table 3.
Theradiicomputedinthisstudyareingoodagreementwiththose
reportedbyLietal.(2020);forsixstars(KIC5955122,KIC7976303,
KIC 8702606, KIC 11395018, KIC 11414712, and KIC 12508433),
the derived values fall within the specified uncertainty ranges. For
two stars (KIC 8524425 and KIC 10920273), the masses and radii
lie slightly outside these uncertainties. The mass and radius of KIC
8524425 reported by Li et al. (2020) are0.97±0.04M⊙ and1.71±
0.03R⊙,respectively.Inthisstudy,themassandradiusderivedfrom
𝜈min1 are1.09±0.02M ⊙ and1.83±0.02R ⊙, respectively. For KIC
10018963, the mass and radius derived using logarithmic method
based on𝜈 min1 are identical to those obtained in that study. These
results indicate that subgiant stellar radii estimated using reference
frequenciesyieldresultsthatareconsistentwiththoseobtainedfrom
seismic modelling, despite differences in methodology.
In this study, the mass range obtained for these nine stars using
𝜈min0 and𝜈 min1 (1.09–1.21 M⊙) is narrower than that reported by
Li et al. (2020) (0.94–1.27 M⊙). This difference is likely driven by
variations in the adopted observational parameters, particularly the
metallicity values used in the scaling relations. To test this effect, a
small sample of stars was re-analysed using the [Fe/H] values taken
from Li et al. (2020), and the resulting masses were found to be in
better agreement with those of Li et al. (2020).
6 CONCLUSIONS
Determining the masses and radii of stars with high precision is
crucial for understanding the physical processes occurring in their
interiors.Byusingtheoscillationfrequenciesofsolar-likeoscillating
stars, highly accurate solutions for mass and radius can be obtained.
Inthisstudy,weanalysetheoscillationfrequenciesofstellarmodels
obtained using theMESAevolution code and derive new scaling
relations for𝑀and𝑅based on reference frequencies.
We first derived simultaneous three-parameter solutions for𝑔and
𝑅usingMESAMS and SG models, based on𝜈 min,Δ𝜈, and𝑍 mod.
The differences between the derived scaling-relation values and the
corresponding model values are less than 1 per cent for the radius
and3percentforthemassinmostmodels,indicatingahighconsis-
tency.Themasswassubsequentlycalculatedusingthederived𝑔and
𝑅values. In particular, this study presents, for the first time, radius
and mass scaling relations based on𝜈min for SG models. Further-
more,theserelationsallow𝑀and𝑅tobereliablydeterminedusing
only observational parameters, without the need for detailed stellar
modelling.
The new scaling relations are applied to theKeplertarget stars.
The seismic solutions based on𝜈min show excellent agreement with
theKeplerdata. These findings demonstrate that the fundamental
parameters of evolved solar-like stars can be reliably determined

APPENDIX A: TEST OF MODEL DEPENDENCE USING
CESAMMODELS
Toexplicitlytestthemodeldependenceofthecalibratedscalingrela-
tions, we applied theMESA-calibrated relations to the asteroseismic
parameters of theCESAMmodels. TheCESAMseismic parameters
computed in this study and used in the scaling relations are listed
in Tables A1 and A2. The resulting radii and masses were directly
compared with the trueCESAMmodel values, as shown in Figs A1
and A2.
Regardingtheradiusrelations,theradiiobtainedusingboth𝜈 min0
(𝑅log,0) and𝜈 min1 (𝑅log,1) are systematically slightly larger than the
CESAMmodel radii. When all models are considered together, the
meanradiusdifferenceisapproximately2percent.Theresidualpan-
els show that the differences remain nearly constant across the ex-
aminedradiusrange,thusindicatingnosignificantradius-dependent
trend. The radius comparisons clearly show improved agreement for
the1.1M ⊙ models.AsseeninFig.A1(a),themeanradiusdifference
is1.26percentforthe1.1M ⊙ models,whileitreaches3.05percent
forthe1.0M ⊙ models.Thisindicatesthattheradiusscalingrelation
performs more consistently and appears better calibrated in the 1.1
M⊙ mass regime. A similar trend is observed in Fig. A1(b) for the
𝑅log,1 relation, where the 1.1 M⊙ models show smaller systematic
offsets and lower dispersion.
Forthemasses,theagreementwasexaminedconsideringthelim-
itedmassrangeoftheCESAMmodels(1.0–1.1M ⊙).Whenallmodels
are considered together, the mean mass difference is∼7 per cent,
with an internal scatter of 1-3 per cent. There is a significant differ-
ence compared to the∼3 per cent mass discrepancy obtained when
using models generated with the same evolutionary code. This sug-
gests that the discrepancy may arise from hidden dependencies on
the physical assumptions adopted in the construction of the stellar
models used to calibrate the scaling relations. Similar behaviour has
been reported by Valle et al. (2025), especially for mass estimates in
model-based corrections to classical scaling relations.
In general, these results indicate that the scaling relations cali-
brated using theMESAmodels can be successfully applied to in-
dependentCESAMmodels. Within the explored range, the radius
estimates show no strong dependence on the adopted stellar evo-
lution code, whereas the mass estimates exhibit larger systematic
differences. This suggests that the model dependence of the masses
should be investigated in more detail in future studies.
