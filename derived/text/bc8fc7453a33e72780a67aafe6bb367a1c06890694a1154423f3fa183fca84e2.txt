THE GEOMETRY AND DYNAMICS OF SPIRAL MINIMAL PRODUCTS
HAIZHONG LI AND YONGSHENG ZHANG
Abstract. We study spiral productsGγ(t,x,y) = (z 1(t)f1(x),z 2(t)f2(y)), formed from spheri-
cal C-totally real immersionsfi :M ki
i →S 2ni+1, withk1 +k2 >0 , and a profileγ= (z 1,z 2) in S3.
The product is minimal precisely when the factors are minimal andγ is, after reparametrization,
a geodesic of¯g=|z1|2k1|z2|2k2gS3 wherever ¯gis positive definite. The profile flow is Liouville
integrable. On every regular two-turning component of the doubly spiral parameter domain, the
complete-cell phase map is real analytic with open dense full-rank locus; hence ordinary-closing
profiles of arbitrarily large primitive order are dense. At fixed regular nonzero momentum, an
exact Routh square completion reduces the profile Hessian to a scalar Sturm form and two
nonnegative squares. For compact minimal inputs and an ordinarily closed profile of primitive
orderm γ, the full-period immersion satisfiesInd(Gγ)≥Ind(f 1) + Ind(f2) + 2mγ−3.
At the contact momentum level, factor-adapted selection produces, from any prescribed pair
of compact connected embedded special Legendrians, compact embedded special Legendrian
products of every sufficiently large prime closing order. Their second fundamental forms are
uniformly bounded, while volume and normal Morse index grow at least linearly with the
order. Real spherical minimal embeddings yield analogous families. Applied to finite-holonomy
horizontal lifts, the contact construction gives Delaunay-type minimal Lagrangian immersions
in complex projective spaces. For canonical lifts of compact embedded inputs and a regular
ordinarily closed contact profile, the primitive spherical quotient is embedded, while its Hopf
quotient is embedded exactly when the reduced relative winding number is1; the latter satisfies
a corresponding additive Hamiltonian-index bound.

A spiral product couples two spherical immersions through a profile curve inS3. For minimal
C-totally real inputs, minimality reduces to a weighted geodesic equation, while ordinary return,
quotient return, Morse index, and embeddedness remain distinct global problems. Theorems A–C
treat the reduced dynamics, dense closing, and Routh–Sturm index growth. On the contact
slice, Theorem D constructs compact embedded special Legendrian products in spheres, whereas
Theorem E separates spherical from projective embeddedness and identifies the exact obstruction
after Hopf projection.

Castro–Urbano initiated product construction for producing special Lagrangian immersions
in complex Euclidean space, from two minimal Legendrian factors and a suitable Lagrangian
surface [8]. In spheres, Castro–Li–Urbano then formulated the Legendrian construction in
form (0.4) from Legendrian factors and a Legendre profile inS3. They derived the profile
equation governing contact-stationary Legendrian products and isolated its minimal, or special
Legendrian, specialization; Hopf projection and coning yielded Hamiltonian-minimal and minimal
Lagrangians in complex space forms, as well as Hamiltonian-minimal and special Lagrangian
cones in complex Euclidean space [9]. Haskins–Kapouleas subsequently developed the profile
equation into the twisted-product construction, analyzed its closing and obtained embedded
special Legendrian examples with equatorial factor inputs [22]. We place this contact sector inside
the full two-momentum weighted-geodesic system. The present work adds the generic full-rank
and dense-closing theorem beyond the contact slice, the Routh–Sturm index bounds, factor-
adapted embeddedness for arbitrary prescribed embedded factors, and, after Hopf projection,
the reduced-winding criterion and Hamiltonian-index inheritance.

On the contact sliceµ1 +µ2 = 0, equivalently C1 =−1in the chart µ1̸= 0, the profile
is horizontal—equivalently, Legendrian inS3—so a spiral product ofC-totally real factors is
again C-totally real; in maximal dimension, it is Legendrian. A connected minimal Legen-
drian is automatically orientable and, after a constant phase rotation, special Legendrian [9,
Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 2.2]; its cone is then special Lagrangian [19].
Special Lagrangian cones provide natural tangent-cone models for singular fibers in the
Strominger–Yau–Zaslow picture [42]. Classical embedded links include the Harvey–Lawson
tori [19], Joyce’sU(1)m−2-invariant examples [25], and Haskins’sS1-invariant tori inS5 [20].
Heller–Pedit–Ouyang recently proposed a way to construct compact embedded special Legendrian
surfaces in S5 with unbounded genus [23]. At the immersed level, integrable systems and spectral
curves yield special Legendrian tori [32, 7], while Haskins–Kapouleas constructed higher-genus
links by gluing [21].
Embeddedness is important in applications requiring regular cones or multiplicity-one tangent
cones with smooth links, including the regularity, uniqueness, and bridge results of [26, 11, 13].
Example 6.14 exposes a genuine global obstruction: the spiral-product construction need not
preserve embeddedness, even when both factors are embedded special Legendrians and the contact
