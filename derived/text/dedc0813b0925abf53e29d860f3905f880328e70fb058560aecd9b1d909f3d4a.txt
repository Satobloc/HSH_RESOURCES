Abstract
A new family of maximal supergravities in four dimensions, involving gaugings
of the trombone scaling symmetry, has been recently introduced. Using excep-
tional generalised geometry, we show some supergravities in this class to arise
by consistent truncation ofD= 11 supergravity. The seven-dimensional reduc-
tion manifold is locally equivalent to the topologically-twisted internal manifold
of the AdS 4 geometries that arise near the horizon of M5-branes wrapped on
supersymmetric three-cycles of special holonomy manifolds. The dimensional
reduction involves a mixture of conventional and generalised Scherk-Schwarz
prescriptions, and provides the first maximally supersymmetric consistent trun-
cation to four dimensions in the context of the M5-brane.

1 Introduction
In [1], we constructed a family of gaugings of maximal supergravity in four dimensions
[2, 3, 4, 5] that can be regarded as a generalisation of specific dyonic CSO gaugings of
the type discussed in [6, 7]. The gauge groups, TCSO(p,q,r; N), of these supergravities
contain CSO(p,q,r)≡SO(p,q)⋉ R (p+q)r, withp+q+r= 5, and a three-dimensional
groupG 3 of Bianchi type N, as subgroups. In a convenient duality frame, SO(p,q) is
gauged electrically,G 3 magnetically, andR (p+q)r dyonically. IfG 3 is unimodular, the
TCSO(p,q,r; N) theories simply reduce to concrete gaugings in the class of [6, 7]. For non-
unimodularG 3, the TCSO(p,q,r; N) theories are new, and involve a gauging of theR +
trombone scaling symmetry of ungaugedD= 4N= 8 supergravity, along with a gauging
of a subgroup of the ungauged duality group, E 7(7). These TCSO(p,q,r; N) theories are
the four-dimensional counterparts of a similar class ofD= 5N= 8 gauged supergravities
recently introduced in [8, 9].
It was also argued in [1] that a specific theory in this class, TCSO(5,0,0; V), should
be related to configurations of M5-branes wrapped on certain supersymmetric three-
dimensional cycles, Σ3, of special holonomy manifolds, of the type discussed in [10, 11] fol-
lowing [12], see [13] for a review. The submanifold Σ3 is either a special lagrangian (SLAG)
three-cycle or an associative three-cycle (A3C), depending on whether the special holon-
omy manifold the M5-branes are put in is a Calabi-Yau three-fold or a seven-dimensional

manifold of G2-holonomy [10, 11]. Also, Σ 3 should be endowed with an Einstein metric of
negative curvature so that Σ3 is identified with the hyperboloidH 3, possibly compactified,
H3/Γ, with a discrete group of isometries Γ. The construction of [10, 11] can be extended
to let Σ 3 still have an Einstein metric, but of positive or vanishing curvature, thereby
identifying Σ3 with the three-sphere,S 3, or the three-torus,T 3. A large numberNof M5-
branes in this configuration develops a supersymmetric anti-de Sitter, AdS 4×(Σ 3 ⋊S 4),
throat near its horizon that solves, for Σ 3 =H 3 [10, 11], the field equations ofD= 11
supergravity [14]. Here, we have used the symbol⋊to denote that the four-sphereS 4 is
fibred over Σ3, as a consequence of supersymmetry being implemented via the topological
twist of [15, 16].
Three-fold evidence supports the expected relation [1] ofD= 4N= 8 TCSO(5,0,0; V)
supergravity to the AdS4×(Σ 3 ⋊S 4) solutions of [10, 11]. Firstly, the SO(5) and the type
VG 3 subgroups of the gauge group should respectively originate from theS 4 and the
Σ3 ∼H 3 submanifolds of the internal eleven-dimensional geometry AdS 4×(Σ 3 ⋊S 4)
(recall thatH 3 is locally diffeomorphic to the groupG 3 of Bianchi type V). Secondly, the
TCSO(5,0,0; V) supergravity was shown to admit two supersymmetric AdS vacua with the
right symmetries, supersymmetries and linearised mass spectra to potentially uplift to the
two eleven-dimensional backgrounds AdS4×(Σ3⋊S4) of [10, 11]. Finally, some submaximal
D= 4 supergravity models [17, 18] were previously known to arise from fully non-linear
consistent truncation ofD= 11 supergravity on the seven-dimensional manifolds Σ 3 ⋊S 4.
These submaximal four-dimensional supergravities [17, 18] can be retrieved [1] as consistent
subsectors ofD= 4N= 8 TCSO(5,0,0; V)-gauged supergravity.
This logic provides a strong smoking gun that the entireD= 4N= 8 (tromboneful)
TCSO(5,0,0; V) supergravity locally arises from fully non-linear consistent truncation of
D= 11 supergravity [14] on the seven-dimensional M5-brane geometries Σ3 ⋊S 4 of [10, 11],
with Σ3 =H 3. This is indeed the case. In fact, we first found ourD= 4 supergravity by
consistent truncation and, in this paper, we will provide a full account of this construction.
Similarly, we will recover the (tromboneless) TCSO(5,0,0; IX) and TCSO(5,0,0; I) theories
as consistent truncations ofD= 11 supergravity on the seven-dimensional manifolds
Σ3 ⋊S 4 with, respectively, Σ 3 =S 3 and Σ 3 =T 3. More generally, we will show that
the subfamily ofD= 4N= 8 supergravities with gauge group TCSO(5,0,0; N), with
subgroupG 3 of arbitrary Bianchi type N, arises fromD= 11 consistent truncation on
G3 ⋊S 4 where the fibration ofS 4 onG 3 is as in the Σ 3 ⋊S 4 cases of [10, 11]. This
truncation goes through consistently even ifG 3 does not generically admit an Einstein
metric. In any case, our construction provides the firstD= 11 truncation toD= 4N= 8
gauged supergravity in the context of the M5-brane.
In the previous discussion, as well as in the main text, we have been rather cavalier
in leisurely exchanging the compact Riemann surface Σ 3 that theD= 11 M5-branes
wrap, with the group manifoldG 3. This is because the local construction in either case is
identical. Global aspects, however, are important, particularly in the physically relevant
case of compact hyperbolic wrapped cycle, Σ 3 =H 3/Γ, the case with (previously known
[10, 11]) AdS 4 solutions. In this case, the Σ 3 =H 3/Γ and the Bianchi type V groupG 3
are still locally identical, but they differ globally: the former is compact while the latter,
2

===== PAGE 4 =====
being non-unimodular, is not. This means that ourD= 4N= 8 truncations on Σ 3 ⋊S 4
only hold locally, and only extend to bonafide global constructions within the supergravity
subsectors that do not induce deformations of Σ 3. As we will see, these are the specific
subsectors ofD= 4N= 8 TCSO(5,0,0; V) supergravity discussed in [1], which were
previously obtained [17, 18] from straight reduction ofD= 11 without passing through
the intermediateD= 4N= 8 supergravity.
We will build our new consistent truncations using exceptional generalised geometry
(ExGG) [19, 20, 21]. Like similar duality-covariant reformulations [22, 23, 24], see [25] for
a review, of the higher-dimensional supergravities, ExGG makes manifest theR +×E 7(7)
global symmetry of (ungauged)D= 4N= 8 supergravity already in eleven-dimensions.
While the traditional construction of consistent truncations is a famously intricate and
obscure problem, this is greatly streamlined in ExGG [26, 27, 28] by suitably adapting
techniques from the conventional approach to supergravity truncations, including Scherk-
Schwarz [29] andG-structure methods [30]. In the ExGG language, consistent truncations
are associated to the existence of generalisedG-structures [27, 31], see also [32, 33]. In
particular, a consistent truncation fromD= 11 toD= 4N= 8 supergravity requires the
internal seven-dimensional manifold to be equipped with a generalised identity structure
(GIS), whose constant instrinsic torsion is identified with the embedding tensor of the
maximal four-dimensional supergravity [27, 31].
We will begin by reviewing the family ofD= 4N= 8 TCSO(p,q,r; N) supergravities
[1] in section 2, in order to set up conventions. Section 3 contains our central result:
the construction in ExGG of a GIS onG 3 ⋊S 4 with constant intrinsic torsion given, as
expected, by the embedding tensor of the four-dimensional TCSO(5,0,0; N) theory. In
order to do this, we first build a GIS on the direct product manifoldG 3×S 4, and then
adapt a twisting prescription given in [31] to obtain the GIS onG 3 ⋊S 4 for the two
fibrations associated to the two different, SLAG and A3C, AdS 4×(Σ 3 ⋊S 4) M5-brane
solutions of [10, 11]. These results not only establish the consistency of the truncation of
D= 11 supergravity either onG 3×S 4 or on the physically more relevant fibred manifolds
G3 ⋊S 4. They also imply, interestingly, that the resulting truncatedD= 4N= 8 theory
is the same, TCSO(5,0,0; N), in all cases. As we will also explain in section 3, this happens
because this type of ExGG twists [31] turn out to be generated, at least in our context and
certainly not in an obvious way, by scalars of theD= 4N= 8 supergravity. In section
4 we particularise our generalN= 8 truncation to the specific supergravity subsectors
considered in [1], in order to make contact with the submaximal truncations of [17, 18].
Finally, section 5 concludes with further discussion.

2.1 Field content and embedding tensor
We work in the embedding tensor formalism [3, 4, 5], see [34] for a review, where bosons and
fermions formally lie in representations ofR +×E 7(7) andR +×SU(8), respectively. Here,
R+ is the trombone scaling symmetry, E7(7) is the duality symmetry group of the ungauged
theory [2], and SU(8) its maximal compact subgroup. We will focus on the bosonic sector.
The metric,g µν, gauge fields,A µM, and two-form potentials,B µνα , respectively sit in
the1 2,56 1 and133 2 ofR +×E 7(7), while the scalars parametrise a coset representative,
VMN, of E 7(7)/SU(8), with metricM MN = (VV T)MN. We use indicesµ= 0,1,2,3,
M = 1,...,56 andα= 1,...,133 to label spacetime vectors, and the fundamental and
adjoint representations of E 7(7). The latter indices are respectively raised and lowered
with the symplectic form, Ω MN, of Sp(56) and the Killing-Cartan form,κ αβ, of E 7(7).
While manifestlyR +×E 7(7) covariant, the above field content is redundant at face value.
Duality relations must be imposed that ensure the propagation of only 128 bosonic (and
128 fermionic) degrees of freedom [3, 4, 5].

gauged theory, the (local) symmetry is typically reduced to a subgroup of the former. In

3 Eleven-dimensional origin
In this section, we will show thatD= 4N= 8 TCSO(5,0,0; N)-gauged supergravity
locally arises by consistent truncation ofD= 11 supergravity onG 3 ⋊S 4, where the
fibrations ofS 4 onG 3 are as in the M5-brane solutions of [10, 11]. Prior to doing that, we
will review some aspects of the E 7(7) ExGG formulation ofD= 11 supergravity in order
to fix our conventions, and we will construct an auxiliary truncation on the direct product
manifoldG 3×S 4.
3.1E 7(7) exceptional generalised geometry for M-theory
Recall that the bosonic field content ofD= 11 supergravity [14] contains the metric,
g11, along with a three-form,A, and a six-form potential, ˜A. The forms are on-shell
related by Hodge duality in order to ensure the propagation of 128 bosonic degrees of

3.4 Equivalence of the direct product and twisted truncations
As reviewed in section 2.2, the twoD= 4N= 8 gauged supergravities encountered by the
twisted consistent truncations of section 3.3 are related by duality transformations between
each other. They are also duality-related to the truncatedD= 4N= 8 theory obtained
in section 3.2. All three truncated theories are therefore equivalent. In conclusion,D= 11
supergravity admits consistent truncations on the direct product manifoldG 3×S 4, and
on the SLAG and A3C fibred manifoldsG 3 ⋊S 4, down to one and the same maximal

5 Discussion
We have constructed new maximally supersymmetric consistent truncations ofD= 11 su-
pergravity to four dimensions. The internal seven-dimensional reduction manifold, Σ3⋊S4,
arises near the AdS 4 horizon of the supersymmetric wrapped M5-brane configurations of
[10, 11]. The M5-branes are wrapped on the SLAG or A3C cycle Σ 3, which is in turn
embedded into a Calabi-Yau three-fold or a G 2-holonomy seven-dimensional manifold. In
the construction of [10, 11], Σ 3 needs to be diffeomorphic toS 3,T 3 orH 3, possibly com-
pactified with a discrete group of isometries in the latter case. Moreover, for each of these
three choices, Σ 3 must be equipped with the usual Einstein metric of positive, vanishing,
or negative curvature. These three cases are diffeomorphic to the three-dimensional group
manifoldsG 3 of Bianchi types IX, I and V. Our local truncation proceeds consistently for
any fibred manifoldG 3 ⋊S 4, withS 4 fibred over the groupG 3 through the same twists
18

===== PAGE 20 =====
as [10, 11]. For fixedG 3 of Bianchi type N, the resultingD= 4N= 8 theory is the
TCSO(5,0,0; N) supergravity that we recently introduced in [1].
Only for Σ3 =H 3 do the SLAG and A3C supersymmetric AdS 4×(Σ 3 ⋊S 4) configura-
tions of [10, 11] solve the equations of motion ofD= 11 supergravity. Accordingly,D= 4
N= 8 TCSO(5,0,0; V)-gauged supergravity admitsN= 2 andN= 1 AdS vacua [1] that
uplift to the eleven-dimensional solutions of [10, 11]. In any case, ourD= 11 truncations
onG 3 ⋊S 4 are consistent for anyG 3. This means that any solution ofD= 4N= 8
TCSO(5,0,0; N)-gauged supergravity will give rise to aD= 11 solution. For this reason,
it would be interesting to scan for supersymmetric vacua of the TCSO(5,0,0; N) models
by employing the methods of [39, 40, 41]. Using our results, these vacua could be uplifted
toD= 11 supergravity, thus providing new M-theory backgrounds. In any case, our con-
struction provides a generalisation of [10, 11] in that Σ 3 there needs to be equipped with
an Einstein metric. In our case, Σ 3 can be locally taken to be any group manifold, thus of
constant curvature but not necessarily Einstein. Note however, that for non-unimodular
G3, the reduction manifoldG 3 ⋊S 4 will be non-compact. In particular, the Bianchi type
V truncation is only defined locally for Σ 3 ⋊S 4 with Σ3 =H 3. A different generalisation
of [10, 11] has also been given recently in [42, 43], also in the context of the M5-brane. The
latter construction also features an internal space of the form Σ 3 ⋊S 4, but the fibration
is no longer given by the usual topological twist of [15, 16]. The wrapped manifold Σ 3 in
this case no longer has constant curvature, and features orbifold singularities. It would be
interesting to see if our consistent truncations can be extended to these cases.
The configurations AdS 4×(Σ 3 ⋊S 4) of [10, 11] were originally obtained as solutions
of seven-dimensional gauged supergravity, and then uplifted onS 4 toD= 11 using the
consistent truncation of [44, 45]. It should thus be possible to obtain theD= 4N= 8
TCSO(5,0,0; N) supergravities from Scherk-Schwarz reduction ofD= 7 SO(5)-gauged
supergravity on the group manifoldG 3. Alternatively,D= 11 supergravity can be also
Scherk-Schwarz reduced onG 3 to eight-dimensional supergravity [46, 47]. It would be
interesting to determine if the resultingD= 8 supergravity can be further reduced consis-
tently onS 4 down toD= 4N= 8 TCSO(5,0,0; N)-gauged supergravity. More generally,
it would be interesting to determine the higher-dimensional origin of the entire family of
TCSO(p,q,r; N) supergravities of [1]. This should proceed by replacingS 4 with a suit-
able hyperboloid, as in [48, 49]. In at least two situations this is straightforward with the
results that are already available. Firstly, forp+q= 5,r= 0 and arbitraryG 3, the
truncation simply proceeds as theS 4 truncation discussed here, withS 4 replaced with a
four-dimensional hyperboloidH p+q−1, see appendix B. Secondly, for generalp,q,rwith
p+q+r= 5 and unimodularG 3, the gauging TCSO(p,q,r; N) reduces to a specific type
included in the dyonic CSO class of [6, 7]. The latter were identified in [50] as descending
from type IIA, or possiblyD= 11 like in our non-unimodular cases.
The duality-covariant reformulations [19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24], like the ExGG formal-
ism that we have used in this paper, of the higher-dimensional supergravities provide
extremely powerful venues to address consistent truncations. Classification results exist in
this context [28, 51] for maximally supersymmetric truncations of the higher-dimensional
supergravities. Yet, finding concrete examples of manifolds in those classifications that
19

===== PAGE 21 =====
admit a constant intrinsic torsion GIS remains a challenging problem. In particular, the
known maximal consistent truncations that are directly associated to holographic brane
configurations of string/M-theory are still few and far between. Consistent truncations to
D= 4N= 8 supergravities were previously known related to the M2, D2 and D3-brane
[52, 27, 36, 53, 54, 55, 56, 50]. Our construction provides the firstD= 4N= 8 consistent
truncation in the context of the M5-brane.
