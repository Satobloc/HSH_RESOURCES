A Spectral Criterion for Emergent Gravity
Jinku Guo a
aNorthwestern Polytechnical University, Xi’an 710072, China

Abstract:This paper formulates a spectral criterion for emergent gravity from the
energy–momentum tensor commutator. The Weinberg–Witten theorem excludes an el-
ementary massless spin-2 particle in a Poincar´ e-invariant quantum field theory with a
covariant conserved stress tensor. A massless spin-2 excitation, if it appears in the stress-
tensor spectral function, must therefore be interpreted as a collective mode rather than
as a fundamental field. Since the spectral function is defined by a commutator, additive
static vacuum-energy terms do not contribute to the dynamical spin-2 spectral density.
Coarse-graining in momentum space constructs, at each scale, a macroscopic rank-2 ten-
sor field. The irreversibility of this operation promotes the scale itself to a dynamical
order parameter. Its Langevin dynamics is governed by two renormalization-group fixed
points, an ultraviolet repellor at the Planck scale and an infrared attractor at the Hub-
ble scale. Entropy production and energy transfer define an effective temperature, and
the fluctuation–dissipation theorem relates the corresponding noise and dissipation. The
criterion advanced in this paper is whether the spin-2 spectral density can develop an iso-
lated zero-momentum pole. Such a pole is absent in perturbation theory and represents
a nonperturbative reorganization of spectral weight. If the pole is present, Weinberg’s
low-energy theorem fixes the infrared effective action to Einstein–Hilbert form. Newton’s
constant is then read from the pole residue, while the infrared fixed point supplies the
natural curvature scale. The criterion is in principle testable. Lattice gauge theory or
functional renormalization group methods can decide whether the spin-2 pole forms.
Keywords:Emergent gravity; Spectral function; Spin-2 polarization; Langevin equation;
Renormalization group fixed point; Fluctuation–dissipation theorem; Coarse-graining

1 Introduction
The spectral representation of two-point correlation functions in the vacuum, the K¨ all´ en-
Lehmann representation [1], can be decomposed by spin into distinct polarization sectors.
These sectors contain spin-2, spin-1, and spin-0 projection operators [2]. In the Standard
Model, this spectral analysis framework is applied separately to quantum fields of different
spin. The spectral function of gauge fields describes spin-1 propagation, and that of fermion
fields describes spin-1/2 propagation. The energy–momentum tensor is a rank-2 symmetric
tensor operator in quantum field theory. It is the conserved Noether current of spacetime
translation symmetry. Its transversality condition eliminates the spin-1 sector, leaving
only spin-2 and spin-0 contributions [3]. In the spectral function of the energy–momentum
tensor, the spin-2 channel is the lowest nontrivial excitation channel that remains unsup-
pressed.
Several quantum field theory effects can be understood as modulations of the vacuum
fluctuation spectral function under specific conditions. The Casimir effect originates from
mode truncation of the electromagnetic vacuum fluctuation vector spectral function under
boundary conditions [4, 5]. In electroweak theory, originally massless gauge and fermion
fields acquire mass through coupling to vacuum condensates. The spectral function trans-
forms from a massless continuum to a form with mass-threshold structure [6]. In QCD, the
quark condensate signals spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking [7]. Viewed in terms of
the spectral function, this corresponds to a nonperturbative reshaping of quark and gluon
spectral functions [8, 9]. In Hawking radiation, the horizon imposes absorbing boundary

conditions on quantum field modes. From the perspective of an external observer, the
Minkowski vacuum spectral function turns into a thermal spectral form [10, 11].
These examples involve modulations of the spectral function by external conditions.
Even without special conditions such as boundaries or condensates, the vacuum spectral
function itself carries structural information about the fundamental interactions. If we ex-
amine the fundamental interactions themselves from a similar perspective, their coupling
effects should be regarded as encoded in the structure of the vacuum spectral function,
rather than arising from specific external physical conditions that modulate it. This dis-
tinction is central to what follows. If the gravitational interaction is encoded in the spectral
function, the question becomes what specific structure inρ (2) signals its presence.
In quantum electrodynamics, the spectral function of the photon field two-point cor-
relation function fully encodes the effects of the electromagnetic interaction. The free-field
contribution appears as aδ(p 2)-type term, while interaction corrections manifest as a con-
tinuousp 2-dependent component superimposed upon it. The strength of this component is
proportional to the fine-structure constantα[12]. The photon spectral function captures
the complete physical content of a fundamental massless spin-1 particle. Its form is deter-
mined by the structure of the electromagnetic coupling, and the behavior of its propagator
at all momentum scales is characterized by this spectral function. In QCD, the infrared
enhancement of the strong coupling leads to nonperturbative spectral-weight accumulation
in quark and gluon spectral functions at low energies, a phenomenon indicated by lattice
QCD and Dyson–Schwinger equation calculations [13, 14]. In electroweak theory, gauge
and Yukawa couplings determine the positions of mass thresholds, while mixing matrices
further fix the coupling strengths of different mass eigenstates within the spectral func-
tion. The coupling constants of the fundamental interactions determine the structure of
each polarization channel in the spectral function and its dependence on mass parameters.
The spectral function therefore carries the complete information of the interaction on the
two-point propagator.
Following this line of reasoning, could the gravitational interaction likewise be at-
tributed to a modulation of the energy–momentum tensor vacuum fluctuation spectral
function under certain conditions? If a massless spin-2 pole were to appear in the spectral
function, the most direct interpretation would be the existence of a fundamental mass-
less spin-2 field, just as theδ-peak in the photon spectral function corresponds to the
fundamental photon field. However, the Weinberg–Witten theorem rules out this possi-
bility. Jenkins showed that this theorem requires any emergent gravity theory to either
lack a covariant energy–momentum tensor or possess a non-relativistic graviton [15]. In
a Poincar´ e-invariant quantum field theory, no Lorentz-covariant, conserved, spin-2 funda-
mental matrix element exists that could couple to an elementary massless spin-2 particle
between single-particle states [16, 17]. Should such a pole appear, it cannot correspond to
an elementary particle.
The Weinberg–Witten theorem thus presents a sharp theoretical choice. String the-
ory chooses to negate the point-particle assumption, replacing zero-dimensional parti-
cles with one-dimensional extended objects. Loop quantum gravity chooses to negate
the background-dependent assumption, quantizing spacetime itself non-perturbatively, one

consequence of which is discreteness at the microscopic scale. The holographic principle
chooses to accept that gravity is not fundamental, recasting it as an emergent description
of a dual boundary field theory.
Can it signal a composite collective excitation mode? This points to the possibility of
gravity as an emergent phenomenon. Like the Casimir effect or Hawking radiation, it is
a physical effect produced under specific conditions by the collective statistical behavior
of microscopic degrees of freedom. In the Casimir effect, the attractive force between two
plates does not arise from a fundamental Casimir force. It is the macroscopic manifesta-
tion of the vacuum fluctuation spectral function after modulation by boundary conditions.
Similarly, if gravity can emerge from the statistical behavior of energy–momentum tensor
fluctuation spectral functions, it would be an effective manifestation of vacuum fluctuation
collective effects, as opposed to an independent fundamental force in nature.
This non-fundamental yet emergent picture has a classic analogue in condensed mat-
ter physics, the phonon [18–20]. In a crystal, the phonon is not a primitive excitation in
the fundamental Lagrangian. It is the quantized manifestation of collective atomic vibra-
tional modes, whose dispersion relation, polarization structure, and effective action are en-
tirely determined by the statistical correlations of microscopic interatomic interactions [21].
There is no need to pre-install a phonon field in the fundamental description. The crystal
lattice itself provides the background structure required for phonon emergence. Analo-
gously, if the massless spin-2 excitation is emergent, its background is not a crystal lattice,
but the spatial correlation structure of vacuum fluctuations in quantum field theory. From
this viewpoint, curved spacetime can be understood as an effective geometric description of
the inhomogeneity of such spatial correlations [22, 23]. A massless spin-2 mode, should it
emerge, can be understood as the collective excitation of energy–momentum tensor fluctu-
ations on this background. Of course, this analogy has a fundamental limitation. Phonons
exist against a fixed crystal lattice background, whereas one of the central challenges of
emergent gravity theory is precisely to explain how the spacetime background itself arises
from non-geometric degrees of freedom [24]. Nevertheless, it provides a powerful conceptual
template. An excitation mode absent at the fundamental level can emerge at low energies
through the collective behavior of microscopic degrees of freedom. Its low-energy dynamics
are universal, independent of microscopic details.
Temperature, in statistical physics, is another instance of the same logic. In Einstein’s
analysis of Brownian motion [25], the irregular motion of a pollen grain is not explained by
introducing a new fundamental force. Instead, the collective effect of countless molecular
collisions is encoded as a random force, whose statistical properties are linked to viscous
drag through the fluctuation–dissipation theorem. Temperature emerges as the macro-
scopic parameter connecting the two, even though it has no definition at the level of a
single molecule. Temperature is not a molecular force pre-installed in the fundamental
Lagrangian. It is an effective description of microscopic statistical behavior after coarse-
graining.
The present paper applies this logic to gravity. It takes as its sole a priori object the
spectral function of the energy–momentum tensor commutator in flat-spacetime quantum
field theory, rather than curved spacetime or a fundamental gravitational field. Through

coarse-graining in momentum space and spectral representation, an order-parameter field
is constructed. The irreversibility of the coarse-graining process promotes the coarse-
graining scale to a dynamical order parameter. Its Langevin dynamics is governed by
two renormalization-group fixed points, an ultraviolet repeller at the Planck scale and an
infrared attractor at the Hubble scale [1, 26, 27].
The core question we investigate is whether the spin-2 spectral density can develop an
isolated zero-momentum pole. The existence or absence of this pole determines whether
the order-parameter field can support long-range spin-2 propagation. If the pole is present,
Weinberg’s low-energy theorem [1, 28] fixes the infrared self-coupling to the Einstein–
Hilbert form. The equivalence principle then follows from the Lorentz structure of the
massless spin-2 pole and from the uniqueness of the energy–momentum tensor as the uni-
versal current to which matter can couple. Newton’s constant is read from the pole residue
at the emergence scale, while the infrared fixed point supplies the natural curvature scale
of the effective description. Because the spectral function is defined from the commuta-
tor, additive static vacuum-energy terms are absent from the dynamical spin-2 diagnostic.
They are not the source of the pole.
The present work follows the spirit of Sakharov’s induced gravity [29, 30], Jacobson’s
thermodynamic gravity [31], and Verlinde’s entropic force proposal [32]. He et al. recently
constructed gravity by deforming the energy–momentum tensor non-locally, a top-down
modification of the operator itself [33]. The present paper provides a bottom-up mech-
anism. Gravity is not regarded as a fundamental interaction; it is tested as a possible
statistical phase of the energy–momentum tensor spectral function under coarse-graining.
In contrast to these works, the present paper places the dynamical mechanism in coarse-
graining criticality and takes the energy–momentum tensor spectral function as the fun-
damental object of the theory, as opposed to a fundamental metric or a thermodynamic
variable. In this picture, gravity stands to the energy–momentum tensor as temperature
stands to molecular motion. Throughout this paper, the term emergence refers specifically
to a statistical phase transition in the spectral properties of vacuum fluctuations. Through
coarse-graining, the spectral weight of the energy–momentum tensor may reorganize, gen-
erating long-range correlations. If this reorganization produces the isolated spin-2 pole, the
macroscopic state is described at low energies by general relativity. No new fundamental
entity is introduced in this process.
The result is therefore a criterion, not a premise that gravity must emerge in every
quantum field theory. The construction starts with the renormalized stress-tensor commu-
tator, defines a coarse-grained spin-2 spectral density, and asks whether its infrared limit
contains an isolated zero-momentum pole. Pole formation is a nonperturbative dynamical
question. If such a pole is absent, the construction yields a coarse-grained statistical tensor
field but not long-range gravity. If the pole is present with positive residue, the low-energy
consequences follow from standard spectral representation and soft-graviton arguments.
This is the sense in which the proposed mechanism is falsifiable.

2.1 Overview of the Argument
The starting point is the spin-2 spectral densityρ (2)(p2) of the energy–momentum tensor
commutator. Additive static vacuum terms drop out of the commutator and therefore do
not contribute to the propagating spin-2 spectral weight. To isolate the contributions of
fluctuations at different length scales, we separate the spectral density into fast and slow
modes. We introduce an exponential window functionW σ(p) =e −p2σ2/2 to separate fast

entanglement with the slow modes. This drives the slow-mode reduced density matrix from

stochastic back-reaction of the discarded fast modes, constitutes its Langevin dynamics.
For this overview, it suffices thatσinherits its dynamics from the irreversibility of the

in composite gravity models using FRG methods [34].

2.2 Spectral Representation of the Energy–Momentum Tensor
Gravity couples to matter universally in general relativity, with all matter fields couple to
the metric through the same energy–momentum tensorT µν, as encoded in the Einstein
equationsG µν = 8πGTµν. The nonlinearity of general relativity reflects a deeper fact, that
the gravitational field itself carries energy–momentum, so the energy–momentum tensor
couples to itself. This is the strong equivalence principle, the statement that gravitational
energy is a source of gravity. It is not an additional assumption but a direct consequence of
the self-coupling encoded in the Einstein–Hilbert action.T µν is both the source of gravity
and the carrier of the gravitational field. This dual role makes it the natural starting point
for any attempt to go beyond the standard geometric picture.
If gravity is emergent, not fundamental, the energy–momentum tensor, not the metric,
should be the basic object of the theory, and the metric should be emergent. In the standard
geometric picture, the metric is the fundamental field whileT µν is derived from it. If the
picture is reversed,T µν, defined on a flat background, would be fundamental, and the
effective metric would be derived from its fluctuation spectrum. As the conserved Noether
current of spacetime translations, the energy–momentum tensor is the only current in flat-
spacetime quantum field theory that carries spin-2 information. Its vacuum fluctuations,
their distribution across momentum scales, and their self-interactions determine whether
long-range, gravity-like correlations can arise at low energies.
The spectral representation of the two-point correlator of the energy–momentum tensor
is needed to characterize this distribution. The spectral functionρ(µ 2) encodes the weight
with which intermediate states of invariant massµcontribute to the two-point correlator
of the energy–momentum tensor fluctuations.
In its standard form, the K¨ all´ en-Lehmann representation expresses the full interacting
propagator of a field operator as a weighted integral over free propagators,

In a free theory, the spectral function reduces to a singleδ-function at the particle mass.
In an interacting theory, the interaction shifts this peak, broadens it into a continuum above
multi-particle thresholds, and introduces new peaks for bound states. The spectral function
captures, in full, how the interaction modifies propagation.
The structure of the spectral function tells us what a given field can propagate. An
isolatedδ-function peak signals a stable single-particle state. The propagator has a pole,
and the force it mediates falls off exponentially at large distances. A continuum above a
threshold signals the opening of multi-particle channels. The behaviour at zero momentum
determines whether the force is long-range. A massless pole produces a 1/rpotential. The
spectral function is a diagnostic tool. Its pole structure dictates the force law.
The spectral function provides a complete, non-perturbative characterization of how
the interaction modifies the propagation of the operator’s fluctuations.
The spectral function serves two purposes. It is a diagnostic tool. It tells us which
particles exist and which can propagate. It is also a bridge. It connects the microscopic
fluctuations of quantum fields to the macroscopic forces they generate. The spectral pole
criterion advanced in this paper rests on this dual role. In this language, the emergence
of gravity becomes a concrete question. Does the spin-2 spectral density of the energy–
momentum tensor develop a massless pole?
Using the vacuum expectation value⟨ ˆTµν⟩as a starting point would be problematic.
The divergent vacuum constant proportional toη µν would, in a geometric interpretation,
lead to a cosmological constant catastrophe. This paper takes the vacuum matrix element
of the energy–momentum tensor commutator as the fundamental object. The commutator
eliminates any constant term that commutes with all operators.
The fundamental degrees of freedom are fluctuations, not static backgrounds. The com-
mutator selects precisely those degrees of freedom. Only operators that do not commute
with ˆTµν contribute to the spectral function. These are the genuine dynamical fluctua-
tions. The commutator in Eq. (2.2) removes any vacuum-energy contribution, sinceE 0ηµν
commutes with ˆTµν. Throughout this paper, “propagation” refers to the dynamical prop-
agation of on-shell degrees of freedom, the process by which a massless pole in the spectral
function generates a long-range force. It is distinct from the instantaneous transmission of
a static force. This principle, encoded at the kinematic level by the commutator, is more
than a technical observation. The spectral function is free of vacuum energy contributions
not because of a fine-tuned cancellation, but because the formalism restricts to the fluctu-
ating sector. For fundamental interactions, the principle holds in the dynamical sense but
is largely tautological, since fundamental particles fluctuate and propagate by definition.
When gravity is not a fundamental interaction, the principle becomes non-trivial. Grav-
ity is not pre-installed in the microscopic action; it is selected, if at all, by the infrared
pole structure of the stress-tensor spectral density. Whether the energy–momentum tensor
fluctuations organise themselves into a propagating spin-2 mode is precisely the question
that the spectral pole criterion answers. The commutator, by restricting to the fluctuat-

2.3 Coarse-Graining of the Spectral Density
Vacuum fluctuations exist on all momentum scales, but fluctuations at different scales
contribute differently to macroscopic physics. Short-wavelength ultraviolet fluctuations
manifest as local noise, while long-wavelength infrared fluctuations produce long-range
correlations. To separate these two contributions systematically, we introduce a coarse-
graining scaleσ >0, equivalent to a momentum cutoffk= 1/σ. At scaleσ, fast modes
with momentumk≳1/σare integrated out, and their effects on the slow modes are
encoded in the effective dynamics. Slow modes with momentumk≲1/σare retained.
The coarse-graining operation is implemented directly at the level of the spectral den-
sity, using an exponential window function [27, 37]:
Wσ(p) =e−p2σ2/2.(2.8)
The exponential window is chosen from both physical and technical considerations.
Physically, coarse-graining should separate fast and slow modes as impartially as possible.
It should introduce no oscillations, respect the positivity of the spectral density, and fa-
vor no momentum direction. The exponential function is a smooth cutoff satisfying these
requirements. In coordinate space it is Gaussian, consistent with the Gaussian statistics
of fast-mode noise. The Gaussian distribution has maximal entropy for a given variance.
This is the natural expression of impartiality. For technical convenience,W σ(p)>0 holds
for all momenta and depends only onp 2, which ensures positivity and isotropy of the
coarse-grained spectral density. The exponential window factors with the exponential form
of propagators, allowing explicit Gaussian integration that simplifies the derivation of evo-
lution equations and heat kernel expansions in later work. The choice of window function
affects numerical coefficients, not the scaling behaviour. The effective temperature always
satisfiesT eff∝1/σ. The qualitative conclusions of this paper do not depend on the specific
regulator.

vacuum expectation values.

3 Order Parameter Dynamics and Effective Temperature
The coarse-graining scaleσis not merely a technical parameter. Its dynamics is organized
by two fixed points, an ultraviolet repeller atσ 0∼ℓ P and an infrared attractor atσ c∼
c/H0.
3.1 The Coarse-Graining Scale as an Order Parameter
Quantum field theory is always defined on a fixed flat background metricη µν. This is
the mathematical prerequisite for the theory to exist. If gravity is emergent, spacetime
curvature can be understood as the effective geometric description, at macroscopic scales,
of spatial inhomogeneities in the statistical intensity of vacuum fluctuations.
What emerges is not the metric itself, but the dynamics of the metric. The metric, as
an effective geometric description of fluctuation inhomogeneity, is emergent. What is not
presupposed is the Einstein–Hilbert action. It emerges from the spectral pole via Wein-
berg’s theorem. Should the spin-2 spectral density of the energy–momentum tensor acquire
an isolated pole at the coarse-graining critical point, its vacuum fluctuations acquire long-
range correlations. The transverse-traceless part of the coarse-grained field Φµν constructed
in§2.4, denoted here byh µν, describes the order parameter of these long-range correlations.
It captures the statistical intensity differences of the energy–momentum tensor fluctuations

equations are equivalent to those in a curved spacetime. The curved partκh µν in the
effective metricg µν =η µν +κhµν does not originate from a pre-existing geometric struc-
ture. It is the macroscopic statistical signature of the inhomogeneity of energy–momentum
tensor fluctuations. There is no a priori curved metric, only the inhomogeneous distri-
bution of energy–momentum tensor fluctuations on a flat background. This is consistent
with the Weinberg–Witten theorem, which forbids a fundamental massless spin-2 particle
in a Poincar´ e-invariant quantum field theory [16]. If long-range spin-2 correlations emerge,
they must do so as collective excitations. The low-energy dynamics of such excitations are
then uniquely fixed by Weinberg´ s low-energy theorem [28] to the Einstein–Hilbert form.

3.3 Entropy Production and Effective Temperature
Every coarse-graining step discards the fast modes in a momentum shell [σ−1,(σ+ dσ)−1].
Discarding these modes has two independent physical consequences. They shift the effective
action of the slow modes, and their microscopic information is lost, increasing the entropy
of the slow-mode reduced density matrix. The ratio of these two effects defines the effective
temperature.
The fast-mode integration is the coarse-graining analogue of Brownian motion: the
discarded modes act as a heat bath determiningT eff for the slow modes via the fluctuation–
dissipation theorem.
The coarse-graining operation is meaningful only when applied to a system with suffi-
ciently many degrees of freedom. If only a small number of modes were involved, discarding
them would produce no well-defined notion of entropy, no thermodynamic limit, and no ef-
fective temperature. The vacuum of quantum field theory supplies the infinite-dimensional
system needed for coarse-graining to define entropy and temperature. Even in the extreme
ultraviolet, where fluctuations at distinct spacetime points become statistically indepen-
dent, every point still hosts infinitely many field modes. Coarse-graining does not need to
wait for statistics to emerge. It operates on a statistical system from the start.
The relevant question is not where statistics begins, but where coarse-graining ceases
to apply. This happens when the number of integrated modes becomes too small to sustain
a thermodynamic description. In the far infrared, only zero modes survive, the effective
temperature approaches zero, and the Langevin equation reduces to classical relaxation.
Throughout the entire range of scales that the framework covers, from the Planck scale to

The following points clarify the physical nature ofT eff.
Origin of the entropy.T eff is a statistical parameter that encodes the ratio of entropy
production to energy transfer intrinsic to the coarse-graining operation. Its physical role
parallels that of thermodynamic temperature in the fluctuation–dissipation theorem, but

graining step, the discarded fast modes are entangled with the retained slow modes. Tracing

from a pure state to a mixed state. The resulting increase in von Neumann entropy is the
physical source of the kernelIS(λn,m 2,k) in equation (3.8). This mechanism is structurally
parallel to the information loss that underlies Hawking radiation, but it requires neither an
event horizon nor a curved spacetime. The entanglement truncation is effected entirely by
the coarse-graining operation in flat spacetime.T eff is not a thermodynamic temperature.
There is no external heat bath. The fast modes, before being integrated out, reside in the
quantum vacuum state, not a thermal state.
The Unruh temperature corresponds to the same formula withσreplaced by the

of the coarse-graining operation in flat-spacetime quantum field theory, independent of
accelerated observers or spacetime geometry.

of the emergence scale, where the classical form of the fluctuation–dissipation theorem is
applicable.

5.2 Weinberg’s Low-Energy Theorem and Einstein Gravity
With the massless spin-2 propagator in the transverse-traceless sector established from
the spectral pole in§5.1, we now turn to the question of how this pole translates into
observable gravitational phenomena. In standard perturbative quantum field theory, one
would proceed from the propagator to asymptotic states via the LSZ reduction formalism.
For a universally coupled massless mode, however, the LSZ formalism is not the appropriate
framework. Any attempt to isolate such a mode as a free asymptotic particle would require
switching off its universal coupling, which is precisely what defines the mode and gives
rise to its long-range effects. The observational content of a universally coupled spin-
2 excitation is fully encoded in its static potential and its radiation, both of which are
directly determined by the pole term of the two-point function and are accessible without
constructing asymptotic free states. It reflects the distinctive nature of such excitations,
which cannot be isolated without spoiling the very long-range correlations that characterize
them.
The application of Weinberg’s low-energy theorem to the present emergent field does
not require LSZ reduction. The theorem concerns the analytic structure of theS-matrix
for matter–matter scattering—specifically, the long-range spin-2 component of such am-
plitudes. Its premises are the existence of a massless pole in the intermediate channel,
Lorentz invariance, and unitarity of theS-matrix. Its conclusion—universal coupling with
an Einstein–Hilbert low-energy effective action—is independent of whether the massless
pole originates from an elementary field, a composite bound state, or an emergent collec-
tive mode of the type considered here.

5.4 Falsifiability of the Criterion
The criterion has clear failure modes. If a nonperturbative calculation of the stress-tensor
spectral density finds no isolated pole atp 2 = 0, the coarse-grained tensor field does not
mediate long-range Einstein gravity. If the spin-2 channel contains only a continuum or
a massive resonance, the resulting force is not the observed long-range interaction. If the
pole appears with negative residue, unitarity is lost. If the pole exists but the low-energy
coupling is not universal, the assumptions needed for Weinberg’s theorem are not met. In
each case the spectral construction still defines a coarse-grained stress-fluctuation field, but
it does not yield emergent gravity.
6 Summary
The criterion proposed in this paper is whether the spin-2 spectral density of the energy–
momentum tensor commutator develops an isolated zero-momentum pole. This is the
proposed signature of emergent gravity from quantum field fluctuations.
The irreversibility of coarse-graining drives the construction. Discarding fast modes
produces energy transfer and entropy production. Their ratio defines an effective temper-
ature; in the free massless zero-mode limit this givesk BTeff =ℏc/(2πσ). The same trace
density that yields temperature also redistributes spectral weight among the surviving slow
modes. The effective temperature encodes the statistical effect of the discarded modes on
the scalar channel. The spectral pole—should it form—encodes their effect on the spin-2
channel. Two macroscopic parameters, one microscopic substrate.
The central criterion is the selection of theδ-function pole. Within the spectral-
flow class considered here, smooth spectral functions are obstructed as fixed points. This
– 29 –

===== PAGE 32 =====
negative result makes the pole question sharp. If the pole forms, Weinberg’s low-energy
theorem fixes the effective action to the Einstein–Hilbert form. Newton’s constant follows
from the pole residue,G= 1/(8πZ). The infrared fixed-point scale gives Λ∼(H 0/c)2.
These relations give the observed orders of magnitude when the residue and the endpoint
scales take their natural values. The effective temperature, evaluated at the Schwarzschild
horizon scale, returns the Hawking temperature, serving as a consistency check rather
than an input. Lattice gauge theory and nonperturbative functional renormalization-group
methods provide direct tests of the pole condition.
The equivalence principle follows, in the pole phase, from the Lorentz structure of the
Standard Model and the uniqueness of the energy–momentum tensor as the spin-2 current.
The Einstein equivalence principle follows from the uniqueness of the effective geometry,
and the strong equivalence principle from the nonlinear completion fixed by Weinberg’s
theorem. Temperature and gravity are not merely analogous. The Introduction drew the
parallel: gravity might stand to the energy–momentum tensor as temperature stands to
molecular motion. The intervening chapters made it precise as a spectral criterion. The
logic that leads from coarse-graining to temperature is the same logic that leads from coarse-
graining to the spin-2 pole question. They are two channels of a single trace density. The
commutator, by restricting to the fluctuating sector, encodes this at the kinematic level.
Gravity is not pre-installed; it is selected by the infrared pole structure.
Should the pole form, the resulting massless spin-2 excitation is not a fundamental
particle. It is the quantum of the emergent order parameterhµν. The non-renormalisability
encountered when quantising gravity as a fundamental field is therefore not the starting
point of the construction; what is quantised are collective modes of energy–momentum
tensor fluctuations.
Recent computations in Lorentzian quantum gravity have found a massless one-
graviton peak and a multi-graviton continuum [39, 41, 42], a spectral structure that shares
the same qualitative structure as the one conjectured here, despite the different spacetime
background. This suggests that such a structure is realizable in a nonperturbative quantum
field theory.
Several directions lie beyond the criterion established here. Reconstruction of the full
nonlinear effective action from complete spectral data is a broader inverse problem. The
coarse-graining analysis is Euclidean, while the Lorentzian causal structure belongs to the
corresponding real-time formulation. The large-Nmodel of Appendix B illustrates the
mechanism in a controlled setting, but spin-2 is not spin-1. In the full Standard Model, the
stronger transversality condition supplies the specific spin-2 test of the pole mechanism.
These directions define the programme beyond the present criterion. This paper fixes the
first step. If the pole exists, the parallel between temperature and gravity is complete. If
it does not, the parallel fails.
The emergence question is therefore reduced to the spectral pole criterion.

A.6 Limitations
The derivation above is simplified at several levels, and the regime of validity of each
approximation requires further investigation.
The strict separation of fast and slow modes is only approximate for a smooth regulator.
The derivation is qualitatively valid. Quantitative details depend on the specific choice of
regulator scheme.
The truncation to linear coupling may require nonlinear corrections whenσis large,
far from the critical point. Near the critical point these corrections are negligible.
The Markovian approximation, Γ(ω)→Γ(0), breaks down exactly at the critical
point. The present framework addresses the flow near the critical point, not the behavior
exactly on it. When the system sits precisely at criticality, a more involved non-Markovian
description is needed.
The continuation from Euclidean to real time involves the standard analytic continu-
ation problem of quantum field theory, which is not elaborated here.
The exponential regulator used in this paper (see Section 2 of the main text) provides
a concrete implementation of the fast–slow mode decomposition. Within this scheme,
the qualitative conclusions of this appendix, namely the existence of dissipative and noise
terms, the emergence of an effective temperature, and the applicability of the fluctuation–
dissipation relation, are robust. Quantitative details depend on the regulator scheme, but
do not affect the central criterion of the main text.
These caveats do not undermine the purpose of this appendix. It shows that the
Langevin equation follows from the framework of fast-mode integration.

B.5 Relation to the full theory
Two differences exist between the present model and the energy–momentum tensor system
studied in the main text.
– 35 –

===== PAGE 38 =====
(1) The Noether current is a spin-1 operator (antisymmetric in the internal indices),
whereas the energy–momentum tensor is a spin-2 operator. The transversality con-
dition for spin-2 is stronger than the conservation condition for spin-1. It eliminates
not only the longitudinal component but also the trace component. This means that
the spectral function of the energy–momentum tensor is subject to more kinematic
constraints. Our preliminary assessment is that stronger constraints make it more
difficult for a smooth function to satisfy all conditions, thereby making the appear-
ance of a pole more likely. The correctness of this assessment remains to be verified
in the full theory.
(2) The large-Nlimit suppresses all loop contributions beyond one-loop order. In the
full theory at finiteN, higher-order loop corrections may quantitatively alter the
critical scale at which the pole forms, or prevent the threshold from being reached.
The qualitative constraints imposed by conservation and unitarity on the spectral
function—positivity, sum rules, and low-energy kinematic singularities—hold at any
order. The large-Nmodel therefore demonstrates a controlled mechanism, not a
general proof for the full spin-2 theory.
B.6 Falsifiability
This model provides a clear path toward testing the central criterion of the main text.
In the large-Nmodel, pole formation follows from the structure of the flow equation. If,
in the full theory, lattice simulations or nonperturbative FRG numerical calculations find
that the spin-2 spectral density of the energy–momentum tensor remains smooth at low
energies without developing an isolated pole, the proposed gravity mechanism is absent.
At minimum, the large-Nmodel demonstrates that the core mechanism operates in a
well-defined setting, providing motivation for searching for the same mechanism in more
complex situations.
B.7 Remarks on the Mechanism in QCD
The pole-formation mechanism studied above in the large-NO(N) model has a well-known
analogue in QCD. This observation is an analogy. It is not independent evidence for
emergent gravity. It serves to illustrate that the type of integral-equation-driven pole
formation examined in Appendix B is not an artifact of our particular toy model. It is a
recurring feature of non-perturbative quantum field theory.
In the large-Nc limit of QCD, meson bound states appear as poles in the Green’s func-
tions. Their spectrum is determined by an integral equation—the ’t Hooft equation [43]—
which follows from the Bethe–Salpeter equation for the quark-antiquark amplitude [44, 45].
The equation takes the form of a self-consistent integral equation for the meson spectral
density. The kernel accumulates spectral weight until a discrete pole emerges. This is
structurally the same feedback mechanism demonstrated in (B.11), though in a different
spacetime dimension.

the analogy establishes is the mathematical structure of feedback-driven pole formation as
a genuine feature of QFT. It does not establish that QCD already exhibits emergent gravity.
The distinction between gauge-driven bound-state poles and coarse-graining-driven spectral
poles should be kept clear.

where Π 0(s) is the single-bubble contribution andλ eff is the effective coupling of the ir-

vanishing of the energy–momentum tensor matrix elements atq= 0 follows from the
conservation law and does not rely on perturbation theory. Whetherλ eff reaches the crit-

relevant diagram classes in the specific model. This includes coupling strengths, particle
spectra, and symmetry constraints. This is precisely the question that the spectral criterion
is designed to answer.
The two classification criteria follow from energy–momentum conservation and dia-
gram topology. A diagram with zero momentum transfer between fast and slow modes
does not introduce an oscillatory factor under coarse-graining. Its contribution can accu-
mulate. A diagram with nonzero momentum transfer introduces an oscillatory factor that
averages to zero in the infrared limit. Among the diagrams with zero momentum trans-
fer, the ladder diagrams formed by irreducible insertions connecting single bubbles are the
class that can produce an isolated pole through geometric series resummation. The single
bubble alone contributes only to the continuum. The conservation law guarantees that the
matrix elements of the zero momentum transfer ladder diagrams are nonzero. This is the
microscopic origin of the non-vanishing injection term. Whether the critical coupling is
reached depends on the full dynamics of the specific model. This is the question that the
spectral criterion addresses.
The classification by momentum transfer uses only the kinematic structure of the
diagrams and the algebraic properties of conserved Noether currents. It does not depend
on the order of perturbation theory or on the strength of the coupling. The same approach
can be applied to other problems in which long-range correlations emerge from coarse-
graining. The ladder diagram approximation is a standard tool for treating bound state
formation in quantum field theory. Its reliability can be checked in solvable models such
as the large-Nmodel of Appendix B. In the Standard Model, non-ladder diagrams may
contribute additional cumulative effects, modify the convergence condition of the geometric
series, or shift the quantitative location of the critical threshold. They do not remove the
kinematic fact that zero-momentum transfer diagrams supply a non-oscillatory injection
channel. All diagrams considered in this appendix contain only matter fields. Gravitational
self-coupling enters only after the pole forms through Weinberg’s low-energy theorem, as
discussed in Section 5.2.
