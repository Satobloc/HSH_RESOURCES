Abstract:A conservation-law-guided classification framework is developed for the exe-
cution of coarse-graining operations in quantum field theory. Coarse-graining produces a
selection at the level of Feynman diagrams. Diagrams with nonzero momentum transfer
are suppressed by oscillatory factors, zero-momentum-transfer ladder diagrams can accu-
mulate through geometric series resummation to produce a spectral pole, and single-bubble
topologies contribute only to the continuum. Conservation laws govern this classification.
For operators protected by a Ward identity, the matrix element at zero momentum is
guaranteed to be nonvanishing. For operators protected by BRST symmetry, the Slavnov-
Taylor identities provide no mandatory suppression. The two cases differ in the strength
of the algebraic guarantee. For unprotected operators the injection term vanishes and the
spectral function remains continuous. The sign of the single-bubble contribution is de-
termined by spin statistics. A positive sign leads to amplificative feedback in the ladder
resummation, a negative sign leads to suppressive feedback. These two attributes, the
nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign of the single-bubble contribution, are the
defining criteria of the classification. As a direct application, a general classification of lo-
cal operators is established within the emergence framework and verified on eight physical
channels and three known solvable systems. The framework indicates which emergence
paths are possible for each operator; whether the critical condition is reached is left for
independent nonperturbative computation. The logical structure of this classification is
parallel to the strategy used in deriving fluid equations from molecular kinetic theory in
classical statistical physics.
Keywords:Coarse-graining; Conservation laws; Ward identity; Spectral function; Oper-
ator classification; Emergent gravity; Functional renormalisation group

1 Introduction
How microscopic degrees of freedom produce macroscopic collective behaviour through
coarse-graining is a question that runs through statistical physics, fluid dynamics, con-
densed matter physics and quantum field theory. In classical statistical physics the rigorous
derivation of the Navier–Stokes equations from Boltzmann’s molecular kinetics has been
completed in recent work, which established the macroscopic fluid equations as the in-
evitable consequence of microscopic molecular collisions in the thermodynamic limit [1]. In
cosmology the effective field theory of large-scale structure shows how symmetry principles
and coarse-graining govern the nonlinear clustering of dark matter. Short-range physics is
absorbed into a finite set of transport coefficients, yielding high-precision predictions for
the observed galaxy distribution [2]. In condensed matter physics the topological order
of the fractional quantum Hall effect, the pseudogap phase of high-temperature supercon-
ductors and non-Fermi-liquid behaviour all involve long-range correlations whose structure
is constrained by conservation laws [3]. In quantum gravity, Sakharov’s induced gravity,
Jacobson’s thermodynamic gravity and Verlinde’s entropic force proposal have approached
gravity as an emergent phenomenon from the perspectives of quantum fluctuations, ther-
modynamic relations and the holographic principle respectively [4–6]. More recently the
development of generalised global symmetries has provided a formal framework in which
symmetry protection constrains the correlation functions of operators at a fundamental
level [7].
What these developments share is a common logical structure. The emergence of
long-range correlations is constrained by conservation laws and the kinematic structure of
coarse-graining, while microscopic details affect only non-universal transport coefficients.
This logic has not yet been systematically implemented in the nonperturbative sector of
quantum field theory. The main tools currently available for nonperturbative problems
have each achieved important successes but share a structural limitation.
Lattice gauge theory discretises spacetime onto a finite lattice and evaluates the path
integral by Monte Carlo methods. It has been successful for the QCD mass spectrum,
hadron structure and quark confinement, but it is constrained by fermion doubling, the
sign problem at finite density and computational cost. Lattice results by themselves do
not reveal which diagrammatic channels are responsible for the structures seen in spec-
tral functions. The functional renormalisation group follows the evolution of the effective
action with the coarse-graining scale through the Wetterich equation. It can track spec-
tral functions, effective potentials and critical behaviour, and is widely applied to critical
phenomena, quantum gravity and strongly correlated systems. It does not, however, au-
tomatically identify which diagram classes accumulate under coarse-graining and which
are suppressed, and its results depend on the choice of regulator. Recent advances in
spectral functional renormalisation group methods [8] have made it possible to compute
spectral functions of quantum field theories directly, though the regulator dependence and

the lack of an analytic classification remain. Holographic duality maps strongly coupled
gauge theories to weakly coupled gravitational theories via the AdS/CFT correspondence,
providing direct access to the strong coupling regime. It applies, however, only to systems
that possess a gravitational dual and is not directly applicable to the Standard Model
itself. Dyson–Schwinger equations start from the quantum equations of motion and solve
for Green’s functions with nonperturbative truncations. They can handle mass generation
and chiral symmetry breaking but depend on the choice of truncation scheme.
The common limitation of these four tools is that they yield quantitative results with-
out supplying an analytic criterion that determines, before any numerical computation,
which operators and which interaction channels possess the potential to develop long-
range correlations. This judgement ordinarily relies on complete numerical computations
whose outcome itself depends on the regulator scheme, computational resources and model
choices.
The idea that conservation laws select physically admissible subsets of Feynman dia-
grams has a precedent in perturbative gauge theory. Boos and Ohl constructed minimal
gauge-invariant classes of tree-level diagrams, known as groves, by requiring that a subset
of diagrams satisfy the Ward identities by themselves [9]. The classification developed here
applies the same underlying logic of conservation laws as a selection criterion to a different
physical setting. Instead of ensuring gauge invariance at tree level, the criterion determines
which operators can develop long-range correlations under the nonperturbative operation
of coarse-graining.
In a previous work [10] the author established coarse-graining as a dynamical principle
for emergent gravity and proposed a spectral criterion, namely whether the spin-2 spec-
tral density of the energy–momentum tensor commutator develops an isolated pole at zero
momentum. Within the spectral-flow class considered there, a smooth spectral function is
not a fixed point of the coarse-graining flow; the isolated delta pole is the selected non-
smooth fixed-point configuration, and Weinberg’s low-energy theorem locks the resulting
low-energy effective theory uniquely to the Einstein–Hilbert form. The present paper takes
the next step and gives the diagrammatic execution of the coarse-graining operation, the
operator-level classification, and the link between the classification criterion and the Stan-
dard Model dynamical channels. A second work [11] tested the operator distinction implied
by the emergence framework in the electroweak sector. Taking the ultraviolet boundary
conditionλ(M P ) = 0 for the unprotected Higgs quartic coupling, two-loop renormalisation
group evolution yielded a Higgs mass consistent with the measured value within theoretical
uncertainties.
The present paper develops a conservation-law-guided classification framework that
supplies an analytic selection mechanism. Within the stated class of local operators, with-
out relying on perturbative expansions or the strength of couplings, and using only con-
servation laws and the kinematic structure of Feynman diagrams, it determines which op-
erators can develop long-range correlations under coarse-graining. The framework makes
the coarse-graining operation of Ref. [10] precise at the diagrammatic level. Whether the
momentum transfer vanishes separates diagrams that can accumulate from those that are
suppressed by oscillatory factors. Whether the diagram topology is a ladder separates

diagrams capable of producing a spectral pole from those that merely contribute to the
continuum. Whether the operator is protected by a conservation law separates operators
with a nonvanishing injection term from those whose injection term vanishes. These criteria
introduce no new physical hypotheses. They systematise, algorithmise and make general
the diagram classification ideas contained in Appendix E of Ref. [10].
The classification logic is structurally similar to the Landau paradigm in condensed
matter [12]. In that framework, symmetry dictates which excitations can emerge at low
energies. Here symmetry plays the same role. Ward identities and BRST invariance de-
termine which operators can accumulate spectral weight under coarse-graining. The CGC
framework is the QFT analogue of the Landau classification for long-range correlations [13].
The execution of coarse-graining involves several interrelated aspects. At the diagram-
matic level it is necessary to clarify which Feynman diagrams make cumulative contri-
butions to the slow-mode effective action after fast modes are integrated out, which are
suppressed by fast-mode oscillations, and whether the selection criteria depend on the order
of perturbation theory or the size of couplings. At the operator level different operators
display sharply different behaviour under coarse-graining. Whether an operator can gen-
erate long-range correlations, and why, is traceable to the conservation properties of the
operator and the spin statistics of the fields to which it couples. A further distinction
is drawn between dynamical ladder accumulation and topological zero-mode mechanisms.
The framework analyses the former; the latter, where relevant, is identified as a sepa-
rate pathway. The classification framework developed here systematically answers these
two questions. It is orthogonal to the standard RG classification by scaling dimension; it
addresses pole accumulation rather than operator growth under scale change. A comple-
mentary verification branch cross-checks the analytic classification against nonperturbative
numerical computations. The classification framework identifies the available emergence
paths. The FRG computation directly evaluates the propagator and the pole condition.
Together the analytic classification and the numerical verification form a complete chain
from analytic discrimination to dynamical confirmation. CGC supplies the structural se-
lection; the FRG supplies the dynamical realisation. The framework also distinguishes
situations where the ladder pathway is closed by topology, as with the scalar glueball
G2. Such channels belong to the topological branch rather than to the dynamical ladder
branch.
2 Three Criteria of Coarse-Graining
2.1 What Coarse-Graining Does at the Level of Feynman Diagrams
The coarse-graining flow of the spectral density derived in Ref. [10] consists of two terms.
A dilution term suppresses spectral weight at all nonzero momenta through the exponen-
tial window. An injection term deposits the spectral weight of the integrated fast modes
back into the surviving slow modes. The two terms are not independent. Their com-
bined effect is equivalent to a set of selection rules operating directly on the Feynman
diagrams that constitute the spectral function. The dilution term governs the suppression
of diagrams whose fast internal lines carry a momentum transfer that oscillates under the

===== PAGE 6 =====
coarse-graining window. The injection term governs the redistribution of spectral weight
through diagrams whose topology permits a cumulative build up over successive coarse-
graining steps. The present section makes this correspondence precise. The two criteria
developed below, one kinematic and one graph theoretic, map the dilution and injection
terms onto explicit diagrammatic selection rules.
Coarse-graining integrates out the fast modes with an exponential window function
Wσ(p) = exp(−p 2σ2/2). This operation is irreversible. In quantum field theory the fast
and slow modes are coupled by interactions, and their degrees of freedom are correlated
in the path integral. Integrating out the fast modes amounts to taking the partial trace
over the fast Hilbert space. The reduced density matrix of the slow modes is obtained.
Because the entanglement between the fast and slow modes is removed, the reduced density
matrix turns from a pure state into a mixed state, and its von Neumann entropy increases.
At the level of Feynman diagrams the effect of fast-mode integration is characterised by
two criteria. The two criteria are independent of each other, respectively kinematic and
graph-theoretic, and do not rely on the specific form of the interactions or on the strength
of the couplings.
The first criterion concerns whether the momentum transfer vanishes. Consider a
Feynman diagram containing internal fast lines and external slow legs. The net momentum
transferqfrom the fast internal lines to the slow external legs is a purely kinematic quantity,
determined by the conservation of energy and momentum at each vertex. Whenq̸= 0
the integration over the fast modes produces an oscillatory factore iq·x. In the infrared
limit of the coarse-graining flow the coarse-graining scaleσbecomes macroscopic, and this
oscillatory factor is exponentially suppressed by the integration over spacetime within the
coarse-graining window, vanishing in the limit. Diagrams withq̸= 0 therefore give a
negligible net contribution in the infrared. They do not participate in the formation of
a spectral pole but instead feed the noise kernelη(t,x) in the Langevin equation (2.4)
of the order parameter. Whenq= 0 the fast modes enter the vertices that connect to
the slow external legs in a back-to-back configuration, meaning that the fast momenta
appear in pairs of +kand−k, with zero net momentum transfer. This is the characteristic
momentum routing of ladder diagrams. In this case there is no oscillatory factor, and the
contribution of the diagram can accumulate over successive coarse-graining steps. This
classification is a standard result of Fourier analysis and is mathematically rigorous. It
does not depend on the order of perturbation theory or on the size of the couplings. It
depends only on the momentum routing of the diagram, and the momentum routing is
itself fixed solely by energy–momentum conservation.
The second criterion concerns diagram topology. Among the diagrams withq= 0
a further distinction must be made according to topological structure. The simplest are
single-bubble diagrams, which contain a single closed fast loop and no irreducible internal
insertions. The contribution of a single-bubble diagram to the spectral function remains
smooth in the infrared limit, producing only a continuumρ cont(s) and no isolated pole.
Another important class consists of ladder diagrams. A ladder diagram is built from several
independent bubbles connected in a chain by irreducible verticesV. The irreducible vertex
Vdoes not itself contain fast propagators. It is an effective four-operator interaction among

The momentum transfer criterion and the topology criterion address two distinct ques-
tions. The first determines which diagrams survive the oscillatory suppression of coarse-
graining. The second identifies, among the survivors, the specific class of diagrams capable
of producing a spectral pole through geometric series resummation. Neither criterion,
however, addresses a further question. The selection rules derived so far assume that the
vertices coupling the fast shell modes to the slow external legs have nonvanishing matrix
elements at zero momentum transfer. If those matrix elements were to vanish, no amount
of kinematic survival or topological accumulation would produce an injection term, and
no spectral weight would flow. Whether the relevant matrix elements are nonvanishing is
decided by a property that lies outside the kinematic and graph theoretic analysis. This
property is the protection of the operator by a conservation law. The analysis of this third
criterion follows.
Together the three criteria form a complete decision tree. The momentum transfer
classification removes the diagrams that cannot accumulate. The topology classification
singles out the ladder diagrams that can produce a pole. The conservation law check decides
whether those ladder diagrams have nonvanishing matrix elements, thereby determining
whether an injection term exists to drive the accumulation.

2.2 How Conservation Laws Guarantee a Nonvanishing Injection Term
Two complementary arguments are given. One is based on the dynamical mechanism
of critical slowing down under coarse-graining, the other on a rigorous proof using the
algebraic properties of the Noether current. The two arguments lead independently to the
same conclusion.

all power-law divergences from physical correlation functions, and the zero-frequency di-
vergence becomes the only singularity that cannot be cancelled. For unprotected operators
the surviving cutoff-dependent divergences absorb the zero-frequency divergence.
2.2.2 Algebraic argument
The injection term ∆ σ in the coarse-graining flow is the spectral weight deposited into
the slow modes after the fast modes are integrated out. At the perturbative level the
nonvanishing of the injection term relies on the Seeley–DeWitt coefficienta 2 of the heat
kernel expansion [14], which assumes that nonperturbative effects do not cancel this coef-
ficient. The present work establishes the nonvanishing of the injection term as a rigorous
consequence of conservation laws, without relying on perturbative estimates.

The injection term is regenerated at every step of the coarse-graining process. Because
it originates from the shell of fast modes that is integrated out at the current coarse-
graining scale, and every such shell contains the same matrix elements of the protected
operator, the injection term is nonvanishing at every step. The injection term therefore
acts as a nonvanishing source that continuously deposits spectral weight throughout the
entire coarse-graining flow, not merely at some particular scale.
For protected operators the matrix elements of the zero-momentum-transfer ladder di-
agrams are nonvanishing, and the injection term continuously injects spectral weight. For
unprotected operators the injection term vanishes, and the spectral function remains con-
tinuous in the infrared limit. The injection term is nonvanishing not because perturbation
theory suggests it might be, but because the algebraic properties of the conserved Noether
current require it to be. The result is no longer an estimate. It follows from the algebra.
The dynamical mechanism and the algebraic argument, though logically independent,
converge to the same physical conclusion for complementary reasons. The dynamical mech-
anism reveals the physical origin of the nonvanishing injection term. Critical slowing down
generates a zero-frequency singularity that cannot be absorbed for protected operators,
because all divergences that could absorb it have already been eliminated by the Ward
identities. The algebraic argument supplies a proof for specific operators that does not

The fluctuations of an ordinary composite operator are gradually diluted by coarse-
graining and eventually fade away. The fluctuations of the energy–momentum tensor are
different. Their spectral weight is protected by the Ward identity. It cannot disappear.
It can only be redistributed across momentum space. It is this forced redistribution that
makes it possible for spectral weight ultimately to condense into an isolated pole at zero
momentum.
2.3 Reliability of the Ladder Approximation
The ladder approximation is a tool for the quantitative resummation step. The classifi-
cation conclusions do not depend on it. The nonvanishing of the injection term and the
sign of Π 0 follow from algebraic and group-theoretic arguments that do not involve any
resummation topology. The FRG computation determines the critical scale. It solves the
full Wetterich equation and automatically includes all diagrammatic topologies. It does
not require a separate completeness proof for the ladder approximation.
The following three arguments provide supporting evidence for the ladder approxima-
tion as a reasonable starting point for the FRG computation.
2.3.1 Guarantee from conservation laws
The conservation laws provide a guarantee that does not rely on the ladder approximation.
The nonvanishing of the injection term is an algebraic consequence of the conservation
laws. Even if non-ladder diagrams produce additional contributions, they can only shift
the quantitative position of the critical condition, not the qualitative conclusion that the
injection term is nonvanishing. Whether an operator possesses emergence potential does
not require the ladder approximation to be valid. No matter whether crossed diagrams,
vertex corrections or other multi-loop topologies are suppressed, the Ward identity or BRST
symmetry guarantees the nonvanishing of the injection term. If the ladder approximation
were quantitatively imprecise, that would merely correct the critical scale, without altering
the judgement that the operator has emergence potential.
2.3.2 Spectral sparseness of the emergence window
The spectral sparseness of the emergence window supplies an independent physical basis
for the ladder approximation. When the coarse-graining window contains only a single ef-
fectively active field mode, the topological distinction between crossed diagrams and ladder
diagrams loses its meaning. A crossed diagram requires at least two independent active
modes to build its crossed momentum routing. When only one mode is active, the topolog-
ical structures of crossed and ladder diagrams degenerate into different representations of

===== PAGE 11 =====
the same diagram. The independent contributions of vertex correction diagrams and other
multi-loop topologies are likewise restricted to a single momentum configuration. Any di-
agram built on a single active mode has its possible structures severely compressed. The
ladder approximation is therefore not an approximation in this regime. It is exact when
the mode count is exactly one, and highly accurate when the active modes form a small,
sparse set. This conclusion does not rely on a large-Nexpansion or on any suppression
parameter, but is a rigid consequence of counting the modes of the internal space that lie
within the emergence window.
Whether this spectral sparseness condition is realised depends on the spectrum of the
internal space at the relevant coarse-graining scale. In the regime in which the emergence
mechanism operates, the internal space is highly curved and its nonzero spectral gaps are
large compared with the coarse-graining scale. With the Litim regulator [16], a sharp
step function used widely in FRG practice for its optimal convergence in the derivative
expansion, only modes whose eigenvalue lies belowk 2 contribute to the trace density [17].
Under these conditions the only mode that satisfies this condition is the scalarJ= 0 zero
mode, whose eigenvalue is zero and therefore always lies below the cutoff, together with the
nearby modes that remain within the window. Using the exponential window exp(−p2σ2/2)
of Ref. [10] instead gives every mode an exponentially small contribution rather than a sharp
cutoff, but the scalar zero mode continues to dominate under any reasonable regulator. The
compact-spectrum branch of the framework performs the quantitative mode counting for
the specific internal space; the present argument uses only the verified sparseness of the
active mode set.
A completeness theorem covering all possible non-ladder diagram classes is addressable
within the FRG, where the functional resummation automatically captures all diagram
topologies. The current verification covers the known relevant classes, and the crossed
and ladder topologies have been shown to degenerate on a sparse spectrum. The FRG
treatment subsumes the completeness question: the critical scale is determined by the full
Wetterich equation, which is not restricted to any particular diagram class.
2.3.3 Colour factor suppression
Colour factor suppression operates in the multi-mode region. At the equilibrium configu-
ration, where the number of active modes is about nine, the ratio of crossed diagrams to
ladder diagrams lies between roughly 0.1 and 0.5. In the perturbative regime the group
theory of SU(3) gives a colour factor suppression for crossed ladders,R= 1/(2C 2
A)≈1/18.
Emergence, however, takes place inside the window whereN eff = 1, not in the multi-mode
region. The colour factor suppression in the perturbative regime is auxiliary. It shows that
even in regions without the protection of spectral sparseness the contribution of crossed
diagrams remains at a moderate level of suppression.
2.4 Systematic Execution Steps
The conservation-law-guided classification framework is implemented as a six-step pipeline.
(1) Given a target composite operator, all connected irreducible Feynman diagrams
are generated at the one-loop level. The generation can be performed using the built-in

field type enumerator, which automatically determines the coupled field types from the
conservation property of the operator and then produces for each field type aq= 0 bubble
and aq̸= 0 variant, or it can call an external tool such asQgraf[18] for cross-checking.
For the four core operators of the emergence framework the expected numbers of diagrams
are six for the energy–momentum tensor, four for the gauge field strength, two for the
fermion bilinear and four for the Higgs quartic coupling.
(2) Each generated diagram is classified by its net momentum transferqfrom the
fast internal lines to the slow external legs. Diagrams withq= 0 proceed to the next
step; diagrams withq̸= 0 are labelled as suppressed and feed the Langevin noise kernel.
This is a purely kinematic judgement that does not depend on the specific form of the
interactions. For the energy–momentum tensor one expects threeq= 0 diagrams and
threeq̸= 0 diagrams; for the gauge field strength, two of each; for the fermion bilinear,
one of each; for the Higgs quartic coupling, two of each.
(3) Theq= 0 diagrams are sorted by topology into three classes. Single-bubble dia-
grams, which contain only one closed loop without any irreducible insertion, are labelled
as the continuum class and contribute only to the continuum. Ladder diagrams, in which
several bubbles are connected in a chain by irreducible vertices, are labelled as the accumu-
lation class and can produce aδpole through geometric series resummation. Non-standard
topologies such as crossed ladders and vertex corrections are labelled as the pending class.
At the one-loop level allq= 0 diagrams are single-bubble diagrams; ladder diagrams are
multi-loop structures generated automatically by the resummation module.
(4) A conservation law check determines whether the ladder diagrams in the accumu-

3 Operator Classification
The distinct critical behaviour exhibited by the energy–momentum tensor and the Higgs
quartic coupling under coarse-graining has its origin in symmetry. The former is the con-
served Noether current of spacetime translations. Its vacuum correlation functions are
protected by Ward identities, power-law divergences cancel automatically within commu-
tators, and the zero-frequency divergence produced by critical slowing-down propagates
freely to the spectral function, accumulating as an isolated pole at zero momentum. The
latter has no such protection. Its logarithmic divergence survives in physical correlation
functions, the zero-frequency divergence is absorbed, and the spectral function stays con-
tinuous at the critical point.
A local operator is called a protected operator if its vacuum matrix elements are pro-
tected by conservation laws or Ward identities, so that cutoff-dependent divergences cancel
automatically in physical correlation functions and infrared observables are independent of
the ultraviolet regularisation scheme. A local operator is called an unprotected operator if
its vacuum expectation values enjoy no such protection, are sensitive to cutoff-dependent di-
vergences, and its ultraviolet boundary values depend on the regularisation scheme. These
two definitions are exhaustive. Every local operator is either protected by Ward identities
or it is not. There is no intermediate case.
3.1 Basic Principles
Under coarse-graining different operators display sharply different behaviour. The origin
of this difference lies not in the details of perturbative calculations but in two definite
algebraic attributes of the operator. These two attributes are direct consequences of the
diagrammatic analysis carried out in Section 2. Their physical meaning can be illustrated
concretely by comparing how Feynman diagrams contribute to the coarse-graining flow for
different operators.
The first attribute is whether the injection term is nonvanishing. The injection term
∆σ is the spectral weight deposited into the slow modes after the fast modes are integrated
out. For a protected operator, the algebraic relation between the Noether current and
the conserved charge guarantees that this term is nonvanishing at every coarse-graining
step. It acts as a persistent source that drives the accumulation of spectral weight toward
zero momentum. For an unprotected operator, no such guarantee exists, and the spectral
function can only be diluted as coarse-graining proceeds.
The second attribute is the sign of the single-bubble contribution Π 0. Even when
the injection term is nonvanishing, whether the injected spectral weight can be amplified
into a pole depends on the sign of Π 0. This quantity is the contribution of the simplest
closed fast-mode loop, the single-bubble diagram, evaluated at zero momentum. Its sign
is determined entirely by the spin statistics of the fields propagating in the loop. Bosonic
fields give a positive contribution, fermionic fields give a negative contribution. The sign of
Π0 controls the direction of the self-consistent feedback in the ladder resummation. When
Π0 is positive, each rung of the ladder enhances the effective coupling, and the geometric
series drives the dressed polarisation toward a pole. When Π 0 is negative, each rung

suppresses the coupling, and the series remains convergent. A positive Π 0 combined with a
nonvanishing injection term is therefore the structural condition for dynamical emergence
through the ladder pathway; at the critical value the resummed channel forms the pole.

realised in the Standard Model: the energy–momentum tensor (nonvanishing injection,
positive Π0), the gauge field strength and the conserved vector current (nonvanishing in-

3.2 Symmetry Origin of the Criteria
The two classification criteria, the nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign of Π 0,
are not empirical or numerical properties. Both follow from symmetry, the first from the
continuous symmetries of the Standard Model that give rise to Ward identities, the second
from the Lorentz representation of the fields that contribute to the single-bubble diagram.
Nonvanishing injection term.The injection term is the matrix element of the operator
at zero momentum transfer. A nonvanishing value is guaranteed when the operator is the
Noether current of a continuous symmetry. The Ward identity∂ µJµ = 0 implies, by a
standard argument, that⟨f|J µ(0)|i⟩|q=0∝⟨f|Q|i⟩, whereQis the conserved charge. For
Tµν, this charge is the total energy–momentumP µ; for a conserved vector currentJ µ, it is
the corresponding global charge. The matrix element is nonvanishing for any state carrying
the corresponding quantum number.
For gauge-invariant operators such asF a
µνFaµν, BRST symmetry provides a different
kind of algebraic control. The Slavnov–Taylor identities ensure that the matrix element
is not forced to vanish by gauge invariance. This is weaker than the Noether current
case. There is no conserved charge whose expectation value supplies a direct proof of
nonvanishing. What the Slavnov–Taylor identities do supply is the absence of a mandatory
suppression. Throughout this paper, the term protected for BRST-invariant operators is
used in this restricted sense.
Operators with no continuous symmetry protection have no such algebraic constraint.
Their injection term may be zero, or scheme-dependent; in neither case can it serve as a
stable seed for ladder accumulation.
Sign ofΠ 0.The sign of the single-bubble contribution is determined by spin statistics,
which follows from the representation theory of the Lorentz group. Bosonic fields give
positive contributions; fermionic fields give negative contributions. The sign is an algebraic
fact, independent of coupling strengths, regularisation schemes, or perturbative orders.
The classification therefore rests on two independent symmetry inputs, namely the
Ward/BRST structure that guarantees the injection term and the Lorentz representation
that fixes the sign of Π 0. Both are rigid because both follow from group theory, not from
dynamical approximations.
Protection is necessary but not sufficient.The classification identifies the structural
emergence channels. Pole formation is completed when the corresponding critical coupling
is reached. Conversely, an operator may satisfy the injection and sign criteria yet be blocked
by the absence of a protected zero-eigenspace, as with the scalar composite channelG 2 on
the compact background. The symmetry criteria therefore select the dynamical ladder
pathway, while the zero-eigenspace condition separates it from the topological branch.

3.3 Protected Operators
Protected operators are those whose zero-momentum-transfer ladder matrix elements are
forced to be nonvanishing by conservation laws under coarse-graining. Three protection
sources exist.
The Ward identity originates from conserved Noether currents of spacetime transla-
tions. The energy–momentum tensor satisfies∂ µTµν = 0. Its zero-momentum matrix
element equals the matrix element of the total energy–momentumP µ, which is nonvan-
ishing for any particle state that carries energy and momentum. The BRST symmetry
protects the gauge field strengthF a
µν. It is BRST closed and its matrix elements are pro-
tected by the Slavnov–Taylor identities. The structural logic is identical to that of the
energy–momentum tensor. The conservation law eliminates the mechanism that would
otherwise force the matrix element to vanish at zero momentum. Noether’s theorem pro-
vides protection for conserved currents arising from global continuous symmetries. Gauge
currentsJ µa are protected by covariant conservation,D µJµa = 0, which operates through
the same structural logic as the ordinary Ward identity.
The proof for the energy–momentum tensor is based on the algebraic fact that the
matrix element of a Noether current at zero momentum equals the matrix element of the
conserved charge (2.5), which supplies a direct argument for nonvanishing. The proof
for the gauge field strength is based on the same structural logic. The Ward identity
provides no mandatory suppression at zero momentum transfer, a weaker but still algebraic
guarantee. Zero-momentum-transfer ladder diagrams can accumulate because the injection
term acts as a nonvanishing source that deposits spectral weight at every coarse-graining
step. The spectral function may develop an isolated pole under coarse-graining. Whether
this potential is realised depends on the dynamics of the system under the full coarse-
graining flow.
3.4 Unprotected Operators
Unprotected operators are those whose zero-momentum-transfer ladder matrix elements
have no conservation law protection.
The injection term of an unprotected operator lacks the algebraic constraint that forces
it to be nonvanishing. For a protected operator the Ward identity or Slavnov–Taylor iden-
tity supplies an algebraic relation between the matrix element atq= 0 and an indepen-
dently known quantity, namely the conserved charge forTµν or a BRST-invariant observable

forF a
µν, and this relation directly implies that the matrix element cannot vanish. For an
unprotected operator no such relation exists. The matrix element atq= 0 is not con-
strained by any symmetry, and there is no algebraic reason for it to be nonvanishing. The
classification framework operates under two premises. First, coarse-graining criticality ex-
cludes fine-tuning. Second, physical observables must be scheme-independent. Given these
premises, the CGC assignment for an unprotected operator is a vanishing injection term.
A nonzero zero-momentum source would have to be protected by an algebraic relation;
without such protection it is not stable under coarse-graining and cannot serve as a ladder
seed. The framework systematically identifies which operators lack such a protected source.
The zero-injection classification is therefore the structurally selected assignment under the
stated premises. Neutrino mass operators, whether of Dirac type ¯L˜ϕνR or Majorana type

ladder resummation.
Without a nonvanishing injection term, no source exists to deposit spectral weight
continuously into the slow modes. The spectral function remains continuous under coarse-
graining, and no isolated pole develops. The ultraviolet boundary value of an unprotected
operator is therefore not set by its own coarse-graining dynamics; it is fixed within the
subtraction scheme that defines the renormalised flow. This is the subject of the following
subsection.

4 Irreversibility of Coarse-Graining and the RG Time
In the derivation of the Navier–Stokes equations from Boltzmann’s molecular kinetics,
microscopic collision diagrams are classified by their wave number. Diagram classes not
protected by conservation laws are suppressed in the thermodynamic limit. Those that
are protected survive, and their resummation yields the macroscopic fluid equations [1].
The classification framework developed here follows the same sequence of classification,
selection and resummation. Feynman diagrams are classified by whether the momentum
transfer vanishes. Those withq= 0 can accumulate and those withq̸= 0 are suppressed.
The nonvanishing of the injection term for protected operators is an algebraic consequence
of the conservation laws. For protected operators with positive feedback, the resummation
of ladder diagrams produces a massless spectral pole when the critical value is reached.
4.1 Irreversibility of Coarse-Graining
The classification framework established in Sections 2 and 3 determines whether a given
operator possesses the potential for emergence, and along which path that emergence pro-
ceeds. The framework evaluates the algebraic and diagrammatic attributes of an operator
at a given coarse-graining scale. The dynamical branch then follows the effective coupling
that drives the ladder resummation and identifies the scale at which the critical value for
pole formation is reached. This section examines that dynamics. The irreversibility of
coarse-graining supplies an arrow of time, the RG time, which drives scalar condensation
and defines a characteristic window within which the critical condition can be met.
Reference [10] established coarse-graining as the single dynamical principle governing
the emergence framework. Coarse-graining is irreversible. Integrating out the fast modes
removes their entanglement with the slow modes, turning the reduced density matrix of
the slow modes from a pure state into a mixed state. Information is permanently lost.
The irreversibility manifests itself as a definite direction of the coarse-graining flow, from
the ultraviolet toward the infrared, from high energies toward low energies. The RG time
t= ln(k/Λ) inherits this irreversibility. It flows only in one direction, and the informa-
tion carried by the integrated fast modes cannot be recovered. No additional hypothesis
is required. The directedness of the RG time is an intrinsic property of the Wilsonian
renormalisation group [23].
This directedness of the RG time has a direct consequence for the emergence frame-
work. The coarse-graining flow drives the system from the ultraviolet toward the infrared
along a preferred direction. If the effective potential of a scalar field possesses a negative
curvature at the ultraviolet scale, the RG flow will gradually reshape it. As more fast
modes are integrated out, the shape of the effective potential evolves. Under appropriate
conditions the curvature can change sign, and a scalar field that initially had no vacuum
expectation value can acquire one. The irreversibility of coarse-graining is the dynamical
origin of this process. No external time coordinate needs to be introduced.

In the effective field theory of large-scale structure in cosmology, the bias functional

4.2 Scalar Condensation Driven by the RG Flow
The operator classification established in Section 3 identifies which operators possess the
potential for dynamical emergence. For a protected operator with a positive single-bubble
contribution, the ladder resummation can produce a spectral pole. Whether this potential
is realised depends on the dynamics of the system under the RG flow. Scalar condensation
provides a concrete setting in which the RG flow drives the system through the critical
region where the conditions for emergence can be met.
The mechanism described above can be made quantitative within the functional renor-
malisation group. Consider a scalar field whose effective potential at the ultraviolet scale
has a negative squared mass. The Wetterich equation [24] governs the evolution of the ef-
fective potential as the RG scalekis lowered. At each step the trace density, summed over
the spectrum of the theory, deforms the potential. As modes are progressively integrated
out, the effective potential changes shape. If the parameters are such that the curvature
at the origin switches sign at some critical scale, the scalar field acquires a nonvanishing
vacuum expectation value. The trajectory of the vacuum expectation value as a function
of the RG scale is a pure FRG output. It contains no phenomenological parameters.
The local potential approximation provides a technically simple framework in which
to carry out this computation [25]. In this approximation the spatial dependence of the
scalar field is neglected. Only the homogeneous mode is retained, and the second functional
derivative of the effective action reduces to the second derivative of the effective potential.
The accuracy of this approximation is controlled by the anomalous dimension of the scalar
field. When the anomalous dimension is small compared to the relative change of the vac-
uum expectation value over the relevant range of scales, the local potential approximation
is reliable. The quantitative branch of the framework evaluates this condition within the
FRG flow.

5 Synergy between Analytic Classification and Numerical Computation
Section 4 added the dynamical dimension of coarse-graining and the arrow of time supplied
by the RG time. The analytic classification and the functional renormalisation group
address this dynamics from complementary directions.
The classification framework is an analytic instrument. It does not directly compute
spectral functions. Instead it classifies Feynman diagrams, determining which diagram

B Operator Hierarchy from the CGC Classification
B.1 Motivation
The CGC classification in Section 3 gives a static answer. It tells which operators have
nonvanishing injection terms and which do not, but it does not tell which operators drive
which, nor does it explain why some unprotected operators nevertheless acquire nontriv-
ial infrared behaviour. This appendix reorganises the classification into a hierarchy that
exposes the driving relations between operators.
B.2 Protection and Self-Driving
Protection and self-driving are distinct concepts. Protection is algebraic. It fixes whether
the injection term is nonzero. A protected operator has a nonzero injection term. An
unprotected operator has a vanishing one. Protection comes from Ward identities or BRST
symmetry and is rigid, independent of the renormalisation-group scale. Self-driving is
dynamical. It determines whether an operator can develop a spectral pole through its own
dynamics alone. An operator is self-driven when its own spectral dynamics, through ladder
resummation, can produce a pole independently. An operator is driven when its infrared
behaviour is dominated by contributions from other operators in its renormalisation-group
flow.
– 32 –

===== PAGE 34 =====
The two notions are independent. An unprotected operator has no self-sustained injec-
tion term and cannot accumulate spectral weight by itself, but its renormalisation-group
flow can still be driven by other operators through beta functions. Protection fixes the
source of the injection term. Self-driving fixes the source of the infrared dynamics.
B.3 Four Levels
Level 1: Self-Driven (Tµν).T µν is the Noether current of spacetime translations, pro-
tected by the Ward identity. Its injection term is nonvanishing and its infrared behaviour
is set by coarse-graining criticality. It needs no other operator to drive it. The self-driven
status of Level 1 rests on the spectral pole proof in Ref. [10] and on the CGC classifica-
tion. Two channels emerge from Level 1: the spin-2 projection gives emergent gravity, and
the spin-0 projection gives a massless scalar collective mode. Both arise from the same
self-driven dynamics.
Level 2: Protected but Not Self-Driven (gauge couplings, Yukawa couplings,
Jµ).The gauge couplingsg 1,g 2,g 3 are protected by BRST symmetry and gauge invari-
ance, with nonvanishing injection terms. The conserved vector currentJ µ is protected
by the Ward identity. The Yukawa couplings, especiallyy t, are constrained by gauge
symmetry. These operators cannot drive dynamical emergence through ladder resumma-
tion. ForJ µ and the gauge field strength operatorF 2, the single-bubble contribution is
negative because fermion loops dominate. In all cases, the positive feedback required for
pole formation is absent. The gauge and Yukawa couplings thus occupy the middle level.
They are protected but lack the conditions for self-driven emergence. This characterisa-
tion follows from the renormalisation-group equation structure and the absence of positive
single-bubble feedback.

no self-sustained sources. Their evolution is driven entirely by Level 2 operators, and their
ultraviolet boundary values are not set by coarse-graining criticality. The Higgs quartic
couplingφ 4 is the clearest example. Its beta function is

B.4 Structure Diagram
The hierarchy has a top-down structure. Level 1 containsT µν, the unique self-driven
operator. Level 2 contains the gauge and Yukawa couplings and the conserved vector
currentJ µ. They are protected but not self-driven. Level 3 contains the unprotected
operatorsφ 4, ¯ψψ, and∥φ∥ 2. They are driven entirely by Level 2 operators. Level 4 is a
parallel branch containingG 2, which is BRST-protected but topologically blocked.
The ordering is logical priority, not a causal chain. The full dynamics is not one-
way. Once Level 3 matter fields appear, they feed back intoT µν through the Einstein
equation. The hierarchy is an ordering of logical dependence, not a claim about temporal
causality. The driving chain from Level 1 to Level 2 to Level 3 is the RG realisation of the
hierarchy. Functional-renormalisation-group computation supplies the quantitative form of
this flow. The hierarchy gives the structural reading of how unprotected operators inherit
their infrared behaviour.
B.5 Consequences
The hierarchy leads to several consequences.T µν anchors the entire structure. It is self-
driven and needs no external input, which explains why the gravitational channel is inde-
pendent of the other interactions. The gauge and Yukawa couplings at Level 2 are protected
but not self-driven. They cannot produce spectral poles through ladder resummation, and
the hierarchy provides a framework for understanding where their infrared values come
from. The unprotected operators at Level 3 are fully driven. Their ultraviolet boundary
values are not set by coarse-graining criticality. The hierarchy fixes their logical position
and identifies their boundary data as inherited from the infrared fixed-point structure of
Level 2.
The Higgs quartic couplingφ 4 illustrates how the hierarchy determines ultraviolet
boundary conditions for unprotected operators. Its injection term vanishes and it sits at
Level 3, driven by the gauge and Yukawa couplings at Level 2. Its ultraviolet boundary
value is not determined by its own dynamics. In the absence of an external scale, zero is the
natural choice. The renormalisation-group stability ofλ= 0 supports this identification.
A companion paper adopts this boundary condition for the phenomenological analysis of
the Higgs sector [11].
