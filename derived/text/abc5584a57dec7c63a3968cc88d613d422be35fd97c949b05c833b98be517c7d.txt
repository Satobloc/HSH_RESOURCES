Assessment of the 49Ti(d,x) route to produce the theranostic radionuclide 
47Sc based on cross-section measurements

Abstract. The availability of the theranostic radionuclide 47Sc is hindered by the lack of a 
reliable high purity production method. The aim of this work is to evaluate a less considered 
pathway exploiting deuteron beams on enriched 49Ti targets. The investigation started from the 
measurements of the production cross sections of the emergent radionuclide 47Sc, as well as of 
the co-produced contaminants which play an important role on the final purity and in the 
evaluation of the dose eventually administered to a patient. Enriched 49Ti targets, manufactured 
at the INFN-LNL (National Institute of Nuclear Physics-Legnaro National Laboratories), were 
activated in several irradiation runs carried out at the GIP ARRONAX (Group of Public Interest 
Accelerator for Research in Radiochemistry and Oncology in Nantes-Atlantique) facility 
cyclotron utilizing the tunable 15÷35 MeV deuteron beam. The obtained cross section trends, 
which constitute the first measurement for some radionuclides, are compared to theoretical 
curves and literature data, when available. The experimental cross sections are then discussed 
to assess 49Ti(d,x) as a possible production route for 47Sc. The results demonstrated that 
49Ti(d,α)47Sc can be considered as a valid alternative production route for the supply of this 
medically interesting radionuclide. 
Keywords. Production cross sections, deuteron-induced nuclear reactions, theranostic 
scandium, 47Sc, enriched 49Ti targets, cyclotron-based production
1. Introduction
As a potential theranostic radionuclide, 47Sc has drawn attention in recent years thanks to its 
emission of low-energy β − with a mean energy of 162.0 keV (NNDC website, 2025). This 
energy is very similar to the β − emission of the gold-standard 177Lu (mean energy 133 keV), 
making it a valid alternative for the internal radiation therapy of small and metastatic tumours 
[De Nardo, 2025]. However, the key advantage of 47Sc is its possibility to be paired to a PET 
(Positron Emission Tomography) diagnostic counterpart, namely 43Sc or 44gSc, both β + 
emitters. This configuration is identified as true theranostic pair, enabling a more precise 
assessment of the treatment plans based on the patient’ response at the disease imaging stage. 
Nowadays, the most common pair is 68Ga/177Lu, a couple with similar but not identical

===== PAGE 2 =====
chemical properties. In fact, they have different coordination geometries and chemical 
structures, resulting in two radiopharmaceuticals with different stability and biodistribution. 
Thus, the use of true theranostic pairs, such as 43Sc/47Sc or  44g Sc/47Sc, will constitute an 
improvement of clinical procedures. Moreover, 43Sc and 44gSc have the potential advantage to 
have a longer half-life than 68Ga (3.891 h/4.0420 h vs. 1.1285 h) which can better match 
biomolecules with a slower biodistribution profile. Additionally, 47Sc has a γ emission of ideal 
energy and suitable intensity (see table 1) to be used with SPECT (Single Photon Emission 
Computed Tomography) cameras to perform pre- and post-therapeutic imaging to monitor the 
treatment effectiveness.
First step for the establishment of the 47Sc production chain consists in individuating the most 
convenient nuclear reaction (one or more) for a medically suitable final product [Pupillo, 2022]. 
The main aspects to be considered are the quantity of the radionuclide of interest produced, 
47Sc, as well as the presence of co-produced contaminants, especially the isotopic ones with 
comparable or longer half-lives (like 48Sc and 46Sc), since cannot be chemically separated. 
Those co-products can degrade the quality of the final product, evaluated in term of 
radionuclidic purity for which a conventionally acceptable threshold value of 99% is 
considered in the context of this work. In last decades, many different production pathways 
have been investigated, involving cyclotron facilities, nuclear reactors, and photoproduction 
with electron linear accelerators [Mikolajczak, 2021].
Both fast (nuclear reaction: 47Ti(n,p)47Sc) and thermal (generator system: 46Ca(n,γ)47Ca47Sc) 
neutrons were investigated for the production at nuclear reactors. The latter demonstrated 
significant advantages in the elevated radionuclidic purity and higher yield, counterbalanced 
by the cost, influenced by enrichment and availability, of the 46Ca enriched material with 
respect to the 47Ti [Domnanich, 2017]. However, the development of a recovery method can 
minimize this drawback. Nevertheless, the issue about the availability of nuclear reactors, 
especially the ones delivering fast neutrons, remains. 
The use of bremsstrahlung radiation for the photoproduction of 47Sc was studied with three 
different natural target materials: titanium (reaction of interest: 48Ti(γ,n)47Sc), vanadium 
(51V(γ,α)47Sc), and calcium ( 48Ca(γ,n)47Ca47Sc). The limiting factor of these production 
methods is the low amount of produced activity while maintaining an adequate purity. Several 
options can be explored to overcome this downside. The use of enriched target materials is a 
possibility which can increase the yield, as well as the purity since the co-production of Sc-
contaminants is limited [Loveless, 2019], particularly in the case of calcium targets. In fact, 
natV is composed by 51V at 99.750%, and natTi is composed by 48Ti at 73.72%, and, 
consequently, approaching the 100% enrichment level will not improve significantly the 
amount of produced activity. Contrariwise, given the low natural abundance of 48Ca (0.187%), 
the required enrichment level and the limited availability translate in elevated costs 
[Mikolajczak, 2021]. Alternatively, the increase of the involved masses of target materials can 
support a more fruitful production, but its mass-dependent behaviour is not deeply studied yet 
[Snow, 2021]. Moreover, the cost-effective aspect of combining material enrichment with 
larger mass should be considered. In the end, higher end-point photon energies rise the 
produced yield but at the expense of the purity due to reach of the thresholds of the 
contaminants’ nuclear reactions.

Charged-particle-induced production can rely on proton, deuteron, and alpha beams. Alpha 
particles have been exploited through the 44Ca(α,p)47Sc reaction. It was assessed that low 
energies (<20 MeV) are necessary to keep the purity high, resulting in a low physical yield 
[Aikawa, 2022]. On the other side, two target materials demonstrated to be suitable for the 47Sc 
production with proton beams. Recently, production studies involving natV [Cingoranelli, 2024] 
and enriched 50TiO2 targets [Dellepiane, 2022; Cingoranelli, 2023] showed the validity of this 
production route reporting highly pure products (radionuclidic purities > 99.5%) with activity 
quantities satisfying the needs for at least preclinical trials. In this work, instead, the alternative 
production using deuteron beams is considered. The advantage of using deuteron beams can 
be the need of less target material due to the deuterons shorter range in comparison to protons 
at a given energy, which is a positive aspect especially when enriched expensive materials are 
involved. Moreover, a pure 47Sc production is expected thanks to the (d,α) channel which 
typically opens faster than others, at low energies. Cross section measurements of the 
49Ti(d,x)47,46,48Sc,48V nuclear reactions, performed in the context of the SPES_MED project 
funded by CSN3 of INFN (National Institute of Nuclear Physics), are presented. Only 
49Ti(d,x)47,46Sc trends were already measured in the past [Chen and Miller, 1964], and so, 
thanks to this study, the required knowledge to evaluate this production pathway is extended.
1.  Material and Methods
In the experiments dedicated to the investigation of the 47Sc production with deuteron beams, 
enriched 49Sc targets, previously used in proton-induced nuclear reactions measurements [De 
Dominicis, 2025], were recycled. Approximately, three years of cooling elapsed to assure that 
all the radionuclides expected to be produced in the new irradiations ( 48V and Sc-isotopes 
reported in table 1) were almost completely decayed from the previous ones. However, 
preliminary γ spectroscopy measurements were performed to evaluate the presence of eventual 
residual activities of longer-lived radionuclides previously produced. Some details about target 
manufacturing are here reported in brief since they were already described in previous works 
[Mou, 2024; Cisternino, 2023; Cisternino, 2022; Skliarova, 2020]. The targets were 
manufactured at the INFN-LNL (Legnaro National Laboratories) starting from metallic 49Ti 
powder purchased from the Oak Ridge National Laboratories (Tennessee, USA) with the 
enrichment level reported in table 2. Due to its spongy form and variable dimensions of the 
grains (from few µm up to 2 mm), the powder needed a cryomilling process [Cisternino, 2023] 
before exploiting the HIVIPP (High Intensity VIbrational Powder Plating) technique to obtain 
a thin Ti deposition on a natAl substrate [Cisternino, 2022; Skliarova, 2020]. EBS (Electron 
Backscattering Spectroscopy) analysis, performed at the AN2000 accelerator sited at INFN-
LNL, were then performed to determine the thickness of the deposited Ti layers [Mou, 2024].

Four stacks including 3 or 4 49Ti thin targets were arranged to obtain a maximum of 15 cross 
section experimental values for each radionuclide. Each stack was bombarded at a different 
deuteron impinging energy ranging from 16 to 30.7 MeV. In each stack, besides the material 
of interest 49Ti, natNi foils (25 µm) were inserted to monitor the beam intensity through the 
IAEA (International Atomic Energy Agency) recommended reactions natNi(p,x)61Cu, 56,58Co 
(Monitor reactions website, 2024; Hermanne, 2018). Following the natNi in the stack, thin natAl 
foils (10 µm) were included as catchers of the nuclei escaping from natNi foils due to their 
residual recoil kinetic energy. In addition, thicker natAl layers (100÷750 µm) have been used as 
beam energy degraders between one 49Ti sample and the following one. All the used materials 
had 99% as minimum purity, and were purchased from the Goodfellow Cambridge Ltd. 
(Huntingdon, UK). All the listed layers were cut into discs of 12 mm diameters to fit the target-
holder, which was then placed in front of the beam. Irradiation runs took place at the GIP 
ARRONAX facility (Nantes, France) [Haddad, 2008] with a duration between 40 min and 1 h, 
and a mean beam intensity in the interval 80÷100 nA.
Following the bombardments, a proper cooling time of few hours was waited before 
performing γ spectroscopy measurements through four different HPGe (Hyper Pure 
Germanium) detectors available at GIP ARRONAX, which were previously calibrated with 
point-like certified 241Am and 152Eu sources from CERCA-LEA (Tricastin, France). The 
activity in each 49Ti, natNi and thin natAl foil was measured repeatedly in the days after the EOB 
(End Of Bombardment) to follow the produced radionuclides decay and/or to check for the 
eventual presence of interferences in the γ peaks. 49Ti and natNi foils underwent also a long 
acquisition about 1 month after the EOB to measure the long-lived radionuclides, namely 46Sc, 
48V, 56Co, and 58Co, when all the other short-lived co-products have decayed. Each γ spectrum 
acquisition lasted between 30 min (e.g. the first ones soon after the EOB to measure short-lived 
radionuclides), and 14 h, (e.g., the overnight and distant from EOB ones which allowed to have 
a better statistic of long-lived radionuclides).
Data analysis was performed through the jRadView software developed at INFN-LNL for 
internal use with which the γ lines of the produced radionuclides (table 1) were identified and 
the corresponding activity evaluated. The interference in the common peaks, namely 983.525 
keV and 1312.105 keV, shared between 48V and 48Sc was considered and the contribution 
subtracted. The extracted data were used to calculate cross sections and related uncertainties 
according to the formula proposed by Otuka et al. [Otuka, 2017].
The deuteron beam energy associated to each foil was evaluated throughout simulations of the 
energy loss starting from the nominal energy with the SRIM2013 Monte Carlo code [Ziegler, 
2010]. Thanks to the choice of three different monitor reactions, it was possible to also validate 
the nominal beam energy with the technique proposed in [Mastren, 2019], but adapted to

===== PAGE 5 =====
deuteron beams. The energy uncertainty was extracted from simulations with SRIM2013 code 
assuming an initial intrinsic uncertainty of the deuteron beam energy of 0.5 MeV and deriving 
the energy straggling effect across the stacked-target.
2. Results and Discussion
The evaluation of each utilized beam energy independently from its corresponding beam 
intensity, exploiting the three different IAEA monitor reactions taking place in the natNi foils, 
gave positive results indicating that the nominal energy was effectively the energy of the 
deuterons impinging on the stacked-targets. This endorses the use of the nominal beam energies 
involved to derive the energies in the enriched 49Ti layers to be associated to the calculated 
cross sections.

with the theoretical data estimated by TENDL-2025 library [Koning, 2019]. For the 47Sc and 
46Sc produced radionuclides the excitation function previously measured by Chen et al. [Chen, 
1964] are also indicated for comparison.

case the TENDL-2025 data do not reproduce the experimental trend. Indeed, experimentally

The measured nuclear reactions allowed to investigate the 49Ti(d,x) as a potential route to 
produce the promising theranostic 47Sc for nuclear medicine applications. For this purpose, the 
trends of the isotopic contaminants were evaluated in comparison to the 47Sc one. In fact, their 
presence in the final product can contribute to the radiation dose delivered to patients and 
medical staff and cannot be removed with the radiochemical purification process. Actually, the 
validity of 49Ti(d,x)47Sc as a viable alternative production pathway was already highlighted 
with a theoretical analysis based on the existing cross section trends in a previous work 
[Barbaro, 2024], but the necessity of additional experimental measurements was expressed. 
Thanks to the new results here proposed, which extend the volume of available literature 
datasets, it is possible to confirm that the production of 47Sc through the 49Ti(d,x) route is truly 
possible. Specifically, from the trends it can be observed that below 10 MeV 47Sc is produced 
almost without the co-production of 46Sc and 48Sc, and so a high final radioisotopic purity 
(>99% for more than 380 h after EOB), one of the parameters to be considered for medical 
applications, is guaranteed. Concerning 48V, it is supposed that a 100% efficient chemical 
separation is achievable. Supposing an 80 hrs-irradiation (corresponding to one 47Sc half-life) 
of a 200 µm thick 49Ti metallic target an activity of 70 MBq/µA is potentially obtainable at 
EOB, evaluated starting from experimental cross sections. The yield could be incremented 
expanding the energy range: for 12 MeV impinging energy the yield grows by 40%, while for 
15 MeV the activity produced rises by 60%. However, in these other two different scenarios 
the productions of 46Sc and 48Sc start (radionuclidic purities of 95.7% and 98.9% at EOB, 
respectively). Consequently, dosimetric considerations on the contribution to the dose given to 
a patient due to the presence of these two Sc-contaminants are necessary. A paper discussing 
the 49Ti(d,x) production route from a dosimetric point of view in many different irradiation 
conditions is under submission [De Dominicis, 2026]. An alternative to significantly increase 
the amount of activity is to use very high currents, however this needs a remarkable 
technological thick targets development to withstand such irradiation conditions.
3 Conclusions
In this study new cross section results for the nuclear reactions 49Ti(d,x)46Sc, 47Sc, 48Sc, and 
48V in the energy range 4.4÷32.4 MeV were measured to investigate a possible production 
route for the theranostic radionuclide 47Sc, particularly promising for the therapy of oncological 
diseases thanks to its β- emission. The results about 47Sc and 46Sc were compared with the only 
previous literature data available [Chen, 1964] showing slightly different trends. In addition, 
TENDL-2025 library excitation curves [Koening, 2019] were included for comparison 
resulting in a good agreement in most cases.
From the analysis of the resulting experimental trends, it was observed that a 47Sc production 
meeting the purity conditions for medical applications is potentially feasible, at least for 
deuteron energies below 10 MeV. The maximum reachable activities are not very high, e.g. 70 
MBq/µA supposing an 80 h-irradiation but can be an opportunity to foster at least preclinical 
studies with this emerging radionuclide. Different irradiation conditions can be taken into 
consideration to improve the final yield but further investigations for dosimetric implications 
due to the open of 46Sc and 48Sc production channels are needed. Those insights are discussed 
in a dedicated paper recently submitted, where also a comparison with the 50Ti(p,x) production 
route, the only other feasible pathway to obtain 47Sc through enriched Ti targets, is included 
[De Dominicis, 2026].

===== PAGE 9 =====
In conclusion, it should be mentioned that deuteron cyclotrons are not very spread worldwide. 
However, the aim of this work is to propose the 49Ti(d,x) as an additional production route, 
rather than the only one, to be considered as complementary to other methodologies to satisfy 
the market request that there would be in case 47Sc gets routinely used in clinical procedures.
