Abstract
The cosmological expansion history of the exponential f(T) gravity model proposed
by Linder is determined from a modified Friedmann equation that generally does not
admit a closed-form analytical solution for the Hubble parameter, requiring repeated
numerical evaluations in cosmological analyses. Motivated by this limitation, we
present an analytical reconstruction of the model by introducing a Lambert–W-based
hybrid expansion ansatz for the Hubble parameter. The free parameters of the
reconstruction are first determined through a least-squares optimization by
minimizing the deviation from the numerical solution of the modified Friedmann
equation, yielding a maximum relative difference of approximately 1.08% over the
redshift range 0 ≤ z ≤ 4. The reconstructed model is subsequently treated as an
independent analytical cosmological model and constrained using recent late-time
observations, including Cosmic Chronometer (CC), DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic
Oscillation (BAO), Pantheon+SH0ES, and Union3 Type Ia supernova datasets through
Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analyses. The resulting parameter constraints are
mutually consistent across the individual datasets and become significantly tighter
for the combined likelihood. Furthermore, various cosmological diagnostics
demonstrate that the reconstructed model successfully describes the transition from
an early decelerated universe to the present accelerated epoch while remaining in
close agreement with the standard Λ CDM cosmology at late times. These results
demonstrate that the proposed hybrid ansatz accurately reproduces the numerical
exponential f(T) expansion history while providing a computationally efficient
analytical framework for cosmological parameter estimation and future observational
studies.

Introduction
Modern cosmology continues to confront profound challenges in providing a
coherent explanation for the accelerated expansion of the late-time universe.
Cosmological observations from various supernova-like type Ia Supernovae (SNe)Ia
Riess, 1998, Riess, 2004, the Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation(CMBR)
Komatsu (2009), and Large Scale Structure (LSS) Eisenstein (2005); Seljak (2005);
Tegmark (2004) collectively indicate that the universe is presently experiencing an
accelerated expansion during its late-time evolution Cai (2016); Hinshaw (2013);
Perlmutter (1999); Riess (1998); Wang (2008). This phenomenon is generally
attributed to an elusive component known as dark energy, which is characterized by
a fluid with negative pressure. It is widely accepted that dark energy constitutes
nearly two-thirds of the total energy of the universe. Recent observational evidence
indicates that the combined contribution of dark energy and dark matter constitutes
roughly  of the total energy budget of the universe Weinberg (1989). Among
the numerous theoretical proposals put forward to describe dark energy, the
cosmological constant ( Λ ) remains the most straightforward and widely supported
candidate. The resulting Λ CDM model demonstrates remarkable agreement with a
wide range of observational data. Although the cosmological constant provides a
simple explanation for cosmic acceleration, it is accompanied by two significant
theoretical issues, commonly referred to as the fine-tuning problem and the
coincidence problem. Carroll (2001); Copeland et al. (2006a); Di Valentino et al.
(2021); Padmanabhan (2003). These persistent issues have encouraged the
investigation of alternative directions in cosmology, prompting the development of
models that extend beyond the conventional framework.
To address these challenges, researchers have explored a variety of possible
directions. One line of work introduces additional or non-standard forms of matter
and energy to account for the observed acceleration. Another, more structural
approach involves modifying Einstein’s general relativity itself. The latter has drawn
considerable attention, mainly because it offers the possibility that the accelerated
expansion could arise from the gravitational sector, without the need to introduce a
separate dark energy component. General relativity, despite its remarkable success in
explaining gravitational phenomena over a wide range of scales, does face difficulties
when applied to late-time cosmic acceleration. This has motivated the development
of modified gravity theories that attempt to extend or go beyond the standard
framework Clifton et al. (2012); Nojiri et al. (2017). In many cases, these theories are
constructed by altering the curvature-based Einstein–Hilbert action, leading to
modified field equations that can naturally accommodate an accelerating universe.
Prominent examples include f(R) gravity De Felice and Tsujikawa (2010); Nojiri and
Odintsov (2011); Sotiriou and Faraoni (2010), f(R, T) gravity Harko (2011); Myrzakulov

Bahamonde et al. (2015); Bahamonde et al. (2023), f(Q, T) gravity Xu et al. (2019) and
f(T) gravity Aldrovandi and Pereira (2012); Bahamonde et al. (2023); Bamba et al.,

One possibility is to extend the matter sector by introducing additional dynamical

frameworks, such as curvature, torsion, and non-metricity Beltran Jiménez et al.

Among these, torsion-based modifications offer a particularly promising direction
due to their distinct geometric structure and the novel physical insights they provide.
In particular, the torsion-based formulation has been extensively studied in recent
years. In contrast to curvature-based gravity, where gravitational effects arise from
the curvature of spacetime, the teleparallel formulation attributes gravitation to
spacetime torsion. In this framework, gravity is described through the Teleparallel
Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR), which provides a formulation dynamically
equivalent to General Relativity at the level of the field equations, while employing
torsion instead of curvature as the fundamental geometrical quantity. This viewpoint
opens new avenues for constructing modified theories of gravity that are dynamically
distinct from their curvature-based counterparts. In particular, the extension to f(T)
gravity has emerged as one of the most extensively studied modifications, owing to
its ability to modify gravitational dynamics while preserving second-order field
equationsBahamonde et al. (2023). This feature makes it mathematically simpler and
computationally more tractable compared to curvature-based extensions such as f(R)
gravity. Moreover, f(T) gravity provides a compelling framework for explaining the
observed late-time acceleration of the universe without introducing an explicit dark
energy component. Recent studies have investigated its cosmological implications
and observational consistency. Several recent studies have explored the cosmological
implications of extended teleparallel gravity models Kofinas and Saridakis (2014);
Lohakare et al. (2023). In particular, Lohakare et al. Lohakare et al. (2023) investigated
an extended f(T, T ) gravity framework constrained by observational datasets,
demonstrating that the model successfully reproduces the cosmic expansion history.
Their analysis predicts a smooth transition from an early decelerating phase to a late-
time accelerating universe with a quintessence-like behavior, while also satisfying
the energy conditions at the background level. SK Tripathy et al. Tripathy et al. (2024)

The structure of this paper is outlined as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the
hybrid scale factor ansatz within the framework of teleparallel f(T) gravity and derive
the corresponding Hubble parameter in terms of the Lambert-W function. Section 3
presents the exponential f(T) gravity model proposed by Linder, along with the
power-law and exponential f(T) parameterizations considered for comparison. In
Section 4, we validate the proposed analytical Lambert-W hybrid reconstruction by
comparing its expansion history with the numerical solution of the original
exponential f(T) model and with other analytical parameterizations. Section 5 is
devoted to the observational constraints on the reconstructed model using Cosmic
Chronometer (CC), DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO), Pantheon+SH0ES, and
Union3 Type Ia supernova datasets, together with their combined analysis. Section 6
investigates the physical and dynamical properties of the reconstructed model
through the deceleration parameter, effective equation-of-state parameter,
statefinder diagnostics, Om(z) diagnostic, and the energy conditions. Finally, Section 7
presents the conclusion and discussion, summarizing the main results and outlining
possible directions for future investigations.
