ABSTRACT
Spectral lines of the hydrogen atoms and the alkali-like ions have been identified in the spectra
of galaxies and quasars. These spectral lines are used to yield the redshifts of the objects. We
show that finding the total redshift requires taking into account velocity-dependent relativistic
corrections of the order of𝛼2𝑣2
𝑐2 to spectral lines of galaxies (𝛼is the fine structure constant,
𝑣is the atom velocity). Although these velocity-dependent corrections follow from the the
energy-mass equivalence in the special relativity theory, this effect has not been previously
taken into account in calculations of the redshift and, respectively, the Hubble constant. In the
nearby Universe, the total redshift is determined by the combined effect of the Doppler shift
and the relativistic velocity-induced shift of the spectral lines. These relativistic corrections for
the lines2𝑃3∕2,1∕2 →1𝑆 1∕2 of the moving hydrogen atoms were calculated. It allows us to
obtain the dependence of the Hubble parameter𝐻(𝑧𝑜𝑏𝑠)on the total redshift. Comparing the
Hubble parameter with the well-known small redshift data, the present-epoch Hubble constant
𝐻0 = 51.2±11.0km/s/Mpc has been obtained. Of course, this value is significantly smaller
than those presented in the well-known studies. The smaller value of𝐻0 is due to this new
velocity-induced contribution to the redshifts of galaxies.
