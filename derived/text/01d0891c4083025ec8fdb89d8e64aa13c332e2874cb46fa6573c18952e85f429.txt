Teleparallel Gauss–Bonnet gravity beyond flat FLRW cosmology
Shivam Kumar Mishra 1,∗ and B. Mishra 1,†
1Department of Mathematics, Birla Institute of Technology and Science-Pilani,

We investigate the cosmological dynamics of teleparallel Gauss–Bonnet gravity in spatially curved
FLRW spacetimes. We construct a class of spatially curved tetrads that provides a unified de-
scription of open, flat, and closed FLRW geometries and allows the teleparallel torsion scalar and
Gauss–Bonnet invariant to be expressed directly in terms of the spatial curvature parameter. Using
a scalar-field representation of the theory, we derive an autonomous dynamical system that remains
well-defined in both expanding and contracting cosmological phases. We analyze the critical points
and their stability for flat and spatially curved backgrounds, revealing a rich phase-space structure
with distinct curvature-dependent asymptotic behaviors. In particular, the curved system admits a
stable attractor on the flat submanifold, where the curvature contribution is dynamically suppressed,
whereas other asymptotic solutions allow spatial curvature to persist.
I. INTRODUCTION
GeneralRelativitysuccessfullydescribesgravityintheweak-fieldregimeandsatisfieslocaltests, butitscosmological
application faces unresolved issues, particularly the nature of the dark sector. This motivates modified gravity theories
as possible explanations for cosmic acceleration [1–3]. Among them, modified teleparallel gravity reformulates gravity
in terms of torsion rather than curvature, building on the teleparallel equivalent of general relativity (TEGR) [4–11].
Its extensions promote the torsion scalar and related invariants to general functions [12, 13].
The original formulation of TEGR suffers from loss of local Lorentz invariance due to setting the spin connection
to zero and treating the tetrad as the sole dynamical variable. Although the resulting boundary term does not affect
TEGRfieldequations, itbecomesdynamicallyrelevantinmodifiedteleparalleltheories, makinglocallyLorentz-related
tetrads inequivalent [14, 15]. This led to the distinction between “good” and “bad” tetrads [16, 17]. A Lorentz-covariant
formulation was later established by treating both the tetrad and teleparallel spin connection as fundamental variables
[11, 18–20]. In modified theories, variation with respect to the spin connection yields consistency conditions related to
the antisymmetric part of field equations obtained via variation with respect to tetrads [21, 22]. Nevertheless, finding
the appropriate tetrad–spin-connection pair for general geometries remains technically challenging [18, 23].
This difficulty is particularly important for spatially curved FLRW cosmology. Unlike curvature-based and sym-
metric teleparallel theories, modified teleparallel gravity lacks a known good tetrad yielding field equations directly
parameterized by the spatial curvaturek. Consequently, the casesk= +1andk=−1are usually treated separately,
with a general structure inferred afterward [24–28]. This approach is cumbersome and may fail for non-minimal TEGR
extensions [29]. Beyond its technical inconvenience, this limitation obscures the dynamical role of spatial curvature
in teleparallel cosmology [30, 31].
In this work, we address this problem by constructing a class of tetrads for FLRW spacetimes that yields all relev-
ant teleparallel quantities directly in terms of the curvature parameterk, thereby providing a unified and consistent
description valid for open (k=−1), flat (k= 0) and closed (k= +1) cosmologies. The construction is achieved
by applying a suitable local Lorentz rotation to the diagonal tetrad, resulting in configurations that preserve homo-
geneity and isotropy at the level of the torsion invariants. As an application of this framework, we investigate the
dynamics of the curved tetrads in the context of teleparallel Gauss-Bonnet theory. Gauss–Bonnet modifications of
gravity generalize TEGR by promoting the teleparallel Gauss–Bonnet scalarTG to a nontrivial function in the gravit-
ational action. TheF(T,T G)framework and field equations were introduced in Ref. [20], followed by the background
cosmological equations and model studies in Ref. [32]. Its dynamical properties were subsequently investigated in
Ref. [33], while early-universe scenarios and observational constraints were explored in Refs. [34–39]. Gravitational
waves in this framework propagate at the speed of light [40], consistent with multimessenger constraints [41, 42],
unlike the curvature-based analog [43]. The perturbative sector and stability properties were developed in Ref. [44],
while the evolution of matter density perturbations was studied in Ref. [45]. These studies have established several
interesting cosmological and perturbative properties of the theory. However, a unified treatment of spatially curved
FLRW backgrounds remains lacking. By reformulating the theory in terms of an effective scalar-field representation,

VI. CONCLUSION
The central technical contribution of this work is the construction of tetrads (22) for FLRW spacetimes that
explicitly incorporate the spatial curvature parameterkwithin a single unified expression, simultaneously valid for
openk=−1, flatk= 0, and closedk= +1geometries. The construction is obtained through a suitable local Lorentz
transformation of the diagonal tetrad and preserves the homogeneity and isotropy of the torsion invariants. The
admissibility of these tetrads is established by the vanishing of the antisymmetric sector of the field equations. Their
regular behavior in the flat limitκ→0provides a smooth and consistent recovery of the standard spatially flat results.
The ensuing expressions forTandT G in Eqs. (29)-(30) render the role of spatial curvature explicit and reveal an
important structural asymmetry. Although the standard Gauss–Bonnet invariant¯Gand its teleparallel counterpart
TG coincide on flat FLRW backgrounds, they differ by curvature-dependent boundary contributions wheneverk̸= 0.

This inequivalence has direct implications for the dynamics of theories that couple nonlinearly to these invariants.
Particularly, it distinguishes teleparallel Gauss–Bonnet gravity from its curvature-based counterpart at the dynamical
level.
As an initial application of this framework, we investigated the−T+F(TG)sector of teleparallel Gauss–Bonnet
gravity using a scalar-field representation combined with dynamical-systems techniques. The phase-space structure is
richer than in the flat case. The late-time attractor
k
P4 lies on the flat submanifoldΩk = 0and trajectories within its
basin of attraction asymptotically suppress the curvature contribution. The saddle points
k
P5 and
k
P6 characterized by
q=−1, correspond to transient de Sitter-like phases within phase space. The curved attractor line
k
P1 demonstrates
that spatial flatness is not a universal dynamical outcome. Trajectories lying in its basin asymptotically approach
states with non-vanishingΩk with the sign of the curvature contribution preserved. The coexistence of flat and curved
attractors shows that spatial curvature qualitatively alters the late-time dynamics.
The tetrad construction is not specific to theF(T,T G)theory. The irreducible torsion components derived in
Appendix B can be used in a broader class of torsion-based theories. The tetrad construction and the resulting
torsion invariants are independent of the matter sector. Thus, the framework can be extended to cosmological models
containing perfect fluids or minimally coupled scalar fields.
An important next step is to examine the implications of the present construction at the level of cosmological
perturbations, where differences between teleparallel and curvature-based formulations are expected to become more
pronounced. Such an analysis would simultaneously serve as a perturbative consistency test for the tetrad, since
differently rotated tetrads for closed cosmology are known to produce overconstrained perturbative systems [28].
Extending the dynamical systems analysis to include radiation and matter components would allow a more complete
cosmological history to be mapped out, including the transitions between matter-dominated and accelerated epochs
in the presence of non-zero curvature. Applying the unified tetrad to other modified teleparallel theories would help
determine whether the curvature-suppressing behavior is generic to torsion-based gravity or specific to the Gauss-
Bonnet sector.
