ABSTRACT 
We present a unified derivation of multiple fundamental physical constants and structural parameters from two 
relations — τ = ℏ/E and s = ℏc/E — applied to the boundary condition of Maxwell's spherical electromagnetic 
resonator. The first electromagnetic mode of the sphere (k·s = π, E_tangential = 0 on surface) has exactly two 
antinodes and one node along any diameter, proved analytically from the Helmholtz equation. The ratio of 
antinodes to total characteristic points, 2/3, is the single geometric fraction from which a chain of physical results 
follows without new postulates. 
We derive: (i) K = 1/ π as the ratio of the chronal and EM closure conditions; (ii) c as the unique Z₀ -invariance 
speed over one chronal cycle; (iii) spin -1/2 from the EM half-cycle; (iv) baryon asymmetry η = α⁴·(1/π)·(2/3) = 
6.018×10⁻¹⁰ (0.91% from Planck 2018, no free parameters); (v) the Koide ratio Q = 2/3 — first geometric 
explanation of this 40 -year-old observation; (vi) three lepton generations from three characteristic points; (vii) 
muon parameters δ_μ = 2/5 and γ_μ = 7/5 exactly; (viii) Koide angular symmetry θ_μ/θ_τ = 3π−1 and θ_e/θ_τ = 
3π+1 (0.022%); (ix) the exact rational θ_τ = 2/9 = 2/3² from the self-consistent Koide system; (x) lepton masses 
m_μ = 105.652 MeV and m_e = 0.510963 MeV predicted from m_ τ alone (0.006–0.007%). All results trace to 
three primitive numbers: 2 (antinodes), 3 (characteristic points), π (mode closure).

II. ANALYTIC PROOF: 2/3 FROM THE HELMHOLTZ EQUATION 
This section provides the rigorous derivation of the 2/3 fraction from the spherical resonator mode equation, 
closing open question #1 from the architecture. 
2.1 The Helmholtz equation and its solution 
The first radial mode of the spherical resonator satisfies the Helmholtz equation along any diameter (radial 
coordinate r ∈ [0, s]): 
d²Ψ/dr² + k²Ψ = 0    →    Ψ(r) = A·cos(kr) + B·sin(kr) 
Boundary conditions: (a) the field must be finite at r = 0 (no singularity), giving B = 0; (b) the tangential 
component of E vanishes on the surface r = s (Maxwell boundary condition): 
Ψ(s) = 0    →    cos(ks) = 0    →    ks = π/2 + nπ 
For the first mode (n = 0): k·s = π/2... but this gives the TE mode. For the TM mode relevant to the standing wave 
along the diameter, the condition is: 
dΨ/dr|_{r=s} = 0    →    −A·k·sin(ks) = 0    →    ks = nπ 
First non-trivial mode (n = 1): k·s = π. The standing wave solution is: 
Ψ(r) = A · cos(π·r/s) 
2.2 Counting nodes and antinodes 
Nodes (Ψ = 0): cos(π·r/s) = 0 → π·r/s = π/2 → r = s/2. On the interval r ∈ [0, s]: exactly one interior node. 
Including the two surface points r = 0 and r = s as characteristic points (where the boundary condition is applied): 
3 total characteristic points. 
Antinodes (|Ψ| = maximum): 
|cos(π·r/s)| = 1 → r = 0 (value +1) and r = s (value −1). Exactly 2 antinodes. 
N_antinodes / N_total = 2 / 3    Q.E.D.
