Abstract
We present a parameter-free topological derivation of the PMNS lepton mixing
matrix and neutrino mass spectrum within the Active Vacuum Framework (AVF).
The electron neutrino is constituted as an unanchored alternating four-body ring
of primitive charge quanta (ξ, ¯ξ,ξ, ¯ξ) withC 4 symmetry, whereq(ξ) = +e/3 and
q( ¯ξ) =−e/3. Static stress partitioning on this ring yields a heavy Majorana anchor
stateM ν = 20.21 MeV, a spatial-overlap Dirac massm D = 1.125 keV, and a
light active massm light
ν = 0.0626 eV via a topological Type-I seesaw, matching the
atmospheric scale without free parameters. Formation of theM ν anchor induces a
fractional vacuum deformationδ ν = 1/6, which screens the tree-level reactor angle
to sin2θ13 = 0.02220 (0.00σpull from NuFIT 5.3 [12]). Adiabatic transport of the
C4 ring through three inequivalent vacuum sectors locks the topological Berry phase
toγ B = 3π/2, uniquely predictingδ CP = 270◦. The atmospheric angle is derived
from a first-order torsional perturbation of the ring, giving sin2θ23 = 0.581 (−0.2σ).
The solar angle is derived analytically as sin 2θ12 = (1/3)(1−δ ν/2) = 11/36≈
0.3056 (+0.13σ), completing a parameter-free prediction of all three mixing angles
fromδ ν = 1/6 andλ 12 alone. The leptonic Jarlskog invariant isJ CP =−0.0331
(+0.0σ).
1 Introduction
The Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata (PMNS) matrix [1,2] encodes the quantum me-
chanical mismatch between neutrino flavour and mass eigenstates, serving as the leptonic
analogue of the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix in the quark sector [3, 4].
By parameterizing three mixing angles (θ 12,θ 23,θ 13) and a leptonic CP-violating phase
(δCP), it provides the mathematical foundation for neutrino flavour oscillations and offers
crucial insight into the flavour structure of the lepton sector [5].
Despite remarkable experimental progress, the origin of lepton flavour structure, neu-
trino mass generation, and the observed PMNS mixing pattern continues as one of the
important open problems in particle physics. Current global analyses determine the mix-
ing parameters with increasing precision, while leaving several fundamental questions
unresolved, including the neutrino mass ordering, the origin of leptonic CP violation,
and the underlying flavour symmetry responsible for the observed mixing pattern [6].

Discrete flavour symmetry models based on groups such asA 4 andS 4 naturally re-
produce the approximately tribimaximal (TBM) mixing baseline, but require additional
symmetry-breaking effects, extra scalar fields, or unconstrained parameters to accommo-
date the measured non-zero reactor angleθ 13 and leptonic CP violation [7,9].
Early theoretical efforts to explain the PMNS matrix were closely connected to Grand
Unified Theories (GUTs) based onSO(10) andSU(5) symmetry groups, where quark and
lepton mass matrices are related through common Yukawa structures and concepts such
as Quark–Lepton Complementarity [7]. A significant development is the Tribimaximal
(TBM) mixing pattern by Harrison, Perkins, and Scott [8], which provides a simple
leading-order description of leptonic mixing with sin 2θ12 = 1/3, sin 2θ23 = 1/2, and
θ13 = 0. This success stimulated extensive studies based on non-Abelian discrete flavour
symmetries, particularlyA 4 andS 4 [9]. However, the subsequent findings of a non-zero
reactor angle (θ 13≈8.5 ◦) by the Daya Bay and RENO experiments [10] necessitated
departures from exact TBM symmetry through symmetry-breaking corrections via the
introduction of ad-hoc flavon field alignments and unconstrained vacuum expectation
values, together with additional model parameters, thereby reducing the predictive power
of the original first-principles constructions.
To recover predictive power, more recent studies have explored modular flavour sym-
metries [11], where Yukawa couplings are replaced by modular forms of a complex modulus
fieldτ, leading to highly constrained flavour structures with fewer free parameters. In
parallel, alternative approaches based on topological constructions and vacuum-inspired
geometric models have also been investigated, seeking to relate flavour mixing directly to
underlying geometric or dynamical structures rather than to additional flavour symme-
tries [15].
The objective of the present work is to investigate whether the observed leptonic
flavour mixing can be derived from first principles without introducing phenomenological
flavour potentials, flavon fields, or unconstrained scalar sectors. Building upon our earlier
topological construction of leptons [14], we examine whether bound states of primitive
charge quanta (ξ, ¯ξ) and their associated geometric overlap amplitudes are sufficient to
determine the structure of the PMNS matrix. Specifically, we derive the leptonic mixing
angles (θ 13,θ 12,θ 23) and the leptonic CP-violating phaseδ CP from the underlying topo-
logical configuration, thereby providing a unified geometric origin for neutrino flavour
mixing. The formulation is further constrained by the quark-sector AVF construction
developed in [15], where the same charge geometry successfully reproduces the CKM
matrix, allowing quark and lepton flavour mixing to be described within a common the-
oretical model. By modelling leptons as topological bound states of primitive charge
quanta carrying charges±e/3, we establish the causal sequence
Topology (C4)−→M ν −→δ ν −→ {θ13, θ12} −→ {δCP, θ23},(1)
where each stage is determined uniquely by quantities obtained at the preceding level.
The construction is fully consistent with the quark-sector AVF formulation of [15], in
which the same geometric charge structure reproduces all nine CKM matrix magnitudes
within 0.51σof the PDG 2024 central values. The fundamental coupling scaleλ 12 =
0.23082 is inherited directly from the quark sector, providing a universal electroweak–
geometric scale that links quark and lepton flavour mixing within a single topological
framework.
