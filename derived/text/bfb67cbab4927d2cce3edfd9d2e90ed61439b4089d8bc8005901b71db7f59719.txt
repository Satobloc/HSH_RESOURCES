BV Pushforward of Palatini–Cartan gravity
Giovanni Canepa, Alberto S. Cattaneo ∗
July 10, 2025
Abstract
The goal of this note is to show that the standard BV formulation of gravity in the
Palatini–Cartan formalism is equivalent, using a BV pushforward, to an AKSZ-like version
compatible with BFV data on the boundary.
1 Introduction
Gravity in the Palatini–Cartan (PC) formalism 1 has the advantage of using differential forms,
which makes the boundary (and lower-dimensional corners) analysis more transparent. On the
flip side, in dimension 4 and higher, its BV formulation [BV77] is not directly compatible with
the boundary BFV formulation [BF83]. As such, PC gravity cannot be directly presented as
an extended field theory, which would be the first, semiclassical, step towards a quantization
satisfying Segal’s axioms [Seg88].
A possible way out, on a “globally hyperbolic” space–time M = Σ×I, Σ being a Cauchy
surface, was studied in [CCS21a] (see also [AV98; Ale00; Bar01; DKS09; RP09; BTT13; CS19b;
MRC19; Mon+20; CCS21b]), where the AKSZ construction [Ale+97] was applied to the BFV
description of the boundary’s reduced phase space. The resulting theory automatically has
a compatible BV-BFV structure [CMR14] and is BV embedded in the BV description of PC
gravity. The goal of this paper is to show that this embedding is actually a BV equivalence, via
BV pushforward.
More explicitly, we consider PC theory in N space–time dimensions, N≥ 4. We pick once
and for all a vector bundle V over space–time M isomorphic to its tangent bundle and carrying
a Minkowski (or Euclidean) inner product. The action reads

If M has a boundary Σ, we require e to induce a nondegenerate metric on it (we might
also more strictly require the metric induced by e to be positive-definite, which would make Σ
space-like). Under this assumption, it was shown in [CS19b] that the reduced phase space can be
obtained as the reduction of a coisotropic submanifold (i.e., defined by first-class constraints) of
a symplecting space of boundary fields consistingV-coframes and connections on Σ satisfying an
appropriate condition (called the structural constraint). As such, the reduced phase space can
be cohomologically resolved in terms of the BFV formalism. This is explicitly done in [CCS21b].
The problem is that the bulk BV theory does not communicate well with the boundary BV
theory, due to some irregularity in a quotient appearing in the course of the construction [CS19a].
In this paper, also extending the classical construction of [CC25], we show how to remedy
this when working on a space–time M of the form Σ×I, where I is an interval or a circle, and
e is required to induce a nondegenerate metric on each slice Σ ×{t}, t∈I. (Under the stricter
condition that each of these metrics is positive-definite, this makes M into a globally hyperbolic
manifold for each allowed coframe fielde.) We can now split the BV fields of the theory into their
Σ and I components. It turns out that part of the Σ component of the connection is redundant,
as already observed at the classical level in [CC25]. Here we show that upon gauge fixing it
can be integrated away. This is a partial BV integration, known as a BV pushforward, which in
the present case leads to an equivalence between the original theory and the one resulting after
pushforward. The latter is then shown to be that described in [CCS21a] as a result of the AKSZ
construction for the BFV structure associated to Σ. In conclusion, we prove that PC gravity, on
Σ×I, is equivalent to a theory with a well-defined BV-BFV structure, in the sense specified in
Section 2.2. In particular, this means that the expectation values of corresponding observables
in the two theories are the same, hence carrying the same physical information.
Summary of the paper
In Section 2, we recall the BV pushforward and its properties. In Section 3, we recall the BV
formalism for PC theory and for the AKSZ construction starting from BFV on a boundary. In
Sections 4 and 5, we compute the BV pushforward showing the equivalence between the two
theories, ignoring boundary effects. This is legitimate if I is a circle or if I is an open interval
and we let the fields decay to zero towards its boundary. If I is a compact interval, which is
the interesting case to relate bulk to boundary, we need a more refined analysis, taking care of
boundary conditions. This is explained in Section 6.

6 Dealing with the boundary
In the previous sections, we have ignored the fact that the interval I has boundary points. Two
simple ways out are either to work on Σ×S1 or to take I as an open interval (we can even take
I = R) and to assume fast decay of the fields towards the boundary of I. In any of these two
cases, the results of the paper hold exactly as stated so far.
It is however interesting to consider also the case whenI is a compact interval (we also assume
that Σ is a closed manifold). In this case we have to deal with the boundary structure and to
impose appropriate boundary conditions when performing the BV pushforward.
6.1 The pre–BV-BFV formalism
If a BV theory, for a closed space–time manifold, is put on a compact manifold with boundary,
the master equation may be spoiled. Actually, what typically does not hold anymore is the
condition that the cohomological vector field Q is the hamiltonian vector field of the BV action
S: in fact, the original equation ιQω = δS, true for a closed space–time manifold, may now be
spoiled by boundary terms arising by integration by parts in the computation of the functional
derivativeδS. We recollect here the details of the construction to circumvent this problem (this
is an adapted reformulation of the construction of [CMR11; CMR14; CMR18]).
Definition 15 (Relaxed BV manifold) . A relaxed BV manifold is a quadruple ( F,S,ϖ,Q ),
whereF is a graded manifold and S,ϖ,Q are respectively a function, a closed 2-form, and a
vector field onF satisfying the following:
• Q is cohomological, i.e., it is odd of degree 1 and [ Q,Q ] = 0;
• ϖ is odd of degree −1 and S is even of degree 0. 6
Given this, we define
α :=ιQϖ−δS,
ϖ :=δα =−LQϖ.
Immediate consequences of Cartan’s calculus, degree considerations, and the condition [Q,Q ] = 0
are the following:
1. α and ϖ are even of degree 0.
2. There exists a unique odd function S of degree 1 satisfying ιQϖ =δS.
This shows that (F,S,α,Q ) is an exact preBFV manifold according to the following
Definition 16 (BFV). A preBFV manifold is a quadruple ( eF,eS,eϖ, eQ), where eF is a graded
manifold and eS,eϖ, eQ are respectively a function, a closed 2-form, and a vector field on F satis-
fying the following:
• eQ is cohomological, i.e., it is odd of degree 1 and [ eQ,eQ] = 0;
• eϖ is even of degree 0 and eS is odd of degree 1;
• eQ is a hamiltonian vector field of eS, i.e., ι eQeϖ =δeS.
