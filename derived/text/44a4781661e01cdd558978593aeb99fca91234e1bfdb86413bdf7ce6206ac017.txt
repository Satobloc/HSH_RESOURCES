Abstract
The Gell–Mann–Nishijima relation is given by:Q=T 3 + Y
2 . It provides the funda-
mental connection between electric chargeQ, weak isospinT 3, and hyperchargeY.
Although it successfully reproduces the observed electric charges of all elementary
particles, the physical origin of hypercharge and its relation to chirality remain im-
plicit within the conventional formulation. Besides, it does not yield a consistent
definition of hypercharge across fundamental fermions and composite particles. In
this work, a generalized Gell–Mann–Nishijima relation is developed by introducing
an additional internal quantum coordinate, the hyperspin projectionH 3, associated
with the chiral degree of freedom. The resulting generic charge relation,
Q=T 3 +F+H 3,
whereF= (B+SN+CN+BN+T N−L)/2 is the generalized flavour contri-
bution constructed from the conserved matter quantum numbers, provides a single
unified formula valid for all fundamental fermions and all composite hadrons with-
out any arbitrary hypercharge assignments. The hypercharge is shown to have two
physically distinct components,
Y=Y F +YH , Y F =B+SN+CN+BN+T N−L, Y H = 2H3,
whereY F is determined by conserved matter quantum numbers andY H arises
from the internal chiral orientation. The internal quantum state is represented
by the complex hyperspin coordinateH=T 3 +iH 3, which satisfies the invariant
T 2
3 +H 2
3 = 1/4, providing a unified geometrical interpretation of weak isospin and
chirality. The combined projectionP 3 =T 3 +H 3 =± 1
2 is shown to be chirality-
independent, equalling the quark-model isospinI 3 for both handedness states of
the same flavour. The formulation is validated for the complete set of Standard
Model fundamental fermions across all three generations. The formalism is fur-
ther extended to representative composite hadrons, including the pion triplet and
nucleon isospin doublet, through the additivity of constituent quantum numbers.
Keywords: Gell–Mann–Nishijima relation; hyperspin; chirality; weak isospin; hyper-
charge; electroweak interactions; fundamental fermions; composite hadrons; particle quan-

1 Introduction
The classification of strongly interacting particles into symmetry multiplets has been
one of the major achievements of modern particle physics. During the early 1950s, the
discovery of strange particles, such as the Λ baryon and theKmesons, presented an
apparent paradox: they were produced through the strong interaction on timescales of
approximately 10 −23 s but decayed through the weak interaction with lifetimes of the
order of 10−10 s. To explain this behaviour, Nakano and Nishijima [1], and independently
Gell–Mann [2], introduced strangenessSas a conserved quantum number for strong
interactions. This development led to the Gell–Mann–Nishijima (GNN) relation,
Q=I 3 + B+S
2 =I 3 + Y
2,(1)
whereQis the electric charge,I 3 is the isospin projection,Bis the baryon number,
andY=B+Sis the hadronic hypercharge. The relation provided the foundation for
the SU(3) flavour classification known as the Eightfold Way [3, 4], culminating in the
successful prediction of the Ω − baryon and subsequently the quark model with fractional
electric charges [5]. With the development of the electroweak theory, the original hadronic
relation was reformulated in terms of weak isospinT 3 and weak hypercharge,
Q=T 3 + Y
2,(2)
which forms one of the fundamental relations of the Standard Model. Although this
expression reproduces the observed electric charges of all fundamental fermions, the hy-
percharge values for left- and right-handed fermions are assigned differently without an
internal geometric explanation. For quarks,Y= 1/3 is assigned to bothq L doublets,
whileY= 4/3 andY=−2/3 are assigned to the singletsu R andd R respectively —
values that are introduced as representation labels of the gauge symmetry without a
common physical origin.
In this work, we propose a generalized Gell–Mann–Nishijima relation by introducing
an additional internal quantum coordinate, thehyperspin projectionH 3, associated with
the chiral degree of freedom. The generic charge relation,
Q=T 3 +F+H 3,(3)
whereF= (B+SN+CN+BN+TN−L)/2 is the generalized flavour contribution,
which is valid for every particle, such as fundamental fermions and composite hadrons
alike, without any additional arbitrary assignments. Comparison with Eq. (2) shows that
the hypercharge decomposes as
Y=Y F +YH, Y F =B+SN+CN+BN+TN−L, Y H = 2H3.(4)
The componentY F is determined entirely by the conserved matter quantum numbers
and is the same for both chiral states of the same flavour. The componentY H = 2H 3
carries all the chirality dependence. The varying hypercharge assignments of the Standard
Model therefore arise naturally from a single source: the chiral orientation of the internal
hyperspin coordinate. The present work pursues three specific objectives:
i.Generic charge formulation.A single charge relationQ=T 3 +F+H 3, valid
for all fundamental fermions and all composite hadrons, is established in Section 2.

2 Generalized GNN Relation in Hyperspin Space
2.1 Generic Charge Relation and Hyperspin
The Standard Model describes fermions through their electroweak quantum numbers.
Left-handed fermions transform as weak-isospin doublets withT 3 =± 1
2, whereas right-
handed fermions are weak-isospin singlets withT 3 = 0. The conventional charge relation
Q=T 3 +Y/2 reproduces all observed charges, but requires distinct hypercharge values
to be specified separately for each chiral state.
In the present work, left- and right-handed chiral sectors are represented within a
common internal quantum space by introducing an additional internal coordinate, termed
hyperspin. The weak-isospin projectionT 3 and the hyperspin projectionH 3 are orthogonal
components of a single internal quantum state, so that chirality becomes the orientation
of the hyperspin coordinate rather than two independent quantum assignments.

5 Discussion and Outlook
The generalized hyperspin formulation provides a single unified charge formula,
Q=T 3 +F+H 3, F= B+SN+CN+BN+TN−L
2 ,
that is valid for all particles without exception. The Standard Model hypercharge is not
an independent fundamental quantity but decomposes naturally into two physically dis-
tinct components:Y F , determined by conserved matter quantum numbers and chirality-
independent, andY H = 2H3, determined by the internal chiral orientation.
The practical consequence is that the “arbitrary” hypercharge assignments of the
Standard Model are not arbitrary at all: they follow from two rules applied universally.
(i) Every quark carriesY F = 1/3; every lepton carriesY F =−1. (ii) Every LH state
addsY H = 0; every RH up-type state addsY H = +1; every RH down-type state adds
YH =−1. These two rules, combined withQ=T 3+Y/2, reproduce all known hypercharge
assignments simultaneously.
The representation of the internal quantum state by the complex coordinateH=
T3 +iH 3 with invariant modulus|H|= 1
2 suggests that chirality is a geometric orientation
in a two-dimensional internal space. The phase angleθ= tan −1(H3/T3) takes the values 0
(LH) andπ/2 (RH) for CP-symmetric states. CP-violating interactions may correspond
to intermediate orientations; the extension of the hyperspin formalism to CP violation
and quark mixing is a natural direction for future work.
The chirality-invariant combined projectionP 3 =T 3 +H 3 reconciles the electroweak
assignmentT 3 = 0 for RH quarks with the quark-model assignmentI 3 =± 1
2 for all
quarks: the quark-modelI 3 isP 3, notT 3 alone.

6 Conclusions
A generalized Gell–Mann–Nishijima relation has been developed using the hyperspin
coordinateH=T 3 +iH 3. The generic charge relation,
Q=T 3 +F+H 3, F= B+SN+CN+BN+TN−L
2 ,
is valid for all fundamental fermions and all composite hadrons within a single unified
formula. The main conclusions are:
Universal applicability.The generic relation covers all particles. For fundamental
fermions (SN=CN=BN=TN= 0) it reduces toQ=T 3 + (B−L)/2 +H 3. For
composite hadrons (H 3 = 0) it reduces to the classical GNN relationQ=T 3 +YF/2.
Hypercharge decomposition.The Standard Model hypercharge decomposes asY=
YF +Y H, whereY F =B+SN+CN+BN+TN−Lis chirality-independent and
YH = 2H3 carries all chirality dependence. This eliminates the apparent arbitrariness of
SM hypercharge assignments.
Hyperspin invariant.T 2
3 +H 2
3 = 1
4 holds for all fundamental fermions across all
three generations and both chiralities, making chirality a 90 ◦ rotation of the hyperspin
coordinate.
Chirality invariant.P 3 =T 3 +H 3 =± 1
2 is the same for both chiral states of every
flavour, identifyingP 3 with the quark-model isospinI 3.
Validation.The formulation has been verified for all 24 chiral states of the three fermion
generations (Table 4) and for representative composite hadrons (Table 5).
Classical GNN recovered.The classical GNN relation is theH 3 = 0 limit of the
generic formula, confirming backward compatibility with all existing results.
