Abstract
In this work, we investigate static and spherically symmetric traversable wormhole
geometries in the linear  gravity model. By adopting
the Morris–Thorne metric with a finite redshift function 
and an anisotropic fluid satisfying a radially varying equation of state
 we reconstruct the shape function 
from the modified field equations. The resulting reconstruction equation is a first-
order linear differential equation, whose formal integral solution is obtained after
imposing the throat condition . The flare-out condition, regularity of the
reconstruction equation, and asymptotic flatness are used to identify an admissible
parameter branch of the model. For representative parameter choices, the
reconstructed solutions satisfy the required wormhole conditions. The radial null
energy condition of the physical anisotropic matter sector is violated near the throat,
as expected from the flare-out condition, while the tangential NEC remains non-
negative over the representative numerical diagnostic domain. This indicates that the
exoticity is mainly localized in the radial sector close to the throat. We also analyze
the null geodesics and gravitational lensing properties of the reconstructed geometry.
The off-throat circular null-orbit condition is governed by the redshift function and
shows that no off-throat photon sphere exists for . However, in the two-
sided wormhole extension, the throat itself can act as an unstable light ring.
Therefore, the finite deflection angles obtained for photons with closest approach
radius  describe the weak-to-moderate lensing branch away from the throat-
light-ring limit. The embedding diagram confirms the regular flare-out structure of
the reconstructed spatial geometry. These results show that the linear 
model admits reconstructed wormhole configurations with localized energy-
condition violation and characteristic off-throat lensing features.

Introduction
In General Relativity (GR) and its extensions, wormholes arise as nontrivial
topological configurations connecting asymptotically distinct regions through a
throat, with their physical viability depending on the relation between geometry and
the supporting matter source. The concept originated with the Einstein–Rosen bridge
[1] and earlier geometrical insights by Flamm [2], while Wheeler and collaborators
later introduced the notion of multiply connected spacetime [3]. The modern
framework of traversable wormholes was formulated by Morris and Thorne [4], who
demonstrated that a static, spherically symmetric geometry described by a redshift
function  and a shape function  can, in principle, allow passage between two
regions of spacetime. However, such configurations require the violation of classical
energy conditions, particularly the null energy condition (NEC), at or near the throat
[5], [6]. Furthermore, Olum [7] established that any form of superluminal travel
necessarily involves negative energy densities, thereby linking traversable wormholes
to exotic matter.
Within GR, this requirement poses a fundamental limitation. Known solutions such
as the Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole [8], [9] rely on phantom scalar fields that violate the
NEC, and extensive analyses have shown that ordinary classical matter cannot sustain
wormhole geometries [6], [10]. This difficulty has motivated the study of modified
theories of gravity, where additional geometric contributions may effectively mimic
exotic matter and support wormhole configurations without requiring unphysical
sources. Among the most extensively studied extensions is  gravity, in which the
Einstein–Hilbert action is generalized to an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar . In
this framework, higher-order curvature terms can support wormhole geometries
while allowing the matter content to satisfy, or nearly satisfy, the energy conditions
[11], [12], [13]. Recent investigations have further demonstrated the viability of
wormholes in  gravity with additional physical ingredients such as Casimir
effects and dark matter sources [14], [15]. Extensions such as  gravity [16],
[17] and  gravity [18] introduce explicit matter–geometry couplings,
leading to modified field equations where effective stress–energy contributions arise
from curvature. Several studies have shown that in such theories the violation of the
NEC can be minimized or confined to localized regions near the throat [19], [20], [21].
More recent works have confirmed that viable wormhole solutions can be obtained
in these frameworks under suitable parameter choices and matter distributions [22],
[23], [24].
Beyond curvature-based modifications, wormholes have also been investigated in
alternative gravitational frameworks. In Gauss–Bonnet and  gravity, higher-
curvature invariants provide additional support for wormhole geometries [25], [26].
Teleparallel and symmetric teleparallel theories, such as  and  [27], [28],
[29], [30], as well as their extended matter–geometry coupled framework 
gravity [31], introduce torsion and non-metricity as fundamental geometrical

wormhole solutions sustained by scalar fields [32], [33], [34]. These results

The  gravity model proposed by Haghani and Harko [50] unifies the
dependence on , , and  within a single action, thereby extending both 
and  theories. The resulting field equations contain explicit matter–
geometry coupling terms that modify the effective stress–energy sector. In the
context of wormhole physics, previous studies have shown that such matter–
geometry couplings can redistribute the effective sources entering the gravitational
field equations, allowing the required energy-condition violation in some
constructions to be reduced or partially attributed to the effective geometric sector
[51]. Additional studies incorporating non-commutative effects and different
equations of state suggest that wormhole solutions in this framework are not
restricted to a particular matter prescription [52].

curvature sector remains second order and contains no additional higher-derivative

algebraic dependence on  and . Moreover, for the parameter branch considered
here, Eq. (32) shows that the physical and effective null contractions are proportional.
Consequently, the radial NEC violation required at the throat remains a genuine
property of the physical anisotropic matter sector in the present construction. Thus,
the model provides a comparatively transparent setting in which the effects of the
two algebraic matter–geometry couplings can be studied in the reconstruction of the
wormhole shape function.
From an observational perspective, gravitational lensing provides a powerful tool to
probe compact objects and distinguish wormholes from black holes. Early work by
Cramer et al. [53] suggested that wormholes could act as gravitational lenses with
distinctive observational signatures. Subsequent studies analyzed lensing in specific
wormhole spacetimes, including Ellis and Morris–Thorne geometries [54], [55], [56].
Depending on their metric functions, wormholes may exhibit lensing features
different from those of black holes, including finite deflection angles in
configurations without off-throat photon spheres [57], [58]. More recent
developments have extended these analyses to strong-field regimes, including
photon trajectories, shadows, and other lensing observables [59], [60], [61], [62].
These studies suggest that gravitational lensing may provide a useful avenue for
probing wormhole geometries in astrophysical settings.

Although traversable wormholes have been widely investigated in modified gravity
theories such as , , and  gravity, many analyses proceed by
prescribing one or both metric functions and then determining the associated matter
content. Against this background, the present work focuses on a specific
reconstruction route in which the shape function is not prescribed independently. We
prescribe a regular non-constant redshift function and a radially varying radial
equation of state and reconstruct  directly from the modified field equations. The
resulting geometry is then subjected to throat and asymptotic viability tests, physical
energy-condition diagnostics, an analysis of off-throat circular null orbits and the
throat light ring, and a numerical evaluation of photon trajectories and deflection
angles. This combined reconstruction and lensing analysis constitutes the specific
focus of the present work.
The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the Morris–
Thorne geometry, formulate the field equations of  gravity, and
specialize them to the linear model considered here. In Section 3, we prescribe the
redshift function and radial equation of state, reconstruct the shape function, and
establish the analytical and numerical viability conditions of the resulting wormhole
branches. Section 4 examines the physical energy conditions and quantifies the
localization of the NEC-violating matter. In Section 5, we analyze the null geodesics,
off-throat circular-null-orbit condition, throat light ring, impact parameter, photon
trajectories, deflection angle, and embedding geometry. The main results and
limitations are summarized in the concluding section.
