Geometric Quantum Confinement and Spontaneous Localization:
Solving the Schr¨ odinger-Newton System in a Cubic Acoustic Cavity
lefliti mohamed∗
(Dated: July 17, 2026)
We investigate the interplay between quantum mechanics and gravitation within the framework of
analogue gravity. By confining a quantum fluid (Bose-Einstein condensate) in a three-dimensional
cubic trap, we model the coupled dynamics of the wavefunction and its self-induced gravitational
field via the Schr¨ odinger-Newton (SN) equations. The Dirichlet boundary conditions imposed by the
rigid walls naturally quantize the system into discrete eigenmodes, while the nonlinear gravitational
coupling generates a mass-dependent energy shift and leads to a finite decoherence time for macro-
scopic superpositions. We derive analytical expressions for the self-energy and the gravitational
decoherence rate, showing that the system provides a geometric mechanism for the quantum-to-
classical transition. Our predictions are compared with current analogue gravity experiments (BECs
and water tanks), and specific testable regimes are identified for future laboratory implementations.

I. INTRODUCTION
The unification of quantum mechanics and general rel-
ativity remains one of the most profound challenges in
modern physics. While a complete theory of quantum
gravity is still elusive, important insights have emerged
fromanalogue gravity[1, 3, 4], where condensed matter
systems simulate curved spacetimes. Among the most
promising phenomena is theSchr¨ odinger-Newton(SN)
equation [5–7], which models the self-gravitational in-
teraction of a quantum system and provides a possible
mechanism for wavefunction collapse.
The SN equation couples the quantum wavefunction
ψ(r,t) to its own gravitational potential Φ g(r,t):

∇2Φg = 4πGm|ψ|2.(2)
Despite its conceptual appeal, the SN equation has
been notoriously difficult to solve analytically, particu-
larly for spatially extended systems. In this work, we
adopt ananalogue gravityperspective: we regard the
quantum fluid (e.g., a Bose-Einstein condensate) as an
analogue spacetime, and we confine it within a rigidcu-
bic cavityof sideL. The cavity imposes Dirichlet bound-
ary conditions on the wavefunction, naturally discretiz-
ing the spectrum and allowing for exact analytical solu-
tions in the linear limit, with perturbative treatment of
the gravitational self-interaction.
Our approach builds upon the extensive theoretical
framework of analogue gravity, as reviewed in the sem-
inal work of Barcel´ o, Liberati, and Visser [2, 4]. The
cubic geometry provides an ideal testbed for studying:
1. The quantization of the acoustic metric.
2. The backreaction of the quantum field on the ge-
ometry (self-gravity).

V. COMP ARISON WITH ANALOGUE
GRA VITY EXPERIMENTS
A. Bose-Einstein Condensates
Recent experiments by Steinhauer and collabora-
tors [11–13] have demonstrated the realization of acous-
tic black holes in BECs. Our cubic geometry can be
implemented using optical lattice potentials to trap the
BEC in a box-like configuration [14, 15]. The predicted
decoherence timeτ deph∼10−2 s form∼10 4 amu falls
within the reach of current BEC technologies.
B. W ater T ank Experiments
Water tank experiments by Rousseaux et al. [16, 17]
and Weinfurtner et al. [18, 19] have observed classical
analogues of Hawking radiation. Our model extends
these experiments into the quantum domain, where self-
gravity could be measured as a small perturbation to the
surface wave dispersion.
C. Optical and Metamaterial Analogues
The effective metric formalism also applies to op-
tics [20, 21] and metamaterials [22, 23]. Our results can
be translated to these systems by replacing the gravita-
tional couplingGwith an effective nonlinearity param-
eter.
VI. CONCLUSIONS AND OUTLOOK
We have presented a comprehensive analytical treat-
ment of the Schr¨ odinger-Newton system confined in a
cubic acoustic cavity. Our main findings are:
1. The cubic geometry naturally quantizes the wave-
function into discrete eigenstates.
2. Gravitational self-interaction introduces a mass-
dependent energy shift ∆E∼−Gm 2/L.
3. The energy difference between eigenstates leads to
a finite decoherence timeτ∼ℏL/(Gm 2).
4. Our predictions are consistent with existing ana-
logue gravity experiments and suggest new testable
regimes for future laboratory investigations.
Future work will extend this analysis to:
•Time-dependent boundary conditions (dynamic
boxes).
•Coupling to external potentials (e.g., harmonic
traps).
•Incorporation of modified dispersion relations and
higher-order corrections.
•Full numerical solutions of the SN system in three
dimensions.
