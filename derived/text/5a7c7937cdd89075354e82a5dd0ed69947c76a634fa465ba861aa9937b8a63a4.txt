ABSTRACT
Does the mathematical straight line actually exist in our physical world? Since the observation 
of circular trajectories at all scales is undeniable, this manuscript chooses to answer “no” to that 
question and therefore presents a theory of rotation. From the concept of relativistic rigid body 
rotation introduced in a recent publication, we present in this manuscript the theoretical 
foundation of a mechanics entirely built on the base of a dynamic space trihedron composed of 
rotation, velocity and distance vectors. 
Introduction
The starting point of the rotation theory presented in this manuscript is the relativist 
description of a physical phenomenon that, like a sea serpent, repeatedly returned several times 
to question physicists: rigid body rotation. It was indeed omnipresent right at the beginning of 
modern sciences through, for example, vortex theories of René Descartes or William Kelvin. 
More recently, it was proposed to Albert Einstein by Kurt Gödel [1] as a universe model. It was 
also the main support used by Lev Landau [2] to introduce general relativity. However, rigid 
body rotation presents a big flaw of connection to infinity: at large enough distances, the speed 
can reach and overcome light speed. This difficulty can be avoided, and this is the main idea of 
the following developments, the basement on which the whole theoretical construction is built: 
rigid body rotation has a horizon at finite distance [3]. This latter property of spatial 
confinement [4] is the central constitutive element of relativistic rigid body rotation that shall 
now be described.  
Background of relativistic rigid body rotation 
The relativistic rigid body rotation has already been described in a previous publication 
[3]. Some useful properties are quickly recalled here. First, as any rigid body rotation, it is 
composed of an angular rotation ω, a velocity v and a distance radius r0 from the center O. 
These three vectors are connected by the equation v = ω ∧ r0, figure 1. The entire originality 
lies in the fact that the physical path followed by a light ray emitted from O is not the straight 
line r0.  Instead, this radial path imposed by the Coriolis acceleration, follows a simple circle 
called “space circle”. The arc of space circle to the satellite position is deduced from the angle 
rotation Ω and the half radius vector of the rotation R

curvature, a property necessary for the definition of any mass. With some boldness, let us
