Abstract. Let Σ be a closed orientable surface of genus at least
two, and let X,Y be distinct marked Riemann surface structures
on Σ, possibly with opposite orientations. In this paper, we show
that there are (exactly) countably infinite pairs of CP1-structures
on X and on Y sharing holonomy π1(Σ)→ PSL2C.

1. Introduction
Let Σ be a closed orientable surface of genus g at least two. A quasi-
Fuchsian representationρ: π1(Σ)→ PSL2C is a “typical” discrete and
faithful representation, such that the limit set of the discrete subgroup
Imρ is a Jordan curve on CP1. Quasi-Fuchsian representations are
important for the study of hyperbolic three-manifolds. It has various
equivalent definitions (such as convex cocompact representations), and
also generalizations to representations into other Lie groups. (For other
rank-one Lie groups, see Bowditch [Bow95]. See also [KL18]). In this
paper, we investigate another type of generalization of quasi-Fuchsian
representations within the framework of π1(Σ)→ PSL2C.

LetS be the surface Σ with a fixed orientation, andS∗ be Σ with the
opposite orientation. Let T be the Teichm¨ uller space ofS, the defor-
mation space of all marked Riemann surface structures onS. Similarly
let T∗ be the Teichm¨ uller space ofS∗. Given a quasi-Fuchsian repre-
sentation ρ: π1(S)→ PSL2C, let Λ be the limit set of Im ρ. Then its
complement CP1\ Λ is a union of disjoint topological open disks Ω +
and Ω−, and the quotients Ω+/Imρ and Ω−/Imρ have marked Riemann
surface structures onS andS∗. Bers’ simultaneous uniformization the-
orem ([Ber60]) asserts that, for every pair of Riemann surface structures
X on S and Y on S∗, there is unique quasi-Fuchsian representation
ρ: π1(S)→ PSL2C, such that X and Y are biholomorphic to Ω +/Imρ
and Ω−/Imρ. By this correspondence, the space QF of quasi-Fuchsian
representations, the quasi-Fuchsian space , is indeed biholomorphic to
T× T∗ ([Ber60], see also [Hub06, EK06]). In particular, the quasi-
Fuchsian representation ρ is the unique transversal intersection point
of the Bers’ slices{X}× T∗ and T×{T} in QF.
The quotient surfaces Ω +/Imρ and Ω−/Imρ have not only Riemann
surface structures but also have CP1-structures (or complex projective
structures) on S and S∗, which correspond to holomorphic quadratic
differentials on these Riemann surfaces. From a viewpoint of CP1-
structures, the simultaneous uniformization theorem can equivalently
be stated as follows, without the notion of quasi-Fuchsian representa-
tions: Given a pair of Riemann surface structures X onS andY inS∗,
there is a unique pair of CP1-structure CX on X and a CP1-structure
CY on Y such that
• the holonomy representation π1(Σ)→ PSL2C of CX coincides
with the holonomy representation π1(Σ)→ PSL2C of CY (up
to conjugation), and
• the developing maps ˜S→ CP1 of CX and ˜S∗→ CP1 CY are
injective, where ˜S and ˜S∗ are the universal covers of S andS∗,
respectively.
In this paper, we consider a more general realization problem of a
pair of Riemann surface structures X and Y on either S or S∗ by a
pair of CP1-structures CX and CY sharing holonomy. In this general
setting without the restriction of the injectivity and the orientation, we
show that there are infinitely many realizing pairs:

3. Preliminaries
3.1. Teichm¨ uller rays.(See [FM12, §11] for instance.) The Teichm¨ uller
space T of S is the space of marked Riemann surface structures on S
up to isotopy. Given two (marked) Riemann surfaces X,Y ∈ T, let
K = K(X,Y ) denote the infimum of the quasi-conformal dilatations
Kf among all quasi-conformal mappings X→Y preserving the mark-
ing of the surface. The Teichm¨ uller distancebetweenX and Y on T is
given by
d(X,Y ) = 1
2 logK,
which gives a Finsler metric on T, call the Teichm¨ uller metric.
A geodesic in T in the Teichm¨ uller metric is called aTeichm¨ uller ge-
odesic. Then, given X ∈ T and a measured foliation V on S, there
is a Teichm¨ uller geodesicXt with X0 = X along which V “confor-
mally shrinks”. Namely, by Hubbard and Masur [HM79], there is a
flat surface E = E(X,V ) conformal to X such that V is the vertical
measured foliation. In this paper, a flat surface is an Euclidean surface
with isolated cone singularities whose cone angles are Z≥3-multiples of
π. Then, we can obtain a ray of flat surfaces Et obtained by stretching
in the horizontal direction by et/2 and shrinking in the horizontal di-
rection by e−t/2. The conformal structure of Et gives the Teichm¨ uller
geodesic at unit speed.
3.2. CP1-structures. (General references are [Dum09], [Kap01, Chap-
ter 7], [Gol22, Chapter 14].) Recall that PSL 2C is the automorphism
group of CP1. Then, a CP1-structure on a surface is a ( CP1, PSL2C)-
structure. Namely, an atlas of embedding open subsets covering S into
CP1 such that transition maps are given by elements in PSL2C. Clearly,

August 11, 2025 7
each CP1-structure has a Riemann surface structure since transition
maps preserve complex structure.
CP1-structures can be viewed from various viewpoints, including the
ones we discuss below.
3.2.1. Developing pairs. A CP1-structure can equivalently be defined
using a “global coordinate” on the universal cover ˜S of S. Namely, a
CP1-structure on X is a pair (f,ρ ) of
• a local diffeomorphism f : ˜S→ CP1 (developing map) and
• a homomorphismρ: π1(S)→ PSL2C (holonomy representation),
such that f is ρ-equivariant (i.e. fγ = ρ(γ)f for all γ∈ π1(S)). This
developing pair is defined up to PSL 2C. Namely (f,ρ ) is equivalent to
(αf,αρα−1) for all α∈ PSL2C.
3.2.2. Schwarzian parametrization. Next we explain an analytic view-
point of a CP1-structure. A CP1-structureC = (f,ρ ) onS corresponds
to a holomorphic quadratic differential q = ϕdz 2 on a Riemann sur-
face. The developing map f induces a Riemann surface structureX on
S, and the Schwarzian derivative off induces a holomorphic quadratic
differential on X. Thus a space of CP1-structures on a Riemann sur-
face X is an affine vector space of holomorphic quadratic differentials
on X.
There is a unique marked hyperbolic structure σ uniformizing X.
A hyperbolic structure is, in particular, a CP1-structure, and, in this
paper, we pick this hyperbolic structure to be the zero of this vector
space— in other words, when we take the Schwarzian derivative of f,
the domain ˜X, the universal cover of X, is identified with the upper
half plane of C by the uniformization theorem.
Let P be the space of all marked CP1-structures onS. Let ψ : P→ T
be the projection map which takes each CP1-structure to its complex
structure.

3.2.5. 2π-grafting. A 2π-grafting is, in general, a cut-and-paste oper-
ation of a CP1-structure along an (admissible) loop or multiloop, and
it creates a new CP1-structure preserving the holonomy representa-
tion. Grafting deformation was first developed by Maskit ([Mas69]),
Hejhal ([Hej75]) Sullivan-Thurston ([ST83]). It was further developed
by Goldman ([Gol]). We describe 2 π-grafting of a hyperbolic surface
along a geodesic loop or multiloop, which we utilize in this paper.
Let σ is a hyperbolic surface, and ℓ is a geodesic loop on σ with
weight w∈ Z>0. Let ρ: π1(S)→ PSL2R be the discrete and faithful
representation corresponding to σ (unique up to PSL 2R), so that σ is
isometric to H2/Imρ. Note that the hyperbolic structure σ is in par-
ticular a CP1-structure with holonomy ρ, and its developing map is a
diffeomorphism onto H2 as a subset of CP1. Then, by grafting σ along
ℓ with weight 2πw, we obtain a CP1-structure on S with the Fuchsian
holonomyρ. Indeed, by bending a totally geodesic hyperbolic plane in
H3 by angle 2πw along lifts of the geodesic loop ℓ, the plane is not bent
after all and it retains the same shape. Therefore, as the bending map
is holonomy equivariant, Gr2πℓσ has the same holonomy ρ. (Figure 1.)
Suppose thatM is a Z>0-weighted multiloop on a hyperbolic surface
σ. Then, by 2 π-grafting σ along M, we similarly obtain a new CP1-
structure with the same holonomy ρ.

3.3. Epstein surfaces. ([Eps], see also [And98].) Let ( f,ρ ) be a de-
veloping pair of a projective structure C on S, where f : ˜S→ CP1 is
its developing map.
Each point x∈ H3 determines a unique spherical metric sx on CP1
by normalizing the Poincar´ e disk model ofH3 so thatx is at the center
of the 3-disk. Given a conformal metric µ on C, there is a unique
mapping Ep: ˜S → H3 such that, the spherical metric sEp(x) of CP1
centered at Ep(x) coincides with the push-forward metric of µ at the
tangent space Tf(x)CP1. In fact, this surface is the envelope of the
horospheres centered at the points f(x) for x ∈ ˜S, and Ep is also
ρ-equivariant.
Let C∼= (X,q ) be the Schwarzian parametrization of C. Then the
quadratic differential q gives a singular Euclidean metric on C, where
the singular points are the zeros of the differential. In this paper, we
use the Epstein surface given by this singular Euclidean metric. In this
case, the Epstein surface is well-defined and smooth in the complement
of the singular points (and it continuously extends to the singular points
as a mapping to points on CP1).
3.4. Traintracks. ([PH92]) A traintrack graph on a surface is a (locally
finite) C1-smooth graph G such that, at each vertex p of G, the edges
of G with its endpoint at p are divides into two groups e1,...,e m and
f1,...,f n such that
• the vectors v tangent to the edges e1,...,e m at p coincide,
• the vectors u tangent to the edges f1,...,f n at p coincide, and
• v =−u in the tangent space at p.
In this paper, we use traintrack graphs which are trivalent, i.e. m = 1
and n = 2.
A marked rectangle is a rectangle such that a pair of opposite edges
is marked as vertical edges and the other pair is marked as horizontal
edges. A fat traintrack T is an orientable surface with boundary which
is obtained by taking a union of marked rectangles{Ri} along horizon-
tal edges as follows: Divide some horizontal edges into finitely many

segments, pair up all horizontal edge segments, and glue each paired
horizontal edge by a diffeomorphism. Each rectangle Ri of T is called
a branch. The points dividing a horizontal edge of a branch appear as
non-smooth points on the boundary of T .
More generally, a marked polygon is a polygon with an even number
2n of edges, such that a set of alternatingn edges are marked as vertical
edges and the set of the other alternatingn edges are marked ashorizon-
tal edges. A polygonal traintrack is an orientable surface with boundary
with singular points obtained, similarly by gluing some marked poly-
gons as follows: Divide each horizontal edge into finitely many seg-
ments (if necessary), pair up all horizontal segments of the polygons,
and glue each pair of horizontal segments by a diffeomorphism. Each
polygon of the polygonal traintrack is also called a branch. Similar to
the case of a fat traintrack, the points dividing a horizontal edge of a
branch appear as non-smooth points on the boundary of the polygonal
traintrack. ([Bab23].)
Given a lamination λ on a hyperbolic surface σ, a traintrack neigh-
borhood σ ofλ is a fat traintrack containingλ in its interior, such that,
for each branch R of σ, each component of λ∩R is an arc connecting
opposite horizontal edges of R.
Definition 3.1 (cf. [Min92]) . A traintrack neighborhood σ on a hy-
perbolic surface σ is ϵ-nearly straight, if all boundary geodesics have
curvature less than ϵ at non-singular points and all horocyclic horizon-
tal edges of rectangular branches have curvature less than ϵ.
In fact, for every ϵ > 0, we can always take an ϵ-nearly straight
neighborhood of L in σ by appropriately taking a sufficiently small
neighborhood of the lamination. ([Min92]].)

8.2.1. Alternative proof. We give a short alternative proof when the
orientations of X and Y are opposite.
Proposition 8.4. For every X∈ T, there are infinitely many pairs of
CP1-structures on the Riemann surfaceX and on its complex conjugate
X∗ which share real holonomy π1(Σ)→ PSL(2, R).
Proof. By Tanigawa [Tan97, Theorem 3.2], for every Z-weighted mul-
tiloop M onS, there is a unique (marked) hyperbolic structure σ onS
such that its conformal grafting grMσ ofσ alongM isX. Then GrMσ
is a CP1-structure on S with the Fuchsian holonomy ρσ : π1(S) →
PSL(2, R) associated with σ. Then, by taking the complex conjugate
of the developing map of GrMσ, we obtain a CP1-structure on S∗ with
the same Fuchsian holonomy ρσ, and its conformal structure is X∗.
Since there are infinitely many Z-weighted multiloops on S, accord-
ingly, we have infinitely many pairs of CP1-structures onX and onX∗
sharing Fuchsian holonomy. □
Remark 8.5. Proposition 8.4 provides the isomonodromic pairs in
Ψ−1(X,X∗) with real Fuchsian holonomy. These isomonodromic CP1-
structures on X and on X∗ are related by complex conjugation. Those
properties are strong, and it is plausible that there are other isomod-
romic pairs in Ψ−1(X,X∗) having non-real holonomy. Seemingly, our
approach in §8.2 may have more possibilities to be generalized in order
to construct such isomonodromic pairs with non-real holonomy.
Theorem 8.6. For eachX∈ T and Y∈ T∗, there are infinitely many
pairs of CP1-structures on X and on Y sharing holonomy.
Proof. Let B− be the union of the connected components of B con-
sisting of pairs of CP1-structures on S and on S∗. Let Ψ − be the
restriction of Ψ to B−. Clearly Ψ − : B→ T× T∗ is a complete local
branched covering map since Ψ is. Therefore, the covering degree of
Ψ− is well-defined. Namely, for all pairs ( X,Y ) in T× T∗, their fibers
Ψ−1
− (X,Y ) have the same cardinality counted with multiplicity.
For everyX∈ T, by Proposition 8.4, the cartinarily of Ψ −1(X,X∗)
(counted without multiplicity) is inifnite. Therefore, the covering de-
gree of Ψ|B− is also infinite. Since the uniformization mapping Ψ is a
local branched covering map, for every X∈ T and Y ∈ T∗, the fiber
Ψ−1(X,Y ) is also infinite. □
