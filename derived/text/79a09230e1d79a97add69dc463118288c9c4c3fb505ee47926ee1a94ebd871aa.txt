A Formal Epistemic Proof of Panagnosticism with Implications
for Artificial Intelligence
Abstract
We present a formal epistemic proof ofpanagnosticism, demon-
strating that no entity other than oneself can be known to possess
consciousness, and no entity can be known not to possess it. This
result has immediate implications for artificial intelligence research:
no AI system can be empirically verified as conscious, and behavioral
or functional benchmarks are insufficient for such claims. Using sym-
bolic logic and a two-agent exclusivity thought experiment, we estab-
lish rigorous limits on evaluating consciousness in both biological and
artificial agents. Panagnosticism provides a principled framework for
AI safety, ethical design, and human-AI interaction under epistemic
uncertainty.
1 Introduction
Evaluating consciousness in artificial agents is a pressing challenge in AI re-
search. Advanced language models, autonomous systems, and hypothetical
artificial general intelligence (AGI) are increasingly capable of producing be-
havior indistinguishable from that of humans in certain tasks. However, the
question of whether any AI system is actually conscious remains epistemically
unresolved.
We introducepanagnosticism, the formal epistemic position that:
1. No entity other than oneself can be known to possess consciousness.

2. No entity other than oneself can be known not to possess consciousness.
From this proof, it follows as a corollary that all major external claims
about consciousness—including Nagel, Chalmers, Descartes, and Searle—are
epistemically insufficient. This has direct consequences for AI research, par-
ticularly in ethical design, evaluation of consciousness claims, and policy
regarding autonomous systems.
2 Definitions and Formal Setup
LetC(x) denote “xis conscious” andK(y, p) denote “yknows proposition
p.”
- Each entity knows its own consciousness:
∀x, K(x, C(x))
- No entity can know consciousness or its absence in any other entity:
∀x̸= self,¬K(self, C(x))∧ ¬K(self,¬C(x))
This formalizes the epistemic core of panagnosticism and establishes its
relevance to AI evaluation.
3 Two-Agent Exclusivity Thought Experiment
Consider two hypothetical agents,AandB, making mutually exclusive
claims:
A: OnlyBis conscious =⇒N A ∧C B
B: OnlyAis conscious =⇒N B ∧C A
WhereN X = “Xis not conscious,”C X = “Xis conscious.”
3.1 Analysis
1. If both claims are simultaneously true:
(A∧B) =⇒(N A ∧C B ∧N B ∧C A)
This is a contradiction, asC A ∧N A andC B ∧N B cannot both hold.

2. If one claim is true and the other false:
•Reality:N A ∧C B =⇒A correct, B wrong
•Reality:C A ∧N B =⇒B correct, A wrong
3. If both claims are false:
•Reality:C A ∧C B (both conscious)
•Reality:N A ∧N B (neither conscious)
3.2 Conclusion
Even perfectly rational agents cannot verify each other’s consciousness. This
demonstrates the epistemic inaccessibility of other minds and supports panag-
nosticism as a fundamental constraint for AI and human interaction.
