Ordered Radial Trajectories in Acceleration Space Reveal the 
Internal Structure of the Radial Acceleration Relation 
 
Abstract 
The radial acceleration relation (RAR) establishes a remarkably tight empirical correlation 
between the observed gravitational acceleration in galaxies and that predicted from their 
baryonic mass distributions. Previous studies have primarily examined the RAR as a population-
level relation constructed from measurements spanning many galaxies. However, galaxy rotation 
curves contain an intrinsic radial ordering that permits investigation of how the relation is 
realized within individual systems. 
Using rotation curve data from the SPARC database, we reconstruct trajectories in acceleration 
space by connecting successive measurements of baryonic acceleration and observed 
acceleration in order of increasing galactocentric radius. This approach transforms the 
conventional point-based representation of the RAR into a set of ordered radial paths that trace 
the internal dynamical evolution of individual galaxies. 
We find that galaxies follow coherent and continuous trajectories in the $(\log g_{\rm bar}, \log 
g_{\rm obs})$ plane, evolving systematically from high-acceleration inner regions toward low-
acceleration outer regions. Despite substantial diversity in morphology, mass, and surface 
brightness, these trajectories closely follow the mean radial acceleration relation. The observed 
population-level RAR therefore emerges not merely as a statistical correlation across galaxies, 
but as the collective expression of ordered radial evolution within individual systems. 
These results provide a complementary perspective on the origin and interpretation of the radial 
acceleration relation. Any successful explanation of galaxy dynamics—whether based on dark 
matter, modified gravity, or alternative frameworks—must account not only for the existence of 
the population-level relation but also for its realization as a structured radial trajectory within 
individual galaxies.

1. Introduction 
Galaxy rotation curves remain one of the principal observational probes of gravitational 
dynamics on galactic scales. Measurements extending beyond the optical radii of galaxies have 
long revealed discrepancies between the observed rotational velocities and those predicted from 
the distribution of luminous matter alone (Rubin, Ford & Thonnard 1980; Bosma 1981). These 
discrepancies motivated the development of dark matter halo models within the ΛCDM 
paradigm and continue to provide important constraints on theories of galaxy formation and 
evolution. 
An important empirical development in this context is the radial acceleration relation (RAR), 
which links the observed gravitational acceleration, (g_{\rm obs}), to the acceleration predicted 
from the observed baryonic mass distribution, (g_{\rm bar}) (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 
2016). Remarkably, measurements spanning a wide range of galaxy masses, morphologies, and 
surface brightnesses follow a tight relation with relatively small intrinsic scatter. The existence 
and robustness of the RAR have motivated extensive theoretical investigation within both dark 
matter and modified gravity frameworks (Famaey & McGaugh 2012; Ludlow et al. 2017; Keller 
& Wadsley 2017). 
Most analyses of the RAR treat it as a population-level relation. Individual measurements from 
many galaxies are combined and examined collectively in the ((g_{\rm bar}, g_{\rm obs})) 
plane, revealing a well-defined empirical trend. While this approach has been highly successful 
in characterizing the relation, it does not explicitly consider the ordered radial structure present 
within individual galaxy rotation curves. 
Rotation curves contain information not only about the magnitude of gravitational acceleration 
but also about its systematic variation with galactocentric radius. Each galaxy therefore 
contributes a sequence of measurements that traces a path through acceleration space as one 
moves from the inner to the outer regions of the system. This radial ordering is typically lost 
when measurements are considered solely as members of an aggregate population. 
The present work investigates how the radial acceleration relation is realized within individual 
galaxies. Using rotation curve data from the SPARC database (Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 
2016), we reconstruct ordered trajectories in acceleration space by connecting successive 
measurements according to galactocentric radius. This approach allows the internal structure of 
the relation to be examined directly. 
The central question addressed in this paper is straightforward: does the radial acceleration 
relation arise solely as a statistical correlation across galaxy populations, or is it also manifested 
as an ordered radial trajectory within individual systems? 
We show that individual galaxies trace coherent and continuous paths in acceleration space that 
closely follow the mean radial acceleration relation. The results demonstrate that the RAR is not 
merely a population-level correlation but is also realized through structured radial evolution 
within galaxies. This finding provides an additional empirical constraint that any successful 
explanation of galaxy dynamics must reproduce.

2. Data and Definitions 
2.1 SPARC Galaxy Sample 
This analysis uses data from the SPARC (Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves) 
database (Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2016), which provides high-quality rotation curves and 
baryonic mass models for 175 nearby disc galaxies spanning a wide range of masses, 
luminosities, surface brightnesses, and morphological types. 
The SPARC sample combines extended H I rotation curves with homogeneous near-infrared 
photometry at 3.6 μm, enabling construction of detailed baryonic mass distributions and 
corresponding gravitational acceleration profiles. The dataset has become a widely used 
benchmark for investigations of galaxy dynamics, the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, and the 
radial acceleration relation. 
For each galaxy, the database provides measurements of: 
• galactocentric radius, (r); 
• observed circular velocity, (V_{\rm obs}(r)); 
• gas contribution, (V_{\rm gas}(r)); 
• stellar disc contribution, (V_{\rm disk}(r)); 
• bulge contribution, (V_{\rm bulge}(r)), where applicable. 
2.2 Baryonic Acceleration 
Following standard SPARC analyses, the baryonic circular velocity is computed as 
V_bar²(r) = V_gas²(r) + Υ_disk V_disk²(r) + Υ_bulge V_bulge²(r) 
where Υ_disk and Υ_bulge denote the adopted stellar mass-to-light ratios for the disc and bulge 
components. 
The observed and baryonic gravitational accelerations are then defined by 
g_obs(r) = V_obs²(r) / r 
and 
g_bar(r) = V_bar²(r) / r. 
Throughout this work, accelerations are expressed in cgs units (cm s⁻²), consistent with previous 
studies of the radial acceleration relation.

2.3 Acceleration-Space Representation 
The radial acceleration relation is commonly represented in logarithmic coordinates, 
[x=\log_{10}(g_{\rm bar})] 
[y=\log_{10}(g_{\rm obs})] 
where each measurement contributes a single point in acceleration space. 
In conventional analyses, measurements from many galaxies are combined to form a population-
level relation. In the present work, however, the radial ordering of measurements within each 
galaxy is retained. This allows individual galaxies to be represented as ordered sequences of 
points in the ((\log g_{\rm bar},\log g_{\rm obs})) plane and provides a direct means of 
examining how the radial acceleration relation is realized internally within galaxy rotation 
curves. 
3. Methodology 
The objective of this study is to examine how the radial acceleration relation is realized within 
individual galaxies while preserving the intrinsic radial ordering present in rotation curve 
measurements. Rather than treating each measurement as an independent point contributing to a 
population-level correlation, we reconstruct the sequence of accelerations encountered as one 
moves outward through a galaxy. 
3.1 Construction of Acceleration Trajectories 
For each galaxy in the SPARC sample, the observed and baryonic accelerations were computed 
using the definitions given in Section 2. The resulting values were transformed into logarithmic 
coordinates, 
[x=\log_{10}(g_{\rm bar})] 
[y=\log_{10}(g_{\rm obs})] 
The measurements for each galaxy were then ordered according to increasing galactocentric 
radius. Successive points were connected in this order to generate a continuous trajectory in 
acceleration space. 
This procedure transforms the conventional point-based representation of the radial acceleration 
relation into a trajectory-based representation in which each galaxy traces a path through the 
((\log g_{\rm bar}, \log g_{\rm obs})) plane. The trajectory therefore preserves information 
regarding the radial evolution of the system that is not visible when measurements are considered 
solely as members of an aggregate population.

3.2 Comparison with the Population-Level Relation 
To assess the relationship between individual trajectories and the global radial acceleration 
relation, the reconstructed trajectories were examined relative to the distribution of SPARC 
measurements in acceleration space. 
Particular attention was given to: 
• the overall continuity of individual trajectories; 
• the direction of radial evolution from inner to outer regions; 
• the degree to which trajectories follow the mean RAR trend; 
• variations in trajectory morphology among galaxies of different masses and structures. 
The analysis is primarily geometric and empirical in nature. No alternative gravitational model, 
dark matter profile, or modified gravity prescription is fitted to the data. The objective is instead 
to determine whether the observed population-level relation is reflected in the internal radial 
organization of individual galaxies. 
3.3 Scope of the Analysis 
The present study focuses on the structural realization of the radial acceleration relation rather 
than on the detailed physical mechanisms responsible for it. The analysis therefore does not seek 
to distinguish between competing theoretical interpretations of the RAR. Instead, it identifies an 
observational property of the relation that any successful theoretical framework must reproduce. 
By retaining the radial ordering inherent in galaxy rotation curves, the method provides a 
complementary perspective on the radial acceleration relation and its manifestation within 
individual galactic systems. 
4. Results 
4.1 Ordered Radial Trajectories in Acceleration Space 
The reconstructed trajectories reveal that individual galaxies do not populate the radial 
acceleration relation (RAR) as collections of independent points. Instead, they trace coherent and 
continuous paths through acceleration space when measurements are connected in order of 
increasing galactocentric radius. 
For the galaxies examined, trajectories generally originate in the high-acceleration regime 
associated with the inner regions of the galaxy and progress toward the low-acceleration regime 
characteristic of the outer disc. Although trajectory shapes vary among galaxies, the overall 
evolution is highly ordered and exhibits a common directional progression across the sample.

4.2 Relationship to the Mean Radial Acceleration Relation 
To determine whether the observed trajectories are related to the global RAR, the reconstructed 
paths were examined relative to the population-level distribution of SPARC measurements. 
The trajectories closely follow the locus defined by the mean radial acceleration relation. As 
galaxies evolve through acceleration space with increasing radius, their paths remain largely 
confined to the same region occupied by the global relation. This behavior indicates that the 
population-level RAR is not generated by unrelated measurements drawn from different 
galaxies, but is instead the collective manifestation of ordered radial evolution occurring within 
individual systems.

4.3 Structural Realization of the RAR 
The principal result of this study is that the radial acceleration relation is realized internally 
within galaxies as a structured radial sequence. 
Conventionally, the RAR is interpreted as a tight empirical relation connecting measurements 
obtained from many galaxies. The present analysis demonstrates that the same relation emerges 
naturally within individual galaxies when their radial ordering is preserved. Each galaxy 
contributes not merely a collection of points to the RAR, but an ordered trajectory that traverses 
the relation in a systematic manner. 
This observation provides an additional empirical property of the RAR that must be explained by 
successful models of galaxy dynamics. Any theoretical framework capable of reproducing the 
population-level relation must also account for its realization as a continuous radial trajectory 
within individual galaxies.

4.4 Robustness of the Result 
The trajectory structure arises directly from the observed radial ordering of SPARC rotation-
curve measurements and does not depend on additional fitting procedures, parameter 
optimization, or model assumptions beyond the standard construction of baryonic and observed 
accelerations. 
The observed behavior is therefore a geometric property of the data itself. While the physical 
interpretation of the radial acceleration relation remains an open question, the existence of 
ordered radial trajectories constitutes an empirical feature that is independent of whether the 
underlying explanation is attributed to dark matter, modified gravity, or another mechanism. The 
results presented here demonstrate that the radial acceleration relation possesses both a 
population-level manifestation and an internal realization within individual galaxies. 
5. Discussion 
5.1 The Radial Acceleration Relation as an Internal Dynamical Sequence 
The radial acceleration relation is typically presented as a population-level empirical correlation 
obtained by combining measurements from many galaxies. The results presented here 
demonstrate that the relation also possesses a clear internal realization within individual systems. 
When radial ordering is preserved, galaxies trace coherent trajectories that closely follow the 
same acceleration-space locus defined by the global RAR. 
This finding suggests that the RAR is not merely an emergent statistical property of galaxy 
populations. Rather, it reflects an ordered dynamical sequence through which individual galaxies 
evolve as a function of galactocentric radius. The population-level relation can therefore be 
viewed as the aggregate manifestation of a process already present within individual systems. 
5.2 Implications for Galaxy Dynamics 
The present analysis does not favor a particular theoretical explanation for the radial acceleration 
relation. However, it introduces an additional empirical constraint that successful models must 
satisfy. Within modified gravity frameworks such as MOND, the RAR emerges as a 
deterministic relationship between baryonic and observed accelerations. The existence of ordered 
radial trajectories is therefore broadly consistent with the expectation that acceleration should 
evolve continuously along a well-defined relation. 
Within ΛCDM, the RAR is generally understood as an emergent consequence of the interaction 
between baryonic matter, dark matter halos, and galaxy formation processes. Numerical 
simulations have demonstrated that relations resembling the observed RAR can arise naturally 
under appropriate conditions. The present results imply that such models must reproduce not 
only the global relation itself but also the ordered radial trajectories through which individual 
galaxies populate that relation.

===== PAGE 9 =====
Regardless of the underlying mechanism, the trajectory structure identified here represents an 
observational property that any successful model of galaxy dynamics should be capable of 
explaining. 
5.3 Relation to Previous Work 
Previous studies have established the existence, tightness, and universality of the radial 
acceleration relation across a broad range of galaxy types (McGaugh et al. 2016; Lelli et al. 
2016; Li et al. 2018). Additional investigations have examined scatter about the relation, its 
connection to the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, and its implications for dark matter and 
modified gravity models. 
The present work differs in emphasis. Rather than focusing on the statistical properties of the 
RAR itself, it examines how the relation is populated internally within galaxies. By retaining the 
radial ordering inherent in rotation-curve measurements, the analysis reveals a geometric 
structure that is largely hidden when measurements are treated solely as members of an 
aggregate population. This perspective does not replace existing analyses of the RAR. Instead, it 
complements them by highlighting an additional empirical feature of the data that may provide 
useful constraints for future theoretical and numerical studies. 
5.4 Limitations and Future Work 
The present study is intentionally descriptive and focuses on the geometric structure of galaxy 
trajectories in acceleration space. No attempt has been made to quantify trajectory morphology, 
classify trajectory families, or compare trajectories directly with predictions from specific 
theoretical models. 
Several extensions suggest themselves naturally. Future work could investigate systematic 
variations in trajectory shape as a function of galaxy mass, surface brightness, morphology, or 
environment. Comparisons with hydrodynamical simulations and modified gravity predictions 
may also help determine whether different theoretical frameworks generate distinguishable 
trajectory signatures. More generally, the trajectory-based representation introduced here 
provides a new way of examining the internal realization of empirical scaling relations and may 
prove useful in the study of other dynamical correlations in galaxy evolution. 
6. Conclusions 
Using rotation-curve data from the SPARC database, we have investigated how the radial 
acceleration relation (RAR) is realized within individual galaxies when the intrinsic radial 
ordering of measurements is preserved. 
By reconstructing trajectories in acceleration space through sequential connection of 
measurements in order of increasing galactocentric radius, we find that galaxies trace coherent 
and continuous paths in the ((\log g_{\rm bar}, \log g_{\rm obs})) plane. These trajectories 
evolve from the high-acceleration inner regions of galaxies toward the low-acceleration outer 
regions and closely follow the locus defined by the population-level radial acceleration relation.

===== PAGE 10 =====
The principal results of this study may be summarized as follows: 
1. Individual galaxies trace ordered trajectories in acceleration space rather than 
contributing only isolated measurements to the RAR. 
2. These trajectories closely follow the empirical radial acceleration relation across a broad 
range of galaxy properties. 
3. The population-level RAR emerges not only as a correlation among measurements drawn 
from many galaxies, but also as a structured radial sequence realized within individual 
systems. 
4. The ordered trajectory structure represents an additional observational property that 
successful models of galaxy dynamics should reproduce. 
The analysis presented here is intentionally model-independent. No assumptions are made 
regarding whether the radial acceleration relation arises from dark matter, modified gravity, 
baryonic feedback processes, or another physical mechanism. Instead, the study identifies an 
empirical feature of the data itself. 
The results suggest that preserving radial ordering provides a complementary perspective on the 
radial acceleration relation and reveals an internal structure that is largely obscured in 
conventional point-based representations. Future investigations comparing observed trajectories 
with theoretical and numerical predictions may help determine whether different models of 
galaxy dynamics produce distinct acceleration-space signatures. More broadly, the trajectory-
based approach introduced here illustrates how information contained in the ordered structure of 
observational data can provide additional constraints beyond those available from population-
level correlations alone.
