ABSTRACT
We simulated electron-only systems using dummy atoms at the HF/LanL1MB level to evaluate
molecular orbital theory against quantum confinement models. Linear systems showed expected
electron localization, while introducing curvature lowered the total energy—a behavior uniquely
predicted by geometric confinement theories like those of Da Costa and Mitchell. Clusters with
Platonic solid and C60 geometries matched Li and Blinder’s model, exhibiting lower energies than
linear counterparts due to enhanced orbital overlap. Furthermore, multielectron simulations revealed
that total energy increases quadratically while electronic repulsion scales linearly. This approach offers
a framework for future nanostructure studies.
1. Introduction
Electronic confinement can serve as a foundation for
advanced investigations into transport phenomena and the
electronic behavior of molecules and nanostructures. How-
ever, its theoretical description remains an active topic
of research [1–8]. Different confinement models lead to
distinct formulations and results for this problem [3, 4, 6, 7].
The conventional quantum confinement model, most widely
used and standard in textbooks, corresponds to the direct
application of the time-independent Schrödinger equation to
the domain in question, where the Hamiltonian is reduced in
practice to the Laplacian operator [1, 4].
Alternative confinement models have also been proposed
andutilized;intheseframeworks,inadditiontotheLaplacian
operator, the Schrödinger equation incorporates geometric
operators that depend on the curvature of the space [2, 3, 5–
9]. There is still no consensus in the literature regarding
which formulation is the most appropriate for confinement.
A widely adopted approach for confinement to curves and
surfaces is Da Costa’s model [2], which introduces an exclu-
sively attractive geometric potential dependent on principal
curvatures. Mitchell later established a new formulation [9]
that generalized Da Costa’s results to arbitrary manifolds,
incorporatingadditionaloperatorsintotheHamiltonian,such
as geodesic curvature, and introducing the possibility of
a repulsive geometric potential. On a sphere, for instance,
Da Costa’s geometric potential vanishes, whereas Mitchell’s
dependsongeodesiccurvatures.Thesedifferingformulations
provide both the foundation and the motivation for new
theoretical and computational studies.
Platonic solids have long captivated scientific curiosity.
Originating from mathematical philosophy, these regular

linear chains of dummy atoms, chains with curved sections,
and clusters arranged in Platonic solid geometries.
2. Computational Details
We constructed molecular models using dummy atoms,
i.e., centers consisting solely of wave functions. We built
linearchainsaswellaschainswithacurvedsection,withthe
number of centers corresponding to the number of vertices
of the five Platonic solids: tetrahedron (T), octahedron (O),
hexahedron (H), icosahedron (I), and dodecahedron (D). We
alsopreparedsixty-centerlinearsystemstocomparewiththe
C60 fullerene sphere. The specific angles of each geometry
werepreserved,andweadoptedatypicalcarbon–carbonbond
distance of1.42Å. The curved sections were introduced at
the second dummy atom of the chain, tilted at an angle of
150◦ relative to the rest of the chain (Fig. 2).
ComputationalcalculationswereperformedattheHartree–
Fock (HF) level [14] (Eq.(1)) using the LanL1MB basis set
[15–17] (Eq.(2)), as implemented in the Gaussian software
package [18].

3. Results and Discussion
All calculations, involving one or more electrons, suc-
cessfully converged. The single-electron linear chains of
dummy atoms with length𝐿 exhibited the highest charge
concentration around the center of the chain,𝐿∕2, and the
total energy decreased as the linear chain length increased.
Thisbehavioralignswithpredictionsfrombothconventional
[1,4]andgeometric[ 2,3,7]confinementmodels.Inallpurely
linear systems, the electron exhibited higher kinetic energy
compared to the corresponding systems featuring curved
sections. In contrast, the electronic charge distribution is
attracted toward the curved regions. This finding finds an
analog exclusively in geometric confinement models, such as
those of Da Costa [2] and Mitchell [9]. The conventional
model fails in this prediction, as it still indicates charge
localization around𝐿∕2.
In the case of a sphere (approximated here byC60)
compared with the line, the energy decreased from 8.19 eV
to 4.83 eV, roughly by half. In our interpretation, a higher
packing of orbitals occurs due to the spatial proximity of the
atoms imposed by the geometry. For a sphere, Da Costa’s
geometric potential vanishes; hence, this model provides no
insight into the energy shift in this case. On the other hand,
in Mitchell’s formulation, the sphere possesses a geometry-
induced attractive potential, which implies that the electron
in this description should indeed have lower energy than in a
straight line.
The energies obtained for the single-electron solids
followed the predictions of Li and Blinder. We also observed
an analogous behavior between the volume of the solid and
the electronic volume (𝑉elec) with respect to energy. The
electron energies in the solids were lower than those in
the corresponding linear systems with the same number of
dummy atoms. From the perspective of Molecular Orbital
Theory, this can be interpreted as greater orbital overlap
arising from the close packing of dummy atoms in the
polyhedra, rendering the systems more stable and lower in
energy relative to their linear counterparts.

Bycalculatingdummy-atommolecularmodelsviaMolec-
ular Orbital Theory (MOT) at the Hartree–Fock/LanL1MB
level, we were able to exclusively simulate one or more
electrons in linear chains, linear chains with curved sections,
andclustersarrangedinPlatonicsolidgeometries,comparing
the results with quantum confinement models. For a single
electron in linear chains of length𝐿, the results reproduce
the predictions of confinement models: the energy decreases
with increasing chain length, and the charge is concentrated
around𝐿∕2. When a curved section is introduced into the
