G Plain-language guide to the model
This appendix translates the mathematical model into a physical picture. It is intended as a guide
to the equations, not as an additional derivation. Where the picture sounds mechanical, words such
as “wave,” “turn,” and “background” refer to the internal family pattern defined in the paper. They
do not describe a material object spinning in ordinary space.
G.1 Mass, charge, and spin in this picture
Within the model, mass is energy that remains locked inside a stable particle pattern. A particle
with more locked energy takes more energy to create and more energy to accelerate. The same
energy also contributes to gravity. Mass can therefore help hold a gravitational orbit together. It
does not hold an atomic orbit together by itself; electric attraction supplies that force.
Charge is pictured as a direction and amount of internal twisting. In a Kaluza–Klein picture,
motion around a closed hidden direction appears in ordinary space as electric charge. That hidden
motion also carries energy, so a charged twist can influence the starting rest energy. Charge is not
19

===== PAGE 20 =====
equal to mass: opposite twists can have opposite charge but equal energy, and the final energy
depends on how the twist combines with the rest of the particle pattern.
Spin describes how a particle responds when ordinary space is rotated. The family turn X in
Eq. (20) is not ordinary spin. It turns the three-generation pattern, not the particle in physical
space. The present model does not explain why the fermions have spin one half. In a higher-
dimensional geometry, ordinary spin and charge could be two different transformation rules of one
higher-dimensional field: spin would describe its response to rotations of visible directions, while
charge would describe its response to shifts or rotations of compact directions. They would share a
geometric origin without being the same rotation. This possibility motivates the twisting language
but is not used in the mass-ratio derivation.
The model uses the following chain of ideas. Charge or a neutral symmetry sets a starting
balance. The carrier response turns that balance into a wave strength ρf. Three allowed family
positions then produce cancellation, partial cancellation, and reinforcement. The length selector
determines how strongly those three raw differences are enlarged. The mass law converts the
resulting overlaps into mass ratios. A separate scaleM f remains unknown for each sector.

G.3 Why there are three different masses in each sector
The model uses one closed wave with three equally spaced family positions. At one position the
changing wave nearly cancels the steady contribution, so little internal energy remains. At the
second position the cancellation is weaker. At the third position the two contributions reinforce.
These are the light, middle, and heavy generations.
The same particle does not rotate from electron to muon to tau, or from up to charm to top.
The three generations are three separate stable versions of the same family pattern. They use the
same three positions but retain different amounts of the pattern’s energy. The sector values ρf and
pf determine how close the light state is to cancellation and how strongly the three differences are
enlarged.

H.8 Minimal premises and remaining boundary
The proposed action becomes a mathematically closed effective model after six physical premises
are stated:
1. use the positive leading cubic response of the normalized carrier, with unit response at zero
imbalance;
2. truncate higher invariant responses;
3. interpret each carrier entry as the metric coefficient of one canonically normalized wave
coordinate;
4. induce all sector metrics from one normalized family triplet and impose the parity that
separates radial from rotation coordinates;
5. identify the observed channel with the ground mode of the positive selector;
6. use the internal-profile bridge of Eq. (82), or a dynamically equivalent completion, to turn the
endpoint overlap into a left–right fermion mass.
Premises 1, 3, 5, and 6 lead to exact consequences once adopted. Premises 2 and 4 cannot be
obtained from the symmetries currently used: Eqs. (72), (76), and (78) are counterexamples. This
is the boundary of the present derivation. A deeper theory can cross it only by adding a selection
principle—for example a common parent kinetic term, a derived grading, and a controlled low-energy
expansion. More algebraic manipulation of the existingSU(3) representations cannot do so.
This audit does not settle the separate choices of the initial carrier weights, the neutrino
orientation, the four scales Mf, or the relative family frames that generate full mixing. Those
remain the next physical questions after the action itself.
