Tandem-in-Time Mass Spectrometry with Programmable
Clifford Boundary Synthesis
Vladimir Baranov ∗
Independent Researcher, Toronto, Canada
Abstract
A single analytical electrode cylinder is proposed for tandem mass spectrometry in time.
Twenty-four independently weighted blades synthesize a real Clifford boundary schedule
so that precursor isolation, activation, fragment capture, recompression, and product-ion
analysis are successive states of one intact cage with a detector-facing inter-blade aperture.
Product-ion readout is performed at zero quadrupolar DC by scanning the square-wave
n= 2RF amplitude through the first stability boundary while a non-oscillatory detector-
directedn= 1field displaces the forced periodic orbit and biases the unstable branch.
Unequal RF duty is used as a baseline axis-selectivity refinement. DefiningDas the fraction
of each period for which the detector-axis coordinate is defocused,D >1/2lowers the
detector-axis instability edge and raises the orthogonal edge. In the reduced model,D≃
0.55suppresses off-axis first crossings at substantially smaller DC bias than equal duty
while preserving unit-mass separation for the prescribed cooled ensemble.
The inhomogeneous Hill calculation demonstrates directional selection, not instrumen-
tal resolving power or absolute transmission. A sufficiently strong DC bias can eliminate
opposite-side crossings in the ideal transverse model, but passage through a physical detec-
tor still depends on the three-dimensional field, measured waveforms, scan-rate calibration,
gas history, cooled phase-space covariance, space charge, and collector optics.
Keywords:tandem-in-time mass spectrometry; birdcage electrode array; digital ion trap;
square-wave RF; stability-boundary ejection; Clifford algebra; DC dipole bias

1 Introduction
Tandem mass spectrometry requires precursor selection, activation, product-ion retention, pres-
sure recovery, and product analysis. Triple quadrupoles distribute these functions among Q1,
q2, and Q3, whereas ion traps perform them sequentially in one electrode structure [1, 2, 3, 4].
Linear-trap experiments established mass-selective radial and axial ejection and showed that
observed peak shape depends on the ion cloud, auxiliary excitation, scan rate, aperture field,
and fringe geometry rather than on an ideal stability boundary alone [5, 6, 7]1.
Gas-loaded quadrupoles provide the collision-cell precedent: confinement, reaction gas,
damping, and ion–molecule chemistry jointly determine transmission and interference removal
[8, 9, 10, 11]. Rectangular-wave traps provide the corresponding transfer-matrix and monodromy
framework [12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18]. Multistage tandem operation has already been realized
in digital linear and three-dimensional traps by dual-direction dipolar excitation, frequency-
scanned and synchronized reverse-scan collision-induced dissociation, and in miniaturized digital
instruments [19, 20, 21, 22]. The distinction drawn here is therefore not tandem operation in
a digital trap as such. Nor is the general synthesis of static multipole fields by independently
biased electrodes itself new. Electrostatic multipole lenses and twelve-electrode dodecapole
structures have been developed for deflection, quadrupole focusing, superposed higher-order
fields, and aberration correction [23, 24, 25]; related electric and magnetic dodecapole synthesis
has also been demonstrated in electron optics [26]. The specific proposal here is to use one
24-blade boundary as a programmable RF/DC analytical volume whose transverse order and
temporal role are changed during a tandem-in-time cycle.
The proposed device is a cylindrical array of independently driven blade electrodes. The
namebirdcageis adopted from the NMR birdcage resonator; the analogy is structural, referring
to a cylindrical distributed electrode array rather than to the resonant circuit itself [27]. Here
the angular weights are imposed electronically, so field order, orientation, and temporal sequence
are programmable.
The central readout principle is a separation of functions. During product analysis the
quadrupolar DC component is removed, soa= 0. The square-waven= 2RF amplitude is
scanned through the first stability boundary and supplies the mass coordinate. A detector-
directedn= 1field is superimposed as a DC boundary term. It does not drive a secular
resonance; it breaks the transverse sign symmetry and biases the unstable trajectory toward the
detector-facing gap. Successive masses reach the same calibrated boundary-crossing region one
after another.
The paper extends transverse cylindrical boundary synthesis [28] to a linear trap configura-
tion. It assigns isolation, transverse resonant activation, fragment catch, cooling, and readout to
successive states of one cage. Also, it develops detector-directed DC-biased stability-boundary
ejection ata= 0as the baseline product-scan example. As a practical solution, it gives first-
order constraints on the fragment-catch field, pressure recovery, voltage, timing, directional bias,
and validation. The readout treated here is destructive: the product ions are ejected to a de-
tector. A non-destructive image-current terminal stage is a possible alternative but lies outside
the scope of the present work2.

The intact 24-blade cage is employed as the baseline because it preserves the complete sam-
pled boundary and provides a regular inter-blade detector exit. In the baseline mechanical inter-
pretation, the array is surrounded by a grounded gas-transparent mesh or slotted shield whose
mean radius provides the electrostatic reference boundary, with a dedicated detector opening
aligned with one selected inter-blade gap. The remaining regular gaps face the surrounding
shield or support structure and are not detector channels. The gas-transparent construction is
important because the rapid pump-out assumed below requires radial conductance through the
inter-blade gaps; a close-fitting solid shield would force the gas predominantly into the much
smaller axial conductance.
An open-sector 23-blade variant was evaluated during the design study as a means of ac-
commodating a larger detector entrance close to the cage. Finite-element field calculations
showed that removing one blade concentrates the largest field distortion in the detector-facing
sector, and direct trajectory modelling consequently produced substantially poorer ejection per-
formance, with a substantially broader event-coordinate distribution than for the intact cage.
Because that comparison was a reduced design study rather than an instrumental resolution
calculation, no numerical width ratio is used here. The variant was therefore rejected, and the
intact 24-blade cage with a regular detector-aligned inter-blade aperture is used throughout. A
standing AC dipole is used for baseline precursor activation3.
2 Analyser Architecture and Operating Sequence
The proposed instrument is a single cylindrical analytical cage formed by 24 longitudinally con-
tinuous, independently driven blades. Conventional end electrodes provide axial confinement,
injection, and clearing, while gas admission and differential pumping establish the collisional and
resolving-pressure stages. The distinctive element is the programmable transverse boundary:
the same blade array synthesizes the fields required for precursor isolation, resonant activation,
fragment retention, collisional recompression, and product-ion mass analysis. The baseline con-
figuration does not use beam-type axial excitation. Because the analytical blades are not axially
segmented, no programmable axial acceleration field is synthesized inside the cage; precursor ac-
tivation is therefore performed transversely. Rectangular-wave precursor isolation in segmented
quadrupole structures provides a practical precedent [29]; here the isolation field is synthesized
transversely by the continuous 24-blade cage.

activation, fragment retention, recompression, pressure recovery, and product-ion analysis are

for precursor selection and final mass analysis, a predominantlyn= 6field with residualn= 2

An important feature of the proposed cycle is that the fragment-catch field is already present
while the precursor is activated. After activation, then= 2quadrupole is restored while
damping gas remains in the cage so that the fragment packet can recompress and rethermalize
before pump-out. The resolving stage then begins at low pressure, with the quadrupolar DC
parameter set toa= 0. In this way the same electrode array changes function in time without
requiring physical transfer of the product-ion packet between successive mass-analysis regions.
For product-ion readout, the preferred baseline is DC-biased stability-boundary ejection with
unequal RF duty. The square-waven= 2RF amplitude is scanned and provides the mass co-
ordinate, while a detector-directed DCn= 1field remains continuously superimposed. WithD
defined as the detector-axis-defocusing fraction,D≃0.55separates the two transverse instabil-
ity boundaries so that the detector-axis mode becomes unstable first. The DC field then selects
the detector-facing branch of that instability. The reduced calculations show that opposite-side
escape is not intrinsic to stability-boundary ejection and can be suppressed in the ideal trans-
verse model. No rotating field, auxiliary resonant excitation during the product scan, or final
extraction pulse is required for the baseline readout.
The numerical first-crossing calculations should nevertheless be interpreted as validation
of the operating principle rather than as predictions of instrumental resolving power or abso-
lute transmission. The calculated non-detector-sector fraction depends strongly on the post-
cooling phase-space covariance, and the present circular-boundary model does not determine
whether such trajectories strike a blade, pass through another gap, or are recovered by the
three-dimensional extraction field. Final analytical performance will therefore depend on the
collisionaln= 6→n= 2transfer, the three-dimensional detector aperture and collection
optics, the RF scan law, DC steering, space charge, gas history, and the measured electrode
waveforms. Higher odd DC orders may provide additional near-wall steering, but that possibil-
ity remains to be demonstrated.
The programmable operation is enabled by the underlying cylindrical boundary synthesis. In
the present formulation the transverse harmonics and their orientations are represented in real
Cl(1,3): the bivectorσ 12 supplies the in-plane phase structure, electric fields are relative vectors
inCl +(1,3), and the independently driven blades realize the requiredn= 1,n= 2,n= 6,
and other angular coefficients. The Clifford formulation is therefore used here primarily as a
compact synthesis and bookkeeping framework for a physically programmable electrode array;
the central instrumental proposal is the time-ordered use of those synthesized fields in a single
tandem-in-time analyser.
