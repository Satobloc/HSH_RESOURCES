About this model
Graphene’s low-energy physics is captured by nearest-neighbor tight binding on a honeycomb lattice with two
sublattices A and B. With nearest-neighbor vectors (0,a) and (± √ 3a/2,−a/2), the structure factor φ (k) = e^{iak_y} + 2e^{-
iak_y/2} cos( √ 3 a k_x/2) vanishes at the Dirac points K and K′, producing linear π  and π * bands E_±(k) = ± √ ( Δ ² + τ ²| φ (k)|²)
when sublattice onsite energies ± Δ  are included. Pristine graphene ( Δ  = 0) has touching cones with Fermi velocity v_F ≈
(3/2) τ a; a finite stagger opens a gap 2 Δ  at K and K′ because | φ | = 0 there. This simulator plots E(k) along the standard
path Γ  →  M →  K →  Γ , colors the π  band in the first Brillouin zone, and marks K and K′. It is a two-band, single- π -orbital
model — no next-nearest hopping, trigonal warping, or spin–orbit coupling.
Who it's for:Advanced undergraduate or graduate students in condensed-matter or solid-state physics after Bloch’s
theorem and before the quantum Hall effect or bilayer graphene.

Frequently asked questions
Why are there two Dirac points K and K′?
The honeycomb Brillouin zone has two inequivalent corners related by inversion. Time-reversal symmetry pairs them; each hosts a linear band crossing when Δ  = 0.
What opens the band gap?
A staggered sublattice potential (e.g. on h-BN substrate or in biased bilayer) sets ε _A = − Δ  and ε _B = + Δ . At K and K′ where | φ (k)| = 0, the gap is 2 Δ  independent of hopping τ .

What is φ (k)?
It is the sum of phase factors from the three nearest-neighbor hoppings between A and B sites. Its magnitude sets the band splitting away from the Dirac points.
Is this quantitative for real graphene?
Only qualitatively. Real graphene needs fitted τ  and a ≈  1.42 Å, includes trigonal warping and interactions; this page teaches the canonical two-band Dirac physics.
