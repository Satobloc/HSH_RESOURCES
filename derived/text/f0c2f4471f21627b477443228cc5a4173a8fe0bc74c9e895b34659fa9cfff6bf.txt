1. Introduction 
Compact multi -planet systems provide valuable laboratories for investigating planetary formation, orbital 
evolution, and long-term dynamical stability. When several planets occupy relatively closely spaced orbits, their 
mutual gravitational interactions can produce measurable departures from purely Keplerian motion and can place 
adjacent planets close to mean -motion resonances (MMRs). Such configurations are particularly useful for 
studying resonance capture, resonance breaking, and subsequent orbital diver gence, which can provide 
information about the dynamical history of planetary systems ( Fabrycky et al., 2014; Lithwick & Wu, 2012).   
TOI-2076 is a young planetary system orbiting a nearby K-type star that was identified through observations from 
the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS), followed by photometric and spectroscopic follow -up 
observations (Hedges et al., 2021; Osborn  et al., 2022). Subsequent observations have further improved the 
characterization of its planetary architecture (Damasso  et al., 2024; Barber et al., 2025). The system contains 
multiple transiting planets, with TOI -2076 b, c, and d forming an outer planetary configuration of particular 
interest for dynamical studies. The orbital solution adopted in the present analysis gives periods of approximately 
10.36, 17.17, and 35.12 days for planets b, c, and d, respectively (Osborn et al., 2022).   The proximity of these 
orbital periods to low -order mean -motion commensurabilities makes the system dynamically interesting. In 
particular, the b –c pair lies close to the 5:3 commensurability, while the c –d pair lies close to the 2:1 
commensurability. These relationships can be expressed through the period-ratio offsets

For the adopted orbital periods, these ratios indicate that the planetary pairs are close to, but not exactly at, the 
corresponding commensurabilities. However, proximity to an exact period ratio alone does not establish the 
existence of a dynamical resona nce. A resonant configuration is more appropriately identified through the 
behaviour of its critical resonant angles. In a dynamically resonant system, the relevant critical angle remains 
confined to a bounded interval and undergoes libration, whereas circ ulation through the full angular range is 
characteristic of a non -resonant or near -resonant configuration (Murray & Dermott, 1999).   This distinction is 
important because systems that are close to resonance but are not currently resonance -locked can preserve 
signatures of previous dynamical interactions. Statistical studies of multi-planet systems have identified an excess 
of planetary pairs close to, but slightly offset from, exact low-order resonances, while theoretical work has shown 
that dissipative processes can drive planets away from exact commensurability following resonant interaction 
(Fabrycky et al., 2014; Lithwick & Wu, 2012).   Previous observational studies have established TOI -2076 as a 
young multi-planetary system and have progressively refined its orbital and physical characterization (Hedges et 
al., 2021; Osborn et al., 2022; Damasso et al., 2024; Barber et al., 2025). These studies provide the observational 
foundation for examining the dynamical behaviour of the system. Consequently, the relevant question is not 
simply whether the planetary periods are numerically close to 5:3 and 2:1, but whether the corresponding resonant 
angles exhibit libration or circulation and whether the resulting behaviour remains consistent when the initial 
orbital phases are varied.   Direct N-body integration provides a suitable approach for addressing this question 
because the mutual gravitational interactions among the planets are explicitly propagated throughout the 
integration. Repeating the integrations with different initial mean anomalies allows the robustness of the resulting 
orbital behaviour to be assessed across independent realizations. Such an approach provides a numerical baseline 
for subsequent investigations involving larger ensembles, observational uncertainties, and su bstantially longer 
integration times.   In this study, we investigate the dynamical behaviour of the adopted TOI -2076 b –c–d 
configuration using direct N -body simulations with the REBOUND package and the WHFast symplectic 
integrator (Rein & Liu, 2012; Rein & Tamayo, 2015). Three independent reali zations with randomized initial 
orbital phases are evolved for 2 × 106 years using a timestep of 0.5 days. We examine the evolution of the orbital 
elements, period ratios, and critical resonant angles to assess the dynamical stability of the system and determine 
whether the near-5:3 and near-2:1 configuration exhibit libration or circulation. The objective is to provide a direct 
numerical assessment of the stability and phase-dependent dynamical behaviour of the adopted configuration over 
the simulated interval. 
2. Methods 
2.1 N-body integration 
The TOI-2076 planetary system was simulated using REBOUND, an open -source N-body integration package 
widely used for gravitational dynamics (Rein & Liu, 2012). The integrations were performed using the WHFast 
symplectic integrator (Rein & Tamayo, 2015), wh ich implements the Wisdom –Holman approach for efficient 
long-term integration of planetary systems (Wisdom & Holman, 1991).  The simulations were performed in units 
of years, astronomical units, and solar masses. A fixed timestep of 0.5 days was adopted. This corresponds to 
approximately 1/20 of the orbital period of the innermost planet and therefore provides multiple integrati on steps 
per orbital cycle while maintaining computational efficiency.   The initial orbital parameters of the three planets 
and the host star were taken from the adopted orbital solution of Osborn et al. (2022). For each planet 𝑝, the initial 
state was specified by its mass 𝑀𝑝, semi-major axis 𝑎𝑝, eccentricity 𝑒𝑝, inclination 𝑖𝑝, longitude of ascending 
node Ω𝑝, argument of pericenter 𝜔𝑝, and mean anomaly 𝑀𝑝.  Because the present analysis focuses on the 
robustness of the dynamical behaviour to orbital phase, three independent realizations were generated. For each 
realization, the initial mean anomaly was independently sampled from a uniform distribution,

The dynamical interpretation was based on the temporal behaviour of these angles. Libration corresponds to 
confinement of a resonant angle within a bounded angular interval, whereas circulation corresponds to repeated 
passage through the full 0∘–360∘ range. Libration is therefore taken as evidence for resonant locking, while 
circulation indicates that the corresponding near -commensurability is not dynamically locked over the simulated 
interval (Murray & Dermott, 1999).

is dynamically stable on this timescale rather than undergoing slow chaotic diffusion toward instability.

4. Discussion 
The results presented here show that TOI -2076 occupies an interesting dynamical position: both adjacent planet 
pairs lie close to low -order mean -motion commensurabilities, yet neither pair is dynamically trapped in the 
corresponding resonance over the simulated interval. The b–c pair has a period ratio of approximately 1.653, close 
to the 5:3 commensurability, while the c –d pair has a ratio of approximately 2.087, close to 2:1. However, 
proximity to a commensurability alone does not establish resonance. In the present integrations, the corresponding 
resonance angles circulate through the full 0∘–360∘ range rather than librating, providing direct dynamical 
evidence that the two pairs are near -resonant but not resonance -locked. This distinction is important because 
resonant capture is associated with libration of an appropriate critical resonant angle, whereas a circulating angle 
indicates that the system is not presently trapped in the tested resonance (Murray & Dermott, 1999; Lithwick & 
Wu, 2012). The statistical occurrence of near-resonant architectures among compact multi-planet systems further 
demonstrates that proximity to low-order commensurabilities does not necessarily imply active resonance locking 
(Fabrycky et al., 2014).   The result is particularly relevant because TOI -2076 is a young, compact multi -planet 
system whose orbital architecture has been established through TESS observations and subsequent photometric 
and spectroscopic follow -up (Hedges et al., 2021; Osborn et al ., 2022). More recent observational studies have 
further refined the characterization of the system, including constraints from radial -velocity observations and

additional follow-up analyses (Damasso et al., 2024; Barber et al., 2025). The system therefore provides a useful 
case for examining how closely spaced planets can occupy the neighbourhood of important resonances without 
necessarily being dynamically locked. Our simulations indicate that the adopted present-day architecture remains 
dynamically stable without requiring an active 5:3 or 2:1 resonance over the 2 × 106-year interval examined here. 
All three planets remain bound, with bounded variations in their orbital elements and no evidence of secular 
eccentricity growth across the three independent realizations. Thus, within the limits of the adopted initial 
conditions and integration interval, the near -resonant architecture is compatible with stable, non -resonant 
evolution.  One possible interpretation is that the planets may have experienced stronger resonant interactions 
earlier in their evolution and subsequently moved away from exact commensurability. Lithwick and Wu (2012) 
showed that dissipative evolution can produce div ergent displacement from exact period commensurabilities, 
providing a physical framework for interpreting systems that lie close to, but outside, resonance. The prevalence 
of near -resonant configurations in observed multi -planet systems also supports the b roader relevance of such 
evolutionary pathways (Fabrycky et al., 2014). In this context, the approximately 0.8% offset of the b–c pair from 
5:3 and the approximately 4.4% offset of the c–d pair from 2:1 place TOI-2076 within the broader class of systems 
that reside near, but are not necessarily captured  into, low-order mean-motion resonances.  At the same time, the 
present results do not demonstrate that TOI-2076 previously underwent resonant capture. The current circulation 
of the resonance angles establishes the dynamical state sampled by the integrations, but it does not uniquely 
reconstruct the system's past. The planets could have undergone an earlier resonant phase followed by divergent 
evolution, or they could have reached their present configuration without becoming permanently trapped in 
resonance. Distinguishing between these possibilit ies would require modelling the system's evolutionary history 
rather than integrating only its present -day orbital configuration. We therefore regard the absence of resonance 
locking as a constraint on the current dynamical architecture rather than as defi nitive evidence for a particular 
formation or migration pathway.   The observational characterization of TOI -2076 also makes this distinction 
important. The discovery studies established the three -planet architecture and provided the orbital parameters 
adopted in the present integrations (Hedges et al., 2021; Osborn et al., 2022), while subsequent work has continued 
to refine the physical and orbital characterization of the system (Damasso  et al., 2024; Barber et al., 2025). 
Consequently, a dynamical analysis based on one adopted set of planetary parameters should not be interpreted 
as an exhaustive exploration of the system's allowed phase space. Uncertainties in planetary masses and orbit al 
elements can influence the detailed structure of resonant and secular dynamics. The use of three independent 
initial mean anomalies in the present study reduces the possibility that the reported qualitative behaviour is solely 
an artefact of one particu lar orbital -phase configuration, since all three realizations exhibit the same overall 
behaviour. Nevertheless, a substantially larger ensemble sampling the observational uncertainties would provide 
a stronger assessment of the robustness of the non -resonant configuration.   A second limitation concerns the 
integration timescale. The present simulations demonstrate stable orbital behaviour and persistent resonance-angle 
circulation over 2 × 106 years across three independent realizations. This interval is substantial compared with 
the orbital periods of the planets, but it remains short relative to the estimated age of approximately 300 Myr for 
the system (Osborn et al., 2022; Barber et al., 202 5). Consequently, the present calculations should not be 
interpreted as proof of stability over the entire lifetime of TOI -2076. Very slow secular evolution or chaotic 
diffusion, if present, could occur on timescales substantially longer than those sampled  here and would not 
necessarily be detectable within the current integrations.  Future work should therefore extend the integrations to 
at least 107–108 years and, ideally, use a substantially larger ensemble of initial conditions. In particular, 
systematic sampling of the uncertainties in planetary masses, eccentricities, and orbital phases would allow the 
stability and resonance classification to be tes ted across the observationally permitted parameter space. Such an 
analysis could determine whether circulation of the 5:3 and 2:1 resonance angles remains a robust property of the 
system or whether a subset of allowed configurations enters a resonant or weakly chaotic regime. Combining 
these longer-term N-body calculations with increasingly refined observational constraints on TOI-2076 (Damasso 
et al., 2024; Barber et al., 2025) would provide a more complete picture of its dynamical history.   Overall, the 
present study establishes a clear dynamical baseline for TOI -2076: the system remains stable over the 2 × 106-
year interval tested, while neither the b–c pair at 5:3 nor the c–d pair at 2:1 exhibits resonance-angle libration. The 
system is therefore best described, within the adopted initial conditions and integration timescale, as near-resonant 
but not dynamical ly trapped in either tested commensurability. This result does not by itself determine how the 
planets formed or migrated, but it provides a quantitative dynamical constraint for future evolutionary models of 
this young multi-planet system.

5. Conclusion 
We have presented a dedicated N -body dynamical and resonance -angle analysis of the TOI -2076 multi-planet 
system using three independent REBOUND/WHFast realizations integrated over 2 × 10⁶ years. Across all three 
realizations, the three-planet configuration remained dynamically stable, with bounded, non-secular variations in 
eccentricity and inclination and no evidence of instability over the simulated interval.   Quantitatively, the b –c 
period ratio is approximately 0.8% wide of the 5:3 commensurability, while the c–d period ratio is approximately 
4.4% wide of the 2:1 commensurability. Despite these close period -ratio configurations, the corresponding 
resonance angles circulate rather than librate  in all three realizations, indicating that neither pair is captured in a 
sustained mean-motion resonance over the 2 Myr integration interval. The system can therefore be characterized 
as near-resonant but not resonance -locked on the timescale investigated . These results establish a quantitative 
dynamical baseline for TOI -2076 and provide constraints for future investigations of its orbital evolution. 
However, the 2 Myr integration represents only a small fraction of the system's estimated ~300 Myr age. 
Therefore, the present results demonstrate stability over the simulated interval but cannot exclude slower secular 
evolution or chaotic diffusion on substantially longer timescales.  Future work should extend the integrations to 
10⁷–10⁸ years, using a larger ensemble of initial conditions and systematic sampling of the uncertainties in 
planetary masses, eccentricities, and orbital parameters. Such calculations would determine whether the observed 
near-resonant, non -librating configuration persists over timescales comparable to the system's age and would 
provide stronger constraints on the long -term dynamical evolution of TOI -2076.  Overall, this study establishes 
that TOI-2076 remains stable over 2 Myr, with the b –c pair approximately 0.8% offset from 5:3, the c –d pair 
approximately 4.4% offset from 2:1, and circulating resonance angles across all three realizations. These 
quantitative results provide a clear foundation for future studies of the system's long-term stability and formation 
history.
