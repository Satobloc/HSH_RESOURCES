Abstract:Building on a recent derivation of spin-charge separation in (1 + 1)d paired
Dirac fermions [1], we develop a self-consistent dressing ψ(x) =U(x)χ(x) for the full Fierz-
complete four-fermion model, extending that result and providing a more detailed resolution
of the chiral-difermion phase structure. A key feature of this approach is that the composite
connection Adress
µ = i(∂µU)U−1 encodes obstructions to local trivialization of the Dirac
operator, i.e., the degree to which the background can be absorbed into the dressing. Using
this fact, we prove a trivialization theorem under which three nonperturbative constructions
are unified: spin-charge separation in correlated fermion systems, half-infinite Wilson-line
dressing in gauge theory, and the holonomy of flat connections. Our approach shows that
the three regimes of our model (chiral, difermion, intermediate), are then tied together
by an emergent sl(2,R ) gauge field that binds the spin and charge degrees of freedom. In
particular, the chiral-difermion transition is a deconfinement transition for these degrees of
freedom, diagnosed by closed boost-sector Wilson loops that develop an area law in the
chiral phase for which we compute the associated string tension. This provides a concrete
realization of the conjectured Faddeev–Niemi link between spin-charge separation and
confinement. We close with a unifying geometric picture in which the order-parameter
manifold takes hyperbolic form ρ2−| ∆|2 =σ2, promoted to AdS3∼= SL(2,R ) on inclusion
of the charge and difermion phases. The structural matching to the kinematic stage of
AdS3/CFT2 is identified explicitly, with the conjecture that the dressed model realizes the
inverse Pohlmeyer reduction of the AdS 3 sigma model.

1 Introduction
Spin-charge separation (SCS) is a feature of strongly correlated fermion systems in one
dimension, originally identified for non-relativistic electrons in metals and developed within
the framework of Luttinger-liquid theory [ 2–6]. In the SCS regime, the electron ceases
to exist as an elementary excitation and is replaced by independent spinon and chargon
collective modes, the original fermion appearing only as a vertex operator built from
bosonized phase fields. The standard derivation proceeds by linearizing the dispersion
around the Fermi points of the non-interacting Fermi sea and bosonizing the resulting left-
and right-movers; the SCS content is then identified in the long-wavelength fluctuations
around this background. The present construction takes a complementary route in which
SCS arises in the long-wavelength fluctuations around a different background, the mean-field
condensate of the difermion channel, and the bosonization-side derivation is recovered as
a special case (section 5.1). A related but distinct construction appears in gauge theory:
the Dirac–Lavelle–McMullan dressing [ 7–11], in which a bare fermion is multiplied by a
half-infinite Wilson line that carries its asymptotic gauge flux to spatial infinity, producing
a gauge-invariant physical charged state. A third construction, common in mathematical
physics, is the parallel transport of fermions in non-abelian backgrounds, organized by
the holonomy of a connection on a principal bundle and especially well-behaved on flat
connections through the non-abelian Poincar´ e lemma [12, 13].
The three constructions look superficially different but their algebraic content is the
same. In each case, a bare fermion field is multiplied by the exponential of an integrated
one-form taking values in an appropriate dressing group. In each case, the construction is
most useful when the underlying connection has a particular geometric structure: flatness,
decoupling between commuting sectors, or integrability of the path-ordered exponential.
The differences lie in the physical interpretation of the connection, the regime in which the
construction is useful, and the technical apparatus used.
The most ambitious attempt to make the cross-community parallel structural is the
program of Faddeev, Niemi, and collaborators [ 14–16], and developed through follow-up
work [17–19]. The proposal is that SCS, originally a strict (1+1)-dimensional phenomenon
in strongly correlated electron systems, might extend to the low-energy domain of four-
dimensional non-abelian gauge theory and play a role in color confinement. In this proposal,
an SU(2) Yang–Mills gauge field is decomposed into a chiral spin field ˆn(x) and a charged
sector. The resulting low-energy effective theory takes the form of a coupled O(3) sigma
model and Grassmannian sigma model. The solitonic excitations of this effective theory are
knotted strings, conjectured to be the constituent quarks of the confining phase.
This proposal has been pursued but has resisted concrete realization. In the Faddeev–
Niemi formulation, Lorentz transformations act projectively on the O(3) sigma-model
variables rather than as a linear representation [ 16], indicating that the SCS phase lacks
ordinary Lorentz-invariant kinematics. Lattice studies attempting to extract a mass gap [ 17]
have required explicit symmetry-breaking terms to suppress unwanted Goldstone modes.
The decomposition is also a change of variables in the Yang–Mills action rather than a
statement about which configurations dominate the path integral, and the relationship to

Each of these obstacles, however, is plausibly specific to the four-dimensional non-
abelian gauge-theory setting, and it is natural to ask whether the Faddeev–Niemi vision
admits a concrete realization in a setting where they can be removed. A recent paper [ 1]
provides such a realization, in the setting of relativistic Dirac fermions in (1 + 1) dimensions
coupled to both a chiral (Gross–Neveu) channel and a dynamical complex difermion field.
In that work, SCS is derived rather than assumed. The dressed spinor decomposes as
ψ =χΦ, with each factor transforming under a distinct linear representation of the relevant
Lorentz subgroup. The full in-medium symmetry group takes the product form

the analog of the Faddeev–Niemi projective Lorentz action but with linear, ordinary group
representations sector by sector.

chemical-potential and four-fermion interactions, with no Lorentz-violating terms added

2 Summary of results and outline
We support these claims through three methods. First, we prove a local trivialization
theorem (Proposition 1). The statement is that when the composite curvature F dress
+−
vanishes on a simply connected patch, the Dirac operator i/∂−M (x) is locally conjugate to
the free Dirac operator via U. The matrix U is determined by the integrability condition
∂±U = A±U, with A± built from the chiral projections of the Fierz-complete potential.
The proof is the non-abelian Poincar´ e lemma applied to the chiral projection structure
of the 2D Clifford algebra. The matrix M(x) in our setting is the full Fierz-complete
potential including scalar, difermion, and chemical-potential structure, so the theorem
applies whenever the four-fermion interactions can be reorganized through a flat composite
connection. Second, for nearly-flat backgrounds, we develop a controlled expansion of
the dressed-spinor wavefunction in powers of the curvature, with explicit surface-integral
representation of the leading correction (eq. (4.16)). The resulting equations of motion
decompose into independent chiral sectors. Specifically, the eight real coefficients in the
Lie-algebra-valued generator Ω of U decouple into two independent four-fold blocks via
the Clifford identity γ0γ5 = γ1. This decoupling is automatic in two dimensions and
underlies the spin-charge factorization in the strong-pairing regime, complementing the
group-theoretic analysis of [ 1]. Third, we derive an explicit effective action for the dressed
system by Hubbard–Stratonovich decoupling of the original four-fermion interaction followed
by integration over the dressed fermions χ in the trivialized regime. The resulting Landau–
Ginzburg-like potential for the scalar condensate σ and difermion field ∆ has all coefficients
given as combinations of two loop integrals, and the saddle-point gap equations reduce to
the standard Gross–Neveu and Nambu–Jona-Lasinio conditions in their respective limits.

The phase structure of the Fierz-complete model contains three distinct regimes in
the (µ,T,g s,gd) parameter space. Each regime is characterized by which projection of
the dressing U dominates and by the behavior of the emergent gauge field Aµ that binds
the spin and charge degrees of freedom in the dressed-spinor representation of [ 1]. In the
broken chiral-symmetry phase (⟨σ⟩̸ = 0, ⟨∆⟩ = 0), the dressing lies in the non-abelian
boost subalgebra span{γ0,γ 1}, the difermion phase β is ill-defined (the corresponding U(1)
is unbroken), and the composite connection produces a γ5-valued field strength via its
commutator self-coupling. The identity (1.2) reduces to ρ =±σ, fixing the density entirely
in terms of the chiral condensate. The Lorentz group remains the single bare SL(2,R ),
acting with position-dependent parameters generated by gradients of σ. The fluctuations of
β are large in this phase, and the emergent field strength E =−(∂2

the spin–charge binding is correspondingly large: the spin and charge degrees of freedom
are tightly bound, in an SCS-confining sense. Closed Wilson loops in the boost sector
develop an area law, providing the standard gauge-theory diagnostic of confinement for
the spin-charge degrees of freedom. In the difermion (paired) phase ( ⟨σ⟩ = 0,⟨∆⟩̸ = 0),
the dressing factorizes fully into commuting sectors U =UcUs with Us non-trivial, and the
SCS of [1] is the natural description. The phase β = arg ∆ becomes a stiff Goldstone mode,
its fluctuations are suppressed, E→ 0 in the infrared, and the spin and charge degrees of
freedom propagate independently. The identity (1.2) reduces to ρ =±|∆|, the Lorentz
group is enhanced to the product SL(2,R )→SL (2,R )1×SL (2,R )2 established in [1], and
PT-symmetry breaking signals the transition.
The transition from the chiral to the difermion phase is therefore a deconfinement
transition for the spin–charge degrees of freedom, with Aµ playing the role of a binding
field that becomes weak as β stiffens. In the intermediate (correlated normal) regime,
neither condensate is critical, the bosonic-modes parametrization breaks down, and the
flat-connection geometry of the construction governs the analysis. The composite curvature
F dress
+− is non-zero and encodes the obstruction to bosonization. Geometric tools (holonomy,
characteristic classes, the non-abelian Poincar´ e lemma) organize the spectral structure of
composite-channel excitations. The pole-to-cut transition in spectral functions, characteristic
of bound-state dissolution into the two-fermion continuum, has its origin in this geometric
obstruction. The Lorentz group remains single-factor SL(2,R ) in this regime as well: the
doubling is unique to the difermion phase.
The transitions between regimes are not smooth crossovers between equivalent de-
scriptions. They are loci where one of the three projections of U becomes singular and a
different one takes over, with a corresponding change in the behavior of the binding field
Aµ. The chiral–difermion transition along the locus gs =gd, for example, is the line on
which the chiral-symmetry-broken description (singular Us, largeE, spin and charge tightly
bound) yields to the SCS description (singular Uc,E→ 0, spin and charge propagating
independently). The PT -symmetry-breaking diagnostic of [ 1], in which the SCS transition
is signaled by Lorentz violation, is the symmetry-group face of this same deconfinement
transition: the Lorentz doubling SL(2,R )→SL (2,R )1×SL (2,R )2 is permitted precisely

The qualitative parallel with QCD phase structure (hadronic phase, color-
superconducting phase, quark-gluon-plasma-like phase) is suggestive and was noted in [ 1]
as a motivation. The present model also provides a setting in which the proposal of [ 16, 24]
that SCS may underlie color confinement admits a concrete structural realization. The
SCS-confining picture conjectured there for four-dimensional Yang-Mills theory (charged
scalar condensation, SCS in the IR, weak gauge binding in the bulk, abelian-dominated
low-energy structure) is reproduced feature by feature in the difermion phase of the present
(1+1)d model. The non-abelian gauge structure of the composite connection Adress
µ is then
most pronounced in the intermediate regime, where neither condensate dominates. The
intermediate regime sits structurally in the region of the QCD phase diagram associated
with quarkyonic matter [25, 26] and the BEC-BCS crossover of dense QCD [ 27, 28]. The
specific features that define each of these phases in their respective QCD contexts do not
transfer directly to the (1 + 1)d setting. An inhomogeneous extension of the construction in
the intermediate regime, connecting to the Gross-Neveu chiral-spiral [ 29, 30] that descends
to quarkyonic chiral spirals, is identified as a natural future direction. We discuss this
correspondence in detail in section 7. We do not claim that the (1+1)d Fierz-complete
model is a low-dimensional model of QCD: the gauge structure is too simple, color dynamics
has no analog here, and the (1+1)d setting precludes flux tubes or magnetic monopoles.
The more limited claim is the following: the structural content of the Niemi proposal
(the conjectured equivalences among SCS, charged-scalar condensation, gauge-binding
suppression, and symmetry enhancement) holds simultaneously and self-consistently in
this model. The model thus provides a computable testbed for ideas that have remained
conjectural in four dimensions.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 3 sets up the self-consistent dressing in
general terms, distinguishes externally-driven from backreacted configurations, and proves
the local trivialization theorem (Proposition 1) for a general matrix-valued background.
Section 4 contains the technical core: the specialization of the trivialization to the full
Fierz-complete potential, the chirally decoupled equations of motion in the Fierz-complete
background, the Magnus expansion for nearly-flat connections, and the calculation of the
non-abelian corrections to the emergent gauge field at O(F+−). Section 5 develops the
three representations of the dressing. SCS is the bosonized spin-charge representation,
exact at leading Magnus order in the difermion phase, extending [ 1] to the full Fierz-
complete phase diagram. The Wilson-line/Wilson-loop framework is the confinement-
deconfinement diagnostic for the spin-charge degrees of freedom, with half-infinite Wilson
lines defining gauge-invariant single-particle states in the difermion phase and closed Wilson
loops developing an area law in the chiral phase. The flat-connection holonomy is the
unifying structural language in which both regime-specific representations live. Section 6
derives the effective field theory in the trivialized regime, computes the saddle-point
gap equations, verifies that the algebraic identity (1.2) is preserved at the saddle, and
identifies the chiral and difermion phases with their respective broken-symmetry condensates.
Section 7 develops the structural synthesis. It contains the QCD-confinement analogy via
the Niemi proposal, the three-regime mapping to the QCD phase diagram. It also develops

byγ 5), in line with the spin–charge factorization developed below.

constancy generate non-zeroF +−∼∂±∆ and require the corrections of eq. (4.25).

4.5 Clifford-support diagnostic
Trivializability at leading Magnus order is therefore not, by itself, a regime indicator: all
three regimes are trivializable to this order. The form of the trivialization distinguishes
them. Specifically, the Clifford-basis support of Ω 1 takes a different pattern in each case:
1. In the scalar (chiral) regime, only the imaginary coefficients b,c of γ0,γ 1 are active.
The dressing matrix Ω 1∈span{γ 0,γ 1} generates a position-dependent Lorentz boost,
with the scalar condensateσabsorbed as a local element of SO +(1,1).
2. In the difermion regime, the active coefficients are a,d (imaginary parts of I,γ 5)
and e,h (real parts of I,γ 5). The dressing matrix Ω 1∈span{I,γ 5} generates the
U(1)×U (1)A subgroup of charge and axial phase rotations. This is the algebraic
content of the spin–charge factorization.
3. In the crossover region, all eight coefficients are sourced perturbatively and the two
blocks couple at O(F+−) through [F+−](Γ) in eq. (4.25). The dressing matrix has
support on the full Clifford basis {I,γ 0,γ 1,γ 5}, and the block decoupling that makes
the previous two regimes algebraically simple is lost.
The pattern matches the regime structure announced in the introduction. The scalar and
difermion regimes correspond to the dressing inhabiting a proper Lie subalgebra of the
Clifford algebra (the boost subalgebra and the charge-axial subalgebra, respectively), and
the spin–charge factorization in the difermion regime is the statement that Ω 1 commutes
with itself in the abelian subalgebra span{I,γ 5}. The crossover regime is the regime in
which no such subalgebra restriction applies, and the analysis of its spectral and effective-
field-theoretic structure is the subject of section 6.9.

regime interpolates between the confining and deconfining limits.
The progression from the chiral phase to the difermion phase, via the intermediate

gauge fieldAµ plays the role of a binding field that is strong in the chiral phase, weak
in the difermion phase, and intermediate in the crossover. The Lorentz doubling and the

signatures of the same deconfinement transition.

5 Representations of the dressed spinor
The self-consistent dressing ψ(x) =U(x)χ(x) developed in section 4 admits three natural
representations. The factorization U =UcUs into commuting charge and spin/boost pieces
is the spin-charge separation representation of [ 1], with bosonic spin and charge variables as
the natural low-energy degrees of freedom. The Wilson-line/Wilson-loop framework provides
a non-local single-particle and binding-tension representation, with half-infinite Wilson lines
defining gauge-invariant single-particle states and closed Wilson loops diagnosing the area-
vs-perimeter scaling that distinguishes confined from deconfined phases. The flat-connection
/ formal-geometric language organizes both representations as different parallel-transport
descriptions of a single composite connection Adress
µ . The three representations are not
parallel regime-specific readings. The SCS representation is most concrete in the difermion
phase, where the factorization is exact at leading Magnus order. The Wilson framework
is the diagnostic that tracks the spin-charge deconfinement transition between the two
ordered phases via the scaling behavior of Wilson-type objects. The geometric language
is the unifying structural framework in which both regime-specific descriptions live. We
develop each representation in turn and close with the regime-limit correspondence.
5.1 Spin-charge separation
The SCS factorization U = UcUs is the natural reading of the dressing in the difermion
phase. In that phase Ω 1 lives in the abelian Clifford subalgebra span{I,γ 5}, the charge
and spin/boost factors commute, and the bosonized spin-charge variables of [ 1] provide the
appropriate low-energy degrees of freedom. We develop the construction here and connect
it to the emergent abelian gauge field that mediates the spin-charge binding outside the
difermion phase.
5.1.1 Relation to the standard bosonization derivation
Both the present construction and the standard bosonization treatment of SCS in (1+1)d [4–
6] derive SCS as a long-wavelength effect around an appropriate background. The standard
derivation linearizes the dispersion around the Fermi points ±kF of the non-interacting
Fermi sea and bosonizes the resulting left- and right-movers. SCS then appears as the
decoupling of charge and spin bosonic sectors at the level of quadratic fluctuations around
this background. The present construction takes the underlying theory to be a relativistic
Dirac multiplet, so no linearization is needed: the linearity that bosonization must engineer
is already built in. The background here is the mean-field condensate rather than the Fermi
sea, and SCS appears as the factorization U =UcUs of the dressing in the regime where
Ω1 lives in the abelian Clifford subalgebra. The two approaches are doing structurally
the same thing, identifying SCS in the long-wavelength fluctuations around the chosen
background, applied to different backgrounds. The connection to the standard bosonization
variables is made explicit through the vertex-operator factors e∓ζ/2 appearing in the
chiral decomposition of section 6, which carry the fermion statistics in the dressed-spinor
representation. What the dressing route adds beyond the long-wavelength SCS content
is structural: the regime-specific organization across the phase diagram, the composite

It is important to note that the Wilson line-dressed fermion (and thus the dressing

exponential of the gauge field extending to spatial infinity, which essentially resums an
infinite class of soft “photon” emissions and incorporates the full infrared behavior of the

theory makes it a useful tool for describing physical states beyond the reach of perturbative
expansions based on free scattering states.
5.3 Formal geometric and topological structure
5.3.1 The dressing as a connection
The dressing U(x) takes values in the Clifford group generated by the gamma matrices, and
the composite connection Adress
µ =i(∂µU)U−1 is a one-form valued in the corresponding
Clifford algebra. This is the data of a principal-bundle connection over (1+1)d spacetime,
with structure group the Lie group generated by the Clifford algebra and fiber given by the
spinor representation. The dressed spinor χ(x) =U−1(x)ψ(x) is the field ψ viewed in the
local frame chosen by the connection. The flatness condition F dress
+− = 0 that the dressing
satisfies at leading Magnus order in either ordered phase is the connection’s curvature
equation. A flat connection on a simply connected region admits a global gauge in which
Adress is pure gauge, Adress = i(∂U )U−1, and the Dirac operator is locally trivialized as
in section 4. The two regime-specific frames developed above are different reductions of
this bundle to sub-bundles whose structure group is a proper Clifford subalgebra. The
SCS factorization U =UcUs reduces the bundle to the abelian subgroup associated with
span{I,γ 5}. The Wilson-line construction reduces it to a non-abelian subgroup associated
with the boost generators{γ 0,γ 1}or to the abelianγ 5 sector depending on the regime.
5.3.2 Topological content of the flat sector
The bulk of (1+1)d Minkowski space is contractible, so its fundamental group is trivial and
a flat connection on a contractible patch carries no holonomy. The interesting topological
content of the dressing therefore lives at infinity, where the dressing field is required to
satisfy asymptotic boundary conditions that effectively compactify the spacetime. The
half-infinite Wilson lineW(x;∞) of section 5.2 is the precise mathematical object that detects
this asymptotic structure: it integrates the dressing along an infinite path and is sensitive to
the dressing’s behavior at the boundary at infinity. In the difermion phase, the asymptotic
vanishing of the Goldstone β makes this line finite, and the Wilson dressing carries gauge-
invariant single-particle information. In the chiral phase, no such asymptotic vanishing is
available for the boost-sector fields, and the half-infinite Wilson line diverges. The closed
Wilson loop of section 5.2 probes a complementary feature: it is sensitive to non-flatness
of the connection inside the loop, not to asymptotic conditions. The Wilson-loop area
law in the chiral phase is the statement that F dress
+− ̸= 0 at leading Magnus order in that
regime, with the γ5-valued curvature of eq. (5.18) providing the integrated flux. The two
– 30 –

===== PAGE 33 =====
Wilson-type objects therefore probe two distinct geometric features of the connection: the
half-infinite line probes asymptotic topology, the closed loop probes bulk curvature.
5.3.3 Sub-bundle reduction across the phase diagram
The three regimes of the model correspond to three reductions of the Clifford bundle to
different sub-bundles, each with its own structure group and its own flatness properties. In
the difermion phase, the bundle reduces to the abelian sub-bundle with structure group
U(1)N×U (1)5 generated by {I,γ 5}. The flatness condition is automatic for smooth
Goldstone phases, the holonomy lives in the abelian subgroup, and the natural geometric
object is the half-infinite Wilson line in this abelian sector. In the chiral phase, the bundle
reduces to a non-abelian sub-bundle with structure group generated by the boost subalgebra
{γ0,γ 1}. The connection is flat at leading Magnus order but the curvature F dress
+− acquires
the γ5-valued content of eq. (5.18) at the next order through the commutator self-coupling,
and the natural geometric object is the closed Wilson loop in the boost sector. In the
intermediate regime, no proper sub-bundle reduction is available. Ω 1 has support on the full
Clifford basis, the connection takes values in the full sl(2,R ) algebra, and neither the SCS
factorization nor the Wilson-line dressing in a single sector is fully appropriate. The change
of sub-bundle structure across the phase diagram is precisely the type of phenomenon
that the cobordism category formalism organizes naturally. The cobordism extension
conjectured in section 7 proposes that the three regimes are the three boundary components
of a higher-dimensional manifold whose interior interpolates among the corresponding
sub-bundle reductions. The geometric language of this subsection is therefore the natural
setting in which the cobordism structure of the model lives.
5.3.4 Two representations and one unifying geometric language
Taken together, the three subsections describe one object viewed three ways. The composite
connection Adress
µ is the single geometric datum, and the spin-charge factorization, the
Wilson-line/Wilson-loop diagnostic, and the flat-connection holonomy are the descriptions
it admits in the difermion phase, across the deconfinement transition, and in the bundle-
theoretic language that contains both. The regime-specific representations are therefore
not competing interpretations but reductions of this one connection to the sub-bundle
appropriate to each phase, with the intermediate regime the locus where no proper reduction
exists and the full sl(2,R ) structure of section 4 is active. It is this geometric unification
that we carry into section 7, where the same connection and its order-parameter manifold
acquire a hyperbolic and emergent-AdS 3 reading.

6.8.1 The difermion as a slaved amplitude rather than an independent gap

6.9.1 Integration of heavy modes and high-energy resonances
Because the composite modes are heavy, at low external frequency and momentum (ω,|q|≪
Mσ,∆) the fields σ and ∆ can be integrated out. They do not appear as propagating degrees
of freedom in the infrared, and their only effect is to generate short-ranged four-fermion
interactions among the dressed quasiparticles. The composite channels are “irrelevant” to
the strict low-energy spectrum in the intermediate regime. At higher energies, however,
of order Mσ and M∆, the same fields play a crucial role. Their propagators Dσ(ω,q ) and
D∆(ω,q ) develop resonant enhancements near the edge of the two-fermion continuum, and
the ¯ψψ andψψ channels dominate the in-medium scattering. These resonances continuously
evolve into true bound states as one approaches the chiral and difermion phases, where the
corresponding composite mode becomes light and condenses.

7 Synthesis and broader interpretation
The technical results developed in sections 3–6 admit a unifying physical reading that
consolidates them into a single picture. The chiral and difermion phases of the model
use complementary variables. Quantities that are classical and well-defined in one phase
are strongly phase-fluctuating or suppressed in the other, and the dressing U(x) is the
transformation that interpolates between the two variable sets. The resulting structure has
– 47 –

===== PAGE 50 =====
the form of a weak/strong duality between a fermion-natural description and a collective-
mode-natural description, of the type developed in (1 + 1)d bosonization [ 39, 41, 42]. In
this section we develop the implications of this reading.
We discuss the relation to the proposal of Niemi and collaborators that SCS may
underlie color confinement in non-abelian gauge theory, and identify the limited but precise
sense in which the present model realizes that proposal. We then describe a three-regime
mapping onto the structural regions of the QCD phase diagram. We develop a unifying
geometric picture in which the order-parameter manifold takes hyperbolic form, and identify
a structural matching to the kinematic stage of AdS3/CFT2. Under this matching, the chiral
phase corresponds to an interior bulk region and the difermion phase corresponds to the
asymptotic conformal boundary, so the weak/strong duality reading and the bulk/boundary
correspondence are the same observation viewed in different lights. We place the construction
within the Pohlmeyer-reduction framework [ 43, 44] that relates sigma models on symmetric
spaces to integrable 2D field theories. We state the precise conjecture that the dressed Fierz-
complete Dirac model realizes the inverse Pohlmeyer reduction of the AdS3 sigma model.
We close by combining these threads into a structural-precursor reading of holographic
theories of confinement.
7.1 The Niemi proposal for spin-charge separation as a confinement mechanism
Two distinct correspondences with non-abelian gauge theory run through this section, and
separating them at the outset prevents confusion. The first is a confinement-mechanism
analogy with pure Yang–Mills theory: following the proposal of Niemi and collaborators, the
spin-charge-separated phase plays the role of the confining vacuum. The second is a phase-
diagramanalogy with finite-density QCD, in which the three regimes of the model map
onto three structural regions of the QCD phase diagram (section 7.1.3). The two are related
readings of the same chiral-to-difermion transition, but they belong to different physical
settings; one concerning the Yang–Mills vacuum, the other quark matter at finite density;
and we keep them distinct in what follows. We begin with the Yang–Mills mechanism.
The proposal of [ 14, 16, 24] is that the confining phase of four-dimensional SU(2)
Yang-Mills theory is precisely the SCS phase of the gluon. In the Cho-Faddeev-Niemi
decomposition, the gluon is reorganized into a spin field ˆn(x) and two charged scalars. At
high energies these constituents are tightly bound, and the Yang-Mills Lagrangian describes
asymptotically free massless gauge bosons. In the low-energy domain, the charged scalars
condense and the spin-charge components decouple. The resulting low-energy effective
action takes the form of a coupled O(3) and Grassmannian sigma model, which supports
knotted closed string solitons. The confining vacuum is then structurally analogous to a
high-Tc superconductor: condensation of charged scalars produces an effect that has been
called a dual Meissner effect [ 45, 46], with chromoelectric flux squeezed into the flux tubes
that bind quarks.
The proposal has driven two decades of subsequent work but has resisted concrete
realization. As discussed in section 1, three obstacles remain: projective Lorentz action in
the O(3) sector, absence of a derivation from the path integral, and no explicit construction

of the spin-charge-separated fermion. The present model exhibits these features explicitly,
in the more limited setting of (1+1)d, where the obstacles are absent.

7.1.4 Intermediate regime: relation to quarkyonic matter and BEC-BCS
crossover
The intermediate regime of the present model deserves a closer look in light of recent
work on the QCD phase diagram. In the McLerran-Pisarski proposal of quarkyonic
matter [25], a third QCD phase distinct from confined hadronic and color-deconfined regimes
is identified at large Nc and high quark chemical potential. The phase is characterized
by the simultaneous presence of confinement (color-singlet baryon-like excitations near
the Fermi surface) and quark-like bulk thermodynamics. A complementary picture comes
from the Ginzburg-Landau analysis of dense QCD by Hatsuda, Tachibana, Yamamoto, and
Baym [27, 28]. In their framework, a BEC-BCS-type crossover region with both chiral and
diquark condensates active simultaneously sits between the chirally broken hadronic phase
and the color-superconducting phase. A new critical point driven by the axial anomaly
terminates the line of phase transitions between the two ordered phases.
The intermediate regime of the present model has structural features in common with
both pictures. Both order parameters are active simultaneously, and the regime interpolates
continuously between the chiral and difermion ordered phases. The analysis of section 6.9
identifies the regime as the locus of melted bound states with branch-cut-dominated spectral
functions and short-ranged correlations in both channels. The non-abelian gauge structure
of the composite connection Adress
µ is most pronounced here, and the regime is the natural
home for any inhomogeneous or anisotropic order-parameter structure that the present
analysis does not pursue.

===== PAGE 55 =====
The structural correspondence is genuine, but the specific physical features that define
each of the QCD pictures do not transfer to the (1 + 1)d setting. Quarkyonic matter is
fundamentally a statement about large-Nc color gauge theory with a Fermi surface and
baryon-like excitations, none of which has an analog in the (1+1)d Fierz-complete Dirac
model. The BEC-BCS crossover of dense QCD is driven by the axial anomaly and concerns
the continuity of quark pairing across the chiral transition, neither of which is directly
present in our setting. The honest statement is that the intermediate regime occupies the
structural slot in the phase diagram corresponding to these phases in their respective QCD
contexts, without literally being either of them.
The connection to inhomogeneous-condensate physics is more direct and deserves to
be mentioned here. Quarkyonic chiral spirals [ 26] are inhomogeneous chiral density waves
that descend structurally from the inhomogeneous condensate of the (1 + 1)d Gross-Neveu
model [29], which is in the same four-fermion family as the present Fierz-complete Dirac
construction. The intermediate regime of the present model is the natural setting in which
an inhomogeneous extension of the construction would live. The mean-field analysis of
section 6 assumes a homogeneous ansatz throughout, which is appropriate for identifying the
chiral and difermion phases but may be incomplete in the intermediate regime where both
condensates are heavy and the system may prefer spatially modulated configurations [ 30].
Two ingredients for this physics are developed elsewhere in the paper. The chiral-polarization
remark of section 6.8 shows that the boost factors e±ζ produce a chiral imbalance bounded
by the difermion condensate, which is the order-parameter content that a chiral density
wave would modulate, and the induced kinetic term of section 6.11 supplies the gradient
energy that would govern such modulation, with coefficient fixed by the dynamical mass.
An inhomogeneous extension of the present construction, in which the dressing U(x) allows
position-dependent condensate orientations beyond the leading-Magnus order treated here,
is a natural direction for future work. This direction would also clarify the structural relation
between the present construction and the (1+1)d Gross-Neveu chiral spiral that descends
from Sch¨ on-Thies through Kojo-Hidaka-McLerran-Pisarski to the modern quarkyonic-matter
literature.

our knowledge, they have not appeared in the present condensed-matter or hadronic-physics

7.2.4 Pohlmeyer-reduction context
The worldsheet-into-AdS3 structure of the present construction admits a further reading
within an existing body of work. The Pohlmeyer reduction [ 43] is a classical-level procedure
that maps a non-linear sigma model with target a symmetric space M = F/G into a
reduced two-dimensional integrable field theory by imposing the Virasoro constraints and
gauge-fixing. The simplest cases give the sine-Gordon equation for M = S2 and the
sinh-Gordon equation forM = AdS3. More general symmetric spaces give the Symmetric
Space Sine-Gordon (SSSG) family of equations, whose Lagrangian formulation as gauged
Wess-Zumino-Witten models with integrable potentials was established by Bakas-Park-
Shin [51] and subsequently developed [ 44]. The Pohlmeyer reduction has been applied
to string sigma models on AdS5×S 5 in the context of AdS5/CFT4 integrability, and to
strings in AdS3 in connection with gluon-scattering amplitudes and three-point correlator
computations.
The structural triangle relevant to the present construction connects three vertices.
The first vertex is the bosonic string sigma model with target AdS3∼= SL(2,R ). The second
is the Pohlmeyer-reduced theory, which is the sinh-Gordon equation in the simplest case
and an SL(2,R ) gauged Wess-Zumino-Witten model with an integrable potential more
generally. The third is the fermionic side of (1 + 1)d bosonization. The Thirring model is
the fermionic dual of sine-Gordon by the Coleman-Mandelstam duality [ 39, 41], and the
corresponding sinh-Gordon-Thirring relation is the natural analog for the AdS3 case. The
full triangle is therefore: strings in AdS3↔ Pohlmeyer-reduced sinh-Gordon-like model↔
Thirring-family fermionic theory. This structure is summarized in figure 3.
The (1+1)d Fierz-complete Dirac model of the present paper sits at the fermionic vertex
of this triangle. The current-current and scalar four-fermion interactions of the model are in
the Thirring/Gross-Neveu/NJL family, and the dressed-spinor representation ψ =U(x)χ(x)
produces the embedding into AdS3 via the condensate ratios of eqs. (7.6)–(7.7). The
construction therefore runs in the direction opposite to the standard Pohlmeyer reduction.
The standard direction starts from strings in AdS3 and reduces to a 2D integrable model,
while the present construction starts from a 2D fermionic field theory and produces an
embedding into AdS3. The natural conjecture is that the two directions are inverse to each
other, in the precise sense that the low-energy effective theory of the dressed Fierz-complete
Dirac model realizes the inverse Pohlmeyer reduction of the AdS 3 sigma model.

coincide with the AdS3 metric, possibly up to corrections that vanish in the appropriate
limit. Second, the Virasoro constraints required by the Pohlmeyer reduction must be
verified, at least in the regimes where the EFT is conformally invariant. The difermion
phase, in which the boundary-CFT description becomes exact, is the natural candidate for
this verification. Third, the reduced dynamics for the hyperbolic angle ζ must be derived

7.2.5 Structural matching to emergent AdS 3/CFT2
The AdS3 embedding of eqs. (7.4)–(7.7) sharpens the structural matching between the
present construction and the AdS3/CFT2 correspondence [47, 52, 53]. We list the matchings
first, then identify what is established and what is not. The order-parameter manifold, in the
normalized form, is a single unit-radiusAdS3 with global coordinates (ζ,β,θ N) parametrizing
the hyperbolic radial direction, the spatial angle, and the timelike circle respectively. The
conformal boundary of this AdS3 at ζ→∞ is a two-dimensional cylinder parametrized by
the difermion phase β and the charge phase θN, with the cylindrical topology required for
a 2D conformal field theory. The chiral-phase limit |∆|→ 0 corresponds to the apex ζ = 0
of the bulk, where the geometry is most curved and the boundary CFT description is least
accurate. The difermion-phase limit |∆|/ρ→ 1 corresponds to the asymptotic boundary
ζ→∞ , where the bulk approaches conformal infinity and the boundary CFT description
becomes exact. The bulk isometry SO(2, 2)∼= SL(2,R )L×SL (2,R )R/Z2 matches the SCS
Lorentz doubling of [ 1] as the boundary M¨ obius algebra acting on left- and right-movers, in
the standard AdS3/CFT2 pattern. The conjectured low-energy sigma model with target
SL(2,R ) is structurally the Maldacena-Ooguri worldsheet theory for strings in AdS3 [47].
The cobordism of [1] corresponds to a holographic RG flow with the dimensionless condensate
ratio as the radial direction, interpolating between an interior bulk region and the boundary
CFT.
These structural matchings are not derived holography in the strict sense, and several
elements of a full holographic dictionary remain to be established. The boundary CFT at the
asymptotic null cone has not been identified as a definite 2D theory with a specific central
charge, although the candidate boundary coordinates ( θN,β ) and the candidate M¨ obius
algebra are concrete. The bulk-to-boundary correlators of the dressed Dirac fermions have
not been computed, and their scaling behavior near the conformal boundary has not been
checked against the predictions of a holographic dual. The bulk AdS3 structure here is
on the order-parameter manifold of the dressed-spinor sigma model rather than on the
physical spacetime. The resulting picture is target-space emergent holography of the type
studied in [50], not dynamical bulk-spacetime gravity. What we have established is that the
kinematic stage of the construction is the kinematic stage of AdS3/CFT2. The kinematic
stage of the construction includes the bulk AdS3 geometry built from the two hidden phases,
the 2D cylindrical conformal boundary, the bulk isometry group matching the SCS Lorentz
doubling, and the structural form of the conjectured Wess–Zumino–Witten-type EFT.
This structural matching, even at the kinematic level, has consequences that earlier
framings of the geometric picture did not make available. The SCS Lorentz doubling
acquires a direct geometric reading as the bulk isometry algebra of AdS3, rather than as
a conjectural product of geometric and frame-action factors. The hyperbolic-rather-than-
Euclidean signature of the algebraic identity of [ 1] acquires a direct geometric explanation:
the underlying internal manifold is AdS3, not a sphere. The boundary CFT coordinates
are explicitly identified as the two hidden phases ( θN,β ), which are physical quantities in
the original Fierz-complete Dirac theory. The framework predicts that the difermion phase
should exhibit features of a 2D CFT in some scaling limit, including modular structure under

7.2.6 Weak/strong duality reading and consolidation of the threads
The structural results developed above admit a unifying physical reading that consolidates
the separate threads of the paper. The chiral phase ( ⟨σ⟩̸ = 0,⟨∆⟩ = 0) and the difermion
phase (⟨σ⟩ = 0,⟨∆⟩̸ = 0) of the model use complementary variables. In the chiral phase, σ
is a classical mean-field order parameter and the elementary fermions ψ, ¯ψ are the natural
excitations. The difermion phase angle β = arg ∆ is ill-defined since the corresponding
U(1)∆ symmetry is unbroken, and the difermion magnitude |∆| is small and quantum-
fluctuating. In the difermion phase the situation is reversed. The difermion magnitude |∆|
and its phase β are classical, with β a stiff Goldstone of the broken U(1)∆. The natural
elementary excitations are the SCS-decomposed bosonic collective modes ϕ± of [1], and
the original fermions become composite bound states of these modes with the difermion
condensate. The chiral condensateσis suppressed.
The chiral-phase variables and the difermion-phase variables are therefore strongly
complementary: quantities that are classical and well-defined in one phase are strongly phase-
fluctuating or suppressed in the other. This is the structural signature of a weak/strong
duality between two effective descriptions of the same underlying Fierz-complete Dirac
theory. The dressing matrixU(x) =ψ(x)/χ(x) provides the unified variable that interpolates
between them and serves as the duality transformation. The structural form is that of
(1 + 1)d bosonization [39, 41, 42], in which fermion and bosonic-collective-mode descriptions
of strongly correlated (1 + 1)d theories trade roles as the natural elementary variables.
We do not claim a derived bosonization equivalence in the strict sense, since the chiral
and difermion phases are different vacua of the same theory rather than two variable
representations of the same configuration. We do claim that the relation between the two
phase-EFTs of the present model has the structural form of a weak/strong dual pair.
This reading consolidates the separate threads developed in this section. The Niemi
confinement analogy and the bulk/boundary structure of the emergentAdS3/CFT2 matching
are not independent observations. They are aspects of the same weak/strong duality. The
chiral phase corresponds to the interior bulk region of the AdS3 embedding, where the spin-
charge constituents are tightly bound, the gauge field Aµ is strong, and the bulk-variable
description (fermions, σ) is natural. The difermion phase corresponds to the asymptotic
conformal boundary, where the constituents are deconfined, the gauge field is weak, and the
boundary-CFT-variable description (collective modes, β) is natural. The chiral-to-difermion
phase transition is the holographic RG flow from bulk to boundary, parametrized by the
cobordism-interpolating dimension of [ 1]. The dressing U(x) acquires its various roles in
this picture as follows. It is the duality transformation between complementary variable sets.
It is the geometric alignment map of the spinor with the order-parameter direction on AdS3.

analogy, the unifying hyperbolic geometric picture, and the emergent AdS3/CFT2 structure
are all consequences of the same fact: that the dressing-based reformulation of the Fierz-
complete Dirac model exhibits a structural weak/strong duality between its two condensation
phases.
7.2.7 Toward a holographic theory of confinement
The two threads developed in this section combine to give the construction a further
structural reading. The QCD-Niemi correspondence identifies a field-theory side with the
structural features of confinement. These features include a charged-scalar condensate (the
difermion), an IR weakening of the spin-charge binding interaction, an abelian-dominated
low-energy gauge sector, and a phase-diagram organization mapping onto the structural
regions of the QCD phase diagram. The emergent AdS3 target geometry, built from the
two hidden phases of the order-parameter manifold, provides the gravity-side stage that
holographic approaches to confinement aim to engineer. The bulk/boundary correspondence
of the AdS reading and the Niemi-inverted QCD reading agree: the chiral phase sits at the
interior bulk and is the deconfined-like analog, the difermion phase sits at the conformal
boundary and is the confining-like analog. Together these features have the structural
form of what a holographic theory of confinement would provide. In holographic-QCD
approaches in higher dimensions [ 54], a field-theory side with QCD-like phase structure is
paired with an emergent gravity-side stage, and the confinement transition is realized as
a bulk geometric transition between distinct regions of the AdS spacetime. The present
construction has the kinematic skeleton of this pattern, in a (1+1)d setting where the
construction itself is computable.
The reading is structural rather than derived. The (1+1)d setting precludes flux tubes
and magnetic monopoles, the gauge structure of the dressed connection is the Clifford
algebra rather than su(N), and the target AdS3 geometry is fixed rather than dynamical.
The construction is not a holographic theory of QCD. What it provides is a setting in which
the structural ingredients of holographic-QCD-type constructions are realized simultaneously
and self-consistently. Those ingredients are a confining phase diagram, an emergent AdS
bulk-side stage, and a bulk/boundary correspondence between the two. All three arise
as consequences of the dynamics of a standard four-fermion Dirac model. The natural
sequel is the application of the self-consistent dressing framework of the present paper to
genuine (1+1)d QCD-like theories: the Schwinger model, the ’t Hooft model of large- N two-
dimensional QCD [55], and related Gross–Neveu-type theories with explicit gauge couplings.
If the same emergent- AdS3 structure arises in those settings, with the field-theory side now
a literal QCD-like model, the construction would be in direct contact with the AdS/QCD 2
literature [ 56, 57]. It would then provide a derived bottom-up route to a holographic
dual of two-dimensional QCD. The verification of this conjecture, and the identification of

the precise level k in the SL(2,R ) Wess–Zumino–Witten sigma model conjectured for the
low-energy EFT, are natural extensions of the present construction.
8 Conclusion
We have developed a self-consistent dressing framework ψ(x) = U(x)χ(x) for (1 + 1)-
dimensional Dirac fermions with Fierz-complete four-fermion interactions. The dressing
matrix U(x) is built from the condensate background, and the composite connection
Adress
µ =i(∂µU)U−1 encodes the obstruction to local trivialization of the Dirac operator.
The framework extends the spin-charge separation construction of [1] from the strong-pairing
regime to the full Fierz-complete phase diagram, and organizes three previously distinct
nonperturbative constructions (SCS, Wilson-line dressing, flat-connection holonomy) as
regime limits of the single dressing U(x). The regime structure is characterized algebraically
by the Clifford-support pattern of Ω 1, geometrically by the strength of the emergent gauge
field that binds the spin and charge degrees of freedom, and group-theoretically by which
Lorentz-enhancement pattern is realized. The composite connection acquires Yang–Mills-like
corrections at next-to-leading order in the curvature, with structure constants in the sl(2,R )
algebra of Clifford commutators.
The structural synthesis of section 7 draws these technical results together into a
single physical reading. The chiral and difermion phases use complementary variables,
related by the dressing U(x) in the structural form of a weak/strong dual pair. The two
phases map onto interior bulk and asymptotic conformal boundary of an emergent AdS3
target geometry built from the two hidden phases (θ N,β) of the model. The SCS Lorentz
doubling matches the bulk AdS3 isometry algebra SO(2, 2). The mechanism is explicit: the
dressing U(x) unlocks the spinor-Lorentz constraint of the underlying theory, enlarging
the symmetry algebra acting on the dressed quasiparticles from spacetime SO(1, 1) to
target-space SO(2, 2). The conjectured low-energy sigma model has SL(2,R ) Wess–Zumino–
Witten structure. The construction sits within the Pohlmeyer-reduction framework that
relates sigma models on symmetric spaces to integrable 2D field theories, with the precise
conjecture that the dressed Fierz-complete Dirac model realizes the inverse Pohlmeyer
reduction of the AdS3 sigma model. The combination of QCD-Niemi confinement features
with emergent AdS bulk/boundary structure carries the kinematic skeleton of a holographic
theory of confinement.
A note on priority is also in order. Several structural ingredients of the present
construction have well-developed analogs in the existing literature. The unification of a
chiral condensate and a pairing condensate into a single order-parameter manifold appears in
the chiral-perturbation-theory treatment of dense QCD [27, 28] and of two-color QCD [48, 49].
In those settings the ground state pins the overall condensate magnitude, and the chiral
and pairing fields ride a compact target ( S3 or a related coset). The identification of the
bulk AdS3 with the SL(2,R ) Wess–Zumino–Witten model is the canonical string-theory
result of [47]. The Pohlmeyer reduction [ 43, 44] relating sigma models on symmetric spaces
to integrable 2D field theories is a longstanding framework. What is new in the present
construction is the assembly of these ingredients in a specific bottom-up direction. The
– 66 –

===== PAGE 69 =====
order-parameter identity arises from condensate phases of a (1 + 1)d Fierz-complete Dirac
model. Because it involves the conserved fermion density alongside the two condensate
phases, it can be cast as ρ2−| ∆|2 = σ2, with the density and the difermion magnitude
entering with opposite signature; the target is then the non-compact AdS3 rather than
the fixed-radius spherical target of the 4D analogs. The self-consistent dressing U(x)
realizes the AdS 3 embedding through fermionic condensate dynamics, and the SCS Lorentz
doubling of [ 1] matches the AdS 3 isometry algebra through an explicit unlocking-of-the-
spinor-Lorentz-constraint mechanism. The structural placement of the construction within
the Pohlmeyer-reduction framework is made explicit, with a precise verification agenda left
to follow-up work. The structural-precursor reading of holographic confinement, combining
Niemi-type SCS confinement with the emergent AdS3/CFT2 matching, is the synthesis the
paper contributes. A detailed development of the string-theoretic content of this matching
will appear in a separate paper by one of the authors here.
Open questions
Several questions arise naturally from the present analysis.
First, the explicit calculation of the corrected gauge field Adress
µ atO(F+−) in section 4
establishes that the non-abelian residue is most pronounced in the intermediate regime. A
natural next step is the computation of the effective dynamics of this non-abelian sector.
The leading, quadratic-in-curvature part of these dynamics is computed in section 6.11,
where integrating out the dressed fermions generates an induced Maxwell term for the
composite connection with coefficient fixed by the same one-loop scale as the wave-function
renormalization. The full non-abelian completion remains open. The eqs. (4.42) for
the leading non-abelian correction in the intermediate regime depend on cross-couplings
between the real-coefficient and imaginary-coefficient blocks of Ω 1. The structure of these
cross-couplings, and their consequences for low-energy excitations, has not been worked out.
Second, the QCD-analog mapping above is structural, not dynamical. A natural
question is whether any analog of confinement dynamics, such as a linear potential between
probe charges or a string tension, can be identified in the present model. The (1+1)d setting
precludes flux tubes, but the analog of confining static potentials between heavy probe
fermions in the difermion phase, if any, may be accessible through Wilson-loop calculations
along the lines of section 5.
Third, the three-phase cobordism extension described in section 7, which promotes
the two-boundary cobordism of [ 1] to a manifold with one boundary component per phase,
has a natural candidate setting in the cobordism-category formalism of [ 22, 23], where
multi-boundary cobordisms classify phases connected by topological transitions. Whether
the extension connects precisely to the Kapustin-Freed-Yonekura classification of symmetry-
protected topological phases by cobordism groups remains open; establishing it would
require a careful analysis of the global symmetries of the dressed-spinor representation
in each phase, including any anomalies and their cancellation through the interpolating
boundary structure.
Fourth, the non-abelian corrections we have computed in Adress
µ take values in the
sl(2,R ) algebra of Clifford commutators. This is the symmetry algebra of the in-medium
– 67 –

===== PAGE 70 =====
dressed-spinor representation, but it is not the gauge algebra of any familiar gauge theory.
The interpretation of this algebra as a hidden symmetry of the four-fermion model and
its possible relation to higher-spin or asymptotic-symmetry structures is left to future
investigation.
Fifth, the conjecture stated in section 7 that the dressed Fierz-complete Dirac model
realizes the inverse Pohlmeyer reduction of the AdS3 sigma model is accessible to direct
verification. The agenda involves five distinct calculations. The EFT for ( ζ,β,θ N) must
be derived from the dressed-fermion theory, and the kinetic-term metric must be shown
to coincide with the AdS3 metric. The Virasoro constraints required by the Pohlmeyer
reduction must be checked, at least in the regimes where the EFT is conformally invariant.
The reduced dynamics for the hyperbolic angle ζ must be derived in conformal gauge
and compared to the sinh-Gordon equation. The level k of the conjectured SL(2,R )k
Wess–Zumino–Witten EFT must be computed from the fermionic theory. The explicit
bosonization map between the Fierz-complete Dirac model and the Pohlmeyer-reduced
bosonic theory must be made explicit, generalizing the standard sine-Gordon-Thirring
duality. Verification or refutation of this conjecture would identify the present construction
as a bottom-up realization of inverse Pohlmeyer reduction, or would clarify the obstructions
to such a realization.
Finally, we note that the framework developed here, together with the construction
of [ 1], provides a setting in which several conjectural ideas about strongly-correlated
relativistic fermion physics admit explicit and computable realizations. The (1 + 1)d setting
is intrinsically limited, but the structural connections that emerge are not specific to one
spatial dimension, and we expect them to carry over to higher-dimensional analogs of the
same construction.
