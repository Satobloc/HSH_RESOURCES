Abstract

that the complex structure inherent to the Schrödinger equation can be fully
captured by a coupled system of real-valued dynamics. By employing the standard

The decomposition of the Schrödinger equation into real and imaginary components
is a well-established concept, with origins tracing back to the early development of
quantum mechanics in 1926 [1]. This approach has been explored extensively in the
literature to investigate the geometric and symplectic structures underlying quantum
dynamics. A significant geometric framework, advanced by Volovich, recasts the
Hilbert space of quantum states as a real Kähler manifold [15], [16]. This formulation,
endowed with compatible symplectic and Riemannian structures, provides a unified
setting for quantum unitary evolution and classical Hamiltonian dynamics. By
rigorously analyzing the structural relationship between complex and real quantum
mechanics, this approach demonstrates how quantum operations and entanglement
can be equivalently expressed in real symplectic terms, thereby consolidating the
mathematical foundations of real-valued quantum theory.

The role of complex numbers within the mathematical framework of quantum
mechanics has been a long-standing subject of profound foundational debate. The
central question revolves around whether their presence signifies an inherent
ontological necessity or merely represents a convenient mathematical tool for
calculations. Adherents to the indispensability of complex numbers, as highlighted by
recent investigations [17], [18], [19], [20], contend that the complex structure is an
intrinsic feature of quantum theory. They point to its fundamental appearance in the
Schrödinger equation, the principles of superposition and interference phenomena,
and the description of experiments conducted on elementary quantum networks as
compelling evidence. This perspective posits that complex numbers are essential for
encoding fundamental phase relationships and non-classical correlations that cannot
be adequately captured by formalisms based solely on real numbers without
compromising physical consistency. Conversely, this view has been challenged by
researchers, including the early works of Birkhoff and von Neumann [21], and
Einstein [22], who suggest that complex numbers might be a representational artifact
linked to the Hilbert space formulation of quantum mechanics. Furthermore,
advancements in geometric quantization [23], [24], [25], [26], [27], [28], [29] have
demonstrated the possibility of describing quantum dynamics using real symplectic
structures, thereby questioning the notion of an ontologically privileged status for
complex numbers. The current work addresses this debate by explicitly employing
the standard algebraic isomorphism between the field of complex numbers  and the
real plane . This construction demonstrates that complex arithmetic arises
structurally from the field operations defined on . The proposed reformulation
preserves all physical predictions of standard quantum mechanics — including
probability conservation and spectral equivalence — while interpreting the imaginary
unit  as a symplectic operator , effectively disentangling quantum mechanics from
an a priori reliance on complex numbers. These findings suggest that while
computationally advantageous, complex numbers are not fundamentally ontological
but rather emerge from the geometric structure of , paving the way for a paradigm
where quantum reality is potentially grounded in real symplectic dynamics.
It is important to address historical arguments concerning the viability of formulating
quantum mechanics on a real Hilbert space. Seminal work by Stueckelberg [26] and
more recent geometric analyses by Moretti and Oppio [29] have shown that standard
quantum mechanics cannot be consistently formulated on a real Hilbert space
without introducing an additional operator structure , satisfying , which
commutes with the full algebra of physical observables. Such a structure effectively
reinstates the complex structure at the algebraic level. The present work does not
contradict these results; rather, it makes this structure explicit by identifying  with
the canonical symplectic matrix . By interpreting the symplectic structure as the
geometric realization of the imaginary unit, the framework satisfies the algebraic
requirements identified in [26], [29] while maintaining a strictly real-valued
coordinate representation.

to the study of quantum entanglement, representing a quintessential example of
non-classical correlations that classical physics cannot explain [30]. These states
exhibit perfect correlations or anti-correlations between the measurement outcomes

are limited by the speed of light due to the principle of locality. The Bell inequality
provides an upper bound on the correlations that can be observed in any physical
theory satisfying these principles. However, quantum mechanics predicts
correlations for certain entangled states, including Bell states, that violate this bound.
Numerous experiments have conclusively demonstrated these violations, providing
compelling evidence against local hidden variable theories [31] and strongly
supporting the violation of local realism by certain entangled systems as predicted by
quantum mechanics. This study introduces a framework for quantum computation
within a real vector space and presents a generalized protocol for generating
entangled Bell states.

structure.
