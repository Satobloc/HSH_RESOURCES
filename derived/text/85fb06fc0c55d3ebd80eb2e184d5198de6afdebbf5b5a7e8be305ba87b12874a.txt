1. Introduction 
Green functions and Feynman diagrams are among the most fundamental mathematical 
structures in theoretical physics. Green functions describe the propagation of fields and 
particles through linear differential operators and constitute the mathematical foundation 
of quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, condensed-matter physics, and quantum field 
theory [1–9]. Feynman diagrams, introduced in 1949, transformed perturbative quantum 
field theory into a graphical calculus in which propagators, interaction vertices, and loop 
corrections encode scattering amplitudes in an intuitive and computationally efficient 
manner [10–16]. 
Besides their physical interpretation, Feynman diagrams are finite graphs. Consequently, 
they possess numerous graph-theoretical properties that have become increasingly 
important in mathematical physics. Examples include the first Betti number, Kirchhoff and 
Symanzik graph polynomials, and the Hopf -algebraic structure of renormalization 
developed by Connes and Kreimer [17 –23]. These invariants characterize important 
combinatorial and topological aspects of perturbative quantum field theory. 
Despite these developments, existing graph invariants describe only selected structural 
features of perturbative diagrams. The first Betti number measures the number of 
independent loops, graph polynomials encode spanning trees and momentum flow, while 
Hopf algebras describe the recursive combinatorics of renormalization. To our knowledge, 
no existing invariant simultaneously incorporates propagation, interaction, holonomy, and 
recursion into a single multiplicative architectural quantity. 
The present work proposes such an invariant. 
The central idea is to regard every finite perturbative Feynman graph as possessing an 
underlying Process Green-Function Architecture. Rather than treating propagators, 
interaction vertices, loops, and recursive insertions merely as graphical elements used for 
perturbative calculations, we interpret them as manifestations of four primitive 
architectural sectors: 
• propagation, 
• interaction, 
• holonomy, 
• recursion. 
These four sectors define an architectural coordinate system for perturbative graphs. Every 
finite Feynman graph is assigned a unique coordinate 
(𝑎,𝑏,𝑐,𝑑), 
 
from which a single multiplicative invariant —the Architectural Magnitude—is 
constructed.

Unlike perturbative amplitudes, the proposed invariant contains no coupling constants, 
masses, momenta, spinors, polarization vectors, or gauge -dependent quantities. It is 
intended to characterize only the intrinsic structural organization of the graph itself. 
The objectives of this paper are therefore deliberately modest and mathematically focused. 
First, we define a graph-theoretical mapping that assigns every finite perturbative Feynman 
graph a unique architectural coordinate. 
Second, we introduce the Architectural Magnitude as a multiplicative invariant associated 
with these coordinates. 
Third, we investigate several elementary mathematical properties of this invariant, 
including multiplicativity, invariance under graph isomorphisms, and its behavior under 
elementary graph operations. 
Finally, we discuss the relationship between the proposed invariant and existing graph-
theoretical constructions in perturbative quantum field theory. 
The present work does not propose a modification of quantum field theory, the Standard 
Model, or the Feynman rules. Instead, it introduces an additional mathematical invariant 
defined on the space of perturbative Feynman graphs. Whether this invariant ultimately 
carries physical information beyond its graph-theoretical definition remains an open 
question. Nevertheless, the existence of a natural architectural coordinate system and its 
associated multiplicative invariant suggests a new mathematical perspective on 
perturbative quantum field theory that may warrant further investigation.

mathematical object that characterizes the structural organization of a graph independently

The basic object of the present theory is the Process Green-Function Architecture.
