Abstract 
The classical calculation of the gravitational deflection of light by the Sun is usually said 
to fail because it gives only half of the observed deflection. This paper revisits that 
conclusion within the Hypergeometrical Universe (HU) model. In HU, our thr ee-
dimensional universe is the surface of a four-dimensional hypersphere expanding at the 
speed of light, and particles surf the hyperspherical metric wave associated with this 
expansion. A particle at rest in three-dimensional space is therefore not at rest in four 
dimensions: it moves radially at c. Applying the ordinary kinetic-energy expression to 
this motion gives mc!/2, apparently in conflict with the familiar rest energy mc!. HU 
resolves this through an Archimedean displacement picture in which a particle surfing 
the metric wave displaces an equal mass of that wave. The particle and the displaced 
metric contribution each carry mc!/2, giving the total energy mc!, while a gravitational 
interaction within the three-dimensional hypersurface involves the particle's mc!/2 
share. Using this energy scale in the classical light -deflection calculation gives a 
deflection angle 4GM/(bc!), or approximately 1.75 arcseconds for light grazing the Sun. 
Within HU, the classical trajectory calculation therefore does not fail; the historical 
factor-of-two discrepancy arises from using the total mc! energy where the three-
dimensional interaction involves only one of the two equal energy contributions.
