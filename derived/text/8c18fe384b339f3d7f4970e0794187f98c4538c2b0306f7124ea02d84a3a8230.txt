2.4 Horizon vs. Turning Point
There is a conceptual difference between a gravitational horizon and a confinement turning
point. At a black hole horizon, outward-propagating light does not halt because it has no
energy available to move further; rather, it is trapped because the surrounding gravitational
ambient energy density is fully exhausted due to local depletion (c G
eff→0). Although light
maintains the same velocity at the horizon exactly as it does elsewhere (v=cin vacuum),
what vanishes is the capacity of the background field to translate the propagation outward.
A precise analogue occurs at the proton surface, where a valence quark stops moving,
not because there is no kinetic energy. Instead, its outward movement terminates because
the localized QCD vacuum energy is exhausted (c Q
eff→0). The quark retains a velocity of
v= 0.572catr p (as derived in Section 3.1), and this speed is unchanged by the confinement
condition.
Similarly, colour confinement is governed by the spatial exhaustion of the ambient vacuum
energy rather than a standard application of energy conservation. At the confinement radius
rp, the total ambient vacuum energy available at the characteristic QCD depth vanishes
(Heff

background energy density remaining to sustain the quark’s outward translation.

B.4 The Neutron-Proton Mass Splitting: Structural Framework

The empirical mass difference between the nucleons (m n−m p = 1.293 MeV) is established
within the Standard Model as an explicit combination of two competing dynamical contri-
butions: the current quark mass splitting (m d−m u≈2.5 MeV), which favours a heavier
neutron, and the electromagnetic self-energy contribution, which favours a heavier proton.
Combined, these factors generate the net observed baseline of +1.3 MeV.
Because Eq. (38) is constructed strictly within the flavour-symmetric, isospin-invariant

baseline expression has no internal parameter sensitive to individual light quark flavours or
electromagnetic self-energies. Resolving the fine structure of them n−mp splitting requires
coupling the AVF horizon condition to an explicit electroweak deformation term. This
boundary limits the current iteration of the model to pure strong interaction metrics, leaving
fine flavour adjustments as an open pathway for future multi-interaction coupling.
