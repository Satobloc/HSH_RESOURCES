Abstract 
Green functions provide one of the central mathematical languages of theoretical physics, 
describing propagation, response, and transport in classical field theory, quantum 
mechanics, many-body theory, and quantum field theory. Feynman diagrams provide a 
complementary graphical language for perturbative interactions, in which propagators and 
vertices generate transition amplitudes for particle reactions and decays. However, neither 
formalism introduces an explicit primitive notion of a complete process architecture. This 
manuscript proposes the Process Architecture Principle, according to which every 
physical reaction or decay possesses an associated  Process Green-Function 
Architecture. This architecture is decomposed into four primitive structural sectors: 
propagation, interaction, holonomy, and recursion. The first two correspond closely to the 
conventional propagators and vertices of Feynman diagrammatics, while the latter two 
make explicit structural features that are usually encoded only implicitly through loops, 
topology, self-energy insertions, and recursive diagrammatic expansions. The manuscript 
introduces the Architectural Multiplicity of a Process, Π!"#$, and develops structural 
analogues of transition amplitudes and decay widths. Preliminary comparisons with 
particle decays suggest that particle multiplicity is relevant for rest energies and decay 𝑄-
values, whereas process multiplicity is the natural object associated with transition 
strengths. The product/educt multiplicity quotient is discussed and rejected as a universal 
amplitude factor, but reinterpreted as a possible architectural restructuring index. 
Keywords: Green functions; Feynman diagrams; particle decays; transition amplitudes; 
Green-Function Architecture Theory; process architecture; holonomy; recursion. 
 
1. Introduction 
Green functions are fundamental objects in theoretical physics. Since Green’s original 
work on potential theory [1], they have become central in boundary-value problems, partial 
differential equations, spectral theory, quantum mechanics, many -body theory, and 
quantum field theory [2–10]. In their most general form, Green functions describe how a 
localized source produces a response elsewhere in a system.

Feynman diagrams introduced another essential layer. They encode perturbative processes 
through propagator lines, interaction vertices, loops, and diagrammatic expansions [11–
15]. They are not merely pictures; they correspond to algebraic terms in perturbation theory 
and provide a compact method for computing scattering amplitudes, decay amplitudes, 
loop corrections, self-energies, and renormalized quantities. 
Green functions and Feynman diagrams are therefore deeply connected. Green functions 
describe propagation, while Feynman diagrams organize propagation and interaction into 
process amplitudes. Yet neither formalism explicitly treats the complete physical process 
as a primitive architectural object. 
Green-Function Architecture Theory (GFAT) proposes such a structural layer. Earlier 
GFAT work introduced the idea that particles may possess intrinsic Green -function 
architectures whose structural multiplicity is related to their rest energy  [16-25]. The 
present manuscript extends this idea from static particle architectures to dynamic process 
architectures. 
The central question is: 
Can a particle reaction or decay be described as a Green-function architecture in its own 
right? 
The answer proposed here is yes. Every physical process is associated with a  Process

12. Process Architectures Beyond Feynman Diagrams 
The Process Architecture Principle is not limited to perturbative Feynman diagrams. It 
suggests a broader structural language for physical processes. 
Possible extensions include: 
• non-perturbative Green functions, 
• lattice gauge theory, 
• Dyson–Schwinger systems, 
• topological phases, 
• Wilson-loop structures, 
• quantum gravity. 
In perturbative quantum field theory, Feynman diagrams provide a natural representation 
of process architectures. In non-perturbative settings, the process architecture may not be 
representable by a finite diagram. Nevertheless, the four-sector decomposition may remain 
meaningful: 
𝒫⊕ℐ⊕ℋ⊕ℛ.	
 
This suggests that GFAT may provide a bridge between perturbative diagrammatics and 
non-perturbative structural approaches. 
 
13. Discussion 
The present manuscript proposes a dynamic extension of GFAT. 
The main conceptual advance is the distinction between particle architectures and process 
architectures. Particle architectures are associated with intrinsic rest energies. Process 
architectures are associated with transition strengths. 
The analogy with Feynman diagrams is central. Feynman diagrams already encode 
propagation and interaction explicitly. GFAT proposes that holonomy and recursion should 
also be treated as primitive architectural sectors. 
This does not invalidate Feynman diagrammatics. On the contrary, it places GFAT in direct 
continuity with it. Feynman diagrams remain the standard perturbative computational tool. 
GFAT provides an additional structural interpretation of the same process. 
The strengths of the present framework are: 
1. It gives a structural interpretation of reactions.

===== PAGE 14 =====
14	
2. It distinguishes static and dynamic architectures. 
3. It connects directly to Feynman diagrammatics. 
4. It produces testable decay-energy and lifetime relations. 
5. It identifies the product/educt quotient as non-universal. 
The limitations are also clear: 
1. The process multiplicity Π!"#$ must be rigorously derived. 
2. Selection factors require a systematic theory. 
3. Hadronic decays need a more complete treatment of composite architecture. 
4. The mapping from arbitrary Feynman diagrams to GFAT architectures remains to 
be formalized. 
5. Experimental validation remains preliminary. 
 
14. Conclusions 
This manuscript introduced the Process Architecture Principle, according to which every 
particle reaction or decay possesses an underlying Process Green-Function Architecture. 
The main conclusions are: 
1. GFAT can be extended from static particle architectures to dynamic process 
architectures. 
2. The framework provides a structural extension of Green-function theory. 
3. The analogy with the Feynman diagram method is direct and central. 
4. Propagation and interaction correspond to the standard propagators and vertices of 
Feynman diagrams. 
5. Holonomy and recursion are proposed as additional primitive architectural sectors. 
6. The Architectural Multiplicity of a Process, Π!"#$, provides a natural structural 
analogue of transition strength. 
7. The leading GFAT decay-width formula is 
Γ5678=ΓC𝑔!"#$

8. The product/educt quotient is not supported as a universal amplitude factor. 
9. It may instead represent an architectural restructuring index in selected composite

10. Future work should develop a rigorous Feynman–GFAT mapping and perform 
large-scale validation against decay-width data.
