Abstract
We present the spectrum of stationary black hole solutions associated with the
first non-trivial member of the Dym family of boundary conditions. The analysis
combines the holonomy conditions provided by the Chern-Simons formulation of
AdS3 gravity with the periodic solutions of the stationary Dym equation. The
resulting spacetimes are characterized by two positive real parameters, two positive
integers, and a relative phase, generically describing stationary, non-axisymmetric
black holes.

1 Introduction

in the literature [1–18]. Three-dimensional gravity with a negative cosmological constant
has proven to be a particularly fruitful arena for such exploration, owing to its relative

The present work aims to characterize the spectrum of stationary black hole solu-
tions compatible with the Dym boundary conditions. Our analysis is carried out in the
Chern–Simons formulation, where black holes can be defined in the Euclidean frame-
work [41, 53]. In particular, we impose two conditions:
1. The Euclidean manifold must have the topology of a solid torus.
2. The corresponding solution to the Dym equation must satisfy periodic boundary
conditions in the angular coordinateϕ.
We demonstrate the existence of solutions to theN= 1 Dym equation fulfilling these
requirements, representing non-axisymmetric black hole spacetimes.
The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we derive the sufficient conditions that
the functionLmust satisfy in order for the corresponding Euclidean manifold to describe
a solid torus. Section 3 is devoted to the construction of periodic solutions to the Dym
equation. In Section 4 we study the metric properties of the resulting black hole. We
conclude with remarks regarding the higher-order equations in the Dym family.

To conclude this section, we observe that the absence of axisymmetry in the present
solutions renders the identification of the black hole horizon significantly more delicate.

Stationary non-axisymmetric black holes are comparatively rare in the literature, mainly
due to four-dimensional uniqueness theorems [67–76]. Explicit examples are typically
found in higher or lower dimensions, or with different asymptotic behaviour [31, 32, 35,
77–82]. In our case, the spacetime admits the exact Killing vector field∂ t, which allows us
to define the associated Killing horizonρ=ρ K(ϕ) as the surface where its norm becomes
null. Likewise, the hypersurface where the lapse functionN= 1/ √−gtt vanishes can
be written asρ=ρ N(ϕ). The explicit forms of the functionsρ K(ϕ) andρ N(ϕ), though
straightforward to derive from (4.4), are not particularly illuminating and are therefore
omitted. The interplay among these hypersurfaces, the causal structure of the spacetime,
and its asymptotic behaviour will be addressed in subsequent work.
