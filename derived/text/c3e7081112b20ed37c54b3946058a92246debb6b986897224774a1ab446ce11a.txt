Beauty production studies via quarkonia measurements
Ida Storehaug1 on behalf of the ALICE Collaboration
University of Oslo, Norway
Abstract
Beauty production in proton–proton collisions provides a test of perturbative QCD calculations, constrains beauty-
quark hadronization, and establishes a reference for measurements in heavy-ion collisions. These proceedings report
preliminary ALICE measurements of beauty production at midrapidity in pp collisions at √s=13.6 TeV using
two complementary approaches. In the first approach, the contribution from beauty-hadron decays to J/ψandψ(2S)
production is determined through measurements of their non-prompt fractions, including the first preliminary ALICE
measurement ofψ(2S). In the second approach, B ± mesons are exclusively reconstructed through the decay B ±→
J/ψK±, with J/ψ→e +e−. The resultingp T-differential B± production cross section extends the kinematic reach of
fully reconstructed B± measurements at the LHC towards lowerp T at midrapidity, reaching a previously unexplored
kinematic region. It is broadly described by state-of-the-art perturbative-QCD calculations within uncertainties.
1. Introduction
Beauty quarks have a large mass and are produced in hard partonic scatterings with a large momentum transfer.
They are therefore produced predominantly in the initial stage of the collision, and their production cross section
can be calculated using perturbative QCD. Measurements of beauty production in pp collisions serve three main
purposes: they test perturbative QCD calculations, provide information about beauty-quark hadronization through
comparisons among different beauty-hadron species, and establish the reference needed to quantify modifications of
beauty production in Pb–Pb collisions.
The preliminary results presented in these proceedings were obtained with the ALICE detector at midrapidity in
pp collisions at √s=13.6 TeV. Beauty production is studied by measuring charmonia, bound states of a charm
quark and its antiquark, produced in beauty-hadron decays. The charmonium states considered here are the J/ψand
ψ(2S) mesons. The J/ψmesons are reconstructed through the dielectron decay channel, while theψ(2S) mesons are
reconstructed through the decayψ(2S)→J/ψπ +π−, with J/ψ→e +e−. The two approaches provide complemen-
tary information about beauty production. In the first approach, non-prompt charmonia, originating from beauty-
hadron decays, are measured to obtain information inclusive over beauty-hadron species. In the second approach, the
exclusive reconstruction of individual beauty hadrons in decay channels containing a charmonium state allows the
production of a specific species to be measured.
The exclusive reconstruction of beauty hadrons in ALICE was enabled by the comprehensive detector upgrade
prior to Run 3. The new Inner Tracking System provides improved tracking, impact-parameter resolution, and
secondary-vertex reconstruction close to the interaction point, which are essential for identifying the displaced de-
cay topology of beauty hadrons [1]. The upgraded Time Projection Chamber supports continuous readout at higher
interaction rates [2]. Together with the online selection of signal candidates, these improvements provide both the spa-
tial resolution and the large data samples required to reconstruct exclusive beauty-hadron decays despite their small
branching fractions and limited reconstruction efficiencies. The exclusive measurement of B± mesons presented here
accesses a previously unexplored low-pT region for fully reconstructed B± mesons at midrapidity at the LHC.

2. Beauty production through non-prompt charmonia
The J/ψandψ(2S) mesons are classified as prompt or non-prompt according to their production origin. Prompt
charmonia are produced at the primary collision vertex, either directly from accpair or through decays of higher char-
monium states, whereas non-prompt charmonia originate from weak decays of beauty hadrons. Only the non-prompt
contribution therefore provides access to beauty production and must be separated from the prompt contribution. This
separation is possible because beauty hadrons travel a measurable distance before decaying, typically of the order of
a few hundred micrometers; for example,cτ B+≈491.1µm [3]. The resulting difference in decay length can be used
to distinguish prompt from non-prompt charmonia.
To quantify the contribution from beauty-hadron decays to charmonium production, the prompt and non-prompt
charmonium yields are extracted through a simultaneous maximum-likelihood fit to the two-dimensional distribution
of invariant mass and pseudoproper decay length. The non-prompt fraction,f B, is then defined as the non-prompt
yield divided by the inclusive yield:
fB = Nnon-prompt
Nprompt +N non-prompt
,(1)
whereN prompt andN non-prompt are the extracted prompt and non-prompt charmonium yields, respectively.
With the larger Run 3 data sample, the ALICE measurement of non-prompt charmonium production has been
extended from the J/ψto theψ(2S). The left panel of Figure 1 presents the first preliminary ALICE measurement
of the non-prompt fraction,f B, ofψ(2S) mesons, compared with measurements from CMS [4], ATLAS [5], and
LHCb [6]. Despite the different collision energies, rapidity intervals, and decay channels, the measurements show a
similar increase off B withp T. This indicates that beauty-hadron decays contribute an increasingly large fraction of
the inclusiveψ(2S) yield toward higherp T.
The right panel compares the ALICE measurements of the non-prompt fractions of J/ψandψ(2S) mesons with
each other and with EPOS4 calculations [7, 8]. At lowp T, the measurements show a hint that the non-prompt fraction
may be larger for theψ(2S) than for the J/ψ, which could indicate a larger relative contribution from beauty-hadron
decays toψ(2S) production in this region. EPOS4 provides a reasonable description of the observedp T dependence.
3. Exclusive reconstruction of B ± mesons
The exclusive reconstruction of individual beauty-hadron species provides information complementary to that
obtained from non-prompt charmonia by giving direct access to the parent-hadron production kinematics. In this
analysis, the B± mesons are reconstructed through the decay B±→J/ψK ±, with J/ψ→e +e−.

Candidate J/ψmesons are reconstructed from oppositely charged electron pairs in events recorded with an online
trigger requiring a dielectron candidate. The pairs are required to satisfy a selection on their invariant mass and
are then combined with charged-kaon candidates, identified using information from the TPC and TOF, to form B ±
candidates. Selections on the secondary-vertex fit quality suppress combinatorial background, while a requirement on
the decay length of the B± candidate suppresses prompt J/ψmesons randomly combined with kaon candidates.
The signal yield is extracted from a fit to the invariant-mass distribution of the selected B ± candidates. The
combinatorial background is described by an analytical function, while the correlated-background shapes and their
normalizations relative to the signal are constrained using Monte Carlo simulations, with some variation allowed in
the fit. The left panel of Figure 2 shows the fit in the interval 6<p T <8 GeV/c.
The extracted yields are corrected for the detector acceptance and efficiency and normalized by the branching-
ratio product, BR(B±→J/ψK ±)×BR(J/ψ→e +e−)=(6.09±0.12)×10 −5, and the integrated luminosity,L int =
(49.6±1.9) pb −1. The resultingp T-differential production cross section is compared in the right panel of Figure 2
with the B+ measurement from CMS [9] and the ALICE B0 measurement [10]. The measurements show a similarp T
dependence and are generally compatible within uncertainties.
A primary motivation for measuring beauty production in pp collisions is to test perturbative QCD calculations.
The left panel of Figure 3 therefore compares the measuredp T-differential B± production cross section with predic-
tions from FONLL [11], GM-VFNS with modified factorization and renormalization scales [12], SACOT-m T [13],
andk T-factorization [14]. The right panel shows the ratio of the measured cross section to each model prediction. The
measured spectrum is broadly described within the theoretical uncertainties. The FONLL and modified-scale GM-
VFNS predictions reproduce the experimental values over most of the measuredpT range. The SACOT-mT prediction
tends to lie above the measurement, while thek T-factorization prediction tends to lie below it, although both remain
compatible within uncertainties.
