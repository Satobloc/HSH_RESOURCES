4.1 Born’s Rule and Informational Deficit
Born’s rule [7], which assigns probabilities as the square of the wave function ampli-
tude (|ψ|2), can be understood within our framework as a consequence of projecting
a higher-dimensional deterministic signal onto a lower-resolution observational window.
The probabilitypin this context reflects the degree of informational deficit rather than
an ontological randomness.
The binary entropy derivativeH′(p)then quantifies how rapidly this deficit is resolved
upon measurement. When a measurement is performed, the observer gains information
that collapses the gradient, driving the system toward one of the deterministic boundaries.
This perspective offers a natural resolution to the measurement problem: the “collapse”
is not a mysterious physical process but the inevitable consequence of closing an informa-
tional gap.
4.2 Superdeterminism and the HCSA Framework
Our approach aligns naturally with superdeterministic interpretations while avoiding their
usual philosophical costs. In the HCSA, the apparent violation of statistical independence
in Bell experiments is not a conspiracy but a natural consequence of the hierarchical struc-
ture: measurement settings and outcomes are both part of the same 121-unit deterministic
signal. The gradient analysis shows that what appears as non-local correlation is simply
the manifestation of shared informational structure across different observational tiers.
4.3 Toward a Deterministic Quantum Theory
By treating the binary entropy gradient as the fundamental dynamical object, we open
the door to a fully deterministic reformulation of quantum mechanics. Future work may
explore whether the Schrödinger equation itself can be derived as an effective description
emerging from the underlying mizanic dynamics when viewed through a limited informa-
tional resolution.
The entropy gradient thus serves as both a diagnostic tool and a bridge between
the epistemic limitations of the observer and the ontological determinism of the cosmic
architecture.
