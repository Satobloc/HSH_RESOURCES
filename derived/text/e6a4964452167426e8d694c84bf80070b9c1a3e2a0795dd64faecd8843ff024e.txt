Abstract
The Standard Model assigns an independent Yukawa coupling to each quark
flavour, without any physical explanation for the observed mass hierarchy spanning
five orders of magnitude. We propose that heavy quark masses arise as deformation
energies stored in the vacuum itself: the up and down quarks are primary vacuum
topologies, and the heavier quarks arise when injected energy compresses those
topologies through successive levels of transformation.
Two primary vacuum topologies are considered: (i) an equilateral-triangle ar-
rangement (up-type) and (ii) a collinear arrangement (down-type). Compression
of either topology by the factorx=r 0/rleads to the exact deformation-energy
relation derived directly from the pair potentialV=−k/r+A/r 2 without addi-
tional assumptions. The vacuum stiffness is considered as the local spring constant
which scales asx 4, making each successive compression level much harder than
the one before. The proposed model identifies two hierarchical levels of vacuum
transformation. We propose two hierarchical levels of vacuum transformation. The
first-level vacuum transformation compresses each topology to its lowest Bohr level
(n= 1), giving charm and, through a triangle-collinear topology transition, bot-
tom. The second level vacuum transformation drives the up-type topology into the
x4-stiffened nonlinear spring at leveln= 5, giving top. A relativistic Bohr quan-
tization condition together with the nonlinear vacuum stiffening yields the cubic
Bohr ladderx 2
n =n 3K, whereK=ℏc/(m 0c2r0). The mass of the observed charm,
bottom, and top quark masses are reproduced with a maximum error 2.0% using
no free parameters beyond the light-quark mass values. The strange quark stands
outside both transformation levels as the ground state of the hadronic confinement
potential. Its anomalously long lifetime follows directly from the absence of any
lower confinement level accessible to the strong interaction, and the strangeness
quantum number is identified as the AVF confinement quantum number.
This paper explains the masses of the three experimentally observed heavy
quarks and addresses the anomalously long lifetime of the strange quark. The frame-
work does not exclude the possibility of additional higher-order vacuum-transformation
states under sufficiently extreme conditions. Whether such states become experi-
mentally observable or manifest as new heavy quark resonances remains an open
question.

1 Introduction
Heavy quarks are produced in sufficiently energetic particle collisions, such as those at the
Large Hadron Collider (LHC) [1], through several processes including gluon–gluon fusion,
quark–antiquark annihilation, and two-photon fusion [2]. Whenever the centre-of-mass
energy exceeds the threshold 2m Q, a heavy quark–antiquark pair can be created. The six
known quark flavours are organised into three generations. The first generation consists
of the up (u) and down (d) quarks, the stable constituents of ordinary matter. The second
generation contains the strange (s) and charm (c) quarks; the third contains the bottom
(b) and top (t) quarks. Their masses and electric charges are listed in Table 1.

The Active Vacuum Framework (AVF) [4] approaches the quark spectrum from a vac-
uum energy deformation perspective. In a recent paper [5], it is shown that only the up
and down quarks are equilibrium configurations of the vacuum deformation field, consid-
ered here as primary vacuum topologies. Their masses arise from the balance between
Coulomb attraction and short-range repulsion within the confined charge arrangement,
without Yukawa couplings.
The core hypothesis of the present paper is that the four heavier quarks are not
independent field species but quantised compression states of the two primary vacuum
topologies. When a sufficiently energetic collision transfers energy into a confined quark
topology, that energy is stored as vacuum deformation energy rather than radiated away.
The deformed topology must settle at a quantised compression level determined by a rel-
ativistic Bohr condition in the vacuum’s own elastic medium. As compression increases,
the vacuum stiffness grows as the fourth power of the compression factor, creating qual-
itatively distinct transformation levels. The physical narrative of the model follows the
chain: Primary Vacuum Topologies→Vacuum Compression→x 4 Vacuum Stiffening
→Hierarchical Vacuum Transformations→Heavy-Quark Mass Hierarchy.
We propose that heavy-quark masses arise through two discrete levels of vacuum
transformation. Their mathematical structure and physical consequences are derived in
subsequent Sections. The two levels and one special state are:
i)First Level Vacuum Transformation(n= 1): the lowest Bohr level of each
primary topology. The up-type triangle gives charm; a cross-topology transition gives
bottom.
ii)Second Level Vacuum Transformation(n= 5): the up-type topology compressed
into thex 4-stiffened nonlinear spring, giving top.
iii)Confinement ground state: the strange quark, occupying the lowest rung of the
hadronic confinement potential, below the first transformation level. It is not a
vacuum-transformation state but a confinement state; its anomalously long lifetime
follows directly from this classification.

topologies and pair potentials, and introduces the concept of compression coordinate
and deformation energy. Section 3 derives the two transformation levels and the Cubic
Bohr Ladder. Section 4 works out the mass of each heavy quark individually. Section 5
compares predictions with experiment and identifies testable consequences.

4.3.2 Long lifetime from absence of lower level.
The cubic Bohr ladder defines a decay cascade, such that a compressed topology at level
nunwinds asndecreases, emitting aWboson at each step, on the strong-interaction
timescaleτ strong ∼10 −23 s. The strange quark is at the lowest rung of the hadronic
confinement potential, and there is no rung below it within the same topology family. The
strong interaction has no mechanism to lower the confinement quantum number further;
decay requires the weak interaction to change topology family (collinear→triangular, or
vice versa), which operates on timescale∼10 −10 s.
The conserved quantum numberSintroduced phenomenologically by Gell-Mann and
Nishijima [6, 7] is identified here as the AVF confinement quantum number:|S|= 1 when
the collinear topology occupies its confinement ground state. Strong interactions conserve
Sbecause they act within a fixed topology family and cannot modify the confinement
level. Weak interactions violateSbecause they change the topology family.

5.2 Predictions of the framework
5.2.1 Intermediate vacuum transformation states.
The cubic Bohr ladder predicts additional quantised compression levels between the first
and second vacuum transformations. Using Eq. (10), the intermediate levelsn= 2,3,4
correspond to characteristic masses of approximately 11, 37, and 88 GeV respectively.
The absence of experimentally established quarks at these masses suggests that such
highly compressed vacuum configurations, if produced, relax extremely rapidly through
electroweak processes before forming observable hadronic resonances. Their possible ex-
istence may nevertheless be reflected through broad resonant structures or anomalies in
multi-boson production at future high-energy colliders.
5.2.2 Confinement radius.
The strange-quark confinement radiusR conf = 1.178 fm is a prediction of the present
framework. It represents the natural size of the confinement ground state obtained from
the WKB quantisation condition without introducing additional free parameters. Fu-
ture lattice-QCD and strangeness-sensitive measurements may provide opportunities for
testing this prediction.
5.2.3 Higher-order vacuum transformations.
The hierarchical vacuum-transformation mechanism is not restricted to the currently
observed heavy-quark spectrum. In the Active Vacuum Framework, each heavy quark
represents a quantised vacuum-compression state rather than a fundamental particle gen-
eration. Consequently, the framework does not impose an upper limit on the number
of possible vacuum-transformation levels. Additional higher-order compressed vacuum
states may exist at substantially larger compression factors and correspondingly higher
masses. The absence of experimentally observed states beyond the top quark, however,
does not mean that the hierarchy terminates there. Rather, it indicates that present
collider energies have not yet accessed those vacuum-compression regimes, or that such
highly compressed states relax too rapidly to produce experimentally distinguishable res-
onances. The present work explains the known heavy-quark spectrum without assuming
that it represents the complete set of possible vacuum-transformation states.

The proposed heavy quark model establishes the mass-formation mechanism for the
presently observed quark spectrum, but does not address three areas deferred to sub-

6 Summary and Conclusion
The hierarchical vacuum-transformation picture provides a unified interpretation of the
presently observed heavy-quark mass formation through quantised vacuum-compression
states. The presently known heavy quarks naturally organise into two hierarchical vacuum
transformations together with the strange-quark confinement ground state. The frame-
work itself does not require the vacuum-transformation hierarchy to terminate at the top
quark; additional transformation levels may emerge under sufficiently energetic conditions
beyond those currently accessible experimentally. The core of the physical narrative of
the paper follows the chain: Primary Vacuum Topologies→Vacuum Compression→
x4 Vacuum Stiffening→Hierarchical Vacuum Transformations→Heavy-Quark Mass
Hierarchy. The up and down quarks are primary vacuum states at their natural radii.
When sufficient energy is injected, each primary topology is compressed to a quantised
level set by the relativistic Bohr condition in the vacuum’s elastic medium. The vacuum
stiffness grows asx 4, creating two qualitatively distinct transformation levels that account
for the three observed heavy-quark masses. The strange quark stands apart as the ground
state of the hadronic confinement potential. The main quantitative results established in
this work are:
