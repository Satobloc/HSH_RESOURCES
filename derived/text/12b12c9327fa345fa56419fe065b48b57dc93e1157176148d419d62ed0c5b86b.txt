Abstract
We explore a unified field framework in which the fundamental degrees
of freedom are four-level qudits residing on the sites of a discrete lattice,
withSU(4) gauge variables living on the links. The four basis states of
each qudit correspond to a singlet “gravity-mode” and a triplet of gauge-
modes. Effective symmetry breakingSU(4)→SU(3)×U(1) naturally
decomposes the qudit field into a geometric sector and a gauge sector.
Spatial variations in the singlet amplitude generate a scalar notion of
curvature, leading to metric-like behavior in the continuum limit, while
excitations in the triplet sector give rise to gauge interactions.
Because the singlet and triplet sectors couple through the unified
SU(4) structure, the local geometric density can influence the effective
mass of the triplet fields. At the effective level, this allows for the pos-
sibility of a curvature-driven symmetry breaking in the early universe,
leading to a composite Higgs-like condensate and a correspondinggeo-
metric electroweak epoch. This mechanism is exploratory and relies on
effective operators consistent with the symmetries of the model.
The framework is intended as a conceptual proposal illustrating how
geometry and gauge interactions might emerge from a single discrete quan-
tum substrate. It highlights several avenues for further development,
including the treatment of fermions, quantitative predictions, and the
derivation of effective couplings from the microscopicSU(4) theory.

1 Introduction
The unification of gravity with the gauge interactions of the Standard Model re-
mains one of the central open challenges in theoretical physics. General relativ-
ity describes gravitation as the curvature of a smooth spacetime manifold, while
quantum field theory models the electromagnetic, weak, and strong interactions
as gauge fields defined on that manifold. Despite their successes, these frame-
works rest on fundamentally different principles, and a single description that
encompasses both geometric and gauge phenomena has yet to be established.
Traditional approaches—including string theory [1, 2], loop quantum gravity
[3, 4], and grand unified theories [5, 6]—typically require extended mathemati-
cal structures, high unification scales, or a continuous spacetime background as
a starting point.
In this work, we explore a different possibility: that geometry and gauge
interactions may emerge from asingle discrete quantum substrate. We consider
a lattice whose sites host four-level qudits transforming under the fundamental
representation of a unifiedSU(4) gauge group. The four basis states of each qu-
dit correspond to four interaction modes: a singlet “gravity-mode” and a triplet
of gauge-modes. After effective symmetry breaking, the qudit field decomposes
into a geometric sector and a gauge sector. Spatial variations in the singlet
amplitude define a scalar notion of curvature, while excitations in the triplet
sector give rise to electromagnetic-, weak-, and strong-like interactions. In this
picture, spacetime geometry, gauge fields, and matter-like excitations arise as
emergent phenomena of a single qudit–gauge system.
This framework draws on several converging developments. Lattice gauge
theory demonstrates that discrete formulations can capture essential features
of non-Abelian gauge dynamics [7, 8]. Qudit-based quantum systems naturally
encode multi-state internal degrees of freedom [9, 10]. Approaches to quantum
gravity—including spin networks, causal dynamical triangulations, and tensor
network geometries—suggest that spacetime itself may be emergent from dis-
crete quantum degrees of freedom [11, 12, 13]. Meanwhile, the decomposi-
tion of theSU(4) fundamental representation into a triplet and a singlet under
SU(3)×U(1) provides a natural mechanism for distinguishing gauge and geo-
metric sectors within a unified representation.
A central feature of the model is the coupling between the singlet (geometry)
and triplet (gauge/Higgs) sectors through the unifiedSU(4) potential. At the
effective level, this coupling allows the local geometric density to influence the
mass of the triplet fields. We explore the possibility that the relaxation of
curvature in the early universe could trigger a second-stage symmetry breaking,
leading to a composite Higgs-like condensate and a correspondinggeometric
electroweak epoch. This mechanism is speculative and is introduced here as an
effective description consistent with the symmetries of the model.
The remainder of this paper develops the formal structure of the model, ex-
amines its emergent geometric and gauge behavior, and illustrates how familiar
physical phenomena may arise from the unified qudit–gauge substrate. Sec-
tion III introduces the lattice construction and theSU(4) qudit representation.

2 Background and Motivation
The idea that spacetime and the fundamental interactions may emerge from a
deeper quantum substrate has gained increasing attention across multiple areas
of theoretical physics. While general relativity treats spacetime as a smooth dif-
ferentiable manifold endowed with a metric, and quantum field theory describes
matter and forces as excitations of fields defined on that manifold, several con-
ceptual and technical challenges suggest that this continuum description may
not be fundamental. Among these challenges are the non-renormalizability of
perturbative quantum gravity, the black hole information problem, and the ap-
parent discreteness implied by holographic entropy bounds [14, 15].
Discrete approaches to quantum gravity, including loop quantum gravity
[11, 16], causal dynamical triangulations [12, 17], and spin foam models [18],
propose that spacetime geometry arises from combinatorial or algebraic struc-
tures rather than a pre-existing manifold. Similarly, tensor network models
have demonstrated that geometric notions such as distance, curvature, and
even emergent gravitational dynamics can arise from entanglement patterns
in many-body quantum systems [13, 19]. These developments suggest that ge-
ometry may be a derived concept, emerging from more fundamental quantum
degrees of freedom.
Parallel to these efforts, lattice gauge theory has provided a powerful non-
perturbative framework for studying gauge interactions [7, 8]. By discretizing
spacetime into a lattice and placing gauge variables on the links, lattice gauge
theory captures essential features of non-Abelian gauge dynamics, including
confinement, chiral symmetry breaking, and phase transitions. Importantly,
lattice formulations demonstrate that gauge fields can be defined without refer-
ence to a smooth underlying manifold, relying instead on group-valued variables
associated with discrete structures.
The present work draws inspiration from both of these lines of research. We
propose that the fundamental degrees of freedom of the universe are not fields
defined on a manifold but ratherqudits—finite-dimensional quantum systems—

them suitable for representing the internal structure of matter and interaction

The choice of a four-level qudit is motivated by the structure of the funda-
mental interactions. The Standard Model contains three gauge interactions—
electromagnetic, weak, and strong—while gravity is described separately by gen-
eral relativity. A four-level system provides a natural way to encode these four
interaction modes within a single quantum object. Moreover, the fundamental

The idea of unifying interactions through higher-dimensional gauge groups
has a long history, including the Pati–Salam model [20],SO(10) unification
[21], andE 6-based theories [22]. However, these approaches typically embed
the Standard Model gauge group into a larger continuous group defined on a
smooth spacetime. In contrast, our framework does not assume a pre-existing
manifold or continuous geometry. Instead, geometry and gauge interactions
both emerge from the dynamics of qudits and gauge variables on a discrete
lattice.
The unifiedSU(4) structure plays a central role in our approach. At high
energies or short distances, the fullSU(4) symmetry governs the dynamics of
the qudit field. At lower energies, effective symmetry breaking toSU(3)×U(1)
separates the qudit into a gauge-mode triplet and a gravity-mode singlet. This
breaking pattern is reminiscent of GUT symmetry breaking but differs in that
the singlet is interpreted not as a sterile matter field but as a geometric degree of
freedom. Spatial variations in the singlet amplitude define a discrete notion of
curvature, while excitations in the triplet sector give rise to gauge interactions.
This perspective aligns with the broader idea that geometry may emerge
from quantum information. Recent work has shown that entanglement entropy
can encode geometric features of spacetime [23, 24], and that tensor networks
can reproduce aspects of AdS/CFT duality [13]. In our model, the qudit field
plays a dual role: it encodes both geometric and gauge information, with the
unifiedSU(4) structure ensuring that these sectors are not fundamentally dis-
tinct.
Finally, the lattice formulation provides a natural setting for defining dy-
namics without reference to a background geometry. The gauge variables on
the links define parallel transport and interaction strengths, while the qudit
field defines local internal states. Together, they form a self-contained quantum
system from which geometry, gauge fields, and matter emerge in appropriate
limits.

13.3 Interplay Between Geometry and Symmetry Break-

Absence of fermions and chiral structure.The model currently contains

fermions, chiral asymmetry, anomaly cancellation, and Standard Model quan-

Non-Standard-Model breaking pattern.The internal breakingSU(3)→

Lack of quantitative predictions.While qualitative phenomena such as

Cosmological uncertainties.The geometric electroweak epoch is exploratory.
Its implications for reheating, baryogenesis, dark matter, and nucleosynthesis
have not been analyzed.
14.2 Future Work
Several directions appear promising:
•Deriving effective couplings from the microscopicSU(4) lattice action.
•Incorporating fermions and exploring mechanisms for chiral emergence.
•Developing quantitative predictions for gravitational and gauge observ-
ables.
•Studying the continuum limit more systematically, including possible emer-
gent spin-2 modes.
•Exploring cosmological dynamics, including reheating and domain wall
evolution.
•Investigating whether alternative symmetry-breaking patterns withinSU(4)
yield closer connections to the Standard Model.
These open directions highlight the speculative nature of the present frame-
work while identifying concrete steps toward a more complete theory.
