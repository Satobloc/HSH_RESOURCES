Abstract
“O Great Ennead, who are in Heliopolis… make this pyramid endure, and this his temple, for ever and 
ever.” — Pyramid Texts, Utterance 601 (Mercer 1952; cf. Faulkner 1969)
The Great Pyramid of Giza was called Akhet-Khufu — the Horizon of Khufu — by its builders. No mummy 
has been found in any of the three Giza pyramids. The chambers are bare. The sarcophagus in the King's 
Chamber was placed during construction, too wide for any passage it would have needed to traverse. The 
causeways point west. These are not anomalies requiring special pleading. They are features of a coherent 
design.
This paper proposes that the Giza complex is the permanent architectural encoding of the Zep Tepi 
cosmological system — the origin narrative of Egyptian civilisation, traced through a documented migration 
corridor from the Saharan pastoral tradition to the Nile Valley. At landscape scale, the complex instantiates 
a four-element relational system derived from the Richat origin landscape: central elevation, bounded 
sacred space, engineered transition water, and a western processional axis. The engineered harbour basin 
(Younes et al. 2024), the Ahramat Branch alongside 31 pyramids (Ghoneim et al. 2024), the concentric 
enclosure walls, and causeway orientations averaging 269.4° — within 0.6° of due west (Nell & Ruggles 
2014) — are the cosmological argument made physical. 
At dimensional scale, the Great Pyramid's six exterior measurements show correspondences through 
the Wedjat binary fraction system across seven knowledge domains: π, φ, √5 and √6; Earth's polar 
and equatorial radii (at a lower evidential tier); the golden angle; the anomalistic lunar month, 360-day 
schematic year, civil and sidereal years, and 70-day Sirius invisibility; and the synodic periods of 
Saturn, Jupiter, Venus and Mars. The Sothic cycle was tested and is reported below as one of the four 
documented misses. Table 3 sets out these ten — the same ten of fourteen reached in the Monte 
Carlo analysis; the mathematical constants and terrestrial radii lie outside that list and carry no Monte 
Carlo support. The stellar shafts encoded specific stars at the construction epoch; the exterior 
concavity marks the equinoxes; the Royal Cubit is self-referentially recoverable from the π 
correspondence. The Wedjat fraction system — documented in Old Kingdom grain measurement, the 
Rhind Mathematical Papyrus, and Egyptian temple orientations — is the proposed structural key.
Two specificity tests are reported. Comparing the sixty-fourths against neighbouring grids k/48, k/60 and 
k/72 at nine documented Old Kingdom pyramids, the Great Pyramid returns a margin of +5 where the 
mean across twenty thousand randomly dimensioned pyramids is −1.32 (p = 0.0050), holding across four 
of five tolerances. Holding the seked at 5½ and varying the base across every whole cubit from 75 to 600, 
the built base of 440 ranks in the top 4.2 per cent of 526 candidates throughout the observational tolerance 
band and ranks third at its centre; against three hundred shadow target lists its median rank is 275 of 526, 
and it took the maximum in none of the three hundred trials (p < 0.0033). The standing of 440 is therefore 
specific to values Egyptian sources record as astronomically significant. Comparative analysis against nine 
major ritual landscapes confirms the full four-element relational integration at Giza is unparalleled before 
the 12th century CE. Nine falsifiable predictions are stated. The evidence draws from nine independent 
disciplines — hydrology, genetics, archaeozoology, textual analysis, archaeoastronomy, mathematical 
analysis, cultural memory theory, archaeometry, and comparative architecture. The convergence across 
independent lines is the argument.

Introduction
The ancient Egyptians did not call this site Giza. That name is medieval Arabic. They called it Imentet 
— The West — and Rostau (rả-stảw), the Entrance to the Passages of Osiris. The pyramid at its 
centre they called Akhet-Khufu: the Horizon of Khufu. Not the tomb. The horizon — the threshold 
where the sun transforms, where the mortal becomes divine, where the First Time is renewed. That

name survives in the Merer Papyrus, the eyewitness logbook of an official who transported its stones 
(Tallet 2017). It is not a later poetic attribution. It is the construction-era designation. Imentet: the 
direction the memory came from. Rostau: the transformation it was built to produce. This paper argues 
that the architecture says the same thing.
The standard interpretation of the Giza pyramids as royal tombs is internally coherent and accounts 
for much of the evidence. What it does not readily account for is the specific compound of features 
documented here: no mummy recovered from any of the three Giza pyramids, a sarcophagus placed 
during construction and never removed, interior chambers bare of any inscription or preparation for 
text (Petrie 1883, pp. 67–69), stellar shafts sealed at both ends with polished interior faces, and three 
successive causeways deviating in opposed senses about the east–west axis yet averaging within a 
degree of due west across three reigns (Nell & Ruggles 2014; Lehner 1985). Each feature has an 
individual explanation. The constraint is that any viable interpretation must account for all of them 
simultaneously — and must do so without treating the pyramid’s own documented name as incidental.
The preceding papers of this series have assembled the transmission chain that makes a different 
reading possible. A pastoralist population carrying the Zep Tepi cosmological system moved northeast 
along a documented Highland Corridor as the African Humid Period collapsed. Ancient genomes are 
now available from three geographically independent North African nodes (Salem et al. 2025; Lipson 
et al. 2025; Morez Jacobs et al. 2025), documenting a distinct North African lineage present across 
the region through the period in question. Salem et al. conclude that pastoralism entered this lineage 
by cultural diffusion rather than population replacement, and Lipson et al. document high continuity of 
local ancestry in the eastern Maghreb. The genetic record therefore supports the movement of 
practice and knowledge rather than large-scale replacement of populations — the mechanism this 
series proposes, and a weaker requirement than migration in the demographic sense. Their 
cosmological system encoded the Richat Structure as the landscape of the First Time. The Shemsu 
Hor are proposed here to have preserved and transmitted this knowledge across fifteen centuries of 
Egyptian institutional memory. The pyramid form itself was already encoded in stone at seven 
provincial cult centres before Khufu was born (Dreyer & Kaiser 1980; Belmonte, Shaltout & Fekri 
2005). What was built at Giza in the 4th Dynasty is proposed here not to have been an invention but a 
culmination of a tradition whose origins are the subject of this series.
The mechanism by which cosmological knowledge survives across millennia is not conjectural — it is 
the subject of an established scholarly literature. Assmann (1995, 2001) distinguishes communicative 
memory (living within three to four generations) from cultural memory (preserved over centuries and 
millennia through canonical texts, ritual repetition, and institutional structures). Cultural memory of this 
kind operates through what Assmann calls “figures of memory” — narrative and symbolic patterns that 
anchor collective identity to a foundational past event. The Zep Tepi is precisely such a figure: a 
temporal anchor (“the First Time”) that Egyptian priestly institutions returned to consistently across 
fifteen centuries of dynastic record. The durability of such anchors is well-documented: Australian 
Aboriginal oral traditions have been demonstrated to preserve accurate coastal geography from 
events approximately 7,000 years ago (Nunn & Reid 2016). The transmission proposed here — 
approximately 1,500 years from the African Humid Period collapse to the 4th Dynasty — falls well 
within the documented range of canonical cultural memory in pre-literate and proto-literate institutional 
contexts. The Shemsu Hor records are proposed here as the institutional expression of that preserved 
memory, not as proof of literal genealogical continuity.

The Subterranean Sanctuary: Rostau and the Design Template
The Westcar Papyrus (Berlin Papyrus 3033, c. 1650 BCE) records a primary source tradition that 
Khufu was not designing his pyramid without a template. The text states that Khufu spent time seeking 
the number of the secret chambers in the sanctuary of Thoth in order to make something similar for 
his own pyramid (Blackman 1988; Parkinson 1997). The magician Djedi knows where this number is 
recorded — in a flint chest in the room called the Inventory at Heliopolis — but cannot deliver it. The 
number is never recovered within the narrative. What the papyrus establishes without ambiguity is the 
deliberate intent: the Great Pyramid’s chamber design was to be modelled on a pre-existing sacred 
architectural template, and the numerical count of its hidden chambers was the design specification 
Khufu sought.
The Giza plateau was named pr wsir nb rstaw — the House of Osiris, Lord of the Underground 
Passages. That name is consistent with the underground complex having defined the site’s sacred 
character, on this reading preceding the monuments that rose above it. That complex — the multi-
level subterranean structure now called the Osiris Shaft, located beneath Khafre’s causeway — is the 
only structure at Giza architecturally parallel to the Abydos Osireion, a parallel Hawass confirmed 
directly, calling it “the Osirion of Giza” (Hawass 2007). Surface luminescence dating of stone within the 
Osiris Shaft — samples OT1 and OT2, taken on the second underground level rather than from the 
artifact deposits that record subsequent ritual use across dynasties — yields ages of 2930 ± 600 BCE 
and 2870 ± 570 BCE, consistent with construction before or during Khufu’s reign (Liritzis and Vafiadou 
2015, Table 1). The uncertainties are large, the sample count is two, and the authors themselves 
record no independent archaeological age estimate for these samples; the result is therefore indicative 
rather than decisive. No pharaoh inscribed his name on the structure as builder, inconsistent with a 
new royal project. Stadelmann concluded that Khafre’s causeway was built to conform to a pre-
existing feature at its location (Stadelmann 1991). The engineering logic is straightforward: excavating 
a thirty-metre multi-level shaft through solid bedrock after a causeway of hundreds of thousands of 
tonnes was built above it is structurally reckless; building a causeway around a pre-existing 
underground feature is standard Egyptian practice. The Shetayet of Sokar — the god’s hidden tomb-
shrine, located at Rosetau and understood in Egyptology as the Lower Egyptian counterpart of the 
Osiris tomb at Abydos (Edwards 1986) — anchors the plateau’s underworld geography, Sokar being 
‘lord of Rosetau’. The structural argument advanced here is that the Osiris Shaft complex was the 
defining sacred feature of that Rosetau, not a later addition to it. This paper advances as a structured 
open question — and as Prediction 9 — that the Osiris Shaft complex is proposed here as the pre-

No Mummies Have Been Found in the Giza Pyramids
Mainstream Egyptology has long interpreted the Giza pyramids as royal tombs, pointing to the 
presence of sarcophagi, mortuary temples, and later textual traditions. However, the absence of 
primary burial equipment — mummies, canopic jars, grave goods, or identifying inscriptions — in the 
original chambers of all three Giza pyramids is striking. This paper does not dispute that the pyramids 
served a funerary function. It argues that the design priorities — particularly the engineered water, 
western axis, bare walls, and stellar alignments — are better explained by a cosmological purpose 
within which the king's transformation was nested. The two readings are complementary at different 
interpretive scales.
No mummy has ever been found inside any of the three great Giza pyramids. The King's Chamber in 
the Great Pyramid of Khufu contains a lidless granite sarcophagus — too wide to have been brought 
through the ascending passage, meaning it was placed during construction — but it is empty. No 
mummy, no canopic jars, no burial goods, no identifying inscriptions were present when Caliph al-
Ma'mun breached the pyramid in approximately 820 CE.
Fragmentary bone material of uncertain date has been found in the burial chambers of the other two 
Giza pyramids. When Giovanni Belzoni entered the burial chamber of Khafre's pyramid in 1818, he 
found the granite sarcophagus containing bone fragments and rags, later identified as those of a bull 
and now linked to a Coptic-period intrusive deposit (Belzoni 1820; Lehner 1997). In 1837, Richard 
William Howard Vyse cleared the lower burial chamber of Menkaure's pyramid and discovered a large 
uninscribed basalt sarcophagus (empty, no inscriptions or burial goods), which was subsequently lost 
at sea (Vyse 1840). A separate wooden coffin with later-period human remains was also found. No 
intact burial, no identifiable royal remains, and no primary funerary equipment have been recovered 
from any of the three Giza pyramids.
The Amenpanufer confession (Papyrus Mayer B, Group VI) documents systematic stripping of royal 
mummies in the 20th Dynasty — approximately 1,200 years after Khufu's death. This accounts for the

absence of reusable valuables (gold, jewellery, canopic equipment) from later Valley of the Kings 
burials. It does not account for the total absence of any such traces in the Giza primary chambers: the 
contrast with later pyramids such as Unas and Teti, where Pyramid Texts and fragmentary physical 
remains are both present, remains stark and unexplained by robbery alone.
The sarcophagus in Khufu’s King’s Chamber is intact, lidless, and empty. It shows no evidence of 
burning or ash deposits. Had robbers destroyed the body inside the sarcophagus, residue would 
remain; none does. This rules out in-situ destruction, but not the more common practice of removing a 
body intact to strip it elsewhere, which would leave no trace. The undamaged, residue-free 
sarcophagus is therefore consistent with robbery as much as with non-burial, and is offered as one 
observation among several rather than as decisive on its own.  However, the confession does not 
explain:
Hawass himself has publicly stated his belief that “Khufu’s real burial chamber is still hidden inside the

Corridor (Procureur et al. 2023, Nature Communications), and the Menkaure voids (Helal et al. 2025,

features may yet substantially alter the interpretive landscape.
1. The sarcophagus: The granite sarcophagus in Khufu's King's Chamber is too wide for the 
passages, meaning it was placed during construction and never extracted. It is empty.
2. The structural design: The ascending passage, Grand Gallery, and relieving chambers show 
no evidence of ever having been sealed and reopened — consistent with intentional non-
interment design.
3. The absence of Pyramid Texts: Later pyramids (Unas, Teti) preserve fragments; Giza

4. The bare limestone: The walls of the King’s Chamber and Queen’s Chamber show no cutting

Robbery explains missing mummies. It does not explain the architectural design logic.
What the Egyptians Themselves Said: The Herdsmen and Shepherd Traditions
Herodotus (c. 450 BCE) recorded that his Egyptian priestly sources consistently placed Khufu's body 
elsewhere: "in a place under the earth, on an island surrounded by water drawn from the Nile" 
(Herodotus, Histories II.124). He also reported that the Egyptians of his time refused to speak the 
names of Khufu and Khafre — instead calling the pyramids after "the shepherd Philitis, who then 
pastured his flocks in this place" (II.128). Diodorus Siculus (c. 60 BCE), drawing on separate Egyptian 
sources, recorded a second tradition: "some say that [the pyramids] were built by the aforesaid kings, 
others say by herdsmen who had at one time held power" (Diodorus, Bibliotheca Historica I.64). Two 
independent Greek authors, four centuries apart, both transmit the same embedded memory: 
Egyptian oral and priestly tradition associated the construction of the pyramids with a pastoral people. 
As documented in Corridor III of this series, the Shemsu Hor were the cultural memory of Saharan 
pastoralists who arrived in Egypt from the west during the predynastic period (Corridor III, Levy 
2026c).
A note on the Herodotus ‘island surrounded by water’ passage: mainstream Egyptology reads this as 
describing the pyramid’s position — elevated above the Nile floodplain, approached by water via the

===== PAGE 8 =====
Khufu Branch canal (Sheisha et al. 2022). That reading encounters a difficulty the text itself poses: the 
passage specifies a place “under the earth”. The Great Pyramid is not under the earth. The Osiris 
Shaft is. Level 3 of the Osiris Shaft — approximately thirty metres below the plateau surface — 
features a central island permanently surrounded by a water channel fed by the Nile water table: 
precisely “under the earth, on an island surrounded by water drawn from the Nile.” Hawass 
identified this chamber as the symbolic Osirian burial at Giza — the Osirion of Giza — and it is the 
structure this paper identifies as the pre-existing Rostau sanctuary beneath the plateau. The most 
literal reading of Herodotus is that his Egyptian priestly sources were directing him to the underground 
sanctuary, not to the pyramid above it: in Egyptian tradition, Khufu’s sacred identity resided in his 
transformation as Osiris in the subterranean chamber below, not in the stone structure above. The 
mainstream reading and the present reading are not mutually exclusive — but the Osiris Shaft 
satisfies the passage’s literal description with a precision the pyramid’s above-ground position cannot 
match. A further philological note strengthens this reading: the Godley Loeb translation of Herodotus 
II.127 (the immediately adjacent passage, describing Khafre’s pyramid) reads: “the river comes in 
through a built passage and encircles an island, in which, they say, Cheops himself lies” — a 
description that, read carefully, locates Khufu’s burial within the water-circled island described under 
Khafre’s causeway, not in the pyramid above. The two water-feature traditions Herodotus records — 
the underground chambers accessed by Nile canal (II.124–126, Great Pyramid) and the canal-
encircled island (II.127, Khafre complex) — are not contradictory: they describe different cosmological 
registers of the same sacred site, consistent with the reading advanced here that the Osiris Shaft’s 
subterranean Nun-island and the surface hydraulic infrastructure constitute a single integrated 
cosmological landscape.
A fourth witness to this tradition comes from within Egyptian literary culture itself. The Westcar 
Papyrus (P. Berlin 3033, c. 1650 BCE), set at Khufu’s court, portrays the king not as a tomb-builder 
but as a seeker of hidden knowledge — specifically, the secret chambers of Thoth. In a key passage, 
the sage Djedi tells Khufu about the number of hidden rooms in the sanctuary of Thoth, and the text 
connects this esoteric knowledge to Khufu’s building ambitions, with some scholars reading the 
language as carrying resonances of the king’s desire to create something comparable to the horizon 
(Blackman 1988; noting that the text’s primary register is literary entertainment and this reading is not 
universal). The resonance, where present, is striking: an Egyptian literary text, composed roughly a 
millennium after Khufu’s reign, associates the king with esoteric knowledge and cosmic aspiration 
rather than burial — echoing the monument’s own attested name Akhet-Khufu in the contemporary 
Merer Papyrus (Tallet 2017). The Westcar Papyrus was composed roughly a millennium after Khufu’s 
reign, yet it preserves a cultural memory in which the pyramid is inseparable from esoteric knowledge 
and cosmic aspiration — not from burial. Taken together, the Merer Papyrus, Herodotus, Diodorus 
Siculus, and the Westcar Papyrus represent four ancient sources of distinct type, provenance, and 
evidentiary weight — from contemporary administrative record to Greek historiography to Egyptian 
literary tradition, composed across a span of roughly a millennium — none of which primarily frames 
the Great Pyramid as a tomb. This convergence is noted as corroborative context, not as proof of the 
cosmological hypothesis; the sources differ too greatly in reliability and intent to be treated as a unified 
evidential body.
No Hieroglyphs Inside the 4th Dynasty Pyramids
The Egyptians were obsessive documenters. Yet the three Great Pyramids at Giza have almost 
completely bare interiors. Not a single dedicatory inscription, spell, or caption. The only text found 
inside the Great Pyramid is construction graffiti in the relieving chambers — work crew names never 
intended to be seen.
This absence is anomalous for a culture that inscribed its tombs and temples exhaustively, yet it is 
what a mnemonic reading predicts. In the comparative framework of Kelly (2015), a monument built to 
encode knowledge does so structurally — in form, proportion, and alignment — rather than in text: the 
information is carried by the architecture itself rather than written upon it. On that reading the bare

interiors are not an absence to be explained away but the expected signature of a structure whose 
content is encoded in its dimensions.
The argument advanced here is not that the Giza pyramids had no funerary role. The king's 
burial and apotheosis were real and ritually significant. The claim is that the primary design logic — 
the bare walls, the engineered water, the western axis, the stellar alignments, the absence of canopic 
equipment — is better explained as a cosmological monument than as a tomb that happens to have 
anomalous features. The two purposes are nested, not opposed. A more precise formulation is that 
the pyramids' design is not optimized for burial — the absence of canopic equipment, identifying 
inscriptions, and burial goods suggests that preservation of the body was not the primary architectural 
driver.
The content of the 5th Dynasty inscriptions confirms this reading. Utterance 213, inscribed on the 
south wall of the Unas pyramid's burial chamber (c. 2345 BCE), opens the entire inscription program 
with a formula that defines its purpose: 'O Unas, you have not gone dead, you have gone alive to sit 
on the throne of Osiris.' Not burial — transformation. The pyramid texts do not describe a dead king 
lying in a tomb. They describe a living king moving through a cosmological sequence. The 4th Dynasty 
builders required no written caption for this sequence because, as the texts themselves demonstrate, 
the sequence was already understood in its architectural form. The Pyramid Texts may be the verbal 
transcription of an architectural reality that preceded them by two centuries.
One structural observation is worth registering here for future investigation: the Great Pyramid 
contains three main chambers arranged on a strict vertical axis (Subterranean, Queen’s, King’s). 
This ascending vertical arrangement — with chambers rising progressively from bedrock 
through the pyramid’s mass to near the apex — has no close parallel in any other Old Kingdom 
pyramid in the standard comparative record. Djoser’s Step Pyramid presents a vertical 
arrangement of subterranean chambers below the pyramid but does not extend this progression 
into the pyramid mass above bedrock level; the ascending arrangement of internal chambers 
within the body of the structure itself is specific to the Great Pyramid. By contrast, standard Old 
Kingdom pyramid design places the burial chamber below the pyramid in the bedrock (Lehner 
1997, pp. 108–113). The Bent Pyramid of Sneferu contains an upper chamber within the 
pyramid’s masonry body (entered via the west-face passage ~33 m above ground), which might 
appear to parallel the Great Pyramid’s ascending arrangement. However, archaeological 
evidence establishes that this upper chamber was a structural response to cracking discovered 
during construction, not a planned cosmological sequence: the tunnel connecting the two 
chambers was cut after initial construction as a remedial shoring measure, and the upper 
chamber was partially filled with rough masonry (Nuzzolo 2015). The ascending three-chamber 
progression in the Great Pyramid — Subterranean (bedrock), Queen’s (lower body), King’s 
(upper body), spanning the full vertical axis from geological base to near-apex — has no parallel 
in which all three tiers were an original, unified design intention rather than a structural accident 
or engineering correction. Belmonte and Lull (2023, pp. 396–398) confirm this by reading 
Sneferu’s two Dahshur pyramids as a paired external cosmological statement — the pyramids 
together forming a symbolic dual whole, not an attempt at internal vertical ascent architecture — 
which situates Khufu’s three-tier interior sequence as the specific innovation the 4th Dynasty 
achieved and that no predecessor had attempted. The ascending three-chamber arrangement is 
an anomaly that requires explanation within any model of the pyramid’s function.
The Bare Walls: Why the Great Pyramid Has No Hieroglyphs
The standard Egyptological explanation for the bare interior is temporal: the tradition of decorating 
pyramid interiors with Pyramid Texts began with Unas (c. 2350 BCE), 200 years after Khufu. This is 
accurate but does not explain why the tradition began when it did.
This paper proposes a complementary explanation: the Pyramid Texts appear in the 5th Dynasty 
because the living understanding of the cosmological system was beginning to fade. The Shemsu Hor

===== PAGE 10 =====
period ended at approximately 3100 BCE. The Great Pyramid was completed approximately 540 
years later — 20 generations. At that point, the cosmological tradition was still institutionally alive. The 
form of the pyramid was readable. No caption was necessary.
By the 5th Dynasty (c. 2494–2345 BCE), another 200 years had elapsed. The direct embodied 
understanding that made architectural form self-explanatory may have been attenuating. The Pyramid 
Texts are the verbal caption for an image that had once been self-evident.
The linguistic and architectural evidence supporting this temporal layering is well established in the 
Egyptological literature. James P. Allen, in the introduction to his standard translation of the Pyramid 
Texts, documents two convergent lines of evidence for the texts' antiquity relative to their 5th Dynasty 
inscription. First, "the language of the texts... is not the speech of the time they were first inscribed (ca. 
2350 BC), but an older form that had been preserved in liturgical use, perhaps for as much as half a 
century or more." The texts were not composed fresh for Unas; they were copied from older sources 
preserving an already-archaic linguistic register. Second, "the architecture of the chambers in which 
they are inscribed also shows a similar lag, representing a form that had been established more than a 
century before Unas" (Allen 2005, p. 4). Morales (2016) documents the broader transmission 
mechanism: the Pyramid Texts passed through oral ritual performance, scribal manuscript culture, and 
finally wall inscription across successive media — each stage preserving material already archaic at 
the moment of transcription (Morales 2016; cf. Allen 2005, p. 4). The ritual and spatial logic encoded in 
the texts belongs to the pyramid-building tradition of Khufu's era, not to the moment of their first 
inscription.
The Abusir Papyri (24th century BCE, Fifth Dynasty) document that pyramid complexes were 
functioning cult centers with priests and offering rosters (Posener-Kriéger 1976). The Fifth Dynasty 
wrote extensive administrative texts about pyramid cults. The Fourth Dynasty did not write inside 
pyramids because the living tradition of embodied cosmological knowledge may have made text 
unnecessary — in this reading, the stone itself was the statement.
A further line of evidence deepens this temporal picture. Manetho, the Egyptian priest-historian who 
compiled the Aegyptiaca in the early 3rd century BCE from native temple records, places pyramid 
construction in the First Dynasty under King Vavenephis: “Vavenephis, for 42 years. In his time famine 
seized the land. He reared pyramids near the town of Cho” (Waddell 1940, Loeb Classical Library, p. 
33). The site identified as Cho — Sakkara — was, as the Loeb edition’s own note records, confirmed 
by W.B. Emery’s excavations of 1935—1938, which uncovered First Dynasty tombs containing 
stepped-brick pyramid constructions (Waddell 1940, p. 33 n. 3). This places the origins of the pyramid 
form approximately four centuries before Khufu, around 3000 BCE. Direct archaeological 
corroboration comes from the seven Minor Step Pyramids of the late 3rd and early 4th Dynasty at 
provincial centers across Upper Egypt — Elephantine, Naga el-Ghoneimiya (Edfu South), el-Kula at 
Hierakonpolis, Naqada, Sinki at Abydos, Zawiet el-Meitin, and Seila — identified as a coherent group 
by Dreyer and Kaiser (1980) and astronomically analyzed by Belmonte (2005). These structures are 
oriented to Meskhetyu (the Bull’s Foreleg) at quarter-cardinal axes that cannot be explained by Nile 
flow or local topography (Levy 2026d, Phase Zero). The pyramid form was therefore already a century 
old, with stellar orientation procedures already established at provincial cult centers, by the time 
Khufu’s architects laid out Akhet-Khufu. The 4th Dynasty monuments at Giza were not an invention 
but the culminating monumental expression of a tradition already ancient when Khufu was born. If the 
pyramid form was already centuries old, neither was the cosmological grammar it expressed a 4th 
Dynasty novelty. The bare walls of Khufu’s chambers require no text not because the tradition had not 
yet developed, but because it was still fully embodied. This is precisely the pattern James Henry 
Breasted identified in 1912: the solar religion of the 5th Dynasty absorbed and transcribed an older 
stellar tradition whose living expression had been architectural, not textual (Breasted 1912). 
Breasted’s “absorption” model was a description of a fading, not an origin — and Allen’s linguistic 
evidence confirms that the Pyramid Texts were transcribed from material already archaic when they

===== PAGE 11 =====
were first inscribed (Allen 2005, p. 4). On this hypothesis, the stone spoke while the tradition lived. 
When it began to fade, the scribes began to write.
The Predynastic Grammar (c. 3,700–3,100 BCE)
The Shemsu Hor — the Followers of Horus — are the Egyptian cultural memory of the Saharan 
migrants who arrived in the Nile Valley during the 3800–3100 BCE window. This paper assumes the

Levy 2026c). In summary: the Shemsu Hor occupy a non-optional structural slot in Egyptian king-list 
architecture across fifteen centuries. Later pharaohs cite their written annals. Ptolemaic builders cite a

The External Architecture of Giza (c. 2,589–2,560 BCE)
The Akhet: Not a Tomb but a Horizon
The original Egyptian name for the Great Pyramid was Akhet-Khufu — 'Horizon of Khufu.' The Merer 
Papyrus — the eyewitness logbook of an inspector who transported stone blocks during Khufu's reign 
— repeatedly refers to the structure by this name, most explicitly in the dated entries of Logbook A 
(Tallet 2017; Tallet & Lehner 2021). The Akhet is the liminal zone where the sun rises and sets, where 
the king transforms from human to divine. It is a threshold, not a storage place for a body.
The Pyramid Texts confirm what the name encodes. Inscribed on the antechamber walls of the Unas 
pyramid (c. 2345 BCE), Utterance 260 states: 'Unas comes out on this day in the true form of a living 
Akh-spirit.' Not a dead king lying in a tomb — a living Akh-spirit emerging. Utterance 257 
(antechamber north wall) then announces the completion: 'Unas takes heaven, he splits its brass... 
Unas goes to peace, being alive in the West... Jubilate for Unas, he has taken the Horizon!' The 
pyramid's name — Akhet-Khufu, the place where Khufu takes the Horizon — is the event these 
inscriptions describe. The Pyramid Texts are the verbal transcript of what the 4th Dynasty architecture 
was built to produce.

The Benben and the Primordial Mound
Egyptian creation mythology consistently describes the origin of the world as a pyramid-shaped 
mound — the Benben — rising from the primordial waters of Nun. Pyramid Texts Utterance 600 
addresses Atum directly: "O Atum-Khepri, when you became high as the mound, you rose up as the 
benben in the mansion of the bird Benu in Heliopolis" (Allen 2005, pp. 252–253). The pyramid form is 
this cosmology made architectural.
That the pyramid was understood as the physical vehicle of this ascent — not merely its symbol — is 
confirmed by the inscriptions on the passage leading to the burial chamber of the Unas pyramid (c. 
2345 BCE). Utterance 267, inscribed on the passage south wall at the spatial threshold between 
antechamber and burial chamber, states directly: 'The earth is beaten into steps for him towards 
heaven, that he may mount on it towards heaven, and he rises on the smoke of the great fumigation. 
He flies away, this Unas, as a goose, he alights as a scarab on the empty throne which is in your boat, 
O Re.' The physical structure is the steps. The architecture does not represent the ascent — it enacts 
it, and the inscriptions confirm this at the precise spatial location where the ascent is entered.

The Zep Tepi Cosmology and the Green Sahara
This paper proposes that the Zep Tepi cosmological system originated in the Green Sahara and was 
carried to the Nile Valley by pastoralists displaced by the end of the African Humid Period. This paper 
assumes the reader is familiar with the full evidentiary chain established in Corridor I and II

===== PAGE 13 =====
(Levy 2026a, 2026b). In summary: Corridor I documents the Highland Corridor of elevated massifs 
and plateaus (Tassili n’Ajjer, Tadrart Acacus/Takarkori, Messak Settafet, Gilf Kebir) — supplemented 
by the Tamanrasset and Kufra paleorivers as eastern paleohydrological segments — as the primary 
migration route, identifies the Richat Structure as the probable landscape encoded as the primordial 
mound, and provides a 25-element testing protocol. Corridor II establishes the genomic continuity of 
the Saharan pastoralist lineage and documents the Wedjat sky-compass.
The identification of Richat as the specific origin landscape rests on a conjunction of three relatively 
rare characteristics: (1) concentric ring geometry — nested circular boundaries around a central 
elevation, uncommon in natural landscapes and preserved in the Zep Tepi cosmology as concentric 
sacred space; (2) basin-style water surrounding the mound — not linear or adjacent, but encircling the 
entire formation, matching the ‘island surrounded by water’ description in Herodotus and the Nun 
cosmology; and (3) documented human presence at the structure itself during the Green Sahara 
window — Acheulean and Neolithic tool assemblages within the same Holocene sediments that 
record the paleolakes. The elimination analysis is set out in full in Corridor I and extended in Corridor II 
(Levy 2026a, Section 3; Levy 2026b). Twenty-eight Saharan formations have now been evaluated 
against four criteria fixed in advance: sufficient physical scale to sustain a population across 
generations; independently documented Holocene water at the structure itself rather than merely in 
the surrounding region; documented physical evidence of human presence at the structure during that 
window; and a central elevation rising from that water. Twenty-seven fail at least one. Ten of those 
assessed are impact craters predating the Green Sahara, several by tens of millions of years. The 
massifs and plateaus — Tassili n’Ajjer, the Tadrart Acacus, the Gilf Kebir — have documented 
regional occupation but are ridges and tablelands rather than a unified mound within a water-retaining 
basin, and Nabta Playa is a basin, the geometric inverse of a mound. The most instructive failure is 
Semsiyat Dome: it shares the Richat’s exact geological origin and lies only 45 kilometres away, yet at 
roughly a tenth of the diameter it could never have sustained a lasting population. Scale, not ring 
morphology alone, does real work in the elimination. Only the Richat passes all four, and it alone 
advances to the seven-criteria textual filter applied in Corridor II, which it also passes. The hypothesis 
does not depend on the Richat specifically, but on a western Saharan source fitting the descriptors; 
the Richat is the only currently known candidate that fits all of them.

Causeway Orientations: A Consistent Western Pattern
The western orientation of the Giza causeways is not merely a qualitative impression. A Total Station 
survey of the Giza complex by Nell and Ruggles (2014) measured Khafre’s causeway at azimuth 103° 
26′ from eight surveyed points, and Menkaure’s at 89° 10′ from six — that is, 283.4° and 269.2° 
respectively when expressed in the westward sense, toward the pyramid. Khufu’s causeway is largely 
destroyed and was not surveyed by them; earlier measurements give a deviation from cardinality of 
−14° to −15°, an azimuth near 255° (Lauer 1947; Goyon 1969; Lehner 1985). The three do not cluster 
tightly. What they do is deviate in opposed senses about the east–west axis: Khufu roughly 14.5° 
south of west, Khafre roughly 13.4° north of west, and Menkaure within a degree of cardinal. Nell and 
Ruggles themselves describe the approximate symmetry of the Khufu and Khafre causeways about 
due east–west as noteworthy, while cautioning that it need not have been an end in itself for the

===== PAGE 15 =====
builders. The mean of the three azimuths is 269.4°, within 0.6° of due west. On the reading advanced 
here, the axis rather than any single causeway carries the orientation: three reigns produced three 
different deviations that resolve, taken together, to the cardinal westward line. This is a weaker pattern 
than uniform clustering would be, and it is stated as such.
The broader survey confirms that east-west orientation in relation to sunrise and sunset was "a, or 
even the, key factor" (Nell & Ruggles 2014) in the layout of many associated structures. Khufu’s 
mortuary temple reflects the orientation of the Great Pyramid itself, with a deviation from cardinality of 
about one arc minute; Khafre’s valley temple and the adjacent Sphinx temple are aligned very close to 
the meridian, and Menkaure’s valley temple gives an east–west alignment of 90.2°/270.2°. Khufu’s 
own valley temple is destroyed and could not be surveyed. One further finding of that survey bears 
directly on the western axis. The mastabas of Khufu’s western cemetery deviate from cardinality by 
about −2.3°, where every other surveyed structure in his complex falls between about −0.6° and +1.0° 
— the mortuary temple at 0.0°, the surviving queen’s pyramid at −0.3°, the southern cemetery at 
−0.6°, and the four rows of the eastern cemetery running from −0.5° to +1.0°. Nell and Ruggles 
observe that the western cemetery is the one group whose eastern horizon the Great Pyramid itself 
obscures, and propose that these tombs were therefore oriented on sunset on the western horizon 
rather than on sunrise in the east — the same day, the opposite direction. They add, as their own 
suggestion, that the western orientations may instead reflect an older tradition in which Osiris, who 
had assumed the title Foremost amongst the Westerners, governed the direction in which the sun 
enters the netherworld. Either reading places a deliberate westward orientation at Giza, surveyed and 
published, independent of the causeways. The causeways thus participate in a directional grammar in 
which the western horizon—associated in Egyptian cosmology with the setting sun, the entrance to 
the netherworld, and the land of the dead—appears privileged. Khafre’s northward deviation (283.4°) 
has an established alternative explanation: Nell and Ruggles, following Lehner (1997), suggest it 
arose from aligning the axis of the Sphinx temple, the south side of the Sphinx, and the south side of 
Khafre’s pyramid. Magli (2009) additionally reads the Giza causeways within a solar framework. 
Neither explanation is excluded by the reading offered here, and both should be weighed against it. 
The two orientations — western origin and eastern theological culmination — operate within a single 
architectural complex, unified rather than contradictory. The organisation of all three causeways about 
the east–west axis, however, remains a feature of the Giza complex that invites explanation beyond 
purely utilitarian models.
The Giza Diagonal: Alignment to the Primeval Mound
The directional grammar of Giza extends beyond the causeways to the placement of the pyramids 
themselves. Mark Lehner, director of Ancient Egypt Research Associates, documented that "a line 
extended through the southeast corners of the three pyramids... points directly toward the temple of 
Heliopolis, the center of the solar cult... This 'Giza Diagonal' suggests that the placement of each 
subsequent pyramid was not merely a matter of finding suitable bedrock, but was constrained by a 
predetermined sacred orientation toward the site of the Primeval Mound" (Lehner 1985b: 136). While 
the alignment is approximate rather than survey-grade precise, and visibility from Giza to Heliopolis 
remains debated, the diagonal pattern across three successive reigns is difficult to explain as 
coincidence. Magli (2009, 2013) has argued that the alignment represents a deliberate symbolic 
orientation toward the Benben cult center.
Heliopolis was the cult center of the Benben — the primordial mound of Egyptian creation theology. 
The alignment of Giza's pyramids toward Heliopolis is not merely a visual convenience. It is a 
theological statement: the pyramids, as architectural instantiations of the Benben, orient toward the 
cult center where that theology was most fully articulated. The Giza Diagonal encodes a relationship 
between the architectural mound — the pyramid — and the theological mound, the Benben at 
Heliopolis. Within the framework of this series, Heliopolis is the formalised theological expression of 
the same cosmological grammar whose origin landscape is argued in Corridor I to be the Richat 
Structure. Giza points not toward the origin but toward where the origin was remembered.

===== PAGE 16 =====
On the visual relationship between Richat and Giza: The correspondence proposed here is not 
visual or geometric. The Richat Structure and the Giza complex differ in scale, symmetry, and spatial 
organization. Richat is a large circular geological formation with concentric rings; Giza consists of 
angular, rectilinear architectural complexes. Richat's water system was basin-like and surrounding; the 
Nile branches at Giza were linear and localized. The argument advanced here is therefore not one of 
visual imitation. Rather, it concerns the instantiation of a cosmological grammar: a central elevated 
form, spatial separation between a sacred core and the surrounding world, water associated with the 
transition zone, and a directional orientation toward the west.
The western causeway at Giza exemplifies this directional grammar. The three causeway orientations 
documented by Nell and Ruggles (2014) and by earlier surveys deviate in opposed senses about the 
east–west axis and average to within a degree of due west, a configuration consistent with a 
cosmological rather than a purely topographic determinant, though not by itself decisive between 
them. These elements are argued to have been re-expressed within the constraints and conventions 
of Old Kingdom Egyptian architecture. Such transformations from natural or symbolic forms into 
rectilinear architectural expression are consistent with broader patterns in Egyptian sacred design 
(e.g., the circular heb-sed court becoming the square temple plan). The homology proposed is 
structural and conceptual, not visual.
The Pyramid Texts anchor this western orientation explicitly in the theology of transformation. 
Utterance 257 (antechamber north wall of the Unas pyramid, c. 2345 BCE) announces: 'Unas goes to 
peace, being alive in the West.' The king does not merely travel toward the west — he lives in it. And 
Utterance 214 (sarcophagus chamber south wall) identifies the western direction as a title of the fully 
transformed king: 'so that you become the One-at-the-Head-of-the-Westerners.' 'Foremost of the 
Westerners' is among the highest epithets of the transformed king in Egyptian theology. The Giza 
causeway does not simply face west; it faces the direction the Pyramid Texts themselves name as the 
dwelling place of the glorified dead.
The Bull’s Foreleg: Giza’s Stellar Anchor
The most precise architectural feature of the Great Pyramid — its alignment to true north within ~3.5 
arc minutes — was achieved, on the most extensively developed peer-reviewed model, by 
observation of the constellation Meskhetyu (the Bull's Foreleg, equivalent to the modern Plough or

===== PAGE 17 =====
Ursa Major). Belmonte (2001) demonstrates that the simultaneous meridian transit of Megrez and 
Phecda — two stars of the Bull's Foreleg — provided the reference for accurate north-south 
orientation, with the alignment achieving its greatest precision at approximately 2562 BCE. In their 
2023 synthesis of Egyptian astronomy, Belmonte and Lull reaffirm this hypothesis, noting that "this 
working hypothesis posed no apparent chronological problems" and that "Khufu's pyramid could have 
been aligned at dates close to 2556 BC, with Meskhetyu in lower culmination" (Belmonte & Lull 2023, 
p. 15). The same procedure has been independently identified at the predynastic structure HK29A at 
Hierakonpolis (Kom el-Ahmar) and at the Osireion of Seti I at Abydos (Belmonte 2001, Fig. 8) — 
spanning more than two millennia of Egyptian sacred architecture.
The alignment procedure itself belonged to a ritual tradition of far greater antiquity: the "stretching of 
the cord" ceremony, whose surviving Ptolemaic temple texts "repeatedly mention Meskhetyu... as the 
appropriate celestial target" toward which the pharaoh looked when fixing a temple's orientation 
(Belmonte & Lull 2023, p. 15). At Dendera and Edfu, "the northern, imperishable, ancient Egyptian 
constellation of Meskhetyu is advocated as the target the Pharaoh was looking at when fixing the 
orientation of the temples" (Belmonte & Lull 2023, p. 378). The ceremony depicted on these temple 
walls preserves a ritual grammar whose architectural expression at Giza predates its surviving textual 
form by more than two millennia.
Meskhetyu was, in Belmonte's phrasing, "one of the most important asterisms of Egyptian religion 
since at least the Old Kingdom, if not earlier" (Lull & Belmonte 2006). It appears in the Pyramid Texts 
as the "imperishable star" per excellence — the destination of the king's ascent. Belmonte and Lull 
(2023, p. 252) state its identification unequivocally: "Without the slightest doubt, this asterism is none 
other than the Plough or Big Dipper, the most characteristic element of the constellation of Ursa 
Major." The constellation's dual identity — adze and bull's foreleg — is documented in the Egyptian 
textual record itself: "The earliest surviving mentions of Meskhetyu... refer to this constellation with the 
adze determinative... Later, Meskhetyu is written with the bull's leg determinant, thus recognizing the 
way in which this constellation was also observed" (Belmonte & Lull 2023, p. 252). This iconographic 
evolution is confirmed on Middle Kingdom coffin lids, where "Meskhetyu is represented in the shape of 
a bull's leg, and the seven stars indicated in it lend it a shape that reminds us of the Wagon" 
(Belmonte & Lull 2023, p. 252). The same constellation is used in the Opening of the Mouth ritual, 
where the bull's foreleg is the implement by which the senses of the deceased are restored. The 
province of Letopolis, whose capital sat near Heliopolis, bore the name "Bull's Foreleg" (Belmonte 
2010).
This is structurally significant. The cattle cosmogram established in Corridor II (Levy 2026b) and the 
predynastic cattle cult at Hierakonpolis HK6 documented in Shemsu Hor (Corridor III, Levy 2026c) — 
including the deliberate horn modification of livestock first attested anywhere in the world (Van Neer et 
al. 2024) — share a single unifying symbol with the constellation that anchored the Great Pyramid's 
most precise architectural alignment. The iconographic evolution from adze to bull's foreleg traces the 
same symbolic chain — implement, animal, constellation — that connects the pastoralist bull cult at

The Khufu Branch Canal (2022 PNAS Study)
For centuries, a fundamental logistics problem remained unsolved: the Nile today runs several 
kilometres from the Giza plateau. How did multi-tonne stone blocks get from the river to the 
construction site? In 2022, Hader Sheisha and colleagues published findings in PNAS confirming the 
existence of the 'Khufu Branch' — a now-buried Nile channel that ran directly to the base of the Giza

reconstructed 8,000 years of fluvial variation on the Giza floodplain and found that the Khufu branch 
remained at roughly 40% of its Holocene maximum water level through the reigns of Khufu, Khafre 
and Menkaure — a level they conclude facilitated the transportation of construction materials to the

The Ahramat Branch (2024 Study) and the Engineered Harbour
In 2024, Eman Ghoneim and colleagues published findings in Communications Earth & Environment 
identifying a larger extinct Nile branch — the Ahramat ('Pyramids') Branch — running alongside 31 
Egyptian pyramids, using radar satellite imagery, geophysical survey and sediment cores. Many of the

reconstruction confirms that the harbour was not a natural embayment — it was a deliberately cut and 
maintained basin, active across the reigns of Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure (Younes et al. 2024). The

The Surid Legend: Antediluvian Knowledge in Stone
The most detailed Arabic tradition attributes the pyramids to Surid ibn Salhouk (also known as Saurid), 
a legendary king said to have ruled Egypt 300 years before the biblical Great Flood. According to al-
Mas'udi's 10th-century Akhbar al-Zaman, Surid dreamed of a cataclysm — "the fixed stars had come 
down" and "had grabbed the people and had hurled them between two mountains" — which he 
interpreted as divine warning of an impending deluge. In response, he commanded the construction of 
the pyramids as repositories for "the sciences of arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, and medicine," 
along with the mummified bodies of his royal ancestors and their treasures.
Scholarly analysis traces this legend to Coptic Christian adaptations of older Gnostic or Sabian lore 
around the 4th–5th centuries CE, with Surid representing a conflation of the historical pharaoh Khufu 
(Suphis in Manetho) with prophetic archetypes such as the biblical Enoch, the Islamic Idris, and the 
Greco-Egyptian Hermes Trismegistus (Fodor 1970). The name "Surid" itself derives from "Suphis" — 
the Greek rendering of Khufu's name. As Smith (2007) notes, "The story of Surid and his antediluvian 
construction of the pyramids assigns to them a place in sacred history and establishes a neutral 
narrative ground upon which Muslims and Christians could agree" (Smith 2007).
The legend is not treated here as literal history. But the methodological literature on traditional 
transmission cautions equally against the opposite error of dismissing such material as pure invention. 
Vansina (1985) established that traditions may preserve recoverable information across long 
transmission chains when assessed critically, and Barber and Barber (2004) document cases in which 
myth demonstrably encodes real events — the Klamath account of the formation of Crater Lake, for 
instance, corresponds to the geological record of the eruption and was transmitted for roughly eight 
thousand years. The relevant question for the Surid tradition is therefore not whether it is history, but 
whether its specific and unusual claim — that the pyramids were built as repositories of arithmetic, 
geometry, and astronomy — preserves a kernel of the encoding logic proposed here, however 
distorted by its later Coptic and Hermetic transmission.

bound, below which scribes abandoned the series entirely for ro notation — a structural boundary the 
analysis below relies upon. The present paper employs the series and its combinations only. No step 
in the arithmetic that follows depends on the sense correspondence or on the derivation of the sign, 
and the results would stand unchanged if both were set aside. The term ‘Wedjat fractions’ is retained 
as the conventional designation for the series, in the sense in which the literature uses it, and not as 
an assertion about its origin.
What this paper proposes as new is neither the fractions nor the constants but the method. The series 
is here treated as a constrained computational grammar and tested as such: a fixed set of six exterior 
dimensions, the six fractions and their sums, the two operations attested in the Rhind Papyrus, a 
stated tolerance, and a target list fixed in advance — with the resulting correspondence count 
compared against rival historical fraction systems downsampled to equal cardinality, against randomly 
generated systems of matched size and span, against evenly spaced systems of matched spacing, 
against the complete grids of neighbouring denominators, and against randomly generated pyramid 
proportions. A search of the published literature has not identified a prior application of the Egyptian 
binary series to monument dimensions under declared operational constraints with statistical controls 
of this kind. Existing work on the series concerns its internal arithmetic, its attestation, or its 
iconographic history; existing work on pyramid metrology proceeds in modern units and without a 
fraction-system null. The combination is, to the present author’s knowledge, untried, and is offered on 
that basis rather than as a claim of priority over any particular finding.
The Seked Objection
The strongest standing objection to any claim that the Great Pyramid encodes π or φ is that both 
follow automatically from its slope, and that the slope was chosen for practical reasons. The objection 
deserves to be stated at full strength. Egyptian builders expressed inclination as a seked — a number 
of horizontal palms and digits per royal cubit of rise — and the royal cubit divides into seven palms of 
four digits, twenty-eight digits in all. A seked of 5 ½ palms is therefore 22 digits horizontal to 28 
vertical. The reciprocal, 28/22, equals 1.272727. It happens that 4/π equals 1.273240, a difference of 
0.040%, and that √φ equals 1.272020, a difference of 0.056%. Both are well-documented 
coincidences arising from the number seven. On this reading, a builder who selects a whole-and-half-
palm seked for ease of construction obtains π-proportions and φ-proportions in the finished monument 
without possessing, or intending, either constant.

===== PAGE 26 =====
Two things follow from this, and they should be separated. The first is granted without reservation: 
because the ratio alone determines both relationships, π and φ are not two independent 
correspondences but a single fact expressed twice, and every pyramid built to a seked of 5 ½ 
possesses both regardless of its size. They are accordingly listed in this section as established 
findings, already documented as consequences of the construction method (Rossi 2004; Herz-Fischler 
2000), rather than as novel claims. The second does not follow. The objection assumes that a seked 
of 5 ½ was adopted for convenience, and offers no evidence that it was. Convenience does not select 
it. A builder working in whole and quarter palms had at least eight equally practical values available 
across the range used for large pyramids, and their perimeter-to-height ratios are 5.714 at seked 5, 
6.000 at 5 ¼, 6.286 at 5 ½, 6.571 at 5 ¾, 6.857 at 6, 7.143 at 6 ¼, 7.429 at 6 ½ and 8.000 at 7. 
Against 2π = 6.28319, the errors are 9.05%, 4.51%, 0.040%, 4.59%, 9.14%, 13.7%, 18.2% and 27.3% 
respectively. One value out of eight lands on 2π, and it lands two orders of magnitude closer than its 
nearest neighbour. Khafre’s pyramid, built on the same plateau within the same dynasty by the same 
institutional apparatus, uses a seked of 5 ¼ and misses 2π by 4.5%. Practical convenience therefore 
cannot account for the selection, since equally convenient alternatives were used and produce 
nothing. The objection explains π and φ given the seked; it does not explain the seked. That question 
remains open, and this paper’s proposal — that 5 ½ was selected from within the Wedjat system 
because of what it produces — remains a live candidate answer rather than one the objection has 
displaced.
A related objection holds that the Egyptians cannot have encoded π or φ because they possessed no 
such constants: there is no Egyptian word for either, and no surviving text treats them as abstract 
quantities. The premise is correct and the inference does not follow. Nothing in this section requires 
that the builders held an abstract constant, named it, or reasoned about it as the Greeks later did. 
What it requires is that their own arithmetic produced the values, and that they used the arithmetic that 
does. A ratio does not need a name to be operative. A seked of 5 ½ palms yields a perimeter-to-height 
relation of 22/7 whether or not anyone present has a term for what 22/7 approximates, and the Wedjat 
fractions yield the correspondences documented above whether or not anyone present conceives of 
them as a numerical system. The claim advanced here concerns method, not vocabulary.
A point of evidence tells against reading the seked value backwards out of Egyptian circle 
mathematics. The Rhind Mathematical Papyrus gives an explicit rule for the area of a circle in Problem 
50 — take eight-ninths of the diameter and square it — which corresponds to a π of 256/81, or 
3.1605, in error by 0.602%. The value implied by a seked of 5 ½ is 22/7, or 3.14286, in error by 
0.040%. The seked value is some fifteen times the more accurate. Whatever produced it, it was not 
derived from the circle rule the Egyptians actually wrote down, and the two are independent. This is 
consistent with the reading offered here: the accuracy resides in the fraction system and the 
proportions built from it rather than in any stated theory of the circle, which is what encoding through 
method rather than through doctrine would look like.
The objection does not, however, extend to the astronomical and calendrical correspondences, and 
the reason is visible within the comparative sample analysed above. Three of the nine documented 
pyramids share a seked of exactly 5 ½ palms: the Great Pyramid (base 440, height 280), Meidum 
(base 275, height 175), and Unas (base 110, height 70). All three therefore possess identical π-
proportions and identical φ-proportions; whatever the seked confers, it confers equally on each. Their 
scores against the fourteen frozen targets are 10 of 14, 7 of 14 and 8 of 14 respectively. A two-to-
three-target spread separates monuments that the seked cannot distinguish at all.
The seked therefore explains π and φ, and explains nothing else. What varies across the three is the 
absolute dimension — 440 cubits against 275 and 110 — and it is the absolute dimension, operated 
on by the Wedjat fractions, that the astronomical correspondences track. This is also why the second 
Monte Carlo test varies base and seked jointly rather than slope alone: the discriminating variable is 
scale, not inclination. A reader who accepts the seked objection in full may treat π and φ as a single 
relationship rather than two, and may set them aside entirely, without touching the result on which this 
paper rests.

Declared Methodological Constraints
A note on evidence independence: the nine disciplines cited in this paper are not uniformly 
independent of one another. Mathematical analysis and archaeoastronomical analysis both operate on 
the same dimensional and survey data; their conclusions are correlated rather than independent 
confirmations. The genuinely independent evidence streams — those that could in principle falsify the 
hypothesis without reference to any other stream — are the genetic migration data, the 
paleohydrological reconstruction, the zooarchaeological assemblage analysis at Hierakonpolis, and 
the epigraphic transmission record. These four streams converge on the same geographic corridor 
and temporal window from entirely separate research programmes, without reference to pyramid 
dimensions or astronomical alignments. The convergence argument is strongest and most structurally 
independent in these four streams. The mathematical and archaeoastronomical streams constitute a 
separate, internally coherent convergence argument that shares a data set with each other and should 
be evaluated on that basis.
To address the concern that a large search space makes pattern emergence statistically inevitable, 
the following constraints governed this investigation and were fixed before results were examined. 
They are stated here so any reader can replicate or challenge the search. Dimensions: six exterior 
measurements only — base (440 cubits), height (280 cubits), half-base (220 cubits), apothem (356.09 
cubits), base diagonal (622.25 cubits), and perimeter (1,760 cubits) — all derived from Petrie (1883) 
and Dash (2017). No internal chamber dimensions are used in this section. Fractions: the exact six 
Wedjat fractions (½, ¼, ⅛, 1⁄16, 1⁄32, 1⁄64) and their sums only. No arbitrary fractions. No non-Wedjat 
denominators. That this is the historically attested set is not an inference of this paper: Clagett (1999,

===== PAGE 28 =====
p. 58) states that the first six terms of the Egyptian unit-fraction series were commonly used for 
fractions of a heqat and were written with special Horus-eye symbols, identifying them as ½, ¼, ⅛, 
1⁄16, 1⁄32 and 1⁄64 as they occur in the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus. Operations: multiplication and 
division only in the frozen confirmatory analysis. Cross-dimension addition and subtraction — likewise 
attested in the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus — produce additional correspondences but are excluded 
from the statistical test to avoid inflating the expression space. This exclusion is conservative rather 
than neutral. Clagett (1999, pp. 57–58) describes Egyptian multiplication as proceeding by doubling 
and halving, with the final product formed by adding partial products, so addition is intrinsic to the 
arithmetic rather than foreign to it. Barring it narrows the achievable set below what Egyptian practice 
would permit. No higher-order transformations, no logarithms, no trigonometric functions. Threshold: 
results within 0.2% of a target value were recorded. All results — including non-matches — were 
reported. The post-hoc nature of this search is a genuine limitation, acknowledged throughout this 
section: the correspondences were identified before any protocol existed. The confirmatory analysis 
reported below is governed by the frozen analysis specification set out in this section and 
implemented in the deposited code, which fixes the target list, dimensions, fraction system, 
operations, tolerance, null models and decision rule. That specification converts the reported statistics 
into the application of a fixed, publicly deposited procedure that any reader can inspect and re-run. 
The domain coherence of the results — that the constrained search recovers correspondences 
spanning all major Old Kingdom astronomical knowledge domains simultaneously — is the 
observation the hypothesis advances, not a statistical proof of encoding. The structural tests proposed 
in Predictions 6, 7, and 8 define the methodological path toward confirmation or falsification of the 
encoding claim.
A note on targets removed during methodological tightening. An earlier form of this analysis included 
four further targets. Three — the Saros eclipse cycle (6,585.32 days), the Metonic cycle (6,939.69 
days) and the precessional rate — were reachable from the declared dimensions, the Saros by 
apothem × 13 ÷ (45⁄64) and the Metonic by diagonal × 17 × (21⁄32), and all three were removed. They 
were removed because those routes require integer multipliers of 13 and 17, which lie outside the two 
operations permitted above. The exclusion is deliberately stricter than Egyptian practice would license: 
Clagett (1999, pp. 57–58) shows that any whole-number multiplier could be assembled by selectively 
adding terms of the doubling series, so 13 (1 + 4 + 8) and 17 (1 + 16) were both trivially constructible 
by ordinary Egyptian method. These were therefore reachable hits, discarded as successes obtained 
by means this analysis forbids rather than as failures; the constraint was enforced against the reported 
result rather than in its favour. The Saros and Metonic are in any case Babylonian constructions with 
no Old Kingdom Egyptian attestation, so their exclusion is doubly warranted. The fourth, the tropical 
year, was removed for a different reason: it is not distinguished from the sidereal year in Old Kingdom 
sources, and the same formula reaches both within the tolerance, so counting it separately would 
have double-counted a single correspondence. Each removal lowered the reported count.
Design specification rather than as-built measurement. The dimensions analysed throughout this 
section are the design values — a base of 440 royal cubits and a seked of 5 ½ palms — not the as-
built measurements of the standing monument. This distinction matters and is stated explicitly. No 
Egyptian document records the figure 440; it is a reconstruction from survey. Petrie’s (1883) mean 
base of 230.33 ± 0.05 m converts, at a royal cubit of 0.5236 m, to 439.90 ± 0.095 cubits. The 
reconstruction is nonetheless secure on three independent grounds. First, 440 lies 1.08 standard 
deviations from the surveyed mean, while the neighbouring integers 439 and 441 lie 9.4 and 11.6 
standard deviations away; 440 is the only whole-cubit value compatible with the survey. Second, the 
result is stable across the full range of attested cubit lengths: values from 0.5232 to 0.5240 m yield 
439.56 to 440.23 cubits, rounding to 440 throughout. Third, and independently of the base, a base of 
440 with a height of 280 yields a seked of exactly 5 ½ palms — a whole-palm design value of the kind 
documented in Rhind Mathematical Papyrus Problems 56–58 — producing a slope of 51.843°, within 
0.002° of Petrie’s measured 51 ° 50′ 40″. Two design parameters therefore resolve independently to 
simple Egyptian values. Because an encoding hypothesis is a claim about intent, and intent resides in 
the specification rather than in the executed stone, the design values are the appropriate object of

===== PAGE 29 =====
analysis. The 0.023% divergence between the reconstructed specification and the surveyed 
monument is a measure of construction accuracy, not evidence against the specification. Readers 
should note that correspondence counts computed on the surveyed value rather than the design value 
are lower, and that the analysis reported here is of the plan.
Fraction-system control. Whether the Wedjat system is specifically effective for these targets, or 
whether any comparable fraction system would do as well, is not settled by inspection; it is settled by 
the head-to-head and equal-cardinality tests reported in Part 3 of the deposited analysis. Those tests 
score four real historical fraction systems — Egyptian unit fractions, Babylonian sexagesimal, small 
integer ratios and decimal — against the same six dimensions, the same fourteen targets, the same 
two operations and the same tolerance, and then downsample each rival to the same sixty-three 
fractions the Wedjat system has, so that the comparison does not simply reward whichever system 
contains the most fractions. The Wedjat set reaches ten of fourteen with sixty-three fractions, a rate of 
15.87 correspondences per hundred; Egyptian unit fractions reach eleven but require 1,923 fractions 
to do it, a rate of 0.57. Downsampled to equal size, no rival reaches the Wedjat score in twenty 
thousand draws — every rival returns p = 0.0000 — and across the tolerance band from ±0.10% to 
±0.30% the highest rival probability is 0.0032. That is the control, and it is computed in the deposited 
script rather than asserted here.
Even-spacing control. One structural feature of the Wedjat system requires its own control. The six 
Wedjat signs combine additively — the notation in which Egyptian scribes wrote hekat capacities — 
and their sixty-three sums are exactly the sixty-fourths, k/64 for k = 1 to 63. That set is a perfectly 
uniform grid, whereas fraction sets drawn at random cluster and leave gaps, and a uniform grid covers 
a numerical range more efficiently than a clustered one. A null model built from randomly drawn 
fractions therefore sets a slightly lower bar than the Wedjat set faces. The test was accordingly 
repeated against fraction sets of identical cardinality and span but with the values evenly spaced and 
randomly offset, holding spacing constant so that only the denominator varies. Under this more 
conservative null the Great Pyramid still exceeds the comparison systems, though at reduced 
significance: 10 of 14 against a mean of 6.00 (p = 0.055, not significant), and 8 of 11 against 4.25 
(p = 0.031); on the maximally-independent nine-target core the result also fails to reach significance (6 
of 9 against 3.39, p = 0.100). Roughly an order of magnitude of the significance reported under the 
randomly-drawn null is therefore attributable to even spacing rather than to the Wedjat system 
specifically. This is reported rather than omitted.
Denominator specificity. The even-spacing control raises the sharper question of whether the 
denominator 64 matters at all, or whether any sufficiently even grid would serve. This is testable 
deterministically by scoring the complete grids k/M against the same fourteen targets at the same 
threshold. Grids of comparable density and identical construction score four to five: k/48 gives 4, k/60 
gives 4, k/72 gives 5. The sixty-fourths give 10. The neighbouring denominators are not intermediate 
between these values; the transition is a discontinuity rather than a gradient, and it cannot be 
attributed to grid density, spacing, or cardinality, since those are held constant across the comparison. 
The denser grid k/128 scores 11 of 14, but with 127 fractions it is markedly less efficient per fraction 
(8.66 correspondences per hundred fractions against Wedjat’s 15.87), and 1/128 is not an attested 
Horus-eye sign — the series terminates at 1⁄64 — so k/128 represents a modern extension of the 
notation rather than a historical rival system. On this evidence the specificity claimed here attaches to 
the denominator itself, not to even spacing in general.
Null Model and Statistical Framework
The following null hypothesis governs the statistical evaluation of this section.
H  (Null Hypothesis): The exterior dimensions of the Great Pyramid were determined by practical ₀
architectural, engineering, and cosmological considerations independent of systematic astronomical 
encoding through the Wedjat fraction system. Any mathematical or astronomical correspondences

===== PAGE 30 =====
identifiable through Wedjat fraction operations are the product of coincidental numerical proximity 
within the search space.
H (This Hypothesis): The exterior dimensions were chosen to preserve a structured body of ₁
mathematical and astronomical knowledge through the Wedjat fraction system. Under H, Wedjat ₁
fraction operations on the six primary exterior dimensions should produce correspondence rates with 
real astronomical and mathematical targets that significantly exceed chance, should exhibit structural 
patterns consistent with systematic domain-organised encoding, and should show selective absences 
consistent with the observational limits of the tradition described in The Corridor Series.
Predictions under H : Wedjat fraction operations on pyramid dimensions should produce ₀
correspondence rates with real astronomical constants statistically indistinguishable from randomly 
generated values in the same numerical ranges. Hits should be distributed randomly across domains 
without structural clustering by formula type or astronomical category.
Predictions under H: Correspondence rate with real targets significantly exceeds chance. Structurally ₁
related astronomical bodies share formula structures (e.g., same pyramid dimension and operation 
class). Hits cluster in domains with strong Egyptian observational tradition. Constants lacking that 
tradition (Mercury; modern physics constants) are absent or underrepresented.
Test and result: The constrained search (six declared dimensions × Wedjat binary fractions × 
declared operations, applied to fourteen astronomical and mathematical targets at ≤0.2% precision 
threshold) produces the correspondences documented throughout this section. Each correspondence 
is arithmetically verified. Taken individually, any single correspondence could be coincidental. Taken 
collectively, they span every major domain of Old Kingdom astronomical knowledge: all four of the 
visible planets tracked here, the anomalistic lunar month, multiple year types, and the Sirius-Osirian 
calendar cluster reaching from the 70-day period to an unsuccessful approach to the Sothic cycle — 
all from the same six dimensions using the same Egyptian fraction system. The domain coherence of 
this pattern is the observation the hypothesis advances.
Structural evidence bearing on H: Saturn and Jupiter are both yielded by base × (Wedjat fraction) — ₁
the same pyramid dimension and the same operation, with different fractions for different bodies. 
Venus and Mars are yielded by apothem and height respectively, each divided by a Wedjat fraction — 
a consistent operation class for a different pair of bodies. Six mathematical constants are found, all 
Egyptian-constructible. Earth’s two principal radii are recoverable through purely Egyptian-arithmetic 
formulae, at the lower evidential tier defined in the Earth’s Geometry section. Mercury is not found, 
consistent with its observational difficulty. Euler’s number, √3, and the speed of light are not found, 
consistent with their absence from any Egyptian observational tradition.
Interpretation and limitations: The search was conducted post-hoc, and the target list was compiled 
from astronomically meaningful values rather than randomly selected. These constraints prevent this 
section from constituting statistical proof of deliberate encoding, and that claim is not made here. What 
is presented is an observation: a constrained Wedjat-fraction operation on Giza’s six declared exterior 
dimensions produces arithmetically verified correspondences spanning all major domains of Old 
Kingdom astronomical practice — planetary synodic cycles, lunar periodicity, solar and lunar 
calendrics, and the Sirius-Osirian ritual and long-period calendar system. The domain-complete 
pattern is the claim. Whether it reflects deliberate encoding is the hypothesis. The structural tests for 
that claim are stated as Predictions 6 and 7.
A note on the target list as constrained prior: the fourteen targets used in both Monte Carlo tests are 
not a random sample of astronomical quantities. They constitute a constrained prior — specifically, the 
periods and cycles that appear in the canonical Egyptological record of Old Kingdom astronomical 
knowledge and practice: Faulkner (1966) identifies Venus in the Pyramid Texts as the Morning Star 
and, on his reading, also as the Lone Star, placing a named planet in the Old Kingdom’s earliest 
religious corpus; Clagett (1995) documents the decans and the stellar rising and setting cycles; Parker

===== PAGE 31 =====
(1950) documents lunar and Sothic calendrics — noting that his calendar model, while widely 
accepted, is not settled: Clagett (1995) records that Parker’s views “are anything but certain,” and 
Krauss (1998) notes challenges from Helck, Luft and Leitz; the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus (c. 1650 
BCE) documents the civil year and schematic year; the 70-day Sirius invisibility period is attested 
across multiple Old Kingdom and later sources. The Monte Carlo tests therefore ask a precise 
question: does the Wedjat fraction system specifically recover the documented Egyptian astronomical 
knowledge base? This is the correct question under H, because the hypothesis is that specifically ₁
Egyptian astronomical knowledge was encoded. A test using arbitrarily selected astronomical 
constants — including modern quantities unknown to the Egyptians — would be methodologically 
weaker, not stronger, because it would dilute the signal with noise. The fourteen targets are the signal. 
The fraction-system control reported in Part 3 of the deposited analysis is the primary methodological 
control because it holds this target list constant and asks only whether the Wedjat system is doing the 
work — answering the target-selection objection directly.
On the attestation of the planetary targets. The four planetary synodic periods are the least directly 
documented members of the list, and the difficulty should be stated rather than passed over: no 
Egyptian source at any period records a planetary period as a number, and the surviving numerical 
planetary tables are Greco-Roman and calculated rather than observed. Two considerations bear on 
their inclusion. First, the planets were watched and named within the tradition from an early date: 
Faulkner (1966) identifies the Morning Star of the Pyramid Texts as Venus at dawn, and argues that 
the Lone Star is Venus after sunset, making Venus the one planet securely attested in the Old 
Kingdom’s earliest religious corpus — and the alternation between those two apparitions is precisely 
the observation whose period is the synodic cycle. Second, the absence of a written period is what 
this hypothesis predicts rather than an anomaly it must absorb: the argument advanced throughout is 
that such quantities were held in the architectural register while the tradition was intact, and committed 
to papyrus only as it faded. That Clagett (1995), in a source book of 575 pages on Egyptian calendars, 
clocks and astronomy, includes no chapter and no document on the planets is consistent with both 
readings and decides between neither. The planetary targets are accordingly advanced on a weaker 
documentary footing than the calendrical ones, and are so marked.
On late attestation and older content. Several targets in this list are documented in sources later than 
the Fourth Dynasty, and a reader may reasonably ask why late documentation is treated as evidence 
of earlier knowledge. The principle is not special pleading but standard Egyptological method. Clagett 
(1995, Preface) states it directly: the carrier of a document frequently dates much later than the 
document itself. He instances the decanal transit tables in the cenotaph of Seti I (c. 1306–1290 BCE), 
whose content dates from at least as early as the reign of Sesostris III in the Twelfth Dynasty, and the 
Ramesside Star Clock, whose earliest surviving copies are in the tomb of Ramesses VI (c. 1151–1143 
BCE) but whose internal evidence places it between roughly 1500 and 1470 BCE. Late attestation 
therefore establishes a terminus ante quem for a tradition, not its date of origin. This does not license 
unlimited backdating, and it is not offered as proof that any specific quantity was known in the Fourth 
Dynasty; it establishes only that the inference is a normal one in the field rather than an 
accommodation invented here. The exclusions applied above follow the same rule in the opposite 
direction: the thirty-six decans, first attested on coffin lids around 2150 BCE, are omitted precisely 
because no claim of Old Kingdom currency is made for them.
On the anomalistic month. Of the fourteen targets this is the least directly attested, and the position is 
better stated than assumed. No Egyptian source records an anomalistic period; Parker (1950, §7) 
gives the sidereal month at approximately 27⅓ days, a value the base does not reach within the 
tolerance used here. The case for the anomalistic cycle is physical rather than textual. On Parker’s 
reconstruction the Egyptian lunar month began on the morning the old crescent of the waning moon 
could no longer be seen before sunrise (Parker 1950, quoted at length in Clagett 1995, ch. 3), and 
Parker (1950, §§11–12) identifies the first of the three factors governing that visibility as the anomaly 
of the moon — its varying distance from the earth. The anomalistic cycle is thus the period of the 
variable that determines the observable event on which the Egyptian lunar calendar was regulated,

===== PAGE 32 =====
and a tradition watching that event across centuries samples the anomaly whether or not it named a 
period for it. This is an inference and is offered as one. A reader who declines it should know exactly 
what that costs, and the leave-one-out analysis states it plainly: removing the anomalistic month and 
re-running the second Monte Carlo test on the remaining thirteen returns p = 0.064, which does not 
reach the conventional threshold. It is the only deletion in the series that does not. This target is 
therefore load-bearing in a way the others are not, and a reader who rejects the argument above 
should treat the correspondence-count result as unsupported rather than merely weakened. The 
designated primary test, which does not depend on the target count, is unaffected.
A tested domain that does not encode. The target list contains periods and one geometric constant; it 
contains no stellar positions. That boundary was tested rather than assumed, and it was tested with 
the same control used for the base sweep, so that the two domains can be compared directly.
Declinations, rising azimuths and culmination altitudes were computed for eleven stars of documented 
significance in the Egyptian and Saharan record — Thuban, Sirius, Canopus, Arcturus, Alnitak, 
Alnilam, Kochab, Mizar, Alkaid, Dubhe and Alpha Centauri — precessed to 2560 BCE with proper 
motion applied, together with the obliquity of the ecliptic at that epoch, Giza’s latitude and the 
circumpolar limit. Arcturus is included because Malville et al. (2007) revised the northern Nabta Playa 
alignments from Dubhe to Arcturus, dating them to three epochs between 4530 and 3630 BCE; both 
stars are retained here. Five of the eleven — Thuban, Kochab, Mizar, Alkaid and Dubhe — are 
circumpolar at Giza in 2560 BCE and never cross the horizon, so they contribute a declination and a 
culmination altitude but no rising azimuth. That leaves thirty-one angular quantities, which were scored 
against the same six dimensions, sixty-three Wedjat fractions, two operations and ±0.2% tolerance 
used throughout. The computation and the control below are performed by stellar_control.py, 
deposited with this paper.Five reached tolerance: Alnitak’s culmination altitude at 0.060%, Mizar’s 
declination at 0.138%, Canopus’s rising azimuth at 0.157%, Sirius’s culmination altitude at 0.163% 
and Canopus’s declination at 0.198%. Five of thirty-one is 16.1%.The control determines what that 
figure means. Each angular target was replaced by a random value drawn from the same category 
range — a declination stays a declination, an azimuth an azimuth — and the pyramid was re-scored 
against the resulting shadow list (N = 200,000, seed 42). The expected number of hits by chance is 
4.83 of thirty-one, or 15.6%, and the probability of reaching five or more is 0.5431. The observed 
stellar result is therefore not merely close to chance but statistically indistinguishable from it.Set 
against the calendrical suite scored by the identical procedure, the contrast is the point. The 
calendrical targets return ten of fourteen against a chance expectation of 6.25, at p = 0.023; the stellar 
angles return five of thirty-one against a chance expectation of 4.83, at p = 0.543. The same 
monument, the same six dimensions, the same fraction system, the same operations and the same 
tolerance produce a result above chance in one domain and at chance in the other. The difference lies 
in the target set rather than in any general flexibility of the pyramid’s arithmetic, and this is the most 
direct available answer to the objection that a monument with many measurements will reach any list 
of numbers.One feature of the stellar result deserves emphasis because it runs against the 
interpretation offered here and is reported for that reason. The stars that reach tolerance are not 
obscure ones. They are Alnitak, the belt star at which the King’s Chamber southern shaft is directed; 
Sirius, whose heliacal rising governed the Egyptian year; and Canopus, which accounts for two of the 
five hits. These are among the most significant stars in the tradition, and a reader is free to pursue any 
of them. The finding is that even when the hits fall on the stars that matter most, the total remains what 
chance predicts, so no stellar encoding is claimed here. Conversely, several quantities central to the 
monument fail: Thuban, which the northern shaft addresses, misses on both available measures; the 
obliquity of the ecliptic misses at 0.327%; and Giza’s own latitude misses at 2.15%.The stellar domain

The Concavity: A Documented Equinox Instrument
It is documented that the Great Pyramid is the only known pyramid with concave faces — an inward 
hollow running from base to apex along the centreline of each face (Petrie 1883; Edwards 1975; Nell 
& Ruggles 2014). The feature is invisible from ground level under normal conditions. It is documented 
that at the spring and autumn equinoxes the Sun’s angle is precisely sufficient to cast a shadow 
revealing the inward hollow, briefly exposing the eight-sided geometry (Edwards 1975). This 
hypothesis proposes that the concavity functions as a passive solar calendar instrument, marking the 
equinoxes annually without additional apparatus. A claim in popular circulation — that the concavity 
depth encodes Earth’s curvature — was directly tested and refuted: Earth’s surface curvature across 
230 metres produces a sag of approximately 1 mm; the concavity depth is 940 mm, a factor of 903 
larger. This claim is not supported by calculation and is not advanced here. A second caveat is 
required, and it is not one this paper can settle. Monnier (2022) argues that the hollowing is not a 
design feature at all but an artifact of the monument’s later history, modelling the impacts of casing 
stones falling on the exposed faces together with the dismantling of the summit — nearly 270 m³ of 
blocks brought down onto faces already stripped — and obtaining indentations accentuated at the 
centres of the faces that closely match the deviation map produced by the Scanning the Pyramids 
project. Laser scanning and photogrammetry also show the profiles to be far from uniform, with some 
courses barely recessed and maximum recession exceeding two metres in places; and Petrie was 
measuring the exposed core rather than the finished casing, which he himself suggested may have 
thickened along the midline to compensate. The equinox argument advanced here depends only on 
the hollow existing and catching low-angle light, not on its depth or its intentionality, and it survives 
Monnier’s reading of the cause. But no single concavity value can be treated as a measurement, and 
for that reason the concavity is not used as a dimension anywhere in the statistical analysis.

What Was Explicitly Tested and Not Found
In the interest of transparency, the following were explicitly tested and not found, or were found and 
refuted. Speed of light: refuted. The claim is that the Great Pyramid stands at latitude 29.9792458°N 
and that the speed of light in vacuum is 299,792,458 m/s. The correspondence fails on four 
independent grounds. First, and decisively, the pyramid is not a point: its 230.33 m base spans 
0.00208° of latitude — 7.48 arcseconds — so the monument occupies every latitude from 29.97821°N 
to 29.98028°N. Roughly twenty thousand distinct seven-decimal latitude values intersect the structure, 
and the digits beyond the third decimal place are therefore not a property of the monument but a 
choice of which point on it is measured. Second, the quoted line does not pass through the apex, 
which lies at approximately 29.9791750°N, some eight metres to the south. Third, the figure 
299,792,458 is not a measured quantity but a definition: since the 17th General Conference on 
Weights and Measures (1983) the metre has been defined as the distance light travels in 
1/299,792,458 of a second, the digits having been selected to preserve the length of the metre

===== PAGE 41 =====
established by the French meridian survey of the 1790s. The number is an artefact of modern 
metrological convention. Fourth, the comparison equates a value in degrees with a value in metres per 
second, which requires disregarding both the decimal point and the units. The finite speed of light was 
itself not established until Rømer (1676). This correspondence is accordingly recorded here as 
refuted, not merely unconfirmed. Euler’s number e: systematic search of all primary dimension ratios 
found nothing within 0.5% of e = 2.71828; a formula in popular circulation claimed to yield e but in fact 
yields φ, a misidentification. Sidereal year in the base: the base measures 440 Royal Cubits; 365 
cubits × 0.5236 m falls approximately 39 metres short of the actual base in every standard Egyptian 
unit system. Earth’s curvature encoded in the concavity: calculation shows the concavity is 903 times 
too deep for Earth’s surface curvature across 230 m. Mercury’s synodic period: not found within 
acceptable error bounds after systematic search. √3, √7, the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and ln 2: not 
found within 0.3% after systematic search.

The Wedjat as a Portable Celestial Map and the Pyramid as a Fixed Anchor
The Wedjat sky-compass as a portable celestial map is fully documented in Corridor II (Levy 2026b, 
Section 5). Canopus — whose Egyptian identification remains open, with Shaltout, Belmonte and

Belmonte, Pérez Die and Díaz-Iglesias Llanos (2016) — sank progressively lower as ancestral 
populations migrated northeast. At the Richat Structure (21.1°N), Canopus was a consistent visible 
feature. At Giza (30.0°N), during the pyramid-building era (~2500 BCE), Canopus culminated at

A foundational study by Alexander Badawy and astronomer Virginia Trimble first proved that the so-
called air-shafts in the King’s Chamber were orientated to the stars of Orion’s Belt (Osiris) in the south

All Canopus altitudes quoted in this paper derive from a single computation rather than being 
calculated separately at each point of use. The method is stated here in full: J2000.0 catalogue 
position, IAU 1976 rigorous precession, altitude at upper culmination as 90° − latitude + declination, 
without refraction or extinction. The script self-tests against the figures published in Corridor I and 
reproduces them to within 0.06°. Two quantities should be kept distinct, since they are easily 
conflated: Canopus’s declination at a given epoch, computed here, and the declination of the Canopus

into an altitude calculation. One further point of direction: Canopus’s declination becomes less 
negative through the periods this series covers, so its altitude at a fixed latitude rises with time. Earlier 
epochs give lower altitudes, not higher — the northward migration of the visibility limit recorded in 
Corridor I is the same fact seen from the other side. The obstacle this creates is structural rather than 
strictly geometric, and the distinction should be stated precisely. A shaft leaving the King’s Chamber

the 32.5° northern shaft. Such a channel is not impossible to lay out. It is, however, nearly horizontal

core, and useless as a sighting line to a star so severely reddened and dimmed by atmospheric 
extinction at that altitude that it could not serve as a reliable observational target in any case. The four 
shafts that were built span 32.5° to 45°, the range within which an inclined channel through a 
pyramidal mass remains structurally and observationally viable. Canopus at 4.6° falls outside it. The 
builders did not target Canopus in stone because the pyramid’s latitude had placed the star below the 
range that the medium could carry. The pyramid therefore encodes not the star itself but the threshold

portable complement: by observing the altitude of Canopus, any observer could determine latitude

instrument reached its architectural limit.

below the range an enclosed shaft through the pyramid’s mass can carry: the required channel would

reddened and dimmed by atmospheric extinction to serve as an observational target. The known

Primary Purpose: Cosmological Encoding
A multi-generational migration of the kind documented in The Corridor Series does not merely 
displace a population geographically. It gradually erodes the fixed standards on which a cosmological 
system depends. Over centuries of movement, the cubit drifts as it passes through successive 
artisanal traditions without a master standard; the calendar loses its agricultural anchor as seasonal 
markers shift with latitude; Canopus — the navigational star that oriented the Saharan homeland — 
sinks progressively below the horizon with each generation born further northeast; and the landscape 
that gave the Zep Tepi its relational grammar — the primordial mound, the concentric waters, the 
break in the ring — recedes first into living memory and eventually into mythologised legend. By the 
4th Dynasty, the Green Sahara was a dry wasteland. The people who carried its cosmology no longer 
had a living landscape to point to as their zero point. They had institutional memory, the Shemsu Hor 
transmission chain, and the mathematical encoding of the Wedjat — but no fixed physical anchor. The 
pyramid is the solution to exactly this problem. Positioned at 30°N, oriented to Meskhetyu within ~3.5 
arc minutes, clad at its apex in Aswan granite from the southern threshold, aligned in its causeway 
toward the western origin, and engineered to outlast any other material made by human hands, the 
Great Pyramid is the zero point of the Zep Tepi cosmological system fixed in stone: an indestructible 
calibration standard permanently encoding every stellar reference, every cosmological relationship, 
and the memory of the landscape that gave rise to them — at a single location the desert could never 
take away.
The mythological dimension of this knowledge-preservation function is explicit in the Pyramid Texts. 
The Cannibal Hymn, inscribed on the antechamber east gable of the Unas pyramid (Utterances 273–
274, c. 2345 BCE), describes the king's apotheosis as the absorption of divine knowledge: 'Unas has

swallowed the wisdom (sjA) of every god... Unas' span of life is Eternity, his limit is Everlastingness.' 
The sjA — the divine faculty of perception and knowledge — is precisely what the king incorporates in 
his transformation. This is the ritual framework within which the pyramid's mathematical and 
astronomical encoding operates. The architecture that preserves knowledge externally in stone 
corresponds, in the Unas pyramid's inscription program, to the apotheosis of a king who has absorbed 
the sjA of every god internally. The two are not separate functions. The pyramid is simultaneously the 
external repository and the instrument of internal transformation — the place where encoded 
knowledge and the king's consciousness become the same thing.
On the reading proposed here, the Great Pyramid was built to permanently encode the Zep Tepi 
cosmological system in the most durable medium available. It is not primarily a tomb. It is a three-
dimensional cosmological monument that recreates the primordial landscape from which Egyptian 
civilisation derived its sacred worldview.
This cosmological reading does not replace the funerary interpretation — it prioritizes it. The 
pyramid is the machine of royal transformation precisely because it is the Zep Tepi made stone. The 
king becomes divine by re-enacting the First Time. A more precise formulation is that the 
pyramids' design is not optimized for burial — the absence of canopic equipment, identifying 
inscriptions, and burial goods suggests that preservation of the body was not the primary architectural 
driver.
Independent Convergence: Giza as "Written Landscape"
The reading of Giza as a cosmological landscape is not isolated to the present hypothesis. The 
archaeoastronomer Giulio Magli, publishing in peer-reviewed venues including Cambridge University 
Press, has independently argued that "the Giza plateau can be interpreted as a 'written landscape' 
where the pyramids represent the hieroglyph for the primordial mound" (Magli 2011; cf. Magli 2013, 
2016). Magli's analysis, grounded in the precise orientation data of the pyramids and their relationship 
to Heliopolis, converges on the same core insight that animates the Zep Tepi model: the Giza 
pyramids are not merely tombs but architectural encodings of the Benben, the primordial mound of 
Egyptian creation theology.
A second line of mainstream Egyptological convergence comes from material culture rather than 
archaeoastronomy. Lehner and Hawass (2017), in their definitive synthesis of the Giza evidence, 
document that the royal pyramids represent the culminating monumental expression of a long-term 
Egyptian funerary grammar — the mud-brick mound within an enclosure — that constitutes the basic 
funerary form across millennia and across all classes. Wong (2021), writing in the Cambridge 
Archaeological Journal, approaches the same transition from the material side: he argues that 
environmental factors — favourable limestone geology in Lower Egypt, and multiple strands of 
evidence for wetter climatic conditions during the Early Dynastic period and the Old Kingdom that 
undermined both the production and the durability of mudbrick — played a key role in facilitating and 
accelerating the rise of stone architecture, with the First Dynasty innovations emerging first in the 
private rather than the royal realm. The form is the same: central elevated mass, surrounding 
enclosure, separation of sacred core from outer world. The scale changes; the grammar does not.
The empirical anchor for this tradition is found at Hierakonpolis. The Predynastic temple at Nekhen, 
excavated by Quibell and Green (1902) and re-examined by Hoffman et al. (1986), consisted of a 
sand mound enclosed by a roughly circular stone revetment — precisely the cosmographic structure 
(central elevated form within a concentric ring boundary) that This paper proposes the Giza pyramids 
monumentalize at scale. This is at the same site as HK6, the predynastic cattle cult center 
documented in Corridor III, and predates the Step Pyramid by approximately six centuries. The Zep 
Tepi cosmogram is therefore not a hypothetical construction reverse-engineered from Giza. It is a 
documented architectural form attested at the Shemsu Hor capital in Predynastic times. The Giza 
pyramids are the monumental, state-scale instantiation of a structural grammar that had been

continuously expressed in stone and mudbrick across Egypt for at least seven hundred years before 
Khufu’s reign.
The convergence between Magli's mainstream archaeoastronomical analysis and the Zep Tepi 
framework is not a matter of citation for authority. It is evidence that the cosmological reading of Giza 
is not a fringe interpretation but a model that satisfies a dense set of empirical constraints — 
constraints that are independently documented and peer-reviewed. The question is not whether Giza

Three Registers of Architectural Purpose
• Backward-looking (restoration): This paper proposes the pyramid encodes what migration eroded 
— the relational grammar of the Richat landscape, the Canopus sky of the Saharan homeland, the 
mathematical standards of the Zep Tepi system. It does not recover these things. It permanently 
marks their threshold in stone that cannot drift.
• Translational (encoding): The Richat Structure is circular — the natural, cosmic, divine form of the 
Zep Tepi mound. The Giza complex is square-based — the human architectural expression of the 
same relational system. The circle is not copied; it is translated. The pyramid complex encodes the 
relational grammar — central elevation, bounded space, transition water, directional axis — in the 
architectural language of human construction. The form changes; the cosmological relationship does 
not.
• Forward-looking (insurance): The pyramid was built not only for what was lost, but against what 
might come again. The African Humid Period collapse may have been the founding trauma the entire 
institutional memory was organised around. A civilisation that had lived through one world-ending 
environmental event had every reason to build beyond the reach of any future one. The pyramid does 
not merely preserve that memory. It guarantees it: an archive in the only medium that cannot be 
dissolved by sand, outlasted by drought, or erased by the next turning of the sky.
These three registers define the pyramid’s complete function: a monument designed simultaneously 
as memorial, knowledge repository, and durable preservation against civilisational loss.
Secondary Purpose: Royal Transformation
The pyramid also served as the mechanism of royal apotheosis — the architectural machine through 
which the dead pharaoh underwent transformation from human king to divine being. This is the 
function described in the Pyramid Texts: the king ascending to the Imperishable Stars, merging with 
Ra, becoming Osiris. The two purposes — cosmological encoding and royal transformation — are not 
in conflict. They are the same purpose at different scales. This reading aligns with mainstream 
Egyptological scholarship: Assmann (2005) documents that Egyptian mortuary religion was centred on 
transformation rather than interment, the pyramid functioning as an instrument of royal becoming 
rather than a container for remains (Assmann, J. 2005. Death and Salvation in Ancient Egypt. Trans. 
David Lorton. Cornell University Press).
Mechanism of Transmission
How does a landscape become an architectural system? The proposed transmission does not 
require direct architectural copying of a landscape. It operates through cognitive and ritual encoding.

===== PAGE 47 =====
Landscapes such as Richat are not reproduced visually but abstracted into cosmological principles --- 
central emergence, bounded order, liminal water, and directional transformation. These principles are 
preserved through ritual practice, oral tradition, and symbolic systems (e.g., the Wedjat as a fractional 
and integrative schema), and later instantiated in architecture when conditions allow. The resulting 
structures therefore reflect not the physical form of the original landscape, but its encoded relational 
logic.
This material approach to cultural transmission finds direct support in recent theoretical work on 
memory in ancient Egypt. Olabarria (2025), in a dedicated Cambridge Elements volume on Egyptian 
memory practices, argues that "a performative engagement with monuments was one of the bases of 
the practice of memory" in ancient Egypt. Her framework identifies monuments as "technologies of 
remembrance" --- material anchors designed to fix collective cosmological knowledge in durable form 
across generations. The pyramid, on this reading, is not merely a symbol of the Zep Tepi but a 
memory technology engineered to preserve and transmit the relational grammar of the primordial 
landscape.
Olabarria's analysis operates within the tradition of cultural memory theory established by Jan 
Assmann (already discussed in Section 5 above), but extends it by emphasizing the specific 
mechanisms --- materiality, performativity, and place-making --- through which monuments sustain 
memory across centuries. The present hypothesis operationalizes this framework: the pyramid as 
memory technology, the causeway as processional stage for performative re-enactment, and the 
entire Giza complex as a "place of memory" that re-instantiates the ancestral Saharan landscape in 
monumental form (Olabarria 2025).
The performative dimension of this transmission is consistent with broader Egyptian material culture 
analysis, including Kroft's (2024) examination of decorated ware vessels as vehicles for memory 
expression in formalized ritual contexts.
Olabarria’s Egypt-specific framework sits within a wider comparative literature on how non-literate and 
partly-literate societies encode durable knowledge, which supplies the mechanism this hypothesis 
requires. In cognitive archaeology, Kelly (2015) documents that oral societies stored large bodies of 
pragmatic knowledge — including astronomy, calendars, and navigation — in monuments and 
landscape features functioning as mnemonic devices, reinterpreting Stonehenge, Chaco Canyon, and 
Poverty Point on that basis. In cultural astronomy, Hamacher (2018) demonstrates that oral cultures 
encoded precise astronomical observations, including stellar variability and periodicity, and transmitted 
them across generations for calendrical and ritual use. The cognitive constraints that make such 
transmission faithful rather than degradative — theme, imagery, and rhythmic structure operating 
together — are set out by Rubin (1995), while Vansina (1985) provides the methodological framework 
for treating the resulting traditions as recoverable information rather than invention.
Two points follow. First, the mechanism proposed here is not an ad hoc postulate but an instance of a 
documented cross-cultural phenomenon: monuments serving as knowledge stores, and oral tradition 
as a high-fidelity carrier. Second, the practice of encoding precise astronomical periods is 
independently attested within Egypt itself. Jetsu and Porceddu (2013, 2015) present statistical 
evidence that the Cairo Calendar (Papyrus Cairo 86637) records the 2.85-day period of the eclipsing 
binary Algol and the 29.6-day lunar period in its day-by-day structure — the earliest known 
documentary record of a variable star’s period. That source is Ramesside and therefore far later than 
Khufu, and its Algol identification is debated; it is cited here only to establish that period-encoding was 
a real Egyptian practice, not a modern projection. The claim advanced is accordingly the narrower 
one: that the Great Pyramid may represent a monumental instance of a mnemonic practice for which 
the comparative and Egyptological records already provide precedent.

Borderline case: Saqqara (Step Pyramid complex of Djoser, 3rd Dynasty). The Saqqara complex 
exhibits central elevation (Step Pyramid), delimited sacred space (enclosure wall), and a directional 
axis (the Heb-Sed court and processional elements). The question of water integration at Saqqara has 
been reopened, though not settled, by Landreau et al. (2024, PLOS ONE 19(8): e0306690), who 
conducted transdisciplinary analysis of the Saqqara watershed and found that the Gisr el-Mudir 
enclosure has the structural features of a check dam, with topographic reconstruction supporting the 
existence of an intermittent lake west of the Djoser complex connected to the Dry Moat. On that 
reading the Dry Moat would be more than a symbolic water-proxy; the interpretation is recent and 
contested, and is treated here as provisional (Lauer 1936; Jeffreys 2010; Landreau et al. 2024). On 
this basis, Saqqara satisfies three of the four relational elements: central elevation, bounded space, 
and water at the boundary. The fourth element — a directional axis explicitly connecting water to 
monument — is present processionally but not hydraulically integrated in the manner of the Giza 
causeway. Saqqara is therefore the strongest precursor form of the full system. The element 
distinguishing Giza from Saqqara is not the presence of water but its deliberate hydraulic integration 
with the monument axis.
The intermediate step between Djoser and Khufu — the reign of Sneferu — provides independent 
support for this developmental trajectory. Rather than treating the Bent and Red Pyramids as 
sequential experiments — the second generated by the failure of the first — Belmonte and Lull (2023) 
argue that "the pair of pyramids of Sneferu in Dahshur would actually form a single project conceived 
as such from its inception" (p. 396). The complementary names of the two monuments — Kha 
Sneferu ("Sneferu Appears") for the Bent Pyramid and Sneferu Kha for the Red Pyramid — are 
"undoubtedly evidence that supports the idea of a common project" (Belmonte & Lull 2023, pp. 397–
398). They further propose that the two pyramids may represent "a huge symbolic representation of 
the white crown of Upper Egypt, in the case of the Bent Pyramid... and of the red crown of Lower 
Egypt for the Red Pyramid" (Belmonte & Lull 2023, p. 398). This reading transforms Sneferu's reign 
from a period of trial-and-error correction into a coherent architectural programme — a deliberate dual 
project that anticipates Khufu's own "unitary project" at Giza, where Khufu and Khafre's pyramids and 
the Sphinx were, on Belmonte and Lull's analysis, conceived as a single integrated complex called 
Akhet Khufu, "the Horizon of Cheops" (Belmonte & Lull 2023, p. 400). The full Giza complex thus 
represents not the independent decisions of successive kings but the culmination of a multi-
generational tradition of planned sacred landscapes — a tradition whose earliest monumental 
expression, this paper argues, is the Phase Zero provincial pyramid program.

implied. Third, the Angkor system derives its cosmological grammar from Mount Meru theology in the

does not, however, explain why Giza instantiated it 3,600 years earlier. The parsimony argument for 
transmission over independent invention at Giza rests on the convergence of additional evidence 
domains (genetic, archaeozoological, textual) that are not present for Angkor. Convergent evolution at 
Angkor therefore strengthens, rather than weakens, the argument: if the system can arise 
independently, it requires explanation when it appears alongside a documented corridor, an ancient 
North African genetic record spanning that corridor, and textual memory of a western origin.

widely, the only other known site achieving full four-element integration is Angkor Wat (12th century

system, and the only one for which a documented corridor, an ancient North African genetic record

pattern is consistent with — but does not prove — an evolutionary trajectory from Nabta (precursor)

integration within this expanded sample strengthens the hypothesis that Giza represents a deliberate 
architectural encoding of the Zep Tepi relational system, rather than a coincidental convergence of 
common sacred elements.
A note on the comparative method: The absence of the full four-element system at Göbekli Tepe, 
Stonehenge, or other sites does not constitute positive evidence that Giza's system was transmitted 
from Richat. It serves only to eliminate the alternative explanation that such systems arise universally 
or randomly across cultures. The positive case for transmission rests on the convergence of 
independent lines — hydrology, genetics, archaeozoology, texts, and astronomy — not on the 
comparative table alone.

Cross-Site Test of the Relational Triad
The relational reading advanced above makes a falsifiable prediction. If the Giza configuration 
encodes a cosmological template rather than a site-specific engineering solution, the same relational 
triad — water access at the sacred boundary, a directional axis, and an elevated central feature — 
should recur at other Old Kingdom ceremonial complexes, and should appear even where the site is 
not a royal tomb. The prediction is tested here against four further complexes spanning the Third to 
Fifth Dynasties, each scored on three independent criteria: (1) water access at the sacred boundary — 
a valley temple, harbour, or quay at a canal, lake, or Nile channel; (2) a directional axis — a causeway 
or processional spine linking that water access to the centre; and (3) an elevated central feature — a 
pyramid, benben platform, or sanctuary mound. Each criterion is recorded as present, symbolic or 
partial, or absent.
The triad is complete at every Fourth- and Fifth-Dynasty complex examined. At Dahshur, the complex 
of Sneferu — Khufu’s father, one reign before Giza — pairs the Bent Pyramid with a valley temple, a 
lower causeway, and an associated harbour set within a deliberately managed water landscape, as 
documented by the German Archaeological Institute’s excavations (Alexanian et al. 2010). At Abusir, 
the Fifth-Dynasty pyramids of Sahure and his successors place their valley temples directly on the 
shore of the Abusir lake, linked by causeways to the pyramids above (Verner 2001). In each case 
water access, axis, and elevated centre are all present.
The most diagnostic case is the one the prediction specifically anticipated: a non-funerary ritual site. 
The Fifth-Dynasty sun temples at Abu Ghurab are solar sanctuaries rather than royal tombs — the 
oldest cult buildings explicitly dedicated to Ra — yet they reproduce the identical triad. The valley 
temple of Nyuserre’s solar complex supplies the water access, a causeway supplies the axis, and in 
the position a pyramid would occupy stands a massive benben on a raised platform; the valley temple 
was itself newly excavated by the Italian Archaeological Mission at Abu Ghurab, directed by 
Massimiliano Nuzzolo (University of Turin) and Rosanna Pirelli (University of Naples L’Orientale), 
across the 2024 and 2025 seasons. Ludwig Borchardt had identified the structure in 1901 but could 
not excavate it because of the water table; falling groundwater has since made the work possible. The 
excavation exposed more than 1,000 square metres — roughly half the sanctuary — together with a 
ramp interpreted as having connected the temple to the Nile or one of its branches (Egyptian Ministry 
of Tourism and Antiquities, announcement of 12 December 2025; Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs 
and International Cooperation, 2026). As with the Hawass announcement discussed above, these 
findings are reported from official statements rather than peer-reviewed publication, and the water-
access element at Abu Ghurab is therefore recorded here as provisionally rather than definitively 
confirmed. The benben is the primordial mound itself. That the same water-to-axis-to-elevated-centre 
relation should govern a site with no burial, and should place the explicit primordial-mound symbol at 
its focus, is difficult to reduce to funerary function; it reads as cosmological configuration.
The earliest complex sharpens rather than weakens the pattern. Imhotep’s Step Pyramid at Saqqara 
already assembles the cosmological core — an elevated primordial mound within a bounded sacred 
precinct, itself ringed by the great rock-cut Dry Moat — but in inward-turned form. The moat’s primary 
documented function is a quarry (Welc & Marks 2014), and any reading of it as a symbolic water-
boundary — or, on the recent and still-contested hydraulic interpretation of Landreau et al. (2024), as 
genuine water infrastructure — must remain secondary and provisional; the complex’s axis, likewise, 
is internal (entrance colonnade to Great Court to Heb-Sed court) rather than an external causeway to

===== PAGE 55 =====
the river. What the Fourth Dynasty adds is therefore not the invention of the template but its 
externalisation: the inward-turned enclosure becomes an outward causeway to a real harbour, and the 
symbolic boundary becomes engineered water. A configuration that develops across dynasties, rather 
than appearing fully formed, is precisely the signature of transmitted and elaborated knowledge rather 
than a single functional solution.
One objection must be met directly. The valley-temple-causeway-monument arrangement is the 
canonical Old Kingdom pyramid-complex form, and for the pyramid complexes it admits a 
straightforward functional reading: a valley temple at the water for arrival and the transport of stone, a 
causeway for processional access. Two features of the present sample resist that reduction. First, the 
sun temples carry the triad without a tomb, with a benben rather than a burial at the centre. Second, 
the water is not merely proximate but engineered — the purpose-built harbours and regularised wadis 
at Dahshur and Giza represent deliberate configuration rather than convenient location. The relational 
triad is, on this evidence, best understood as a cosmological template that the Old Kingdom 
progressively externalised and monumentalised — most completely at Giza.

Methodological Note on Evidentiary Weighting
This hypothesis draws on multiple independent lines of evidence: hydrology, genetics, 
archaeozoology, textual analysis, archaeoastronomy, and architectural analysis. No single line is 
conclusive. The argument is cumulative: the convergence of these independent lines — none of which 
was designed to confirm the others — is what gives the hypothesis weight. This is the structure of a 
consilience of inductions (Whewell 1840), not a proof from any single domain. The reader is invited to 
evaluate the hypothesis on the basis of the full convergence.
Why 440 Cubits: Formula-Fitting and Its Alternative
The question of why the Great Pyramid was built to a base of 440 royal cubits has an extensive 
literature, and the approach taken here differs from it in method rather than in conclusion. That 
difference is worth stating explicitly, since it determines what either approach can establish.
The established portion of that literature concerns the seked rather than the base. Petrie (1883) 
observed that a base of 440 with a height of 280 places the ratio 22/7 in the structure, the semi-circuit 
being 22 × 40 cubits and the height 7 × 40; Rossi (2004) and Herz-Fischler (2000) established that the 
π- and φ-proportions follow as consequences of a seked of 5 ½ palms. This explains the ratio of base 
to height. It does not explain the base itself: a seked of 5 ½ produces the same proportions at any 
scale, and Meidum and Unas were both built to it at quite different sizes.
A wider body of work outside the peer-reviewed literature addresses the base directly, and does so by 
a consistent method: an expression is sought which evaluates to 440. Proposals in circulation include 
derivations from the mean number of lunations in a year, from √3 × π scaled by powers of ten, from 
convergents of √3 such as 433/250, and from terms of a golden-section series. Each yields a number 
close to 440, and several are arithmetically elegant.

===== PAGE 56 =====
The difficulty with this method is not that any particular derivation is wrong. It is that the method cannot 
fail. Given π, √2, √3, φ, the lunation count and free choice of scaling factor, an expression evaluating 
to within a fraction of a percent of any given three-digit integer can be constructed. A derivation of 440 
is therefore not evidence that 440 was chosen, because the same procedure would have produced a 
derivation of 439 or 441 with equal facility had the pyramid been built to those dimensions. No such 
analysis reports what its formula yields for bases the Egyptians did not use, and without that 
comparison the result carries no discriminating information.
The approach taken here inverts the procedure. Rather than searching for an expression that outputs 
440, the analysis holds the method fixed — the same six dimensions, the same operations, the same 
target list, the same tolerance — and varies the base across every whole cubit from 75 to 600, a range 
spanning the smallest to well beyond the largest Old Kingdom pyramid. The question asked is not 
whether a formula can be found for 440, but where in that range the correspondence performance 
actually falls, and whether the answer depends on the targets being the ones Egyptian sources 
document.
Measured by the denominator margin defined above, and holding the seked at 5½ throughout, the 
Great Pyramid’s base ranks in the top four per cent of the 526 candidate bases at every tolerance 
within the observational band derived earlier: twenty-second at ±0.10%, fourth at ±0.15%, third at 
±0.20%, and seventh at ±0.25%. Only seven of the 526 bases — 1.33% — maintain comparable 
standing across the whole band. Outside the band, at ±0.30%, it remains in the top eight per cent.
The control is the part that distinguishes this from the formula-fitting tradition. The entire sweep was 
repeated against three hundred shadow target lists, each constructed by replacing every real target 
with a random value drawn from the plausible range of its own physical category, so that a lunar 
period remains lunar-scale and a planetary synodic remains planetary-scale. If 440 were simply an 
arithmetically fertile number, it would continue to rank highly. It does not: against shadow targets its 
median rank is 275 of 526 — the middle of the distribution — and it attained the maximum in none of 
the three hundred trials (p < 0.0033, the upper bound available from 300 draws). The standing of 440 
is therefore a property of its relationship to the specific values Egyptian sources record as 
astronomically significant, not a property of the number itself.
What this establishes is bounded and should not be overstated. It does not demonstrate that the base 
was selected for this reason; a dimension chosen on other grounds may occupy an optimum without 
having been placed there deliberately, and no arithmetical test can distinguish the two cases. What it 
does establish is that the standing of 440 is specific to the Egyptian target set and survives a control 
that the formula-fitting approach does not attempt. The claim advanced is accordingly that the base is 
where the correspondence performance is maximal for the documented targets, and that this is 
unlikely to be coincidental — not that intent has been demonstrated.
The Denominator Margin Across Nine Pyramids
The denominator sweep reported above establishes that sixty-fourths outperform their neighbouring 
grids at the Great Pyramid. It does not by itself establish that this is a property of the Great Pyramid 
rather than of the fraction system. If k/64 outperformed k/48, k/60 and k/72 at every monument, the 
result would reflect an arithmetic peculiarity of the number 64 and would carry no architectural 
information. The sweep was therefore repeated across the nine documented Old Kingdom pyramids 
scored in the comparative analysis above.
Define the denominator margin as the correspondence count under k/64 minus the highest count 
achieved by k/48, k/60 or k/72 at the same monument, with dimensions, targets, operations and 
tolerance held constant. A positive margin means sixty-fourths outperform their immediate neighbours 
at that pyramid; a negative margin means they do not. The Great Pyramid returns a margin of +5, 
scoring 11 against a best neighbouring value of 5. Of the remaining eight pyramids, six return a 
margin of zero or below — Khafre, Meidum and Unas at zero, the Bent Pyramid and Teti at −2,

===== PAGE 57 =====
Sahure at −3 — and the only two positive margins, Menkaure and the Red Pyramid, reach +2. No 
other monument in the sample approaches the Great Pyramid’s margin.
The result was then tested against chance. Twenty thousand pyramids were generated with base and 
seked drawn uniformly from the Old Kingdom ranges, and the denominator margin computed for each. 
The mean margin across random dimensions is −1.32: for an arbitrary pyramid, sixty-fourths typically 
underperform their neighbouring grids rather than outperform them. A margin of +5 or greater 
occurred in 100 of 20,000 cases, giving p = 0.0050.
Unlike the raw correspondence count, this result is stable across most of the tolerance band. At 
±0.10% the Great Pyramid’s margin is +3 (p = 0.043); at ±0.15%, +5 (p = 0.005); at ±0.20%, +5 
(p = 0.005); at ±0.25%, +4 (p = 0.019). At ±0.30% the margin falls to +2 and significance is lost 
(p = 0.116). The random mean remains negative at every tolerance tested. The result therefore holds 
across four of the five thresholds examined and fails at the widest, and that failure is reported rather 
than omitted.
The interpretation is narrow and should be stated as such. The test does not show that the Great 
Pyramid encodes astronomical values; it shows that whatever produces its correspondence count is 
specific to its dimensions and not to the number 64. Sixty-fourths do nothing in particular at Khafre, at 
Meidum, or at Unas — and Unas shares the Great Pyramid’s seked exactly. Scale, not the fraction 
system alone, carries the result.
Dependence on Individual Targets
A correspondence count of ten from fourteen invites the objection that a single fortunate target is 
carrying the result, with the remaining thirteen contributing nothing beyond ordinary arithmetic. This is 
testable directly. Each of the fourteen frozen targets was removed in turn and the second Monte Carlo 
test re-run on the remaining thirteen, at the full N = 100,000 and with the same seed. Were one target 
responsible, its deletion would collapse the significance.
Almost none does. Thirteen of the fourteen deletions leave the result significant, with p-values from 
0.0088 to 0.048. The single exception is the anomalistic month, whose removal takes the result to 
p = 0.064 and out of significance; that target is discussed separately above. The three deletions that 
most improve the p-value are documented misses — removing Mercury gives 0.0088, the Sothic cycle 
0.0093, the synodic month 0.016 — because a missed target is dead weight in the denominator. Had 
one correspondence been load-bearing, its removal would stand out as an isolated failure. No such 
outlier appears, and the result is distributed across the set rather than resting on any member of it.
One feature of the table deserves comment, because it bears on the composition of the target list. The 
four deletions that most improve the p-value are the four documented misses: Mercury at 0.0088, the 
Sothic cycle at 0.0093, the synodic month at 0.016 and the sidereal month at 0.017, every one of them 
better than the baseline of p = 0.024. A missed target is dead weight in the denominator, and 
discarding it raises the score. All four are retained. A list from which failures had been pruned would 
report a stronger figure and mean considerably less, and the presence of two failures that actively cost 
the analysis significance is evidence that the thirteen were not assembled for fit.
The scope of this test should be stated precisely. It establishes robustness to the removal of targets; it 
says nothing about the addition of targets that were never included. That second question — whether 
values the Egyptians tracked have been omitted because they would have missed — is not 
answerable by any test conducted on the present list, and is addressed instead by the differential 
prediction stated under H, which specifies in advance where correspondences should and should not ₁
be expected to appear.
Derivation of the Matching Tolerance
The ±0.2% threshold used throughout requires justification, since a tolerance chosen for convenience 
would render any correspondence count meaningless. It is not chosen arbitrarily. It approximates the 
precision at which the target values were themselves knowable to the builders.

===== PAGE 58 =====
A synodic period is determined observationally by timing a recurring event — a heliacal rising, a first 
stationary point — and dividing the elapsed interval by the number of completed cycles. The 
achievable precision is therefore the timing error divided by the total span observed. Naked-eye 
determination of a heliacal event carries an uncertainty of roughly three to five days, since the date on 
which a star or planet first becomes visible depends on atmospheric extinction, horizon conditions and 
the acuity of the observer. Taking a conservative ±3 days and a record spanning three to five 
completed cycles — a realistic span for a single scribal working life — the resulting uncertainties are 
approximately 0.17% to 0.10% for Venus, 0.13% to 0.08% for Mars, 0.25% to 0.15% for Jupiter, and 
0.26% to 0.16% for Saturn. The lunar periods, being short, carry proportionally larger uncertainty per 
cycle and require correspondingly longer records to reach comparable precision.
The 0.2% threshold sits within that band. It is not the precision of the monument, which is considerably 
finer: Petrie’s survey found the base sides agreeing to within about 4.4 cm over 230.33 m, a relative 
error of 0.019%, roughly an order of magnitude tighter. The binding constraint on any encoding is 
therefore not the accuracy of the masonry but the accuracy of the astronomy the masonry would 
record. A threshold of 0.05% would demand that the encoding be more precise than the observations 
available to produce it; a threshold of 0.5% would admit values the builders could have distinguished. 
The frozen tolerance is the one commensurate with the data.
This derivation was not used to select the threshold, which was fixed before the controls reported 
below were run and is frozen in the deposited code. It is offered as the reason the threshold is 
defensible rather than convenient, and it is stated here so that a reader who disagrees with the 
reasoning can locate the disagreement precisely. The tolerance sweep reported below shows the 
consequences of varying it in either direction.
Monte Carlo Statistical Analysis
Two primary Monte Carlo tests were conducted on the mathematical encoding section (N = 100,000 
each, seed 42, precision threshold ±0.2%), supported by the further controls reported below and 
frozen in the deposited code: a target-value null, a matched-cardinality fraction null, a size-matched 
pyramid null, an evenly-spaced fraction null, a deterministic denominator sweep, and the independent-
core and maximally-independent-core analyses. The analysis uses simple Wedjat operations only — 
dimension × fraction or dimension ÷ fraction — the exact computational form documented in the Rhind 
Mathematical Papyrus (Problems 56–58). Cross-dimensional addition and subtraction operations are 
documented in the Declared Methodological Constraints section and produce additional 
correspondences; they were excluded from the Monte Carlo analysis specifically to avoid inflating the 
expression space and to anchor the statistical test to the most directly defensible operation type. The 
comparative table and the p-values reported here therefore represent a conservative lower bound on 
the encoding density. The same rules apply to every pyramid and every random comparison. The 
complete code and raw output are published alongside this paper for independent replication; 
corrections are welcome. Three files are deposited. The main analysis script giza_analysis.py (Python 
3 standard library only; random seed 42 reset before every stochastic block) performs everything 
reported in this section and produces giza_analysis_results.txt, its complete raw output, also 
deposited. The third file, stellar_control.py, performs the stellar negative control described below: it 
precesses eleven stars to 2560 BCE using the Vondrák et al. (2011) long-term precession model with 
proper motion applied, derives thirty-one angular targets, scores them under the identical rules, and 
runs the 200,000-trial shadow-angle control that establishes the chance expectation. It requires 
astropy, which it installs automatically if absent, and takes several minutes to run; every other analysis 
in this paper uses the standard library alone. The suite has been executed on independent hardware 
and, separately, in two browser-based Python 3.12 environments with no local filesystem and no 
shared state, reproducing every reported figure identically in each case. The stellar control has 
likewise been run on separate machines under Python 3.12 and Python 3.13, returning 5 of 31 
observed against a chance expectation of 4.83 and p = 0.5431 in each case. This establishes that the 
results depend on the deposited code alone and can be regenerated by any reader with a browser. It 
does not constitute independent verification of the analysis itself, which requires a reader who did not

write it; determinism and correctness are separate properties, and only the first is demonstrated here. 
One block of the suite, the shadow-target control, is computationally heavy and may exceed the 
execution limit of some browser runners; it completes without difficulty in a standard local Python 3

A note on the structure of the deposited analysis. Parts 1 to 3 of giza_analysis.py constitute the core 
analysis governed by the specification set out above: the frozen fourteen-target list, the six Giza 
dimensions, the sixty-three Wedjat values, multiplication and division only, and the ±0.2% tolerance. 
Parts 4 to 7 lie outside it. They are post-hoc, they feed no decision rule, they leave Parts 1 to 3 
unaltered, and each departs from the specification in a stated way. Part 4 addresses Earth’s polar and 
equatorial radii, which are not among the fourteen targets, and employs operations the specification 
excludes — pairwise sums of dimensions, together with calendrical multipliers and powers of ten; it is 
reported above at a separately designated lower evidential tier. Part 5 computes the denominator 
margin across nine documented pyramids, comparing k/64 against the neighbouring grids k/48, k/60 
and k/72, which are not the Wedjat set, at dimensions which are not Giza’s. Part 6 varies the base 
across every whole cubit from 75 to 600 with the seked held at 5 ½, varying a dimension the 
specification fixes. Part 7 removes each target in turn and re-runs the second Monte Carlo test on the 
remaining thirteen; it is a diagnostic and not a selection — the target list is not altered on the basis of 
its results, and in particular the four documented misses are retained although the removal of any of 
them would improve the reported p-value. All four were devised after the specification was written 
down, and are reported as post-hoc analyses. They are included because they test the specificity and 
robustness of the core result rather than restate it, and because withholding an analysis that bears on 
the result would defeat the purpose of fixing the specification at all.
Test 1 (Wedjat system specificity): The Wedjat binary fraction system applied to the Great Pyramid’s 
six exterior dimensions achieves 11 of 13 astronomical target correspondences. Against 100,000 
random fraction systems of equivalent structure applied to the same dimensions, the random mean is 
6.26 (±1.65 SD). Giza’s result is 2.27 standard deviations above the random mean (empirical 
p = 0.01497, p < 0.001). Only 1,497 of the 100,000 random fraction systems scored 11 or higher.
Test 2 (Giza dimensional specificity): With Wedjat fractions held constant, the Great Pyramid’s 
dimensions are compared against 100,000 randomly generated Old Kingdom-style pyramids with 
base varied uniformly from 75–440 rc and seked from 4.0–7.5 palms/cubit (Rossi 2004). The random 
mean is 5.76 (±1.93 SD). Giza’s result is 2.20 standard deviations above the random mean (empirical 
p = 0.02419, p < 0.01). Only 2,419 of the 100,000 randomly generated pyramids scored 11 or higher. 
Both results remain statistically significant after Bonferroni correction for two simultaneous tests 
(adjusted threshold p < 0.025): Test 1 p = 0.01497, Test 2 p = 0.02419.
System Specificity: The Designated Primary Test
This paper designates a single primary decision rule, and it is not the correspondence count. At equal 
cardinality — each rival historical fraction system downsampled at random to the Wedjat set’s exact 
size of sixty-three values — the Wedjat system must out-reach every rival across the tolerance band 
from ±0.10% to ±0.30%. That rule was chosen because it is the one test in the suite that does not 
depend on the tolerance, and because it isolates the question the correspondence count cannot 
answer: whether the specific dyadic subset, rather than the size of the toolkit or the general 
reachability of the targets, is doing the work.
The result is reported here in full. In the raw head-to-head at the frozen tolerance, the Wedjat set 
reaches 10 of 14 targets with 63 fractions; Egyptian unit fractions reach 11 with 1,923, Babylonian 
sexagesimal 4 with 118, small-denominator simple ratios 4 with 45, and decimal 8 with 99. Expressed 
per hundred fractions the Wedjat rate is 15.87, against 0.52, 3.39, 8.89 and 8.08 respectively. 
Downsampled to sixty-three values (N = 20,000 random subsets per system), no rival reaches the 
Wedjat score: not one subset in twenty thousand attains 10 of 14 for Egyptian unit fractions, 
Babylonian sexagesimal or decimal, and the same holds on the independent ten-target set (p <

===== PAGE 60 =====
0.0001 in each case). The simple-ratio system contains fewer than sixty-three fractions and cannot be 
downsampled to equal size; it is therefore excluded from the equal-cardinality test and from the 
decision rule, as declared above.
Repeating the test at each tolerance across the frozen band gives the profile the decision rule 
requires. At ±0.10% the Wedjat set reaches 5 of 11 and the highest rival probability is 0.0032; at 
±0.15%, 6 of 11 and 0.0016; at ±0.20%, ±0.25% and ±0.30%, 8 of 11 with rival probabilities of 0.0001, 
0.0004 and 0.0018. The maximum across the entire band is 0.0032. The primary criterion is therefore 
met, and — unlike the correspondence count, whose significance moves with the tolerance — this 
result does not move when the threshold is tightened or loosened. It is the finding on which the 
dimensional argument rests.
Its scope should be stated as precisely as the result. The test establishes that the dyadic subset is 
specifically more efficient at reaching the documented Egyptian target list than the other historical 
systems tested, at equal size and under identical operations. It does not establish that the 
correspondences were deliberately placed, and it does not exhaust the space of possible rival 
systems: a culturally attested system not tested here could in principle perform comparably, and 
identifying one would weaken the claim.
Neither result constitutes proof of deliberate cosmological encoding. Together they establish that the 
observed 11-of-13 target pattern is statistically non-random under two independent null models: 
unlikely to arise from an arbitrary fraction system on these dimensions, and unlikely to arise from the 
Wedjat system on arbitrary Old Kingdom pyramid proportions. These two tests establish that the 
correspondence count is improbable. They do not establish what produces it, and on their own they 
remain vulnerable to the objection that some feature of the fraction system, rather than of the 
monument, is doing the work. The three analyses that follow — the evenly-spaced fraction null, the 
denominator margin across nine pyramids, and the base sweep with its shadow-target control — 
address that objection directly by varying one element at a time while holding the rest fixed. They are 
reported after the primary tests because they are specificity tests rather than significance tests, and 
because two of the three are post-hoc, as declared below. In the assessment offered here they carry 
more evidential weight than the raw count, since each was constructed so that it could have failed. 
The analysis also examines the full Old Kingdom pyramid record. When the identical test is applied to 
nine documented pyramids using their published base and height figures — design values where 
these are securely reconstructed, as set out in the Declared Methodological Constraints — Giza leads 
all three architectural periods:
5th–6th Dynasty (post-Giza, c. 2490–2278 BCE): Teti 5/13, Unas 6/13, Sahure 5/13. Giza Complex 
(c. 2560–2510 BCE): Khufu 10/14, Khafre 7/13, Menkaure 7/13. Sneferu’s Programme (pre-Giza,

after the 4th Dynasty peak. Whether the builders of later pyramids lacked the knowledge, the

expression of the tradition within the documented Old Kingdom record is what the analysis 
establishes.

following conditions, each of which is in principle falsifiable by ongoing or future research:
Genomic convergence fails: If systematic analysis of additional Predynastic and early Dynastic 
genomes finds no contribution from the documented Saharan corridor population, the biological 
transmission chain is severed.

Paleohydrological corridor impassable: If refined modelling demonstrates the Highland Corridor

Second formation satisfies all criteria: If any Saharan formation is identified that simultaneously 
satisfies all four criteria applied across Corridor I and II — sufficient scale, documented Holocene 
water at the structure, documented human presence at the structure, and a central elevation rising

Null model parity demonstrated: If formal Monte Carlo testing shows that the dimensional

across randomly generated geometric sets or comparable Old Kingdom monuments, the selectivity of

Material culture discontinuity confirmed: If systematic analysis of assemblages from the proposed

Shaft orientations structurally determined: If revised archaeoastronomical analysis demonstrates 
that the stellar shaft orientations are primarily structurally or topographically constrained rather than 
astronomically targeted, the cosmological intentionality of the shaft programme is not established by 
orientation data alone.
A note on the independence of evidence domains: Reviewers may argue that the domains are not 
truly independent — all are interpreted through the same framework. This is acknowledged. The 
independence claimed is disciplinary: the hydrology papers do not cite the genetics papers; the 
archaeozoology findings were not designed to confirm architectural hypotheses. Their convergence is 
therefore meaningful but not definitive.
A final limitation on the Richat identification: The identification of Richat as the origin landscape 
rests on the absence of any other known formation that satisfies all four criteria applied across 
Corridor I and II. This makes Richat the leading testable candidate, not a proven origin. The 
hypothesis does not require that Richat was the actual landscape encoded — only that the Zep Tepi 
relational system originated in a landscape with these features, for which the Richat is the only 
currently known candidate. Future high-resolution survey and targeted excavation in annular 
depressions elsewhere in the Sahara could identify additional candidates or falsify this distinction.
Scope, Boundaries, and Limitations
The Zep Tepi Series is structured so that each paper addresses a defined evidentiary scope. Defining 
the scope of the present paper explicitly, and what lies outside it, is an act of methodological discipline 
rather than defensive qualification.
What this paper claims: that the exterior architecture of the Giza complex instantiates a specific four-
element relational cosmological system (central elevation, bounded sacred space, transition water, 
processional axis) whose origin this series locates in the Saharan pastoral tradition documented in 
The Corridor Series; that the exterior dimensions of the Great Pyramid contain mathematical and 
astronomical correspondences consistent with systematic knowledge preservation through the Wedjat 
fraction system; and that the compound evidence across cosmological, hydrological, 
archaeoastronomical, genetic, and mathematical domains makes deliberate encoding the more 
parsimonious explanation for the complex’s design.
What this paper explicitly does not claim: proof of intentional encoding; certainty about transmission 
routes or timelines; that the mathematical correspondences are unique to Giza (the comparative 
analysis reported here covers nine documented Old Kingdom pyramids and twenty thousand 
randomly dimensioned ones; extension to non-Egyptian monuments remains future work); that the 
Shemsu Hor hypothesis accounts for all features of Giza’s design; or that the cosmological reading of

the complex excludes practical and logistical explanations. The paper repeatedly distinguishes 
“consistent with” from “proves” throughout.
Scope limits: this paper addresses the exterior of the Great Pyramid and the Giza complex in its 
landscape setting. The internal structure — chamber acoustics, electromagnetic resonance, the Grand 
Gallery, the sarcophagus, and any proposed interior encoding system — is not addressed here, and 
no claim about it is advanced or assumed. The Wedjat fraction system is treated here only as the 
arithmetic key to the dimensional correspondences documented above; its wider role as a knowledge 
architecture spanning medicine, architecture, calendrics, and theology is beyond the scope of this 
paper, though the medical and botanical strand of that architecture is treated in Levy (2026f).
The interval requiring explanation is the 540-year span between the close of the Hierakonpolis 
tradition (c. 3,100 BCE) and the construction of the Great Pyramid (c. 2,560–2,540 BCE). That interval 
is not left as an inferential gap in this series. It is the subject of the Bridging Paper, Phase Zero: The 
Bull in the Sky (Levy 2026d), which documents a transmission chain running through it: the 
coordinated Minor Step Pyramid programme at seven provincial cult centres, the Djoser complex at 
Saqqara, and the archaeozoological evidence for Shemsu Hor continuity at Hierakonpolis. On that 
evidence the pyramid form, the stellar orientation procedure and the institutional apparatus for co-
ordinated sacred construction were all already in place before Khufu, at provincial scale, encoding the 
same Meskhetyu tradition. The present paper assumes those findings rather than re-arguing them. 
What remains genuinely open is narrower, and should be stated as such: the transmission chain is 
documented at the level of architectural programme, orientation practice and faunal continuity, but no 
single line of evidence traces a specific body of cosmological knowledge from Hierakonpolis to Giza in 
unbroken sequence. The claim advanced here is that the convergence of independent domains 
across the interval makes continued transmission the most parsimonious available explanation, not 
that any one line establishes it on its own.

Testing Protocol
The 'Pyramid as Memory' hypothesis generates several clear, falsifiable predictions that distinguish it

 Prediction 1 (Causeway Orientation): If Giza encodes the Zep Tepi landscape, the Giza

causeway orientation at Giza. A precise azimuth correspondence would constitute strong 
confirmation; qualitative directional agreement constitutes a weaker but still positive result.

exterior, the three-chamber vertical arrangement (Subterranean, Queen's, King's) should 
correspond to the Zep Tepi sequence of water, threshold and transformation, and should do so 
consistently with the Pyramid Texts' description of the king's ascent. This prediction is recorded 
for completeness and is not argued for in the present paper.
 Prediction 3 (Heit el-Ghurab aDNA): Ancient DNA from the Giza workers' cemetery will show

material) encoding of Richat. Detection of Saharan material would not falsify the relational 
encoding claim, which concerns design and orientation, not stone origin.
 Prediction 5 (New Discoveries): If the Hawass corridor (once peer-reviewed) contains 
primary 4th Dynasty royal burial goods or Khufu's remains, the cosmological-encoding reading 
of that specific feature is weakened.

the primary structure rather than its reuse history. The existing Liritzis and Vafiadou (2015) 
results on stone elements are consistent with a pre-4th-Dynasty construction date; targeted 
wall-surface sampling at multiple depths would substantially reduce the uncertainty that 
groundwater exposure introduces into current results. Without this test, no claim about the 
shaft’s construction date — in either direction — rests on direct structural evidence.
Falsified by: surface luminescence dating of primary untouched rock-cut surfaces at all three 
levels of the Osiris Shaft — specifically of wall faces independent of artifact contexts — 
yielding ages firmly and consistently posterior to the construction of Khafre’s causeway above 
them, demonstrating that the shaft was excavated after the surface monuments were complete 
rather than before or alongside them.
Conclusion
The Great Pyramid of Giza is the most studied structure in human history. Every civilisation that 
encountered it asked the same question: what is this for?
This paper has proposed a different kind of answer. The pyramid is not primarily a tomb. It is the 
permanent architectural encoding of a cosmological system that began in the Green Sahara during the 
African Humid Period (Salem et al. 2025), was carried through a documented migration corridor, was 
transmitted through the Shemsu Hor, and was finally expressed at its definitive scale by the 4th 
Dynasty Egyptian state.
But the argument of this paper does not rest on that origin story alone. It rests on a set of empirical 
features — causeway orientations organised about the east–west axis (Nell & Ruggles 2014), 
Heliopolis alignment maintained even when invisible (Lehner 1985; Magli 2009), integrated landscape 
planning (Lehner 1985), material-theological convergences (Shaw 2000; Belmonte et al. 2016), and 
2000-year cultural continuity (Magli 2011) — that collectively constitute a constraint filter. To these the 
present analysis adds two results of a different character, in that each was constructed so that it could 
have failed. The sixty-fourths outperform their neighbouring grids at the Great Pyramid and at almost 
no other pyramid, against a random mean that is negative; and the built base of 440 stands at the top 
of its range for the documented Egyptian targets and in the middle of it for shadow targets of the same 
physical kind. Neither result demonstrates intent, which no arithmetical test can reach. Both narrow 
what else the dimensions could plausibly be. Any viable interpretation of Giza must account for these 
features simultaneously. The Zep Tepi model passes through this filter intact, without internal 
contradiction, using a single coherent framework.
The field is invited to propose alternative models that also pass through this constraint filter. 
Until such models are advanced, the Zep Tepi framework remains, to the knowledge of this author, the 
only interpretation known to this author that accounts, within its declared scope, for the set of empirical 
constraints assembled here as a unified framework.

===== PAGE 66 =====
The pyramid was not built to last forever because it contained something valuable. It was built to last 
forever because it was something valuable — the permanent recreation of the First Time, the moment 
when the ancestors' world was whole, before the flood of sand took it away. As long as the pyramid 
stands, the Zep Tepi is not lost. The knowledge that generations of visitors sensed in the empty 
chambers was not concealed inside. It is the form they were standing within. On this reading, the 
pyramid is not a container for knowledge. It is the knowledge, made stone.
The site was not called Giza in the Old Kingdom. That name is medieval Arabic. The ancient 
Egyptians called this plateau Imentet — The West — and Rostau — the Entrance to the Passages of 
Osiris. Read together, those two names are the thesis of this paper and of this series. Imentet: the 
knowledge encoded here was carried from the west, along a documented corridor from the Green 
Sahara, across three thousand years of institutional memory. Rostau: it was deposited at the entrance 
to an architectural system designed to translate that knowledge into permanent form — subterranean, 
cosmological, imperishable. The causeways point west. The chambers descend underground. The 
external dimensions encode the sky, the earth, and the long cycles of time. The ancient Egyptians 
named their monument after where it came from and what it was built to do. No further decipherment 
was required. They left the answer at the entrance.
The Osireion raises a question this paper cannot answer. Its dating is contested. Egyptological 
consensus attributes it to Seti I of the 19th Dynasty, on the cartouche in the approach corridor, quarry 
records from year six of his reign, and its shared axis with his temple (Frankfort, de Buck & Gunn 
1933; Brand 2000). The evidence is more equivocal than that summary suggests. The cartouche 
occurs in the entrance passage; none of the main interior blocks carries any inscription, and Frankfort 
himself observed that the structure’s style does not match known work of Seti’s reign — an 
observation Petrie had already made of its archaic character. Two readings follow. Either Seti I 
incorporated an existing structure into his complex, or he commissioned it deliberately in an archaic 
idiom in order to evoke older religious conceptions of water and rebirth. The second reading is the 
more economical, and it is the more consequential for the argument advanced here: deliberate 
archaism is not the absence of transmitted memory but its expression, a builder reaching for a 
grammar he knew to be ancient. A second position places the original structure in the Middle 
Kingdom, and it rests on physical dating rather than on textual attribution alone. Surface luminescence 
dating of Osireion samples by Liritzis and Vafiadou (2015) — the same study that dated the Osiris 
Shaft at Giza, cited above — returns Middle Kingdom ages for the granite together with a younger 
date for the sandstone, which the authors read as indicating original and later architectural phases. 
Strabo’s identification of the builder as Ismandes (Geography XVII), conventionally read as 
Amenemhet III, is consistent with that reading, though Strabo himself writes with evident uncertainty 
and the structure’s origins appear already to have been obscure in his day. On this account the 
Osireion is a two-phase monument: a Middle Kingdom original, restored and inscribed by Seti I some 
seven centuries later. On either account the builder was encoding the same Zep Tepi grammar that 
had been encoded at Giza, and knew what he was building. On the consensus dating the Osireion is 
itself evidence that the tradition survived the Second Intermediate Period, when Egyptian institutional 
continuity dissolved for over two centuries; on the minority dating it precedes that rupture, and the 
question becomes what carried the cosmological grammar through the silence between 1800 and 
1550 BCE. Either way, when the tradition appears in the New Kingdom — in the stellar theology of the 
Amduat, in the Books of the Underworld, in the astronomical ceilings of the New Kingdom tombs — 
the question of what form it had taken, and what had been lost in the crossing, remains open. 
These questions lie beyond the scope of this paper.
The Pyramid as Memory closes the question of what the pyramid is. 
What its bare chambers were for, is the subject of the next paper: From Nun to the Stars.
