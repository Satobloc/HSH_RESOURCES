Topological Causation in Lattice QCD:
A Reading of the Field’s Own Results
Dirk Heaalang
Winged Chariot Laboratory
June 21, 2026
Revised Aug 16, 2026 (R6)
“It is fun to speculate about the way quarks would behave if they were physical particles
of finite mass (instead of purely mathematical entities as they would be in the limit of infinite
mass).” — M. Gell-Mann, Phys. Lett. 8, 214 (1964)
Abstract
We note a recurring pattern in the lattice QCD literature concerning the mechanism of
color confinement and the status of topological charge. The field’s operational practice—causal
experiments of vortex removal and vortex-limited Wilson loops, continuum extrapolations,
action-independence checks, and algorithmic responses that navigate rather than dissolve
topology—treats center vortices and topological charge as physical features of the theory.
Its interpretive language, however, continues in places to frame the same structures with a
residual provisionality. The field’s canonical review of the confinement program, published in
2003, already located the real argument for the physical content of center projection in the
empirical correlations between lattice-identified objects and gauge-invariant observables. In
the two decades since, the empirical results have continued to accumulate and strengthen.
The same structural mismatch between operational practice and interpretive framing appears,
in a different register, in the contemporary literature on topological freezing. The passages
examined below are selected as clear illustrations of the language under discussion; they
are not offered as a statistically representative sample of the full literature. The pattern
is documented on the field’s own terms, in the field’s own notation, and in three distinct
vocabularies. The contribution of the present work is the explicit identification of this
practice/rhetoric mismatch. The interpretive question is left to the field.
1 Introduction
The lattice formulation of quantum chromodynamics provides a non-perturbative regulator
that renders the theory calculable. Space-time is discretized into a grid with spacing a, and
physical results are recovered in the continuum limit a→ 0. Within this framework, numerical
simulations have identified specific topological structures—center vortices and topological charge—
that correlate with the non-perturbative phenomena of quark confinement and chiral symmetry
breaking.
The question of whether these structures are physical features of the theory or artifacts of
the discretization has been addressed repeatedly in the literature. The present work does not
propose a new framework, introduce new mathematics, or take any particular stance on their

physical status. It notes a pattern: the field’s operational practice has long treated the topological
structures as physical—via the causal experiments, continuum limits, and action-independence
tests that constitute its standard methodology—while its interpretive language has, in places,
continued to qualify those same structures as though their status were still provisional. A
conclusion stated by the field’s canonical review in 2003 has been continuously supported by
subsequent empirical work.
What follows is strictly a reading of the field’s own materials. Section 2 establishes the relevant
mathematics in standard notation. Section 3 presents the argument as the canonical review itself
stated it in 2003, together with the pattern of interpretive language that has persisted since, while
distinguishing obligatory technical caveats from any residual interpretive gap. Section 4 examines
the contemporary literature on topological freezing as a second, contemporary illustration of
the same structural mismatch. Section 5 names the cumulative pattern—the mismatch between
operational practice and interpretive rhetoric—addresses the universality objection, and draws
the methodological consequence that follows from the field’s own vocabulary of “tricks.” Section 6
states precisely what has been shown and what has not.

2 Mathematical Preliminaries
2.1 The Wilson loop and the area law
In lattice gauge theory the fundamental gauge-invariant observable is the Wilson loop

expectation value in the confined phase obeys an area law:
⟨W(C)⟩∼e −σA,(2)
where σ is the asymptotic string tension. This linear potential is the lattice signature of quark
confinement: the energy required to separate a quark–antiquark pair grows with distance,
preventing isolation of color charges.
The string tension√σ is a physical quantity that can be determined from lattice simulations
and extrapolated to the continuum. Its persistence across different lattice actions and into the
continuum limit is a central empirical fact of the confinement program.
2.2 Center projection and P-vortices
To isolate the degrees of freedom responsible for the area law, the link variables Uµ(x) are fixed
to a maximal center gauge and projected onto the centerZ N of the gauge group:
Uµ(x) =Z µ(x)Vµ(x), Z µ(x)∈Z N.(3)

The projected configuration Zµ(x) identifies the locations of thin center vortices (P-vortices)
on the dual lattice. The center-projected Wilson loop Z(C), evaluated on the projected links
alone, reproduces the asymptotic string tension of the full theory to within a few percent. This
phenomenon is known as center dominance and has been reproduced across multiple gauge-fixing
procedures and lattice actions since the late 1990s.
2.3 Vortex-removed and vortex-limited configurations
The causal role of the identified vortices is tested by two complementary constructions. The
vortex-removed configuration is obtained by
Vµ(x) =Z∗

Wilson loops evaluated on Vµ(x) exhibit a perimeter law rather than an area law: removal
of the P-vortex content destroys the confining behavior of the original ensemble. Conversely,
vortex-limited loops Wn(C) are evaluated on configurations through which a definite number n
of P-vortices passes. These restricted loops recover the area-law dependence on n predicted by a
vortex picture of confinement.
These experiments—vortex removal destroying confinement and vortex limitation restoring it—
constitute the empirical core of the lattice argument that center vortices are causally responsible
for the area law.

gradient-flow time, small instantons may fall through the lattice, and strict integer-valuedness
requires an admissibility condition. These are genuine practical difficulties of the finite- a theory.
In the continuum limit the ambiguities are removed. Gradient-flow definitions of the topolog-
ical charge become unambiguous as a→ 0, and continuum-extrapolated values of the topological
susceptibility χt =⟨Q2⟩/V are standard (see, e.g., the FLAG reviews [ 12]). Once continuum-
extrapolated, χt is a physical, gauge-invariant observable that enters the Witten–Veneziano
relation and contributes to the η′ mass. No serious claim has been advanced that the continuum
topological charge is an algorithmic illusion.
Standard local-update Monte Carlo algorithms sample gauge configurations by a Markov
process. The efficiency of the process for the topological charge is characterized by an integrated
autocorrelation time τQ, which grows sharply as the lattice spacing is reduced. As a→ 0, the
relation
τQ∼a−z (6)
holds with a positive exponent z that is empirically large; in standard hybrid Monte Carlo z≈ 5.
The Markov chain becomes progressively less able to move between topological sectors, and in
the approach to the continuum limit it remains trapped in a single sector for the duration of the
simulation. This phenomenon is known as topological freezing. Algorithmic responses including

freezing. These responses improve the sampling of topological sectors without dissolving the
topological structure itself; the topology remains a feature that the improved algorithms must
navigate.
3 The Argument as the Canonical Review Stated It
The lattice case for the causal role of center vortices in confinement rests on the body of vortex-
removal and vortex-limited experiments accumulated over more than two decades. We summarize
the load-bearing results in the field’s own notation and note a recurring feature of the interpretive
language used to describe them. The passages examined are selected as clear illustrations of the
language under discussion; they are not offered as a statistically representative sample of the full
literature.
3.1 The empirical claim
The decomposition (3) and the vortex-removed construction (4) of Section 2 define the experimen-
tal apparatus. The empirical results obtained with it are threefold. First, center dominance: the
center-projected Wilson loop Z(C), evaluated on the projected configuration alone, reproduces
the asymptotic string tension of the full theory to within a few percent, a result reported in
the late 1990s and reproduced since across multiple gauge-fixing procedures and lattice actions.
Second, vortex removal: Wilson loops evaluated on the vortex-removed configuration Vµ(x)
exhibit a perimeter law rather than an area law, so that removal of the P-vortex content destroys
the confining behavior of the original ensemble. Third, vortex limitation: loops Wn(C), restricted
to configurations through which a definite number n of P-vortices passes, exhibit the area-law
dependence on n predicted by a vortex picture of confinement. These are the experiments which
have for two decades anchored the lattice argument that center vortices are causally responsible
for the area law.
3.2 The interpretive language
The interpretive language with which the field describes the objects identified by center projection
is consistent across decades and across independent authors in one respect, and heterogeneous in
another. Representative passages illustrate the range.
The original reports of center dominance characterize the result in directly causal terms [ 1, 2]:
We find that center vortices identified in the vacuum state account for the full
asymptotic string tension.
The review which superseded them characterizes the same result in more guarded terms [ 3]:
We review lattice evidence showing that center-vortex condensation is a serious
candidate for the mechanism of colour confinement in quantum chromodynamics.
The canonical review of the field’s confinement program introduces the same body of results
with stronger language [4]:
[T]he prevailing view is that quark confinement is the work of some special class
of gauge field configurations—candidates have included instantons, merons, abelian

monopoles, and center vortices—which for some reason dominate the QCD vacuum on
large distance scales. . . the underlying mechanism of quark confinement, via definite
classes of field configurations, is open to numerical investigation.
The same review, in a methodologically careful aside, draws a distinction between the
lattice-identified objects and the physical objects they are taken to represent [4]:
It is obvious that the action of thin vortices, stable or not, is singular in the contin-
uum limit, and their contribution to the functional integral can be neglected. The
configurations of physical interest are vortices which have some finite thicknessdin
physical units.
A contemporary paper from the most active vortex-causation group restates a related two-level
distinction [10] (internal citations omitted):
Although the identified thin vortices are gauge dependent, they do correlate with the
physical thick vortices and it is possible to learn about the latter by studying the
former.
Part of this two-level language is technically obligatory. Center projection is a gauge-fixing
procedure; the resulting P-vortices are gauge-dependent by construction. Any careful paper
must register the distinction between the gauge-fixed identification and the gauge-invariant
physics it is taken to locate. The 2024 formulation just quoted is a clear instance of this
obligatory bookkeeping; the same paper’s title (“Numerical indication that center vortices drive
dynamical mass generation in QCD”) already states the causal claim without hesitation. Not
every restatement of the thin/thick distinction is therefore evidence that the relationship between
the two levels remains open.
What remains visible, however, is a recurring tendency in some of the literature to present the
empirical correlations themselves in language that continues to treat the physical status of the
underlying configurations as more provisional than the 2003Critiquehad already allowed. The
real argument, according to thatCritique, resides in the causal experiments and the correlations
with gauge-invariant observables.
3.3 The argument as the canonical review states it
The most direct statement of the position the field has arrived at appears not in any of the
affirmative passages above, but in the section of the canonical review explicitly devoted to
objections. In Section 6.7, titled simplyCritique, the author of the review reviews the standard
methodological complaints against the center-projection program—the gauge-fixing dependence
of the projection, the variation between adjoint gauges, the sensitivity to Gribov copies—and
writes the following [4]:
[T]he real argument that center projection locates vortex configurations is at present
empirical; namely, the correlation of P-vortices with gauge-invariant observables, as
seen in the results for vortex-limited Wilson loops Wn(C), and the observables of
vortex-removed configurationsV µ =Z∗
µUµ.
Three features of this passage are salient. First, the author of the field’s canonical review
identifies the real argument for the physical content of center projection as residing in the
causal experiments and in the correlation of the lattice-identified objects with gauge-invariant

observables. Second, this statement appears in the section devoted to addressing methodological
objections; it is not an enthusiastic claim made in passing but a considered statement of where
the case rests after the objections have been heard. Third, the passage was published in 2003.
It has not been subsequently retracted, qualified, or revised in the literature that draws on the
review.
The position the field’s canonical review reached two decades ago is therefore the following:
the gauge-fixing apparatus of center projection is a calculational scheme whose own structural
features are not the object of physical claims; the physical claim is that this scheme correlates
with gauge-invariant observables and with causal experiments in a manner that licenses inference
about the underlying configurations. This is a structurally careful position. We do not contest it.
3.4 A converging statement from outside the projection program
The position arrived at in the canonical review is not isolated. J. M. Cornwall, writing in 2009
from outside the center-projection methodological tradition, reaches the same conclusion in
directly substantive terms [6]:
Center vortices have been around for more than thirty years, well-confirmed on the
lattice, and very successful in explaining the basics of confinement. . . basic confinement
in the center vortex picture is topological, coming from gluonic solitons where the
gluons have no direct coupling to the Wilson loop, and makes no reference to any
particular surface.
This passage commits to the topological character of the confinement mechanism without the
intermediate gauge-fixing apparatus that has been the subject of methodological caution in
the projection literature. The substantive content of the position the canonical review reached
through its empirical argument is the substantive content of the position Cornwall articulates
directly. The two routes converge on a single claim about the underlying mechanism.
3.5 Noted tension
The pattern we note in the present section is therefore narrower than a blanket claim of non-uptake.
The lattice case for the causal role of center vortices rests on two decades of vortex-removal
and vortex-limited experiments. In 2003 the field’s canonical review, in a section devoted to
methodological objections, located the real argument for the physical content of center projection
precisely in those experiments and in the correlations with gauge-invariant observables.
Some of the two-level language that has persisted since is the ordinary and necessary distinction
between gauge-fixed identifications and gauge-invariant physics; that distinction does not dissolve
and is not, by itself, a failure to integrate the 2003 conclusion. Other formulations, however,
continue to present the relationship between the lattice-identified objects and the physical
configurations as more open than the empirical case—and the 2003 statement of where that case
rests—already supports.
We note this more limited tension. We do not, in this section, draw further conclusions
from it. Section 4 develops the analogous pattern in the contemporary literature on topological
freezing. The cumulative observation across both domains is the object of Section 5.

4 Illustration: Topological Freezing and Its Algorithmic Re-
sponses
The vortex-causation evidence summarized in Section 3 is now more than two decades old. The
literature on topological freezing, which has intensified since approximately 2017 and remains
active in 2024, provides a contemporary illustration of a related structural pattern. It is important
to be precise about the epistemic status of the quantities involved. Unlike the identification of
center vortices, the physical reality of the continuum topological charge and of χt has never been
seriously contested: χt is gauge-invariant, enters the Witten–Veneziano relation, and contributes
to the η′ mass. The present section therefore does not adjudicate any question of physical status;
it illustrates, in a different and more recent register, the same mismatch between operational
practice and interpretive framing already visible in the vortex literature.
4.1 The phenomenon
As the continuum limit is approached ( a→ 0), the autocorrelation time τQ associated with
the topological charge diverges. Standard Monte Carlo algorithms become trapped in a single
topological sector and are unable to tunnel between sectors with different values of Q. This
topological freezing constitutes a strict algorithmic obstruction. Responses such as parallel
tempering on boundary conditions (PTBC) have been developed to mitigate the freezing. These
responses improve sampling without dissolving the topological structure itself; the topology
remains a feature that the improved algorithms must still navigate.
4.2 The interpretive language
A recent PTBC study of scale setting in SU (N) Yang–Mills theory illustrates the pattern. In its
conclusions the authors write [11]:
Our preliminary analysis shows that, in the presence of a completely frozen topology,
t0 receives a positive bias with respect to the actual value obtained with a properly
sampled topological charge.
Three phrases in this single sentence do substantive work. “Completely frozen topology” treats
the topology as a feature that can be frozen or unfrozen. “The actual value” presupposes that
there exists a fact of the matter independent of any particular sampling. “Properly sampled
topological charge” presupposes that sampling can be done correctly or incorrectly with respect
to that fact. The sentence as a whole therefore commits, implicitly but unambiguously, to the
topological charge being a real physical quantity that possesses values and that can be sampled
properly or improperly. The same paper frames the PTBC method throughout as an algorithmic
improvement that addresses a “well-known problem of standard algorithms in the sampling of the
topological modes.” Both registers—substantive commitment to a physical topological feature
and algorithmic framing of the difficulty—operate simultaneously.
4.3 The phrase “properly sampled”
The commitment implicit in “properly sampled” is significant. One cannot sample an algorithmic
illusion properly or improperly; one can only sample a quantity that possesses an independent
ground truth. By identifying their result as a bias relative to the actual value obtained with proper
7

===== PAGE 8 =====
sampling, the authors indicate that they are attempting to measure a real, independent quantity.
A practitioner may reply that proper sampling refers only to ergodicity of the Markov chain
with respect to the lattice-defined ensemble at fixed a, a reading which requires no commitment
beyond the mathematics of the algorithm. The reply is correct as far as it goes; the weight of
the observation accordingly rests not on this phrase alone but on the broader practice/rhetoric
mismatch developed in Section 5, to which the phrase is a contemporary witness.
4.4 Illustration noted
The contemporary topological-freezing literature therefore exhibits, in a different register, the
same structural pattern documented in Section 3 for the vortex-causation literature. In both
cases the substantive content of the work treats a topological feature of the theory as something
that can be sampled correctly or incorrectly, or whose causal role can be tested by removal and
restoration experiments, while the interpretive language continues in places to frame the feature
in primarily methodological or provisional terms. Two areas of lattice research, separated by two
decades, exhibit a related pattern. Section 5 names that pattern and draws its methodological
consequence.
5 The Pattern and Its Implications
We have noted, in Section 3, that the case for the causal role of center vortices in confinement
was stated by the field’s canonical review in 2003, in a section explicitly devoted to addressing
methodological objections, in terms which locate the real argument in the empirical correlations
between the lattice-identified objects and gauge-invariant observables. We have noted, in Section 4,
that the contemporary literature on topological freezing exhibits a related structural pattern in a
different register. We turn now to that pattern.
5.1 The methodological vocabulary and the universality response
A familiar word is used, across the lattice literature, to characterize the methodological status of
the lattice itself: it is atrick. The word is also used, in the technical vocabulary of theoretical
physics, of Wick rotation, of dimensional regularization, of the Faddeev–Popov procedure, of
the iϵ prescription. In those uses the word is precise and unobjectionable. Each refers to a
calculational device whose structural features are introduced to make a problem tractable and
whose specific structural features do not survive into the final answer. One rotates time into the
imaginary axis, computes, rotates back; the imaginary time is not present in the result. One
treats the spacetime dimension as a continuous variable, isolates the divergence, takes the limit;
the non-integer dimension is not present in the result. The trick, in each case, vanishes when the
calculation is complete.
The lattice’s topological content does not vanish. To wit: the topological charge Q, the
topological susceptibility χt, the causal role of center vortices in the area law, the persistence
of√σ across independent actions and into the continuum limit, the algorithmic obstruction
posed by topological freezing as a→ 0—none of these is a structural feature of any particular

discretization that disappears when the discretization is removed. 1They are features that the
discretization makes accessible to computation, and they remain in the answer. By the criterion
implicit in the field’s standard use of “trick” for Wick rotation and its kin, that which survives
the removal of the calculational scaffolding is not part of the scaffolding.
A lattice practitioner will immediately recognize this criterion as the operational definition of
universality. The Wilsonian program is organized precisely around the survival test: one varies
the discretization (action, spacing, boundary conditions), takes the continuum limit, and retains
only those features that persist independently of the particular scaffolding. Action-independence
of the string tension, continuum extrapolation of χt, and the persistence of center dominance
across gauges are the field’s own implementations of the test. The referee’s one-line reply writes
itself: the author has rediscovered why we take the continuum limit.
We concede the point without reservation. The survival criterion articulated above is not
a novel methodological insight; it is the field’s standard practice. The contribution of the
present work therefore cannot lie in the criterion itself. It lies in the observation that the field’s
interpretive register has not, in every respect, kept pace with its operational practice.
Operationally, the field treats the topological structures as physical. It designs causal
experiments (vortex removal, vortex-limited loops) whose outcomes are interpreted as evidence
of causal responsibility for the area law. It invests in algorithmic responses (PTBC, open
boundaries) that navigate rather than dissolve the topology. It reports continuum limits and
action-independent results for quantities whose physical content is thereby established by the
very universality tests the field applies. Rhetorically, the same literature continues in places to
frame the lattice-identified objects with a residual provisionality, or to characterize the lattice
itself as a “trick” even while its topological content survives the removal of the scaffolding. The
practice/rhetoric mismatch is the pattern documented in Sections 3 and 4; the methodological
vocabulary of “trick” merely restates it in a third register.
The vocabulary of instrumental deferral is older than the lattice itself. The 1972 proceedings
contribution in which the color-octet gluon scheme later named QCD was first proposed opens
by describing a field-theory model of the hadrons that its authors “don’t fully believe,” titles its
second section “Fictitious Quarks and “Gluons’ and Their Statistics,” and closes by proposing a
complete theory of hadrons built on constituents “treated as fictitious” [14]. The same document
cites the SLAC deep-inelastic results as its motivation, and grounds the fictitious framing in the
assumption that quarks are “permanently bound inside the mesons and baryons,” and refers to
the nine colored quarks as “mathematical entities.” Unlike the imaginary time of Wick rotation,
the entities did not vanish from the answer; the mechanism of their permanent binding is the
topological structure whose causal status the preceding sections document. We note this history
as register, not as evidence; it predates the corpus examined here.

The pattern across these three statements—Greensite (2003), Bonanno et al. (LATTICE2024),
and Creutz (2021)—is consistent in its substantive content and uneven in its interpretive
articulation. The substantive content is that the topological structure of QCD, as manifested on
the lattice and surviving into the continuum, is a feature of the theory whose physical relevance
is established by the empirical correlations, causal experiments, and continuum extrapolations
that the field has been accumulating. The interpretive articulation has, in places, continued
to qualify this content in provisional terms. The recurrence of that residual gap, in the face of
operational practice that has long since settled the relevant questions, is the pattern we have
been noting.
5.3 An observation in closing
We close with one further observation, addressed to the lattice community in particular. The
conclusion the canonical review reached in 2003 is the conclusion the empirical work since 2003
has continued to support and strengthen. The vortex-removal experiments are more refined; the

action-independence is more thoroughly established; the algorithmic responses to topological
freezing now demonstrate that the topology must be navigated rather than dissolved. The
argument has not weakened. The operational practice has been consistent with that conclusion
for two decades. What remains, in places, is for the interpretive register to catch up. We note, in
the spirit of recovery rather than imposition, that the conclusion the canonical review’s empirical
case supported is available to the field on its own terms.
6 Conclusions
What we believe has been shown is the following. The lattice literature contains a body of
empirical results—vortex-causation experiments accumulated since the late 1990s, contemporary
work on topological freezing and its algorithmic responses—whose interpretive consequences were
stated by the field’s canonical review in 2003. The subsequent literature has not, in every respect,
integrated those consequences into its interpretive framing in a manner fully consistent with
the strength of its own operational practice. This pattern is documented in Sections 3 and 4
and named, together with its methodological consequence and the universality concession, in
Section 5. The pattern appears across two decades and two areas of lattice research. Its lasting
formulation is the meta-observation that the field’s interpretive register has not, in every respect,
kept pace with its operational practice.
What has not been shown is the following. No specific interpretive commitment beyond the
operational evidence has been asserted as correct. No claim has been made that the standard
interpretation is wrong in a manner that affects calculational outputs. No extension has been
proposed beyond the domain of QCD. The survival criterion of Section 5.1 is conceded to be the
field’s own universality practice; the contribution is the identification of the residual mismatch
between that practice and some of the language in which the results continue to be framed.
The interpretive question is left to the field.
