Phenomena 
1.1 The explanatory question 
Modern physics turns force, mass, energy, field strength, and curvature into compact quantitative 
relations. That success leaves a second question open: which parts of an observed form arise from the 
source, spatial or spacetime structure, inherited state, boundaries, and the interaction presently acting? A

indispensable; explanatory decompression asks what roles were combined and whether the data can

Newtonian gravitation illustrates the issue. In F = Gm₁m₂/r², the masses are sources and responses, r 
expresses spatial relation, inverse-square behavior reflects three -dimensional point-source spreading, and

decomposition defines a constrained space rather than independent causes. The same discipline matters 
when a fitted effective coupling could represent geometry, response, source error, or a mixture. 
1.2 A common family of problems 
The examples are deliberately varied: circular phase closure, spherical point -source spreading, 
constrained transport, inertial continuation, galaxy dynamics, and cosmological sequences. They share 
one structure. A law usually admits a family of possibilities; geometry, source distribution, inherited state, 
boundaries, and present interaction jointly select a realized configuration. Historical claims add a further 
requirement: reconstructed states must be embeddable in at least one lawful sequence. 
1.3 From privileged geometric forms to geometry-relative free motion 
Greek astronomy and natural philosophy often treated the circle and sphere as privileged forms of 
perfection and regularity (Aristotle 1984). Modern physics retained their mathematical value while 
replacing perfection with symmetry,  invariance, and model -dependent geometry. A circle expresses 
centered rotational symmetry; a sphere expresses isotropy about a point; a straight line expresses 
translational uniformity in Euclidean space. Relativity generalizes free continuation to geodesic motion, 
whose coordinate form depends on the metric. The historical shift motivates a methodological rule: 
geometric form should be read through the symmetry, constraint, and theory that generate it, not through 
a universal hierarchy of shapes.

2. Physical Form as an Explanatory Target 
2.1 A working definition of physical form 
The phrase 'physical form' is used here in a deliberately limited sense. It does not invoke a Platonic 
realm of forms, nor does it reintroduce an Aristotelian substantial form as an independent ontological 
entity. It refers to the organized spatial, temporal, dynamical, or macroscopic manner in which  a physical 
system is realized. A spherically symmetric electric field, a periodic oscillation, a quasi -one-dimensional 
current, a temperature gradient, uniform rectilinear motion, and geodesic free fall are all examples of 
physical form in this working sense. 
The advantage of this notion is that it identifies an explanandum lying between ontology and 
numerical prediction. A law may tell us how quantities are related, but the observed phenomenon also has 
a shape , symmetry, dimensionality, temporal organization, and scale -dependent regime. Asking why a 
point-source field is spherical, why a thin conductor supports approximately one-dimensional transport, or

why a lawful process is realized in a specific form.

fitted coefficient may be a downstream proxy for geometry rather than a fundamental parameter. The 
relevant question is therefore not merely which quantity changes with the outcome, but what causal or 
explanatory authority that quantity possesses in the model.

2.4 Role-specific causal audit 
Four questions discipline the use of the framework. First, a formal -admissibility question asks 
whether a proposed decomposition is algebraically, dimensionally, and theoretically legitimate. Second, a 
geometrical-generation question asks whether the specified geometry can actually generate  the claimed 
change in the chosen observable, rather than merely correlate with it. Third, a sequential -historical 
question asks whether inherited state or an ordered pathway is necessary to obtain the realized form. 
Fourth, an empirical-residual question asks whether a deviation remains after geometry, source structure, 
boundaries, propagation, and measurement systematics have been modeled. This role -based audit is 
consistent with interventionist and structural approaches to causal explanation, while remaining 
deliberately less formal than a complete structural causal model (Pearl 2009; Woodward 2003). 
The same audit also requires role classification. A high -impact quantity may be a direct driver, a 
mediator, a moderator, a proxy, a confounded indicator, or a reverse -causal signal. This distinction 
becomes decisive in Chapter 5: 𝐺eff and 𝜀𝑔,eff can be fitted from data, but under the geometry -only 
hypothesis they are downstream recompressions of 𝐽𝛤  and therefore do not automatically possess 
fundamental causal authority. 
2.5 Background dimension and effective dimension 
A central distinction concerns ambient dimension and the effective dimension of a process. A wire is 
a three -dimensional material body, but when its length greatly exceeds its transverse scale and the 
relevant observable is the macroscopic axial current, it can be modeled as a one -dimensional transport 
system. A thin film is embedded in three -dimensional space but may support approximately two -
dimensional collective behavior. A particle constrained to a sphere has two positional degrees of freedom 
even though the sphere is embedded in three-dimensional Euclidean space.

3. Circle, Sphere, and Line: Distinct Forms of Geometric 
Uniformity 
3.1 Symmetry and dimensional role 
The circle, sphere, and line express different invariances. A circle is uniform under rotations about a 
center; a sphere is isotropic under three -dimensional rotations about a point; a Euclidean line is 
homogeneous under translation. These claims concern relational structure, not a metaphysical  ranking of 
shapes. The same curve can also occupy several dimensional descriptions: a circle is embedded in a two -
dimensional plane, has a one -dimensional constrained configuration space S¹ , and may be sampled 
through a still lower -dimensional observable. Embedding dimension, intrinsic dimension, constraint 
dimension, and effective transport dimension must therefore be stated separately. 
3.2 Limits and approximations 
A straight line can arise as the zero -curvature limit of a circle because κ = 1/R → 0 as R → ∞. It can 
also arise locally from a smooth curve or effectively when boundaries suppress transverse degrees of 
freedom. These are distinct mechanisms and should not be collapsed into the claim that every line is 
literally a circle of infinite radius. Likewise, projection or coarse -graining may hide variation without 
changing the underlying geometry. The methodological lesson is to identify which dimension, symmetry, 
and limiting operation support the form used in an equation.

4.3 Geometry and coupling scale 
A physical coefficient often combines geometrical normalization with a coupling scale. In Coulomb's 
law, 4π expresses spherical normalization while ε₀ participates in the chosen representation of 
electromagnetic coupling. In Newtonian gravitation, the inverse -square form reflects spherical spreading 
while G sets the strength with which mass sources gravitational effects. The heuristic distinction can be 
expressed as follows: geometry primarily constrains the form of distribution; coupling constants set the 
scale of interaction. Yet standard notation may package these roles together. A structure -explicit 
reformulation can separate them for explanatory purposes without claiming that the separated factors are 
independent physical entities. The distinction is also not absolute, because constants such as c and ħ 
organize the structure of whole theories rather than merely multiplying a fixed law.

General relativity replaces the Newtonian scalar relation by 𝐺𝜇𝜈  +  𝛬𝑔𝜇𝜈  =  (8𝜋𝐺/𝑐4)𝑇𝜇𝜈 (Einstein 
1915, 1916; Wald 1984). The factor 8π is not the area of a four -dimensional sphere and its factor of two 
does not encode the addition of time. Its normalization follows from the Einstein tensor, the contracted 
Bianchi identity, conventions, and recovery of Newtonian gravity in the weak -field limit. Numerical 
factors acquire meaning from their equations and limiting relations, not from free geometrical analogy. 
4.5 Compact notation and explanatory decompression 
Standard physical equations are optimized for calculation, comparison, and communication. Their 
compactness is indispensable, but it can obscure the distinct origins of their terms. A single coefficient 
may incorporate a geometrical normalization and a coupling strength; a simple point -particle law may 
suppress the field equation from which it is derived; and initial or boundary conditions may be treated as 
external data even though they help determine the realized phenomenon. This paper calls  the recovery of 
these roles explanatory decompression. 
Explanatory decompression is not a proposal to replace standard notation. It is a supplementary 
representation in which geometrical factors, source terms, response variables, coupling scales, inherited 
states, and boundary assumptions are made explicit when doing so clarifies their explanatory functions. 
The criterion of success is not algebraic novelty but improved discrimination: the reformulation should 
show which features are geometrical, which are dynamical, which are constitutive, and which are 
idealized or suppressed. 
Standard equations compress; structural analysis decompresses.

5.11 Methodological contribution of the generative simulation 
The experiment moves the paper from a static parameter rewrite to a constrained generative test. 
Separating 𝜀𝑔0,𝑆 and 𝑇𝑅 is no longer merely a notational preference: once both are compressed into one

robustness, held-out prediction, and cross-direction observation. 
It also generates an observational design. If a real residual represents directional redistribution, 
equatorial enhancement should be accompanied by off -plane compensation and may covary with vertical 
dynamics, disk thickness, polar rings, stream precession, weak -lensing quadrupoles, or the evolution of 
source flattening. Adding more rotation curves in the same direction need not break the degeneracy 
among mass, 𝜀𝑔0,𝑆, isotropic extra mass, and 𝑇𝑅.

uses this closure, and it is not evidence that dark matter is absent.

6. Free Fields and Constrained Transport 
6.1 Two questions about the same charge 
The statements 'an isolated point charge has a spherically symmetric electrostatic field' and 'electric 
current flows along a wire' may seem to attribute incompatible natural forms to charge. The appearance of 
conflict results from conflating two different questions. The first concerns the spatial configuration of a 
field generated by an idealized source in a symmetric environment. The second concerns the macroscopic 
transport of many carriers through a material channel under an applied potential difference. 
6.2 Field dimension and transport dimension 
𝑑field ≠ 𝑑transport 
A thin wire is embedded in three-dimensional space. Its macroscopic current may be modeled as one-
dimensional, but its electric and magnetic fields remain three -dimensional. The magnetic field around an 
ideal straight current, for example, forms circles around the wire and exhibits axial symmetry. The 
dimensionality of the carrier flux is therefore not the dimensionality of the surrounding field. 
6.3 How a conductor produces quasi-one-dimensional current 
𝐉 = 𝜎𝐄 
In an Ohmic medium, the local current density J is approximately proportional to the electric field E. 
The geometry of the conductor, the distribution of surface charges, the material's conductivity, and the 
external circuit jointly establish an electric field with a dominant tangential component. Persistent

transverse transport is suppressed by the material boundary, whereas the longitudinal component 
accumulates into a macroscopic net current. 
𝐉 = 𝑛𝑞𝐯𝑑 
Microscopically, charge carriers do not travel along smooth straight lines. They undergo thermal 
motion and repeated scattering from lattice vibrations, impurities, and defects. The phrase 'current flows 
straight' refers to the direction of the averaged drift velocity, not to each microscopic trajectory. Random 
transverse components largely cancel at the macroscopic scale, and the boundary prevents a sustained net 
flux across the conductor surface. 
6.4 Boundary as a producer of effective form 
The conductor does not compress the entire electromagnetic field into a line. It constrains the 
effective degrees of freedom of material transport. A straight wire supports an approximately straight 
macroscopic current, a curved wire supports current along its curvature, and a ring supports a closed 
circulation. The geometry of the channel is thus not a decorative container added to a pre -existing one-
dimensional current; it helps constitute the current's macroscopic form. 
Spherical symmetry characterizes the free field of an ideal point source; 
linearity characterizes transport constrained by a slender material boundary. 
This case supports a broader philosophical point. Boundaries can be generative. They do not merely 
obstruct an otherwise natural behavior; by suppressing some degrees of freedom and stabilizing others, 
they make new effective regimes possible. Waveguides, thin films, cavities, and one -dimensional 
channels all exhibit this structure. 
7. Cosmology as a Natural Observational Sequence 
7.1 Why cosmology is a historical physics problem 
Cosmology provides a stronger test of the historical component of the framework than heat diffusion 
because the relevant process is not repeatable as a laboratory sequence. We do not observe the universe 
being rerun  from an early hot state to the present. Instead, contemporary cosmology reconstructs an 
ordered history from physically different traces associated with different epochs. Light -element 
abundances, relic radiation, large -scale structure, high -redshift objects, gravitational lensing, supernova 
distances, and other observables constrain different parts of one evolving system. The explanatory 
problem is therefore not only whether each observation can be fitted locally, but whether the inferred 
states can belong to one physically admissible history (Peebles 1993; Weinberg 2008; Planck 
Collaboration 2020).

7.2 Natural observational sequence 
For the present analysis, a natural observational sequence (NOS) is an ordered relation among 
reconstructed physical states, admissible transitions, and epoch -specific observables in a process that

viable bridge between the states required by two others. Such a failure is a sequence inconsistency, not 
merely a low score on an isolated datum. 
7.3 Historical transfer and state inheritance across cosmic epochs 
At a cosmological epoch, the sufficient state may include the scale factor, expansion rate, source 
content, perturbations, and ionization variables required by the model. Later states are constrained by the 
earlier state and transition law. This historical transfer may involve propagation, damping, growth, mode 
conversion, conservation, or changing observational access; it should not be identified automatically with 
a non-Markovian kernel. A late-time modification must remain compatible with the earlier stages through 
which the model claims the universe passed.

7.8 Hubble-constant tension as divergence between inference pathways 
The well-known disagreement between a CMB-based inference of the present Hubble constant under 
a specified cosmological model and late -universe distance-ladder determinations provides an instructive 
example of structural divergence (Planck Collaboration 2020; Riess et al. 2022). The useful point here is 
methodological rather than numerical. The CMB route does not directly observe today’s Hubble constant 
at recombination; it infers a present parameter by propagating information through a cosmological model. 
A distance ladder reaches the same target through a different observational chain. 
The discrepancy can therefore be represented schematically as two natural inference sequences 
terminating in estimates of one present-state quantity: 
𝑂CMB  →  𝑋rec  →  model transition history →  𝐻0,inferred, 
𝑂local  →  calibrated distance sequence →  𝐻0,local. 
A difference between the endpoints is diagnostically important, but it does not identify its own cause. 
Geometry, source content, calibration, observational selection, transition assumptions, or a genuinely 
different gravitational response can occupy different causal roles. Structural analysis asks where the 
sequences first become mutually inconsistent before granting the final scalar discrepancy fundamental 
causal authority.

velocity. Geometry specifies the form in which an already given inertial state continues; it does not by

For an actual body, its present momentum commonly results from an initial momentum together with

This motivates a more precise claim about state inheritance. Classical mechanics allows initial 
velocity to be specified at the boundary of a model without deriving it from an earlier force within that 
model, so no infinite explanatory regress is required. The philosophically careful statement is that a 
concrete inertial state is specified by initial data and is often, in real systems, the retained result of 
previous interactions. History matters because its dynamically relevant consequences are instantiated in 
the present state, not because a past force continues to act after it has ceased. 
8.4 No present force does not imply no causal history 
The absence of a present net force answers the question of why the velocity is not currently changing. 
It does not answer why that velocity has its particular magnitude and direction. These are distinct 
explanatory questions. Newton's first law addresses persistence; inherited state specifies the concrete 
motion at the chosen modeling boundary, and earlier interactions may explain how that state was formed. 
This distinction also prevents a mistaken opposition between law and history. History is not an 
exception to law, and law is not a replacement for history. Earlier interactions occurred lawfully and 
produced states that later laws continue to govern. The present inherited state is the interface through 
which historical contingency enters a law -governed evolution; in a genuinely sequential problem, the 
admissibility of the path to that state is a further explanatory requirement. 
8.5 First and second laws as different explanatory levels 
The second law specifies how force changes momentum. The first law specifies the inertial baseline 
and the persistence of motion when no net force acts. Although in elementary algebra one can set F = 0 in 
F = ma, the conceptual role of the first law is not exhausted by this substitution. It identifies inertial 
structure and distinguishes genuine acceleration from artifacts of non -inertial description. Within the 
present framework, the first law belongs primarily to the geometry and state-preservation layers, while the 
second law belongs to the current-interaction layer.

10.9 Why call the outcome a realized form? 
The term realized form emphasizes that the explanandum is more specific than the general law but 
more structured than a single number. It includes symmetry, dimensionality, topology, spatial distribution, 
temporal organization, historical reachability, and scale -dependent regime. The framework therefore sits 
between law-level description and point prediction: it asks how a lawful possibility becomes this field 
pattern, this transport regime, this worldline, or this cosmological history. 
The decomposition separates causal roles before recombining their physical 
effects; it does not claim that the world itself is separable into independent 
factors. 
11. Philosophical Implications: Structure, Sequence, and Realization 
11.1 Laws do not usually determine a unique concrete world-state 
The first implication concerns the relation between laws and realized histories. A law such as the heat 
equation, Maxwell's equations, the geodesic equation, or the cosmological field equations does not 
generally select one unique realized configuration without additional state and constraint information.

===== PAGE 45 =====
GEOMETRY, HISTORY, AND CONSTRAINT 
45 
Initial conditions and boundary or admissibility conditions are not optional afterthoughts if the 
explanatory target is a concrete phenomenon. In a historical process such as cosmology, a further question 
arises: whether the reconstructed states can be joined by one viable sequence. This observation does not 
weaken the law's universality; it clarifies what universality amounts to. 
Philosophical discussions of determinism have long distinguished the uniqueness of evolution from 
the specification of initial data (Earman 1986). The present framework extends that point from temporal 
determinism to the broader constitution of physical form. A unique evolution from a given state may 
coexist with multiple possible states, domains, and boundaries. 
11.2 Boundary conditions are explanatory, not merely computational 
The second implication is that boundary and admissibility conditions can carry genuine explanatory 
content. In the heat equation, insulation or fixed temperature changes the allowed energy exchange; in 
conductor problems, the boundary helps explain the effective dimension of transport. In cosmology there 
may be no material wall, but a model still defines an admissible space of states and transitions through its 
geometry, field content, conservation relations, matching assumptions, and regime of validity. It would be 
misleading to treat these restrictions as merely mathematical conveniences when they determine which 
physical histories the model allows. 
At the same time, a boundary condition can be effective rather than fundamental. A perfectly 
insulated wall is an idealization of suppressed microscopic exchange. The philosophical point is not that 
boundaries are ultimate entities but that the level of description determines which structural conditions are 
constitutive of the model. 
11.3 Historical attenuation, retention, and temporal structural continuity 
The past remains relevant through selectively retained or transformed state, not as an extra substance. 
Momentum can retain an earlier impulse, stress a manufacturing history, and cosmological perturbations 
information from earlier epochs. Dissipation erases some distinctions, conservation protects others, and 
coarse-graining changes  which variables remain explanatory. An explicit memory kernel and a 
Markovian augmented state are alternative representations when they encode the same sufficient 
information. The philosophical object is therefore not the total past but the inherited structure that links a 
present state to a lawful history. 
11.4 Physical laws and past-present-future sequence compatibility 
Let Hadm be the histories satisfying the governing laws and constraints. A deterministic model may 
select one continuation from a complete state; a stochastic model assigns a distribution; a reversible law 
can constrain past and future without selecting a thermodynamic arrow. Sequence compatibility therefore 
means that states invoked together must belong to at least one admissible history. It is structural rather

than fatalistic. Causal explanation adds mechanism, asymmetry,  intervention, or contrastive dependence, 
but it cannot require a transition forbidden by the dynamics. 
11.5 A moderate, multi-level structural relationalism 
The framework supports a moderate structural relationalism. Entities have scale-specific organization 
and causal capacities, yet their realized behavior also depends on geometrical, dynamical, historical, and 
boundary relations. Structure may be internal, as in molecular bonding or a lattice, or external, as in 
constraints and fields. This view preserves entities while rejecting explanations that isolate them from the 
relations through which their capacities are manifested; it does not require the stronger claim that objects 
are ontologically dispensable (Ladyman and Ross 2007; French 2014). 
11.6 Symmetry, explanation, and source structure 
Symmetry arguments are powerful because they eliminate arbitrary distinctions. An isolated point 
source in isotropic space cannot select one direction without additional structure. A homogeneous 
equation in a symmetric domain may admit symmetric solutions, although symmetry of the law alone 
does not guarantee symmetry of every solution: asymmetric initial or boundary conditions can break it. 
The explanatory role of symmetry therefore lies in the combined structure of law, source, state, and 
boundary (Brading and Castellani 2003). 
This point corrects a common simplification. Spherical symmetry is not produced by spatial 
dimension alone. It arises from the relation between a centered source and an isotropic environment. 
Conversely, a homogeneous and isotropic space need not have a material center. Centered spherical 
symmetry and intrinsic spatial homogeneity are distinct structures. 
11.7 Effective forms, limits, and scale 
Straight lines, one -dimensional currents, and uniform temperature profiles may be exact within 
idealized models or effective within specified scales. A line can be a fundamental Euclidean object, a 
local tangent approximation, a zero-curvature limit, or the residual direction after transverse variations are 
suppressed. The philosophy of physical form must therefore be sensitive to asymptotic reasoning and 
scale-dependent representation (Batterman 2002; Butterfield 2011). 
This does not reduce every macroscopic form to illusion. Effective forms can be robust and 
explanatorily indispensable even when they are not exact at microscopic scales. The wire's one -
dimensional current is physically real as a macroscopic transport regime, though it supervenes on three -
dimensional microscopic motion.

11.8 Decompression, recompression, and causal authority 
Decomposition identifies which parts of an expression arise from convention, source, geometry, 
boundary, or inherited state. Controlled recompression then asks whether an effective coefficient is a new 
degree of freedom or a summary of known structure. In the galaxy case, 𝐽𝑅 remains neutral because the

11.9 Identifiability, prediction, geometry, and ontology 
Analytical separability, empirical identifiability, predictive success, geometrical explanation, and 
ontological establishment form distinct evidential stages. Variables may be separated on paper yet remain 
observationally degenerate. Independent channels may identify a low -dimensional residual without 
showing that it is fundamental. A phenomenological response may predict held -out data while lacking an 
independent geometrical derivation. Even a successful geometrical model remains one empirically 
supported ontology among alternatives unless its distinctive consequences survive comparative tests. 
Five-stage boundary: Analytically separable ≠ empirically identifiable ≠ predictively successful ≠ geometrically 
explained ≠ ontologically established. 
The galaxy example makes the fourth distinction unavoidable. 𝐽𝑅 and 𝑇𝑅 are legitimate empirical 
response coordinates, but their notation does not authorize a geometrical reading. Promotion requires 
independent structure, not merely a good curve fit. The cosmological example adds an equally important 
warning: arranging cross-sectional samples by redshift does not create an individual history. A sequence 
claim requires a declared population transition and a selection-aware observation operator. 
By keeping analytical separability, empirical identifiability, predictive success, geometrical 
explanation, and ontological establishment explicitly non -equivalent, the framework leaves a clear and 
auditable path for subsequent claim -specific, trigger -based multidimensional assessment without 
imposing a fixed set of evaluative dimensions in advance. 
12. Scope and Limitations 
12.1 Explanatory framework versus physical theory 
The role -based framework and the 𝜀𝑔  reparameterization organize explanation; they are not 
substitutes for established field equations or for domain -specific causal models. Their contribution is 
strongest when compact notation combines quantities with different provenance and when separating 
those roles clarifies a model, exposes a hidden assumption, or defines an independently testable residual. 
If the standard theory already makes the relevant structure explicit and auditable, additional 
decomposition should remain minimal.

physical degrees of freedom, influence one another, be linked by equality or dynamical constraints, or 
collapse into a smaller active set in a particular problem. Geometry is not a universal first cause, and 
inherited state is not a universal hidden variable. The adequacy of the decomposition is case-dependent. 
12.2 Limits of the gravitational scalar representation 
Because 𝜀𝑔 = (4π G)⁻¹, it is not an independently measured gravitational permittivity. 𝐴𝛤 is likewise 
not a universal curvature scalar. Scalar recompression is admissible only when the observable -relevant 
response has been reduced without discarding active directional or nonlocal information. Kerr spacetime, 
for example, cannot be represented by changing a scalar area factor; a covariant metric, connection, or 
operator must remain explicit. 
The identity is also dimension-specific: changing spatial dimension changes both sphere measure and 
the dimensions of the coupling. Finally, replacing the constant 𝜀𝑔 by 𝜀𝑔(x) in Einstein’s equation is not a 
complete theory. The contracted Bianchi identity requires compatible dynamics and conservation, 
schematically 𝛻𝜇(𝑇𝜇𝜈/𝜀𝑔)  =  0. A variable response therefore needs additional fields or modified matter 
conservation, as in genuinely developed scalar–tensor theories (Brans and Dicke 1961; Carroll 2019). 
12.3 Empirical status of the numerical cases 
The SPARC computation is exploratory and was designed after the relevant galaxy relations were 
known; it is not a preregistered or fully blind confirmation. The tensor -lattice coefficients were fitted to 
the same Newtonian disk field whose compression error they measure. The flux -coupled finite-volume 
experiment, its convergence scan, the complementary conservation –sequence rank experiment, and the 
pseudo-longitudinal population audit were generated by declared toy laws. They test conservation, 
conditional generation, numerical convergence, model -form sensitivity, negative controls, rank, 
conditioning, and holdout design rather than the truth of those laws. A later empirical paper should freeze 
code, closures, sample cuts, quality controls, priors, derivative estimators, systematics, progenitor 
matching, and external validation data before evaluation.

A successful 𝐽𝑅 or 𝑇𝑅 model in rotation curves would support a low -dimensional effective description. 
It would not exclude dark matter, transfer automatically to lensing or cosmology, or justify the same 
scalar or tensor in strong -field spacetime. Standard halo models, MOND -type theories, and residual -

12.6 Limits of the sequence-compatibility interpretation 
Sequence notation is an analytical representation, not a claim that nature is fundamentally discrete. A 
continuous differential equation may be sampled, discretized, or rewritten as a first -order system on an 
augmented state. Such Markovization changes the representation of the sufficient state; it does not erase 
the physical history by which that state was formed. The relevant invariant is whether the declared present 
state is sufficient for lawful continuation under the chosen model.

Probabilistic models define distributions over future states, and many microscopic equations are time -

uses past, present, and future to audit explanatory linkage inside a specified model; it does not offer an 
independent metaphysics of time. 
Finally, a dynamically possible path need not be the actual cause of an observation. Causal authority 
still requires attention to source structure, interventions or counterfactuals where appropriate, alternative 
mechanisms, confounding, measurement provenance, and empirical discrimination. Sequence 
compatibility is a necessary filter on historical narratives, not a sufficient causal criterion. 
13. Conclusion 
Compact equations are indispensable for calculation, yet their efficiency often depends on 
compressing source, geometry, coupling, boundary, history, and observation into one expression. 
Explanatory decompression makes these roles auditable, after which the governing field equations, 
conservation laws, and admissibility conditions must be restored. Its main scientific gains are practical: it

exposes double counting, identifies null directions and missing measurements, distinguishes a fitted 
coefficient from its possible causes, and shows where an additional closure is required. 
The SPARC stress test and Newtonian disk control show why that discipline matters. A neutral 
residual response can be frozen and tested on held -out galaxies, but the present families do not 
outperform the fixed radial -acceleration relation. Ordinary disk geometry already creates directional 
enhancement and spherical -estimator bias, so it cannot be counted again after entering the baryonic

determinant-one response can redistribute fixed total flux and alter a finite -time configuration, while 
convergence failures still forbid an equilibrium or astronomical interpretation. 
The synthetic conservation –sequence and pseudo -longitudinal experiments add a complementary 
lesson. Lawful sequence linkage can reduce admissible parameter freedom, but it does not itself provide 
empirical rank. Competing mass and response explanations may fit one equatorial rotation channel at one 
epoch; discrimination requires an independently informative forward channel —for example off -plane 
dynamics, lensing, independently calibrated source mass, or a genuinely held -out epoch. Promotion from 
𝐽𝑅 or 𝑇𝑅 to geometry therefore requires independent structural derivation and cross -channel success, not 
rotation-curve fit alone. 
The same workflow can be adapted to other complex physical phenomena, and cautiously to 
nonphysical dynamical systems, when domain -specific laws, transition operators, measurement models, 
and uncertainty structures are supplied. It is not a universal dynamical theory. It is a disciplined way to 
move from compact description to structured comparison, negative control, held -out prediction, and 
controlled recompression. 
This is also a productive interaction between philosophy and science. Philosophy supplies distinctions 
among representation, prediction, explanation, and ontology; modelling forces those distinctions into 
forward equations, conservation residuals, convergence criteria, and possible failure. Scientific results 
then decide whether a conceptual separation has measurable consequences or remains non -identifying. 
Keeping analytical separability, empirical identifiability, predictive success, geometrical explanation, and 
ontological establishment non -equivalent leaves a clear trigger -based path for later multidimensional 
assessment without fixing its dimensions in advance. 
Final methodological rule: Separate roles; restore field equations and conservation; identify likelihood rank 
before using priors; demand cross -channel and held -out sequence divergence; recompress only after the causal 
and ontological limits are explicit.

Appendix A. Reproducibility Structure of the Numerical Audits 
A.1 Exploratory SPARC analysis 
Public SPARC mass -model tables were filtered to SPARC quality classes 𝑄SPARC = 1 or 2. Thirty 
80/20 splits were grouped by galaxy and scored by equal -galaxy log -velocity RMSE. Local -slope 
thresholds were exploratory. Confirmatory work must propagate distance, inclination, mass -to-light, gas, 
derivative, and covariance uncertainties on an independent sample. 
A.2 Newtonian disk–tensor comparison 
An infinitesimally thin exponential disk was integrated at 32 –256 angular nodes. Spherical-shell flux 
closure, Legendre l = 0 + 2 and l = 0 + 2 + 4 compressions, integration tolerance, directional residuals, 
and spherical-estimator bias were audited. The fitted tensor compresses the same Newtonian field; it is 
not a new source of gravity. 
A.3 Flux-conserving history-coupled generator 
The axisymmetric finite-volume code evolves paired Newtonian and coupled branches from identical 
mass-ring states. It checks boundary flux, solver residual, particle step size, and diagnostic evolution 
while β relaxes toward a smoothed source-flattening target. 
A.4 Convergence and robustness 
Audits vary grid, time step, initial flattening, closure, particle count, random seed, domain, 
regularization length, and time horizon. Expanded domains and endpoint signs converge, whereas the 
original-domain and quasi-steady criteria fail; these failures remain part of the reported result. 
A.5 Sequence and pseudo-longitudinal audits 
Synthetic experiments report Jacobian rank, condition number, negative controls, held -out epochs, 
nonparallel-channel coverage, and selection -aware population matching. Recovery under the generating 
law validates the code and design, not the law in nature. 
A.6 Confirmatory lock 
A future release should freeze the 𝐽𝑅  family and BTFR branch, sample cuts, nuisance model, 
derivative estimator, promotion gate, closure, boundary conditions, tolerances, progenitor matching, 
independent validation sample, code version, seeds, negative results, and AI -assistance disclosure before 
evaluation.
