Abstract
We propose a theoretical framework in which spacetime and time are not fun-
damental but emerge from a scalar non-equilibrium fieldΦdefined on an abstract
state spaceZ. The metricκ AB onZis taken to be constant, and time is defined
by the constitutive relationdτ=dℓ/γ∥J∥ κ withJ A =κ AB∂BΦ. Maximizing emer-

Mercury’s perihelion precession, light deflection, Shapiro delay, gravitational time

larities. The model predicts entropic gravity, flat galactic rotation curves without
dark matter, decaying cosmic acceleration without dark energy, black hole echoes,
photon dispersion, and thermal quantum collapse. These predictions are falsifiable
with current or near-future experiments.

1 Introduction: The Primacy of Change
The history of physics can be read as a sequence of successive reductions in the number of
fundamental entities assumed to exist. Newtonian mechanics postulated absolute space,
absolute time, and instantaneous forces acting between material bodies. Special relativ-
ity reduced space and time to a single four-dimensional manifold, Minkowski spacetime,
governed by a fixed metric structure. General relativity further dynamicalized this struc-
ture, replacing the fixed background with a dynamical pseudo-Riemannian metricgµν
whose curvature is determined by matter and energy through Einstein’s field equations.
Quantum mechanics introduced a different conceptual arena: Hilbert space, probability
amplitudes, and non-commutative observables.
Each of these frameworks achieved remarkable empirical success within its domain.
Yet their unification remains elusive. The central obstacle is not computational but
ontological. Quantum theory treats time as an external, absolute parameter that governs
the unitary evolution of states. General relativity treats time as an internal, dynamical
coordinate inseparably woven into the fabric of spacetime. The resulting “problem of

A second obstacle appears at extremes of energy and curvature. Einstein’s equations
predict singularities: regions where curvature invariants and matter densities diverge to
infinity [2]. These are not observed phenomena but mathematical breakdowns, signaling
that the theory ceases to be predictive. Quantum field theory faces an analogous issue
with ultraviolet divergences, managed only through renormalization prescriptions that,
while successful, leave questions about their ultimate justification.
On cosmological scales, theΛCDM model describes observations with high precision
[3]. However, it does so by introducing two components that have never been detected
directly in laboratory experiments: dark matter, required to explain galactic rotation
curves and structure formation, and dark energy, required to explain late-time cosmic
acceleration. Together they constitute approximately 95% of the cosmic energy budget.
The epistemological cost is high: the visible universe is explained by invoking invisible
sectors postulated ad hoc.
These three tensions—the problem of time, the appearance of singularities, and the
dark sector conundrum—suggest that a foundational assumption may be at fault. The
assumption under examination here is the primacy of spacetime. All current theories
take for granted that physical processes unfold within a pre-existing, four-dimensional
continuum. Space and time are treated as containers. Matter and fields move within
them.
We propose to relax this assumption. The starting point is not a container but a
process: change. In a universe where nothing changes, the concept of time is meaningless.
A clock that does not tick, a planet that does not move, a heart that does not beat: in
all such cases, duration cannot be measured. Change is primary. Time is what arises to
measure change.
If change is the transfer of a system from one state to another, then the complete set
of possible states must constitute a mathematical arena. We call this arena state space
and denote itZ. Every pointz∈Zrepresents a complete, possible configuration of the
system. Dynamics is the trajectory of a representative point throughZ.
This shift in ontology has immediate consequences. Spacetime is no longer funda-
mental. It becomes a projection, an effective description extracted from the higher-
dimensional structure ofZand the scalar field that drives motion within it. Time is
no longer an external parameter. It emerges as a measure of how much “resistance” a
system encounters relative to its “capacity” to change. Gravity is no longer the curvature
of spacetime. It becomes the consequence of systems following paths that maximize their
emergent time, in a geometry where resistance to change varies from place to place.
