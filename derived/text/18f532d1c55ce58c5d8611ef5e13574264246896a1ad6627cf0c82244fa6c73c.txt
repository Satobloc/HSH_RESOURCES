Abstract: Using the principle of least action in the general theory of relativity, the equation 
for metric and the equation for motion of charged fluid particles are derived, as well as the 
formula for energy of a spherical body with a fluid-like substance. The calculation shows that 
there is no contribution from the gravitational field in the energy outside the body. Thus, the 
problem of localization of the gravitational field energy in space arises. In order to solve this 
problem, it is proposed to replace the scal ar acceleration field and the scalar pressure field, 
which are represented in the Lagrangian density, with corresponding vector fields. 
Keywords: Lagrangian density ; principle of least action ; general theory of relativity ; 
equation for metric; equation of motion; energy. 
 
1. Introduction 
There are many works that analyze the foundations of the general theory of relativity (GTR) 
and provide corresponding interpretations of the discovered facts. As a theory that successfully 
describes gravitational phenomena through the geometric properties of space -time, GTR 
confirms that the space-time continuum, through its static and dynamic fields, actively interacts 
with the matter particles of physical systems and transfers interactions from one body to 
another. 
Despite the successful predictions of GTR, this theory requires additional work by theorists 
due to several unresolved issues. For example, it remains unclear how to calculate the 
contribution of the gravitational field's energy to the relativistic energy of a physical system 
[1]. In GTR, it is necessary to derive and justify constraints on the theory's parameters, beyond 
which the predictions of the theory become invalid. This applies, for example, to the 
appearance of singularities in black holes [2], as well as dark matter and dark energy in the 
Lambda-CDM model in cosmology [3]. 
One of the simplest objects considered in GTR is a solitary spherical body consisting of 
matter in the form of a fluid. Using covariant approach, in [4] various options of the Lagrangian

density were considered, which make it possible to derive from the principle of least action the 
equations of motion of particles in an ideal fluid in the absence of electric charges of particles 
and dissipation of energy of the particles. At the same time, it is indicated in [5] that description 
of a real fluid as an ideal isentropic rotation-less fluid using scalar functions in the Lagrangian 
density is possible only for a limited period of time. 
The purpose of our work is to present the covariant equation of motion for fluid particles 
inside a spherical body in the general theory of relativity, in the case where the fluid particles 
have an electric charge. In addition, a formula for determining energy is derived. In Section 2, 
the equation for the metric is obtained from the principle of least action, followed by the 
equation of motion for the particles. In Section 3, the equation of motion for the particles is 
derived directly from the principle o f least action, in the form of a balance equation for force 
densities. Section 4 defines the scalar curvature and cosmological constant in the space outside 
the body, and Section 5 calculates the energy outside the body. The problem of localizing 
gravitational energy and solutions to this problem are discussed in Section 6.

5. Energy 
In [9], the main problems of the general theory of relativity associated with the definition 
of gravitational energy in terms of various pseudotensors are described. Gravitational energy 
is considered as a quasi-local quantity, which is most accurately determined only for the entire 
system if the metric becomes asymptotically flat at infinity. 
On the other hand, the relativistic energy density, which determines the inertial mass 
density of a system, is proportional to the time component of the system's four -momentum 
density. In this case, a given gravitational energy pseudotensor can be used to determine the 
gravitational force density, but not to calculate the gravitational energy density. This is due to 
the fact that the time components of the gravitational energy pseudotensor do not form a four-
vector, so the integral over the volume of these components cannot be a four -vector 
contributing to the relativistic energy. This circumstance is pointed out in [10]. An example 
here is the energy-momentum tensor of electromagnetic field, the divergence of which yields 
the electromagnetic four -force density and contributes to the generalized Poynting theorem 
[11]. However, the time components of this tensor do not form the electromagnetic four -
momentum density, as indicated by the well-known 4/3 problem.

6. Discussion of the results 
In (23) and (30), the equations of motion of matter particles are presented, derived in 
different ways from the principle of least action. Thus, the predictions of general theory of 
relativity (GTR) in the description of particle motion are confirmed quite reliably. 
The situation changes when it is necessary to determine the energy of a physical system 
within the framework of GTR. As calculations in Sections 4 and 5 show, outside a massive 
charged spherical body, the scalar curvature 
R  and the cosmological constant 
  become zero. 
In (63), only the electromagnetic energy contributes to the energy of the system outside the 
body, while the gravitational field does not contribute to the system's energy. This contradicts 
the fact that in Newtonian physics, the gravitational field, like the electromagnetic field, has a 
potential and potential energy. The absence of gravitational energy outside the body means that 
in GTR, gravitational energy is localized in a way that is significantly different from the way 
energy is localized in an electromagnetic field. 
In GTR, there are several ways to assign some gravitational energy to the space outside a 
massive body. To do this, expressions with the dimension of energy density are constructed 
using the components of the metric tensor, and almost all of these expressi ons turn out to be 
pseudotensors of the gravitational field [1]. The existence of several such pseudotensors 
suggests that there is still a problem in GTR regarding the contribution of gravitational field 
energy to the system's energy and the method of defining gravitational energy.  
The obvious reason for the problem with the localization of gravitational energy in GTR is 
that in GTR, the effects of gravitation are completely expressed through the metric tensor and 
its time and coordinate derivatives, and the gravitational field is re placed by the metric field. 
As a result of this replacement, the metric field not only curves spacetime, introducing a new

===== PAGE 23 =====
23 
 
degree of freedom into the equations of physics, but also replaces the gravitational interaction 
between bodies. 
One example of a successful solution to the energy problem of the gravitational field is the 
covariant theory of gravitation [13], [16]. In this theory, each known particle interaction is 
associated with a corresponding vector relativistic field [1 7]. The transition from scalar to 
vector fields significantly enriches the theory by introducing new variables in the form of 
vector potentials, and the inclusion of the relativistic velocity transformation rule in the 
equations of motion allows for the con sideration of phenomena in moving reference frames 
[18-19]. 
The use of vector fields in the covariant theory of gravitation leads to the fact that each field 
yields its own contribution to the equation of motion and to the energy of the system. Another 
advantage of the covariant theory of gravitation is that it makes it possible to present formulas 
and calculate the energy and momentum of a physical system in a covariant form [10], [20]. In 
contrast, there is still no consensus in GTR on how to calculate energy and momentum [9], [21-
22]. 
In this regard, in [23], it was proposed to use the results of the covariant theory of gravitation 
in order to find the energy and momentum in GTR. To do this, it is desirable, as a first step, to 
replace the scalar fields used in GTR with the corresponding vector fields. Thus, in the

According to the approach adopted in the general theory of relativity, the equation of

pressure field in the general theory of relativity with corresponding vector fields, similar to 
what was done in the covariant theory of gravitation [10], [13], [15-20], [23].
