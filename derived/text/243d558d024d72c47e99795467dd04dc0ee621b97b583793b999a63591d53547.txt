Exact Kantowski–Sachs and Bianchi type-III cosmologies in nonlinear 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  gravity:
Dynamics and background-level viability

Abstract
We investigate exact anisotropic Kantowski–Sachs and Bianchi type-III cosmologies
in nonlinear modified teleparallel 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  gravity. Starting from the modified field
equations for a two-scale-factor geometry, we construct four exact anisotropic
classes, each determining its own torsion scalar and reconstructing a distinct
compatible nonlinear function 𝑓𝑖 ( 𝑇 ) . Cases I and II describe radially dominated
expanding but decelerating cosmologies, with persistent normalized anisotropy
despite the decay of the shear. Case III is controlled by the parameter 𝑛 and becomes
isotropic in the expansion rates only for 𝑛 = 1, while remaining decelerating for 𝑛 > 0
. Case IV separates into an anisotropically contracting branch for 𝑐14 < 0 and an
accelerating expanding branch for 𝑐14 > 0; the latter terminates at a finite-time
singularity rather than approaching a regular accelerating regime. The reconstructed
functions define distinct branch-dependent nonlinear torsional theories, whose
effective density and directional pressures depend on the Kantowski–Sachs or
Bianchi type-III curvature sector. In the displayed Kantowski–Sachs parameter range,
Case I satisfies the null and weak energy conditions but is more restricted by the
dominant and strong conditions, whereas Case II satisfies all four standard energy-
condition inequalities. For Case I, an equilibrium thermodynamic characterization
gives finite asymptotic temperature and entropy density, while the total entropy
grows with the expanding spatial volume. The solutions therefore provide exact
anisotropic background sectors rather than viable late-time alternatives to the
observed nearly isotropic accelerating Universe; in particular, exact solvability does
not by itself imply isotropization or full theoretical viability.

The origin of this acceleration and the nature of the dark sector continue to motivate
extensions of the gravitational framework [5].
Teleparallel gravity provides one such extension by describing gravitation through
torsion rather than the curvature of the Levi-Civita connection. The teleparallel
equivalent of general relativity (TEGR) reproduces the dynamics of GR, whereas
replacing the torsion scalar 𝑇 by a nonlinear function 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  defines a modified
teleparallel theory [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12]. Its field equations remain second
order in the tetrad variables, and the framework has been widely applied to
cosmological acceleration, reconstruction, and observational tests [13], [14], [15], [16],
[17]. Further formulations and developments of 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  gravity relevant to its
cosmological and covariant structure are given in Refs. [18], [19], [20], [21].
The second-order character of the background equations does not by itself establish
the theoretical consistency of nonlinear 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  gravity. In the pure-tetrad formulation,
nonlinear models generally lose the local Lorentz invariance of TEGR and contain
additional gravitational degrees of freedom [22], [23], [24]. Around highly symmetric
FLRW or Minkowski backgrounds, some of these modes may fail to appear at linear or
low perturbative order, leading instead to strong-coupling concerns [14], [25], [26],
[27]. Their propagation can depend sensitively on the background and perturbative
sector [27], [28], [29]. The present work concerns exact homogeneous backgrounds
and does not attempt to resolve these perturbative issues.
Homogeneous anisotropic space–times provide a complementary setting in which
directional expansion and isotropization can be studied beyond the FLRW geometry.
Kantowski–Sachs and Bianchi type-III space–times are particularly useful because
their two independent scale factors separate radial and transverse evolution, while
their nonvanishing spatial curvature distinguishes them from spatially flat
anisotropic models [30].
Observations, however, strongly restrict large-scale anisotropy at late times. CMB
temperature and polarization measurements constrain the present shear in
homogeneous anisotropic cosmologies [31], while analyses of anisotropic extensions
of 𝛬CDM likewise require a small late-time shear contribution when CMB and BAO
measurements are combined [32]. The exact solutions studied here are therefore not
proposed as alternatives to the observed nearly isotropic late-time FLRW Universe.
Within this restriction, Kantowski–Sachs and Bianchi type-III geometries provide
curved anisotropic sectors in which directional expansion, shear evolution, and
isotropization can be studied analytically [33], [34]. Their different spatial-curvature
sectors also allow the curvature contribution to the effective source variables to be
traced explicitly. Anisotropic backgrounds may additionally provide useful exact
sectors outside the late-time FLRW regime, as illustrated, for example, by anisotropic
inflationary solutions in Ref. [35]. The four metric ansätze considered in the present
work are not assumed to be unique or phenomenologically preferred among the
possible reconstructible anisotropic configurations. They are selected because they
permit closed analytical reconstruction and represent qualitatively different
directional regimes: strongly and mildly radially dominated expansion, parameter-
controlled anisotropy with an isotropic limit, and a sign-dependent sector containing
contracting and singular accelerating branches. Each ansatz determines its own
torsion scalar 𝑇𝑖 ( 𝑡 )  and a distinct compatible function 𝑓𝑖 ( 𝑇 ) ; hence the manuscript

Late-time cosmic acceleration and anisotropic cosmological evolution have already
been widely investigated in modified teleparallel gravity [30], [36], [37], including de
Sitter, power-law, and other accelerating solutions as well as the isotropization
properties of anisotropic backgrounds. The present work does not propose late-time
acceleration as a new phenomenon. The contribution considered here is different.
Starting from the directional field equations of the Kantowski–Sachs and Bianchi
type-III geometries, we construct exact branches through their compatibility
relations and reconstruct a distinct nonlinear function 𝑓𝑖 ( 𝑇 )  for each branch. This
provides a branch-wise classification within the same two-scale-factor framework
while retaining the opposite spatial-curvature sectors 𝑘 = ± 1. The resulting classes
are physically distinct: Cases I and II are radially dominated expanding but
decelerating solutions, Case III has parameter-controlled anisotropy with an isotropic
expansion-rate limit at 𝑛 = 1 while remaining decelerating, and Case IV separates
into a contracting branch and a singular accelerating expanding branch according to
the sign of 𝑐14. Thus, the originality lies in the exact reconstruction and comparative
classification of these anisotropic branches and their associated 𝑓𝑖 ( 𝑇 )  functions,
rather than in the realization of cosmic acceleration itself.
Our analysis follows a reconstruction procedure rather than starting from one
prescribed nonlinear 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  model. Each exact metric ansatz determines its own
torsion scalar and a distinct compatible function 𝑓𝑖 ( 𝑇 ) . The four classes must
therefore be interpreted as separate branch-dependent reconstructions, not as
different cosmological solutions of one universal 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  theory. We obtain four exact
classes with qualitatively different dynamics. Cases I and II describe radially
dominated expanding but decelerating solutions and retain finite normalized
anisotropy. Case III is controlled by the parameter 𝑛; its expansion rates become
isotropic at 𝑛 = 1, although the solution remains decelerating for 𝑛 > 0. Case IV
separates according to the sign of 𝑐14: the 𝑐14 < 0 branch is anisotropically
contracting, whereas 𝑐14 > 0 gives accelerated expansion that terminates at a finite-
time singularity. The effective source sector further distinguishes the Kantowski–
Sachs and Bianchi type-III geometries through their opposite curvature contributions.
The background properties of an exact solution do not by themselves establish the
viability of the underlying nonlinear 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  theory. In particular, nonlinear 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )
gravity may contain additional gravitational degrees of freedom whose perturbative
manifestation depends on the background, and the absence of an additional
propagating scalar mode at low perturbative order can be associated with a strong-
coupling issue [14], [27]. The usual pure-tetrad formulation also presents a local-
Lorentz/frame-dependence problem, while the covariant formulation employing the
tetrad together with the corresponding inertial spin connection provides a frame-
independent description [20]. Accordingly, the effective density, directional
pressures, equations of state, energy conditions, and thermodynamic quantities
studied here are interpreted as properties of the homogeneous anisotropic
backgrounds only. The present analysis does not establish perturbative stability or
direct observational viability of the reconstructed theories. Moreover, unlike
phenomenological 𝑓 ( 𝑇 )  models that have been confronted with background and
structure-growth observations [18], [21], the functions obtained here arise from
particular exact anisotropic branches and must be tested independently before they
can be regarded as viable cosmological models.
