Denability of quantization inputs:
a Padoa-style classication and its boundary
Igor Postanovsky *

Abstract
Quantum theory carries several discrete or numerical inputs that are routinely de-
scribed as not derived from an underlying substrate. We ask a sharper question: for
which of these inputs can not derived be upgraded to a proof of undenability, and for
which is this structurally impossible? Padoa's classical method answers the rst kind and,
we show, cleanly refuses the second. We propose a classication of quantization inputs by
their logical position: an input is amenable to Padoa's method exactly when it is a predi-
cate on a xed similarity type, and is not amenable when it is a parameter of the type itself
or a weight on an ensemble. We then exhibit both poles. At one pole, circulation quantiza-
tion: reformulating a companion result, we show that the predicate is Padoa-undenable
over the structure of realized jets, with a pair whose existence is the nontrivial part; and
we observe that here the method yields more than a verdict, since it computes the ex-
act reduct threshold at which denitions begin to exist, direction by direction. At the
other pole, the spinor sign: the pair is nearly denitional, and the entire mathematical
weight sits in calibrating the reduct, which we isolate as an explicit hypothesis rather than
assert. Finally we show that the amplitude parameter and Born-type typicality weights
fall outside the method. For the amplitude parameter we answer the natural objection
that deformation quantization supplies a common type, and prove a dichotomy for the
single-theory formulation: with the external potential in the reduct the parameter is de-
nable away from a degenerate stratum, and with it removed the parameter is undenable
only because a free function absorbs the dierence. For typicality weights we document
a semantic obstruction with a counterexample. In both cases the two available branches
give, respectively, an answer without blindness and blindness without content, which is
our reason for excluding these inputs structurally. The boundary is oered as a result,
not an apology: the classication tells one in advance which foundational inputs can be
argued about by this method at all.
1 Introduction
Every axiomatization of quantum mechanics arrives with a short list of ingredients that the
formalism uses but does not produce. The value of the reduced Planck constant sets the scale
of noncommutativity; the circulation of a velocity eld is quantized in integer multiples of
it; a spinor changes sign under a full rotation while a vector does not; the Born rule weights
congurations by a squared amplitude. Foundational writing tends to mark each of these with
the same phrase  put in by hand, postulated, not derived  and to pass on. This
paper takes that phrase seriously enough to ask, input by input, when it can be turned into a
theorem and when it cannot.

Related work.Applying Padoa's method to physical theories is an established programme
rather than a new move, and we position this paper inside it. Padoa's principle [2] has been
applied to the McKinseySugarSuppes axiomatization of classical particle mechanics [5] to
discuss the denability of physical notions such as mass and time [7], with Tarski [4] and Beth
[3] supplying the theoretical grounding of the method and Suppes' textbook formulation [6]
the version most often quoted in that literature. The same programme has been continued by
Sant'Anna and Bueno [8], alongside earlier work on the denability of time and spacetime in
classical physics [9]. What appears to be untried is the application toquantization conditions
specically, and that is the slot this paper occupies.
We must also distinguish a dierent use of the word. There is a line of work, due to
BenDaniel [10], in which denability of physical elds means set-theoretic denability within
an axiomatic set theory, and in which quantization of elds is claimed to be equivalent to
denability in that theory. Despite the supercial closeness of the vocabulary, that programme
asks whether eld congurations are denablesets, whereas we ask whether apredicateis
denable from adeclared reductof physical primitives. The two notions of denability are
not comparable, and no result of either transfers to the other. A further distinction separates
our sense from a recent application of Tarski's undenability theorem to physics [13], which
concerns the undenability of truth within a formal language rather than the denability of a
designated predicate from a declared reduct; the two share the word and little else.
Contribution.(i) A classication of quantization inputs by logical position (Section 3),
which we regard as the main content; (ii) the two poles instantiated (Sections 4 and 5); (iii)
the boundary argued rather than asserted (Section 6).

7 Discussion
The paper has sorted four quantization inputs by a single criterion  the logical position
each occupies relative to a similarity type  and found that this position, not the physical
weight of the input, decides whether Padoa's method can reach it. Two inputs sit inside the
method: circulation quantization, where the reduct is the obvious one and the whole diculty
is manufacturing a pair against the rigidity of jets (Section 4); and the spinor sign, where a
pair is immediate and the whole content has migrated into a declaration of what counts as
local (Section 5). Two inputs sit outside it: the amplitude parameter, a parameter of the type
rather than a predicate on it, for which the single-theory formulation returns a dichotomy with
no contentful branch (Proposition 6.1); and Born-type typicality weights, for which the two
available semantics give blindness without content or content without blindness (Section 6.2).
The same shape recurs at both excluded inputs, and it is that shape  an answer without
blindness on one side, blindness without content on the other  that we read as the signature
of a structural exclusion rather than a failure of eort.

At the rst pole the method does more than deliver a verdict over one chosen reduct:
by Corollary 4.4 it computes the threshold at which denitions begin to exist, direction by

Other discrete ingredients of quantum theory  a topological angle, the integer labels of a
superselection sector  present themselves as candidates for the same triage, and the criterion
says in advance where to look: whether the ingredient is a predicate on a xed type, so that
a pair is worth hunting; a parameter of the type, so that a dichotomy is all one will nd;
or a weight, so that the measure-theoretic obstruction of Section 6.2 applies. Carrying the
classication to those ingredients is left to future work.
Three remarks we would make regardless. First, the classication of Section 3 is falsiable
in a useful way: exhibiting a Padoa pair for either input of Section 6, over a declared reduct
and a single theory, refutes the placement. Second, the boundary of Section 6 is argued on both
sides rather than declared: for each excluded input we exhibit the two natural formulations
and show what each returns. Third, the two theorems are not oered as dicult results 
one is a translation and one is close to a textbook fact. They are oered as calibration points:
they show what the two amenable rows look like from the inside, which is what makes the
exclusion in Section 6 informative rather than merely negative.
