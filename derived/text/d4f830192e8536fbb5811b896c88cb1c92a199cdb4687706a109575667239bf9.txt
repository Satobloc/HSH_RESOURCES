1.Introduction
1.1AQuestiontheStandardModelHasNeverBeenAsked
The Standard Model is the theoretical framework describing elementary particles and their interactions
inparticle physics. Since itsestablishment inthe 1970s, it has successfully predictedthe existence of theW
boson, Z boson, charm quark, bottom quark, top quark, and Higgs boson, and has withstood decades of
experimental scrutiny. The core mathematical structure of the Standard Model is gauge field theory— the
SU(3) gauge group describes the strong interaction, and the SU(2)×U(1) gauge group describes the weak
andelectromagneticinteractions(Yang&Mills,1954).
However, the success of the Standard Model has concealed a question that has never been asked: Is it
truly "one model"? Or is it a patchwork of multiple "regimes" governed by different rules at different
physical scales? Traditional physics tacitly assumes that the Standard Model is a unified theoretical
framework— particle properties are described by the same Lagrangian, and symmetries remain consistent
across all energy scales. This assumption has never been systematically challenged. Not because evidence
supportsit,butbecausephysicslackedasetoftoolstodetect"regimeboundaries."
TheFactor Hierarchy Law andTestabilityNormsproposed byTang(2026g) are precisely sucha set of
tools. The Factor Hierarchy Law states: In any complex system driven by multiple quantifiable factors,
factors naturally fall into two levels — regime factors determine which set of rules currently applies, and
executive factors execute under the given rules. The function of a regime factor is not reflected in direct
explanatory power but in itsinteraction effect that modulates the exposure coefficients of executive factors;
whenaregimefactorcrossesacriticalvalue,thefactorweightsoftheentiresystem aresystematicallyreset.
