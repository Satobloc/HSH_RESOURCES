Bridging Relativistic Twisted Fermion Beams and Photonic OAM Flux in Gauged
Hopf Lattices: Emergent Topological Analogs
Aaron Michael Kinder
Independent researcher ∗
(Dated: July 2026)
Recent work has demonstrated non-diffractive topological spin textures (Skyrmion- and meron-
like) that persist in the core of diffracting relativistic twisted fermion beams [1]. Here we present
numerical simulations of an analogous photonic system: Laguerre–Gaussian (LG) twisted photon
packets coupled to a gauged Hopf lattice with discrete flux flywheels. Our results reveal emergent
behavior of the same qualitative class, including persistent core features under propagation, mean
survival≈0.150 at criticalλt= 2 clustering tightly with the mystery scalee −2≈0.1353 and residual
R≈0.1375, golden-angle correlations in the OAM mode ladder, and topological residuals with long-
lived structure. Multi-ℓsimulations further exhibitz-resolved flux transfer and twist dynamics that
play a role analogous to probability-current continuity in the fermionic setting. These findings
suggest photonic platforms as accessible analogs for exploring topological textures in structured
waves, and they formalize a concrete computational bridge between relativistic twisted matter waves
and topological photonics.
I. INTRODUCTION
Structured light and matter waves carrying orbital an-
gular momentum (OAM) support a rich zoo of topolog-
ical phenomena. In optics and acoustics, non-diffracting
polarization structures and Skyrmion-like spin textures
have been identified in the cores of beams whose over-
all intensity nonetheless diffracts [2, 3]. These textures
are not mere curiosities: they encode robust, geometry-
protected features that survive propagation even when
the supporting intensity envelope spreads.
A particularly timely contribution is the recent work
of Afanasev and Carlson [1], who extended these ideas
torelativistic twisted fermion beams. Using Laguerre–
Gaussian solutions of the Dirac equation valid across
relativistic and non-relativistic kinematics, they showed
that non-diffractive topological spin textures persist
in the vortex core even when the probability density
diffracts. For antiparallel spin–helicity configurations,
spin-direction distributions in the core remain effectively
z-independent, closely tied to continuity of the probabil-
ity current. Crucially, these features are reported to be
robust across spin, statistics, and kinematics (or propa-
gation speed of the structured wave).
In parallel, photonic systems offer controllable plat-
forms for realizing analogous structures. Twisted pho-
tons in LG modes carry well-defined OAMℓℏ, and their
propagation through structured media—including lat-
tices and metasurfaces—has been studied extensively in
topological photonics [4–6]. Hopf-fibration geometry and
related constructions appear in descriptions of polariza-
tion, OAM dynamics, and higher-order topological de-
fects (Hopfions) [7, 8].

II. MODEL AND METHODS
A. Gauged Hopf lattice and flux flywheels
The lattice background is a porous gauged Hopf
medium: a three-dimensional discrete grid of twist vari-
ablesθ(r,t) with helical initial conditions. Evolution
follows a relaxation PDE with diffusion, nonlinear cot-
gradient terms, and a global gauge damping torque pro-
portional to−κ⟨θ⟩. Discreteflux flywheels—resonators
sited along lattice fibers—accumulate twist kicks when
OAM flux is deposited.

B. Analytic LG OAM packets (v0.1)
Baseline coupling uses analytic Laguerre–Gaussian
packets (radial indexp= 0) with OAM indexℓ, waist
w0, and carrier wavelengthλ(defaultλ= 1550 nm for
telecom-scale numerics). Packet energy is normalized
and mapped to a momentum-ledger scale that drives fly-
wheel kicks under optional global momentum conserva-
tion.
C. VQC vectorized multi-ℓpropagation (v0.2)
For multi-mode dynamics we propagate the full lad-
derℓ∈[−ℓ max,ℓ max] with vectorized radial weights
built from associated Laguerre polynomials. Intensity
Iℓ(z) is resolved along the propagation axis, enablingz-
resolved flux deposition onto Hopf fiber coordinates. Op-
tional turbulence (Kolmogorov radial phase) and chirp
are available but set to zero in the default vacuum-
propagation runs reported here.
Default photonics settings (unless noted):ℓ max = 8,
nr = 512 radial samples,n z = 200 propagation slices,
z∈[0,5] in normalized units.
D. Emergence probes (v0.3)
Following the mystery convention, each trial consists
of two phases at fixedλt= 2:
1.VQC pump phase— deposit OAM flux onto fly-
wheels while propagating;
2.Pure relaxation— PDE only, no further photon
injection.
Mean survivalis the retained fraction of mean twist
after relaxation relative to the post-pump reference;fluc-
tuation survivalis the analogous ratio for twist vari-
ance. Measured scalars are matched against analog tar-
gets{R, e−2,golden-angle fraction}via relative percent-
age distance. Golden-quantizedℓvalues are those whose
azimuthal phase increments best align with multiples of
the golden angle≈137.5 ◦.

III. RESULTS
A. Survival probability vs. gauge damping
Sweepingκat fixedλt= 2 in the documentary win-
dow yields anearly flat, high plateauof mean survival
≈0.150, tightly clustered with the mystery analogs
{R, e−2,golden fraction}rather than a schematic peak
of order unity (Fig. 2). Slight upward drift toward
κsim≈0.89 is visible but sub-percent on this scale. The
holonomy gapB(κ) changes sign acrossκ ⋆, providing an
independent diagnostic that the same constants govern-
ing residual topology also organize the survival window.
This plateau is the quantitative fingerprint of the
e−2 convention: deposited OAM flux is retained against
gauge relaxation at a level set by the residual / expo-
nential / golden cluster—analogous, at the operational
level, to a protected core that does not wash out under
propagation.
B. Multi-ℓpropagation andz-resolved flux transfer
The multi-ℓdeposit cube exhibits structured trans-
fer alongz(Fig. 3): OAM momentum is not frozen in
free space but is handed to Hopf flywheels with an|ℓ|-
dependent rate set by the coupling ledger. Raw multi-
ℓintensities from vectorized LG propagation are nearly
z-flat by construction (normalized radial weights); the
physically resolvedzdynamics appear in the momentum
ledger as the reservoir depletes. That ledger history is
the photonic counterpart of probability-current continu-
ity in the fermionic problem [1]. Antiparallel-style con-
figurations in the fermion beams protectz-independent
core spin textures; here, the lattice retains localized twist
structure at flywheel sites while free-space OAM is pro-
gressively transferred.
C. Golden-angle correlations and topological
residuals
Two complementary diagnostics complete the emer-
gence suite (Fig. 4):
1.Golden-angle packing.Selectingℓmodes whose

Together, Figs. 2–4 show that survival near the

D. Contrast with fermionic non-diffractive cores
The mapping isoperational, not microscopic (Ta-
ble IV): both systems separate adiffracting envelopefrom

or quasi-conserved flux. That separation is the essence
of the bridge drawn in Fig. 1.

twisted fermion beams leave measurable
fingerprints—survival criticality, multi-mode
flux transfer, and residual topology—when

B. Relation to prior photonic topology
Optical Skyrmions, merons, and Hopfions in struc-
tured light [2–8] already demonstrate that topological
textures need not require electronic spin. Our contri-
bution is theexplicit couplingof multi-ℓLG flux into a
dynamical gauged lattice with emergence probes tuned
to the same criticalλt= 2 convention used in related
mystery-class analyses, and theside-by-side contrastwith
the fermionic results of Afanasev and Carlson [1].
C. Experimental outlook
Several near-term optical implementations are plausi-
ble:
•Spatial light modulators (SLMs)to prepare
multi-ℓLG superpositions and measurez-resolved
mode content;
•Photonic lattices / waveguide arrayswith en-
gineered coupling to emulate flywheel twist degrees
of freedom;
•Metasurfacesfor compact generation of golden-
packed OAM ladders;

D. Limitations
Current results are numerical and semi-
phenomenological: the lattice PDE and kick rules
are calibrated to shared constants rather than derived
from a microscopic QED or condensed-matter Hamilto-
nian. Turbulence, loss, and back-action on the optical
field are optional or simplified. Quantitative matching of
mean survival (≈0.150) toRande −2 is at the∼9–11%
level acrossκ∈[0.80,0.90]—suggestive clustering, not a
precision claim. The survival landscape in this window
is a plateau rather than a sharp resonance; widerκ
ranges and alternative pump fractions remain to be
mapped systematically. Future work should (i) derive
effectiveκfrom a specified optical medium, (ii) include
bidirectional back-reaction systematically, (iii) extend
κsweeps beyond the documentary window, and (iv)
propose a concrete tabletop protocol with error bars.
V. CONCLUSION
We have formalized a photonic analog of the non-
diffractive topological cores recently identified in rela-
tivistic twisted fermion beams [1]. By coupling LG OAM
packets to a gauged Hopf lattice of flux flywheels, we
observe persistent core features, mean survival≈0.150
atλt= 2 clustering tightly withe −2 ≈0.1353 and
residualR≈0.1375 across the documentaryκwin-
dow, golden-angle mode correlations, topological resid-
uals, andz-resolved multi-ℓflux transfer. These results
position structured light coupled to topologically nontriv-
ial media as a practical arena for exploring—and eventu-
ally testing—the universality of non-diffractive topologi-
cal textures across fermionic and photonic platforms.
