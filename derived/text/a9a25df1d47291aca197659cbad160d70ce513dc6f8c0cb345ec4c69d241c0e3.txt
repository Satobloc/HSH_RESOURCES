SPARC-calibrated galactic wormholes in Einstein–Cartan gravity with Dekel–Zhao and
Hernquist dark-matter halos

Abstract
We construct static, spherically symmetric galactic wormholes in Einstein–Cartan
gravity using dark-matter halo distributions calibrated from SPARC rotation curves.
The Dekel–Zhao (DZ) and Hernquist profiles are fitted to NGC3198 and DDO154
through Bayesian posterior sampling. Hernquist is mildly favored for NGC3198,
whereas DZ is preferred for DDO154. Joint posterior samples preserving parameter
covariance are propagated into the Einstein–Cartan reconstruction, with both halo
families used to assess model dependence. The throat radius is parameterized by
, while the matching radius is fixed at . Imposing the smooth
Levi–Civita metric-sector condition  determines the Weyssenhoff
throat-spin amplitude. Across 0.1 ≤ q  ≤ 1, all propagated realizations satisfy the
adopted geometrical admissibility and positive-mass conditions. At , the
material and effective radial null energy conditions are violated at the throat. The
material WEC and SEC are recovered by the matching radius, whereas the material
DEC and all effective WEC, SEC, and DEC remain violated. The exterior masses are
approximately 1.75 × 10 M  for NGC3198 and 3.04 × 10 M  for DDO154. Changing
the halo family modifies these masses by about 4.0% and 0.34%, respectively, without
altering the qualitative throat or optical conclusions. The throat supports a radially
unstable circular null orbit, with no additional photon sphere in the retained interior
or Schwarzschild exterior. A near-throat expansion yields a logarithmic strong-
deflection law with . These results constitute a SPARC-calibrated, model-
conditioned Einstein–Cartan reconstruction rather than direct observational evidence
for wormholes or microscopic spin.

Introduction
Traversable wormholes are nontrivial spacetime geometries characterized by a
minimum areal radius connecting two spacetime regions. In the Morris–Thorne
construction, regular traversal requires a finite redshift function together with a
shape function satisfying the flare-out condition at the throat [1], [2]. Within general
relativity, this geometrical requirement implies violation of the radial null energy
condition at or near the throat [3], [4], [5]. Modified gravitational dynamics can alter
the relation between the material stress tensor and the effective source of the
geometry, making it important to distinguish material energy conditions from their
effective counterparts. Finite-radius matching to an exterior vacuum spacetime also
provides a natural way to localize non-asymptotically flat wormhole interiors [6].
Einstein–Cartan gravity offers a particularly natural framework in which this
distinction can be investigated. Cartan generalized the Riemannian formulation of
gravitation by allowing the affine connection to possess an antisymmetric part,
identified with spacetime torsion [7]. The gauge-theoretic interpretation of gravity
developed by Kibble and Sciama established a direct relation between intrinsic
angular momentum and torsion [8], [9]. In the resulting Einstein–Cartan theory, the
metric is sourced by energy and momentum, whereas torsion is sourced algebraically
by the spin density of matter. Consequently, torsion does not propagate
independently in vacuum in the minimal theory, but generates local corrections
quadratic in spin after it is eliminated from the field equations [10], [11], [12]. A
convenient macroscopic description of matter carrying intrinsic spin is provided by
the Weyssenhoff fluid, originally formulated by Weyssenhoff and Raabe and
subsequently developed within a systematic variational Einstein–Cartan framework
[13], [14]. Covariant treatments of this fluid show that its averaged spin contribution
can modify the effective density and pressures while preserving a fluid description at
macroscopic scales [15].
The possible role of spin and torsion in sustaining wormhole geometries has
consequently received increasing attention. Einstein–Cartan solutions supported
without conventional phantom fields have been obtained for nonminimally coupled
scalar and electromagnetic sectors [16]. Static wormholes sourced by a Weyssenhoff
fluid have been shown to admit parameter regions in which the ordinary material
variables satisfy the standard energy conditions even though the effective source
governing the throat violates the corresponding effective condition [17]. Dynamical
extensions have also been constructed in which spin corrections influence the
temporal evolution of the throat [18]. More recently, Yukawa–Casimir matter
distributions have been considered in Einstein–Cartan gravity together with energy-
condition, equilibrium, embedding, tidal, and optical diagnostics [19]. Related
compact-object studies in alternative gravitational frameworks have also shown that
departures from general relativity can modify orbital dynamics and quasi-periodic-
oscillation phenomenology, including in Horndeski and Hu–Sawicki gravity [20], [21].

Galactic rotation curves provide one of the principal observational probes of the dark-
matter distribution [22], [23], [24]. In particular, the SPARC database combines
spatially resolved rotation curves with homogeneous near-infrared photometry for a
large sample of disk galaxies and therefore provides a useful basis for constraining
halo models [25]. A wide variety of spherical halo profiles has been developed to
describe galactic dark-matter distributions, including the Einasto, NFW, Burkert, and
more general double-power-law families [26], [27], [28], [29], [30], [31]. In the
present work we consider two spherical profiles with complementary properties. The
Dekel–Zhao (DZ) profile provides a flexible description of both cuspy and cored inner
halo structures while retaining analytical expressions for several dynamical
quantities [32]. The Hernquist profile, by contrast, is a finite-mass spherical model
with simple closed-form density, enclosed-mass, and potential functions [33], [34].
These properties make the two profiles particularly convenient for propagating
rotation-curve constraints into an Einstein–Cartan wormhole reconstruction.
Dark-matter profiles have already been employed as candidate sources for galactic
wormholes. Wormhole geometries compatible with halo regions and approximately
flat galactic rotation curves were studied in general relativity by Rahaman and
collaborators [35], [36], and their lensing properties were considered by Kuhfittig
[37]. Bose–Einstein-condensate and other halo models have also been examined as
possible wormhole sources [38], [39]. More recently, the Dekel–Zhao profile has been
used to construct wormholes in general relativity, generalized geometry–matter
coupling, symmetric teleparallel gravity, and quantum-improved gravity [40], [41],
[42], [43]. The Hernquist profile has likewise been proposed as a source for galactic
wormhole configurations [44]. Photon dynamics and shadow morphology have
additionally been explored for slowly rotating wormholes immersed in cuspy and
cored dark-matter environments [45]. Complementary compact-object studies have
also examined how surrounding dark-matter distributions affect particle dynamics
and oscillatory observables, including Schwarzschild-type geometries embedded in
Dehnen-type and perfect-fluid dark-matter environments [46], [47]. Despite this
progress, most wormhole studies select representative halo parameters directly; the
uncertainties of observationally fitted rotation-curve parameters are rarely
propagated into the reconstructed wormhole geometry.
The optical behavior of wormholes provides an additional means of characterizing
their geometry. Light propagation in the Ellis wormhole was investigated in early
studies of geometrical optics [48], and subsequent analyses derived weak- and
strong-field deflection angles for static wormholes [49], [50]. Lensing in scalar–tensor
wormholes has also illustrated how the matter sector and the energy conditions
affect observable photon trajectories [51]. More generally, the logarithmic strong-
deflection formalism developed for static spherical spacetimes provides a systematic
method for extracting critical impact parameters and relativistic-image observables
[52]. Strong-lensing studies have exhibited critical-curve structures in scalar-field
spacetimes [53], while the geometric theory of photon surfaces clarifies the role of
circular null trajectories in static spacetimes [54]. Related compact-object studies
have examined photon motion and shadows of regular black holes, weak
gravitational lensing in quantum-corrected geometries, and plasma lensing around
short-hairy black holes [55], [56], [57]. For a galactic redshift potential inferred from a
nonrelativistic rotation curve, an ordinary off-throat photon-sphere condition is not
generally expected to be satisfied. The relevant critical orbit may instead be

Motivated by these considerations, the present work develops a SPARC-calibrated,
model-conditioned reconstruction of static galactic wormholes in Einstein–Cartan
gravity. We fit both the Dekel–Zhao and Hernquist halo models to the rotation curves
of NGC3198 and DDO154 and propagate joint posterior samples, preserving their
parameter covariance, into the wormhole sector. For the primary reconstruction we
adopt the mildly preferred Hernquist posterior for NGC3198 and the DZ posterior for
DDO154 marginalized over an intrinsic velocity-scatter parameter. The alternative
halo family is also propagated for each galaxy as a discrete model-dependence test.
Following earlier phenomenological constructions in which galactic dark-matter
profiles are employed as matter sources for wormhole geometries [35], [36], [39], the
spherical halo density is employed here as a phenomenological material source for
the Weyssenhoff sector. The halo circular velocity determines the spherical
contribution to the redshift potential through the circular-geodesic relation derived
in Section 3.2. These identifications, together with the spin-profile closure introduced
below and the phenomenological throat scale , constitute explicit
modeling assumptions rather than quantities directly measured by SPARC.
Finite-radius localization of a wormhole interior by matching to an exterior vacuum
geometry has standard precedent [6]. In the present construction, however, the
specific choice  is an observationally motivated prescription
introduced to avoid extrapolating the fitted halo beyond the last measured rotation-
curve point. The dimensionless throat-spin amplitude is then determined by smooth
matching of the Levi–Civita metric sector to a Schwarzschild exterior.
The complete halo-posterior uncertainty is propagated into the shape function, flare-
out condition, material and effective energy conditions, exterior mass, critical impact
parameter, and local strong-deflection coefficient. We additionally quantify the
sensitivity of the redshift potential to the omitted baryonic contribution and test the
dependence of the Einstein–Cartan reconstruction on the discrete choice of halo
profile. Finally, the throat-supported circular null orbit and its strong-deflection limit
are analyzed analytically and numerically.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 summarizes the Einstein–Cartan field
equations, the Weyssenhoff spin sector, and the static wormhole geometry. Section 3
describes the SPARC data, halo models, Bayesian likelihood, posterior constraints, and
halo-derived redshift potentials. Section 4 develops the posterior-matched shape
function, geometrical admissibility conditions, material and effective energy
conditions, and the smooth metric-sector Schwarzschild junction. The throat light
ring and strong gravitational deflection are studied in Section 5. The numerical
results and their physical interpretation are presented in Sections 6, and 7
summarizes the main conclusions.
