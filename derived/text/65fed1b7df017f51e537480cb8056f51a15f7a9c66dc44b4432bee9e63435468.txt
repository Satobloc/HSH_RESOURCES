Abstract:This paper studies the numerical consequences of a fixed compact spectral
datum, taken here to be the Laplace and Dirac spectra ofRP 3, together with a specified
scale-flow prescription. The structural content supported by this datum, namely the gauge
algebra, the fermion representation content, and the positive colour-singlet scalar scale,
is recorded in the companion paper, and the present paper evaluates its numerical con-
sequences. The calculation closes the dimensionless window capacity and evaluates a set
of effective couplings, mass ratios, and low-energy scales. After the structural datum and
the closure prescriptions are fixed, no continuously adjusted phenomenological parameter
is introduced in the numerical chain. The resulting quantities are compared with represen-
tative Standard Model and cosmological reference values, and the deviations are reported
together with the sensitivity to the main closure choices. The deviations are central-value
comparisons rather than a global statistical fit. The results are scheme-conditioned conse-
quences of the spectral construction, with core outputs and downstream phenomenological
implications distinguished by the tier classification.
Keywords:spectral datum, compact internal space, effective couplings, scale flow, posi-
tive scalar mass, numerical evaluation

1 Introduction
The companion paper [1] develops a spectral approach to gauge structure and to a posi-
tive colour-singlet scalar mass. Its basic object is a discrete spectrum with multiplicities,
and physical quantities are defined as convergent spectral sums over that spectrum. Its
main structural statement is a classification inside a specified admissible closure class: the
chiral carrier, compact internal spectrum, anomaly-free determinant condition, minimal
non-redundant zero-mode completion, and endpoint spectral-sum closure define the class.
Within this class the internal-space geometry, gauge algebra, four-dimensional carrier and
its 3 + 1 reconstruction, fermion representation content, and positive colour-singlet scalar
mass are selected. The present paper adopts that spectral datum and evaluates the nu-
merical output of the scale-flow prescription built on it. The resulting values provide the
quantitative realisation and observational comparison of the construction.
The gravitational and operator-selection parts of the construction were developed in
Refs. [2, 3]. The present paper uses the compact spectral datum and structural closures of
the companion paper, and evaluates the resulting fixed point, couplings, mass scales, and
cosmological endpoint quantities.
The datum is the Laplace and Dirac spectrum of a compact three-dimensional internal
space of constant positive curvature, the real projective spaceRP 3 =S 3/Z2. The core
inputs of the computation are therefore two-fold. They are this spectrum, inherited from
the companion paper, and the closure prescriptions recorded in Section 2, namely the
window profile, the pole criterion, the transverse-traceless kernel, and the normalisation
conventions. Changing one of these prescriptions is treated as a counterfactual replacement

of the closure, not as a continuous perturbation of a fitted parameter inside the same
calculation. The core outputs are the dimensionless window capacitykLthat closes the
fixed point, the generation count, the effective gauge-coupling normalisations, the primary
fermion mass ratios, and the electroweak scale. These are the quantities whose status is
most direct within the construction.
Beyond these core outputs, the same chain yields the QCD scale, proton and glueball
mass estimates, CKM and CP relations, and cosmological fractions. These form the down-
stream tests of the extended construction. The baryon asymmetry, dark-energy density,
acceleration scale, and primordial helium fraction follow the effective relations stated in
their defining sections and are classified as Tier D phenomenological implications.
The construction can be compared with other spectral and algebraic approaches.
Connes noncommutative geometry takes a spectral triple as the primitive object and derives
the action from the spectral action principle [4–6]. The present construction shares the
use of spectral data as carriers of geometric and physical information, but defines physical
quantities directly as convergent spectral sums without passing through an action func-
tional, so the two approaches are complementary. The algebraic classification of the finite
geometry underlying the Standard Model [7, 8] provides a natural consistency condition,
and the heat-kernel expansion of the spectral action [6, 9] is structurally the same object
that the spectral sums evaluate, here obtained from the spectrum rather than from the
action. Algebraic quantum field theory likewise avoids a path-integral measure [10, 11],
but reconstructs observables from nets of local algebras [10, 12], whereas here the algebraic
structure is induced from the spectrum. The isomorphism between the spectral-scale flow
and the standard renormalisation group equations, recorded in Appendix D.2, provides
a bridge between the two [13, 14]. These references locate the construction in relation to
existing spectral and algebraic formalisms; the numerical outputs themselves are computed
in Sections 5–9 and compared with reference values in Section 10.
The computation is organised around one dimensionful observational anchor, the New-
ton constantG N, which converts the dimensionless spectral output into physical units. Ev-
ery mass scale descends from this anchor through the spectral geometry. The dimensionless
chain is fixed by the adopted spectral datum, the window profile, the pole criterion, the
normalisation conventions, and the stated downstream effective relations. Appendix D and
Section 10.5 quantify the response to counterfactual changes of these defining choices.

served anchor, and the identityG N = 1/(8πM 2

11 Discussion and conclusion
This paper has evaluated the spectral construction whose structural content is recorded
in the companion paper [1]. The calculation begins with the Laplace and Dirac spectrum
ofRP 3, the structural constants and closure prescriptions of Section 2, and the Newton
constant as its dimensional anchor. Appendix B records the resulting output set, while
Appendix D quantifies its dependence on the discrete and functional prescriptions.
The most direct outputs are the window-capacity fixed pointkL= 2.49353, the asso-
ciated generation count, the torsion modulusτ= 1/50, and the effective gauge-coupling
normalisations. The fermion mass ratios, electroweak scale, and low-energy scales descend
from these quantities in an acyclic chain, withG N converting the dimensionless output
into physical units. Their comparison with reference values therefore tests the specified
window, pole, and matching prescriptions as a single correlated construction.
The Standard-Model field content is recovered from the spectral datum through zero-
mode projection. The gauge fields are the Kaluza–Klein zero modes of the internal connec-
tion, their kinetic terms are the zero-mode projection of the field-strength square, and their
self-interaction vertices are the corresponding cubic and quartic overlaps. The fermions are
the retained spinor modes, with kinetic terms supplied by the restricted Dirac operator and
gauge interactions by the connection–current overlap. The scalar is the trace-zero mode,
with its kinetic term supplied by the projected Laplacian and its Yukawa couplings by
two-spinor–scalar overlap integrals. This is the same overlap structureO e that enters the
charged-fermion masses of Section C.9. The resulting four-dimensional Lagrangian has the
Standard-Model form: the zero modes supply the fields and the overlap integrals supply
the couplings.
The comparison reports signed central-value residuals. The three gauge couplings agree
with their reference values to better than 0.01%, the fine-structure constant to 0.20%, the
electroweak scale to 0.01%, and the electroweak precision observables of Section 10.6 to
within a percent. The fermion mass ratios, neutrino mass-squared splittings, and down-
stream cosmological entries also lie within a percent, while the QCD scale differs by about
one percent. The larger residuals trace respectively to the scale-invariant bottom anchor,
the two-loop QCD running, and the spectral-level approximation of the lightest glueball.
The neutrino comparison uses the absolute mass texture together with the finite spectral-
tilt propagation relation of Appendix C.10.
The core computation closes at the quantities of Sections 5, 6, 7, and 9. The absolute
neutrino scale and its two oscillation splittings are Tier B outputs of the same numerical
chain. The homogeneous cosmological quantities of Section 8 are downstream closure
evaluations, and their compressed propagation entries are comparison diagnostics. The
PMNS and CP relations and the primordial abundances are the formal Tier D extensions
recorded in Appendices E and F.

The computation is carried by a small set of structural constants and a set of quantities

value, the reference value where one exists, the signed deviation, a short record of the
derivation, and the tier (A–D) defined in Table 4 of Section 10. One quantity, the Newton
constant, is the observed anchor of Section 2.1. The remaining quantities are computed, and
none is fitted. The computed values are conditional on the structural choices recorded in the
companion paper and on the closure prescriptions of Section 2. The comparison reports the
signed central-value deviations of these values against reference values that are kept outside
the closure chain and are never inputs to the computation. These signed percentages are
bookkeeping residuals for comparison only; they do not include experimental covariance
matrices, theoretical scheme uncertainties, or a look-elsewhere correction, and should not
be read as statistical significances. Where a direct measurement exists the reference value
is the Particle Data Group central value. Where the comparison is made at the emergence
scale, as for the gauge couplingsgi(MG), the reference value is the two-loop Standard-Model
extrapolation used in this paper, so the two sides are compared at the same scale.
