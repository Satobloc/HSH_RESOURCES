Abstract
Chiral nanocomposites have attracted wide attention due to their fascinating size-
dependent characteristics. Such nanomaterials could be precisely synthesized by 
templates from random-coiled unimolecular micelles with poor chiroptical activities. 
Helical polymers possessing secondary chirality usually displayed the stronger chiral 
induction than random-coiled polymers. However, the controllable preparation of 
helical unimolecular micelles still remains challenging, which hindered the 
investigation into the effect of morphology and size on chiroptical properties. In this 
study, multi-armed star-shaped poly(phenylacetylene)s (PPAs) with target composition, 
molecular weight and size, were first precisely prepared from the novel multi-sited 
initiator via living polymerization. Based on acid-base interactions, chiral transfer from 
PPAs to various dyes was accomplished in composite membranes and suspension. 
Notably, the chiral nanocomposites consisting of star-shaped PPAs displayed enhanced 
helical stability, Cotton effect and circularly polarized luminescence (CPL) compared 
with linear analogues. Furthermore, this system displayed the triple-mode 
(photoluminescence, circular dichroism and CPL) detection towards the volatile 
organic compound for environmental monitoring.
Keywords: Poly(phenylacetylene)s; Unimolecular micelles; Chiral induction;

Introduction
Chirality, as one of the most ubiquitous structural properties in nature, has been 
found the widespread applications including biomedicine 1, 2, asymmetric catalysis 3-5, 
and optoelectronic devices6-9. For the preparation of chiral nanomaterials, the template 
method as highly selective methodology was widely utilized 10. Such methods were 
classified into hard-template and soft-template approaches. The hard templates derived 
from metal-organic frameworks 11 and silica 12, 13 , could exhibit enhanced structural 
stability. Meanwhile, soft templates (such as DNA 14, proteins 15, and polymers 16) 
display the satisfactory chiral tunability and stimuli-responsiveness to the external 
environmental factors. 
Compared to other soft templates, polymer-based templates usually exhibit 
satisfactory designability, and their corresponding self-assembly behavior paved the 
way for chiral nanomaterials17-19. Such assemblies of linear polymers required specific 
conditions and exhibits poor structural stability and weak size-controllability. Notably, 
star-shaped unimolecular micelles with each arm covalently linked to the core-structure, 
showed structural stability even under harsh conditions. By living polymerization, such 
star-shaped unimolecular micelles act as the dimension- and size-controllable 
nanoreactors, representing an ideal platform for the precise construction of 
nanomaterials20, 21 . If chiral monomers were employed, the corresponding chiral 
templates achieved the production of chiral nanocomposites with target size and 
morphology22, 23. However, the previous work mainly focused on polymers with point 
chirality and random-coiled mainchain. Thus, the chiral transfer from templates to 
nanostructures was not efficient enough.
Helical polymers containing secondary chirality, are believed to outperform 
random-coiled polymers in the field of chiral induction, enantio-separation, and 
asymmetric catalysis 24-26. The poly(phenylacetylene)s (PPAs) is one of the 
representative helical polymers, which have attracted significant attention due to their 
dynamic helicity, living polymerization, and application potential 27-29. However, only 
chiral three-armed PPAs could be obtained via living polymerization, displaying

construction of multi-armed star-shaped PPA-based templates is still challenging and

In this study, chiral multi-armed star-shaped block PPAs were precisely prepared, 
through living polymerization from multi-sited initiators. To the best of our knowledge, 
this is the first reported chiral multi-armed PPA-based unimolecular micelles 
possessing target molecular weight and size. The relationship between chemical 
structures, morphology, molecular weight and chiroptical activities of PPAs was 
figured out, while the chiral induction from PPA-based templates towards aggregation-
induced emission (AIE) dyes and circularly polarized luminescence (CPL) were also 
systematically studied. Furthermore, the CPL-system exhibited higher sensitivity 
towards volatile organic compounds than the fluorescent sensor, indicating the 
application potential of environmental monitoring.
Results and Discussion
The initiator with multiple sites plays an important role in the synthesis of PPA-
based unimolecular micelles. Firstly, the thiol derivative and allyl-decorated β-
cyclodextrin (PP-β-CD) was synthesized by condensation and nucleophilic substitution, 
respectively (Figure S1-S2). Based on click reaction, the phenylboronic acid pinacol 
ester was successfully introduced into β-cyclodextrin as the multi-sited initiator NPBA-
β-CD. The grafting yield of NPBA-β-CD was estimated as 90% (Figure S3). Besides, 
various chiral (MSAla, MSLac, MSAsp and MRAsp) and achiral phenylacetylene 
derivatives (MT) bearing tert-butyl esters were also prepared with high yields (Figure 
S4-S7).

To investigate the structural effect on circular dichroism (CD), the CD 
measurement of monomers and polymers was conducted. For the chiral monomer 
MSAla in Figure 1a, only slight CD signal was observed below 300 nm. After 
polymerization, the corresponding star-shaped PSAla50 displayed new absorption 
above 300 nm, attributing to the conjugated backbone. Meanwhile, the enhanced 
Cotton effect in the range of 300-400 nm implied the formation of predominantly one-
handed helical main-chain (gCD = 2.9×10⁻³). For the achiral homopolymers, the silent-
CD suggested that the helical conformation was induced by chiral pendants (Figure 1b).
After the introduction of achiral P4H block (Figure 1c), copolymer PSAla50-b-
P4H showed weaker Cotton effect (gCD = 9.9×10-4) than PSAla50, due to the decreased 
content of chiral repeating units. When chiral units were transformed to lactic acid or 
aspartic acid, the weaker chiroptical activities of PSLac50-b-P4H (gCD = 6.9×10⁻⁴) and 
PSAsp50-b-P4H (gCD = 1.3×10⁻⁴) were observed. The enhanced CD of PSAla50-b-P4H 
may be attributed to: (1) the better chiral transfer from pendant to mainchain as the 
stronger optical activity of MSAla; (2) the helical conformation was stabilized by 
intramolecular hydrogen bonds in PSAla50-b-P4H 35, 36 . Increasing the degree of 
polymerization, PSAsp100-b-P4H exhibited similar CD spectrum to PSAsp50-b-P4H, 
indicating the negligible effect of molecular weight on chiroptical properties. When the 
configuration of pendants was changed (PSAsp50-b-P4H and PRAsp50-b-P4H), the 
mirror-imaged Cotton spectra suggested that the optical activity of backbones 
originates from the configuration of side groups31. After deprotection, the CD intensity 
of star-shaped copolymers was further decreased (PSAspa50-b-P4H, g CD = -4.8×10⁻⁴; 
PSAlaa50-b-P4H, g CD = -1.1×10⁻⁴; PSLaca50-b-P4H, g CD = -5.3×10⁻⁵). Without the 
group of tert-butyl esters, the less steric hinderance of pendants was believed to destroy 
the helical screw-sense in mainchain (Figure 1d) 37.

the mono-sited initiator (Figure S22 & Table S2). In Figure S23a, the dissymmetry

Apart from the chiral membrane, the chiral suspension was also investigated. In 
detailed, the THF solution of PPA-based unimolecular micelles and 1-AE was injected 
into the aqueous solution of cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) surfactant. 
Following by heating at 353 K to evaporate THF solvent, the CPL suspension was 
obtained with the highest g lum as -2.5×10-3 (Figure S31-S33). For 1-CE and 4-CE, the 
relative strong solvation of AIE dyes went against the photoluminescence and the CPL 
was hardly characterized (Figure S34).
In addition, volatile organic compounds (VOCs) with low boiling points and vapor 
pressure are the representative environmental pollutants, which would cause headaches, 
nausea, vomiting, and other disease in our living room45, 46. Organic n-propylamine was 
selected as the analyte for VOC detection. Under the fumigation of n-propylamines, the 
decreased PL signals of PMMA-based membranes was observed with time (Figure 4a). 
That was because n-propylamines acted as the plasticizer to enhance the molecular 
motion of dyes and weaken their AIE properties in composite membranes. Besides, n-
propylamines could compete with AIE dyes on the acid-base interactions between 
amines and PPAs. And the induced Cotton effect of dyes around 325 nm was weakened, 
while the characteristic absorption peak still maintained (Figure 4b). Actually, the CPL 
performance was influenced by both AIE and acid-base interactions (Figure 4c). Thus, 
CPL-system exhibited enhanced sensitivity towards VOCs compared with PL and CD 
detection (Figure 4d), which exhibited the potential in environmental monitoring.

Conclusion
In this study, a series of multi-armed star-shaped poly(phenylacetylene) 
derivatives were precisely designed and synthesized, through Rh-complex catalysts. 
The controllable and living characteristics of polymerization was accomplished on the 
novel multi-sited initiator NPBA-β-CD. By regulating the feed ratio, the tailor-made 
PPA-based unimolecular micelles with target molecular weight and size could be 
obtained with high yields. The β-CD core had the stabilizing effect on the helical 
structure of conjugated backbones. Compared with linear analogues, star-shaped PPAs 
displayed the enhanced Cotton effect and larger activation energy of helix inversion. 
After deprotection, the carboxyl-modified unimolecular micelles were employed as 
chiral nanoreactors to prepare AIE composites with induced CD and CPL properties, in 
the form of PMMA-based membranes and CTAB-stabilized suspension. Due to the 
star-shaped morphology, the enhanced chiral induction of helical unimolecular micelles
