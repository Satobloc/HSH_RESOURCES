Abstract
The Boltzmann Brain (BB) problem challenges the coherence of cosmological models in which
extremely rare entropy-lowering fluctuations eventually dominate over ordinary observers. Re-
cent finite-dimensional proposals suppress BB formation dynamically by restricting the universe
to a periodic, non-ergodic orbit in Hilbert space. In this work we develop a more structural
resolution within the Quantum Hodon Incidence Structure (QHIS), a discrete, operationally
defined substrate of elementary informational events referred to as hodons. QHIS models im-
pose a holographic-style capacity bound on every operational region, ensuring that its accessible
informational content is finite and determined solely by the combinatorial properties of its in-
cidence boundary. When combined with a commensurable-spectrum global Hamiltonian, this
constraint yields a cosmology that is periodic, non-ergodic, and fundamentally capacity-limited:
ordinary observers fit within the modest informational budgets of suitable regions, whereas
Boltzmann-Brain-level observers require correlation structures far exceeding the capacity of any
region and are therefore excluded in principle.
We construct explicit toy models illustrating (i) minimal observers supported by small op-
erational regions, (ii) Page-curve-like behavior arising from finite correlation capacity across
incidence boundaries, and (iii) a small- M periodic QHIS cosmology in which no region attains
the complexity required for BB formation. These results suggest that informational granularity,
rather than geometric volume or temporal duration, determines the space of physically realizable
observers. From a quantum-gravity perspective, the QHIS capacity bound provides a discrete,
pre-geometric analogue of holographic entropy constraints and may serve as a structural precur-
sor to generalized entropy in the QES/island framework. In such universes, ordinary observers
arise naturally, while Boltzmann Brains are excluded by the fundamental informational structure
of the world.
Keywords: Quantum gravity; holographic entropy bounds; informational capacity; Boltzmann Brains;
Page curve; discrete pre-geometric models; commensurable-spectrum Hamiltonian; non-ergodic evolution;
incidence structures; operational regions; correlation capacity; emergent geometry; generalized entropy;
quantum extremal surfaces.

curve-like profile thus becomes an operational signature of finite informational capacity, and the
mechanism that limits entanglement growth also limits the complexity of observers. In this way, the
capacity-limited Page curve and the structural exclusion of Boltzmann Brains arise from a single
underlying principle.
When a commensurable-spectrum Hamiltonian is imposed on the global QHIS Hilbert space,

problem is reframed as a question about the granularity of observers permitted by the informational

2 Background
This section reviews the three ingredients on which the present work builds: (i) the Boltzmann
Brain problem in semiclassical cosmology, (ii) Carroll’s commensurable-spectrum proposal for a
finite-dimensional, exactly periodic universe, and (iii) the Quantum Hodon Incidence Structure
(QHIS) framework, which provides a discrete, operational foundation for information flow and
emergent geometry. These elements supply the conceptual tools needed for the capacity-limited,
periodic cosmology developed in later sections.
2.1 Boltzmann Brains in semiclassical cosmology
In standard ΛCDM cosmology, the late-time universe approaches a quasi-de Sitter phase with hori-
zon temperatureTdS and finite entropySdS. Quantum and thermal fluctuations in this background
can, in principle, generate highly ordered structures with extremely small probability. Among these,
the most conceptually troubling are Boltzmann Brains (BBs): spontaneously assembled, self-aware
subsystems arising from rare entropy-lowering fluctuations. Although the probability of forming a
BB is exponentially suppressed, the de Sitter epoch persists for exponentially long timescales. If
the product of duration and volume is sufficiently large, the expected number of BBs exceeds the
number of ordinary observers produced by cosmological evolution.
This leads to the well-known paradox: in a sufficiently long-lived de Sitter universe, the “typical
observer” is a BB rather than an evolved, environmentally embedded observer. Such a universe
is empirically incoherent, since BBs possess disordered memories and atypical observational his-
tories. Avoiding BB domination has therefore become a consistency requirement for any viable
cosmological model.

2.3 The QHIS framework
The Quantum Hodon Incidence Structure (QHIS) framework provides a discrete, operationally
grounded model of information flow on a pre-geometric substrate. Its mathematical structure is
closely aligned with several approaches to quantum gravity in which causal relations and finite
information content play foundational roles. The directed incidence relations of a QHIS parallel
the causal order of causal set theory, while the assignment of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
to links resembles spin-network and tensor-network constructions. The holographic density B
plays a role analogous to the Bousso bound or covariant entropy bound, but formulated in purely
combinatorial terms without reference to geometric area. This makes QHIS compatible with both
pre-geometric and semiclassical regimes, and positions it naturally within the broader landscape of
discrete quantum gravity models.

3 Observers as Informational Patterns in QHIS
In conventional cosmology, the notion of an “observer” is typically treated as an external idealiza-
tion: a coarse-grained subsystem with sufficient internal structure to register, store, and process
information. Within the QHIS framework, however, observers must be described internally as pat-
terns of informational degrees of freedom distributed across a finite set of hodons. This perspective
is closely aligned with operational and information-theoretic approaches to quantum gravity, in
which observers are not added to the theory but emerge as dynamically coherent substructures
of the underlying substrate. The present section develops an operational notion of an observer
in QHIS, identifies a minimal informational threshold for observerhood, and contrasts it with the
vastly larger complexity required for a Boltzmann Brain-level observer. These distinctions play a
central role in the capacity-limited cosmology constructed in later sections.

3.1 Observers as structured QHIS subregions
Let R⊂ P be an operationally defined subset of hodons. The quantum state accessible to an
observer confined to R is the reduced density operator (2.7). In QHIS, an observer is not identified
with a single hodon, but with a structured pattern of correlations distributed across many hodons.
This reflects the broader view, common in quantum-gravitational and quantum-informational ap-
proaches, that observers correspond to dynamically stable, causally-embedded subsystems rather
than fundamental entities.
A QHIS observer is characterized by:
1. Internal differentiation: the ability to realize at least two distinguishable internal states
corresponding to different observational outcomes.
2. Memory: stable correlations among hodons in R that encode a short history of past inputs.
3. Update dynamics: conditional dependence of future internal states on both internal mem-
ory and incoming signals through the incidence boundary ∂R.
4. Causal coherence: compatibility of the above structures with the directed incidence rela-
tions and causal weights of the QHIS, ensuring that the observer is embedded consistently
within the underlying causal substrate.
These criteria ensure that an observer in QHIS is a physically realizable, causally coherent
subsystem capable of registering, processing, and responding to information. In this sense, QHIS
provides a discrete analogue of the “observer as subsystem” paradigm familiar from tensor-network
models, quantum error-correcting codes, and entanglement-based approaches to emergent geometry.

From the perspective of QHIS, this separation is crucial: the holographic capacity bound of

support BB-level observers. In a capacity-limited QHIS cosmology, no such regions exist. This
provides a structural, rather than dynamical, exclusion of BBs and aligns with the broader theme
in quantum gravity that the entanglement structure of a region constrains the types of subsystems
it can support.
4 Capacity-Limited Regions and Structural Exclusion of Boltz-
mann Brains
The QHIS framework imposes a finite informational capacity on every operationally defined region
of the universe. This section develops the consequences of this constraint for the existence of
observers. Under mild assumptions, the holographic capacity bound implies a strict upper limit on
the complexity of any observer that can be supported within a region R. When this upper limit
lies only slightly above the minimal threshold for observerhood, Boltzmann Brain-level observers
become structurally impossible . This yields a capacity-based resolution of the BB problem that
does not rely on dynamical timescale arguments and is naturally aligned with the broader theme
in quantum gravity that entanglement structure constrains the types of subsystems a region can
support.
4.1 Holographic capacity of an operational region
LetR⊂P be an operationally defined subset of hodons. The incidence boundary∂R is the minimal
set of links whose removal eliminates all positive-propensity paths between R and its complement.
In QHIS, the information accessible to observers confined to R is determined not by its volume or
temporal duration, but by the combinatorial structure of its boundary, as encoded in the capacity
definition (Eq. (2.6)).
Crucially, the capacity I(R) is finite for every operational region. No subsystem of the uni-
verse can support arbitrarily large amounts of structured information, regardless of cosmological
timescales. This finiteness is a structural feature of the QHIS substrate and does not depend on
any semiclassical approximation.

LetS(ρR) denote the von Neumann entropy of the reduced state accessible to an observer confined

larger than Sobs,min, then R can support minimal or near-minimal observers but cannot support
observers of significantly greater complexity.
From a quantum gravity perspective, this window plays a role analogous to the entanglement
constraints that determine which subsystems can emerge as semiclassical observers in holographic
or tensor-network models. The QHIS formulation makes this constraint explicit in a pre-geometric
setting.
4.3 Boltzmann Brains and capacity thresholds
In semiclassical cosmology, the enormous Hilbert-space dimension of a de Sitter horizon patch per-
mits a BB in principle, albeit with extremely small probability. In QHIS, however, the holographic
capacity bound implies that a BB can exist in R only if
I(R)≥SBB. (4.2)
If no operational region satisfies Eq. (4.2), then BB-level observers are structurally excluded : the
informational substrate of the universe simply does not contain regions with sufficient capacity to
support the correlations required for a BB. This exclusion is independent of global dynamics. Even
if the universe were to persist for arbitrarily long times, or even if the global state were ergodic,
BBs could not arise in any region whose capacity lies below SBB.
This structural exclusion mirrors the logic of holographic entropy bounds in semiclassical grav-
ity: certain high-complexity configurations are forbidden not because they are dynamically improb-
able, but because they violate fundamental capacity constraints of the underlying substrate.

5 Periodic QHIS Cosmology
This section develops a cosmological model in which the global QHIS state evolves periodically
under a commensurable-spectrum Hamiltonian. The resulting universe is non-ergodic, cyclic, and
capacity-limited: it traverses a finite sequence of macrostates, each of which respects the holographic
capacity constraints of Sec. 4. Such a model naturally supports ordinary observers while structurally
excluding Boltzmann Brains. We also construct a capacity-limited Page curve that arises without
reference to semiclassical geometry or quantum extremal surfaces, illustrating how finite correlation
capacity alone can reproduce the qualitative features of black-hole evaporation.
From a quantum gravity perspective, the periodic evolution generated by a commensurable-
spectrum Hamiltonian provides a discrete analogue of relational or emergent time: the “clock” of
the universe is encoded in its internal correlations rather than in an external time parameter. This
aligns with approaches in which global time is not fundamental but arises from the dynamics of
subsystems.

7 Discussion
The constructions developed in this work suggest a shift in how cosmology may be conceptualized
when informational structure, rather than geometric structure, is taken as fundamental. In a QHIS-
based universe, the capacity of an operational region is determined not by its spatial volume or
semiclassical horizon area, but by the combinatorial properties of its incidence boundary. This shift
has significant implications for the Boltzmann Brain problem, the emergence of observers, and the
interpretation of cosmological dynamics. It also aligns naturally with quantum gravity approaches
in which causal structure, entanglement, and information flow are more primitive than geometry.
The capacity-limited Page-curve-like behavior observed in QHIS is closely related to entanglement-
entropy dynamics in semiclassical gravity. In holographic settings, the Page curve arises from the
competition between quantum extremal surfaces and island contributions. In QHIS, an analogous
saturation and purification emerge from the finite correlation capacity across incidence boundaries,
without invoking extremal surfaces or bulk geometry. This suggests that the QHIS capacity bound
may serve as a discrete precursor to the generalized entropy functional appearing in the QES/island
formula, withv(∂R) playing the role of a pre-geometric “area” andS(ρR) representing the accessible
entropy of the region.
7.1 Structural versus dynamical avoidance of Boltzmann Brains
Most existing approaches to the Boltzmann Brain problem rely on dynamical arguments: the rate
of BB nucleation, the duration of de Sitter phases, or the global measure on cosmological histories.
Carroll’s commensurable-spectrum proposal refines this strategy by restricting the universe to a
periodic, non-ergodic orbit in Hilbert space. In such a model, BBs remain possible in principle,
but the universe never evolves long enough in any direction for them to appear with appreciable
probability.
The QHIS framework provides a qualitatively different mechanism. The holographic capac-
ity bound S(ρR)≤ I(R) limits the informational complexity of any region R. If this capacity
lies only slightly above the minimal threshold for observerhood Sobs,min, then BB-level observers
are structurally impossible : their informational requirements exceed the maximal capacity of any
region, regardless of cosmological timescales or global dynamics. This yields a structural resolu-

7.2 Observer granularity as a physical parameter
A central conceptual contribution of this work is the recognition that the granularity of observers
is a physical parameter of the universe. In a capacity-limited QHIS cosmology, the informational
substrate supports only observers whose internal complexity fits within the boundary capacity of the
regions they inhabit. Minimal or near-minimal observers are permitted, whereas highly complex,
self-contained observers such as Boltzmann Brains are not.
Demonstrating that ordinary observers naturally emerge within regions whose capacities lie
only slightly above the minimal observer threshold is therefore essential. It shows that the universe
is not sterile: it supports structured, causally embedded, memory-bearing observers like ourselves.
At the same time, the same capacity constraints prevent the formation of BB-level observers, whose
internal correlation structure would require informational resources far exceeding any region’s ca-
pacity. Thus, the universe supports “small” observers but forbids “large” ones, and this asymmetry
is built into the informational substrate itself.
7.3 Relation to holography and emergent geometry
The capacity-limited nature of QHIS regions echoes familiar themes in holography, where the
entropy of a region is bounded by the area of its boundary. However, the QHIS bound is more
primitive: it applies even in non-geometric or pre-geometric regimes, and it is defined purely in
terms of incidence relations and causal weights. Geometry emerges only after coarse-graining, and
the holographic bound survives this transition.
This suggests that structural exclusion of BBs may be a generic feature of holography-inspired
theories of quantum gravity. If the fundamental substrate enforces finite informational capacity
for all operational regions, then BB-level observers may be excluded in a wide class of models,
not only in QHIS. The framework also resonates with entanglement-based approaches to emergent
geometry, where the structure of correlations determines effective spatial relations.
7.4 Non-ergodicity, Page-curve-like behavior, and the role of periodicity
The commensurable-spectrum Hamiltonian plays a complementary role. While capacity constraints
alone can exclude BBs, periodicity ensures that the global state remains confined to a finite set of
macrostates, each of which respects the capacity bounds. This prevents the universe from exploring
exotic or pathological configurations that might arise in a fully ergodic evolution.
Equally important is the emergence of Page-curve-like behavior from finite correlation capac-
ity across incidence boundaries. In semiclassical gravity, the Page curve reflects the fact that a
region cannot sustain arbitrarily large entanglement: correlations grow, saturate at a boundary-
determined maximum, and then purify. In QHIS, the same qualitative behavior arises without
reference to geometry or quantum extremal surfaces. Instead, it emerges from the finite correlation
capacity imposed by the holographic bound.
This correspondence is structural rather than analogical. The Page-curve-like profile is the
dynamical fingerprint of the same structural constraint that forbids BBs. If correlations across ∂R
cannot grow beyond a small, boundary-determined maximum, then no region can accumulate the
vast internal correlation structure required for a BB. Thus, the capacity-limited Page curve and

7.5 Implications for cosmological naturalness
Capacity-limited periodic cosmology offers a new angle on the problem of cosmological naturalness

a measure over initial conditions but is determined by the periodic orbit of the commensurable-
spectrum Hamiltonian. The “initial” state is merely one point on a closed trajectory. Moreover, the
informational capacity of regions constrains the complexity of macrostates, preventing the universe
from entering high-entropy configurations that would otherwise dominate.
7.6 Limitations and future directions
The toy models presented here are highly idealized. A full QHIS cosmology would require:
 a detailed specification of the incidence structure and causal weights,
 a derivation of the holographic density B from more fundamental principles,
 and a demonstration that realistic cosmological dynamics can emerge from QHIS under coarse-
graining.
Nevertheless, the examples illustrate that capacity-limited periodic cosmology is a viable framework
for addressing the BB problem and may shed light on deeper questions about the informational
foundations of spacetime. It would be valuable to explore whether QHIS admits a continuum
limit compatible with semiclassical gravity, whether its capacity bound can be related directly
to generalized entropy in the QES/island framework, and whether its periodic dynamics can be
embedded in a broader class of relational-time or discrete-time quantum gravity models.

8 Conclusion
The Boltzmann Brain problem has long been regarded as a deep challenge for cosmological models
containing long-lived de Sitter phases or large Hilbert spaces. In such settings, exceedingly rare
entropy-lowering fluctuations eventually dominate over ordinary observers, threatening the empir-
ical coherence of cosmology by rendering our observational situation atypical. Carroll’s proposal
of a finite-dimensional, commensurable-spectrum universe offers a dynamical loophole: exact pe-
riodicity and non-ergodicity restrict the effective duration of high-entropy epochs, suppressing BB
formation by limiting the universe’s exploration of Hilbert space.
In this work we have shown that, within the QHIS framework, a sharper and more structural
resolution is available. QHIS models impose a holographic counting bound on every operational
region, ensuring that its accessible informational capacity is finite and determined by the com-
binatorial properties of its incidence boundary. When combined with a commensurable-spectrum
global Hamiltonian, this constraint yields a cosmology that is periodic, non-ergodic, and fundamen-
tally capacity-limited. Ordinary observers, whose informational complexity is modest, fit naturally
within the allowed capacities of suitable regions. In contrast, Boltzmann Brain-level observers re-
quire correlation structures far exceeding the capacity of any region and are therefore impossible

A key insight of this work is that Page-curve-like behavior emerges naturally in QHIS from finite
correlation capacity across incidence boundaries. In semiclassical gravity, the Page curve reflects
the inability of a region to sustain arbitrarily large entanglement. In QHIS, the same qualitative
behavior arises without reference to geometry, horizons, or quantum extremal surfaces. The Page-
curve-like profile becomes the operational signature of finite informational capacity, and the same
mechanism that limits entanglement growth also limits the complexity of observers. This unifies
two phenomena—entropy saturation and observer granularity—under a single structural principle.
We illustrated these ideas with explicit toy models: a minimal observer supported by a small
hodon region, a capacity-limited Page curve, and a small- M periodic QHIS cosmology. These
examples demonstrate that ordinary observers arise naturally within the informational budgets of
capacity-limited regions, while BB-level observers cannot be realized in any region of the universe.
They also show how Page-curve-like behavior can emerge from purely informational constraints,
independent of semiclassical geometry.
The broader implication is that the informational structure of the universe may be more fun-
damental than its geometric structure. If the substrate of the universe enforces finite informational
capacity for all operational regions, then the space of physically realizable observers is sharply
constrained. This perspective suggests new avenues for addressing cosmological naturalness, the
emergence of low-entropy initial conditions, and the foundations of holography.
Several open questions remain. A full QHIS cosmology requires a detailed specification of the
incidence structure and causal weights, a deeper understanding of the holographic density B, and
a demonstration that realistic cosmological dynamics can emerge under coarse-graining. It would
also be valuable to explore whether capacity-limited periodic cosmology can be embedded within
or derived from more complete theories of quantum gravity.
Nevertheless, the results presented here indicate that capacity-limited periodic cosmology offers
a promising and conceptually robust framework in which ordinary observers arise naturally and
Boltzmann Brains are excluded by the fundamental informational structure of the world. This
structural resolution highlights the central role of informational capacity in shaping the space of
possible observers and points toward a deeper informational foundation for cosmology and quantum
gravity.
These results suggest that informational capacity may play a structural role in quantum gravity,
analogous to geometric area in semiclassical holography. The QHIS framework therefore offers a
potential bridge between discrete, pre-geometric models and entanglement-based descriptions of
emergent spacetime. Further work is needed to investigate whether QHIS admits a continuum
limit compatible with semiclassical gravity and whether its capacity bound can be related directly
to generalized entropy in the QES/island framework.
