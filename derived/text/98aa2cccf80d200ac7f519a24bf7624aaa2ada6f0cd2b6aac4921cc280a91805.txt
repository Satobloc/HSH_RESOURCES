1. Introduction 
 
1.1 Why this paper exists 
A falsifiable theory that is never conclusively tested is a debt that compounds. Paper IV 5 
published its own negative audit (P4 reversed; unification correlation is low; tradeable signal 
inverted in 3/4). Leaving those unexamined invites two bad equilibria: a critic executes it with 
inferior data and controls the narrative, or the program silently lapses and the arc’s honest -
audit reputation decays into an unfalsified-forever framework — the precise failure mode that 
McCauley (2004) and Yee (n.d.) attribute to econophysics generally. 10 
This paper closes the debt. Our data advantage is substantial: Paper IV tested its predictions on 
4 windows with 7 temperature bins each. The Thermodynamic Quantum Field Theory archive 
built in the companion diagnostic paper (Sun, 2026e) provides  311 rolling windows  (July 
2023 – August 2026) with train/validation splits and out -of-sample discipline; the live dual -
temperature system (Sun, 2026f) provides minute-frequency measurement of two independent 15 
temperature operationalizations since July 2023. 
 
1.2 Evaluation protocol 
To avoid the accusation of post -hoc criterion shifting, the checklist below serves as the pre-
specified protocol, published before the new pipelines were analyzed: 20 
• R1 (P1):  The mass –temperature relation is confirmed only if the bin -level slope γ is 
negative and the underlying m 2(T) scatter is monotone declining. A near -zero slope 
through non-monotone scatter remains a non-confirmation. 
• R2 (P2): The thermal Kramers law is evaluated separately at two aggregation bases (daily

adjudicated directly.

without a confirmation threshold; consistency across independent pipelines is the finding.

established by 𝑐𝑜𝑟𝑟(𝑇𝐶, 𝑇𝐾). 
• R6 (Signal):  The signal is judged on its live rank correlation and hit -rate asymmetry, 
against an NEI-sorted control portfolio. 35

2. Literature Review 
2.1 Falsification and the econophysics credibility problem 
The programmatic critique of physics-inspired finance — that its analogies are rhetorical rather 
than functional (Mirowski, 1989; McCauley, 2004; Yee, n.d.) — is answered only by 
predictions whose  functional forms  could fail. Paper IV’s P1 –P4 were designed  to that 15 
standard and largely failed it; this paper is the receipt. Our posture follows the replication-crisis 
literature in economics and finance (Lillo & Farmer, 2004, on the OOS fragility of scaling laws; 
Bouchaud et al., 2004, on power -law estimation risk), and  the methodological tradition of 
publishing verdicts, not just confirmations (Simmons et al., 2011; Ioannidis, 2005). Sornette’s 
LPPL program (Sornette, 2003; Sornette, Johansen & Ledoit, 1996) is the closest precedent 20 
for a physics-inspired crash hypothesis whose empirical record has been described as mixed; 
we noted in Paper IV that our P1–P4 results were “continuous with that history,” and this paper 
completes that continuity with a formal verdict. 
 
2.2 Volatility-of-volatility as a state variable 25 
The financial -economics consensus treats second -order uncertainty as first -class: Hull and 
White (1987) and Heston (1993) made volatility stochastic; Huang, Sialm, and Zhang (2024) 
show vol -of-vol, not vol, drives volatility -managed portfolio performance; Park (2024) 
documents a distinct vol-of-vol risk premium. Paper IV’s ambition was stronger — that VVIX 
is not merely a state variable but the temperature, entering functional forms (mass softening, 30 
Arrhenius rates, well degradation, RG relevance). Our verdicts speak directly to this literature: 
VVIX-conditioned state variables (NEI) retain strong pricing content (NW t = 6.35), while the 
stronger functional -form claims fail. The distinction between “V VIX is informative” and 
“VVIX is temperature” is, post-verdict, sharp.

4. Verdicts 
4.1 P1 (mass softening): REFUTED 
Paper IV required γ < 0 and monotone scatter. With 311 windows and the live minute test, the 
relation is estimated as m2 = 0.0010 + 0.0000·T with t = −0.0 — a coefficient indistinguishable 10 
from zero at roughly forty times Paper IV’s effective sample size, with the scatter remaining 
non-monotone. Verdict: the Debye-screening analogy fails in this system.  Mean-reversion 
strength of the VIX microstructure is not linearly eroded by VVIX. What would resurrect P1: 
a non-linear (threshold or regime -dependent) specification with economic justification, or an 
alternative mass estimator (e.g., jump-robust curvature) — neither currently motivated by the 15 
scatter. 
4.2 P2 (thermal Kramers law): SUPPORTED IN SIGN 
The log-crossing-rate/ΔU-T relation is positive in 100% of 311 windows at daily aggregation 
(R²  = 0.58–0.63 by year: 0.582, 0.584, 0.634, 0.586) and at R²  = 0.831 in minute event-time in 
the live pipeline (M3). The direction is thus the most robust fact in the arc. Two qualifications 20 
are material: 
1. Aggregation dependence. The measured R²  is a joint property of the law and the estimator: 
daily window OOS ≈ 0.6; minute event-time ≈ 0.84; Paper IV’s 7-bin windows 0.55–0.90 
were sampling noise around this aggregation-dependent pair. 
2. Amplification revision.  Paper IV reported extreme -hot/extreme-cold crossing -rate 25 
contrasts “of one to two orders of magnitude.” The live pipeline measures hot/cold 
amplification =  2.01×. We regard the live figure — computed with estimators on 
continuous minute data — as the better estimate and withdraw the orders -of-magnitude 
claim. The qualitative content (escape is thermally accelerated) stands; the quantitative 
drama does not. 30 
4.3 P3 (critical temperature): NON-IDENTIFIABLE AS POSED

143 (live pipeline), and a z-scale range of −1.22 to +1.73 across Paper IV’s windows. We argue 
the instability is not a power problem but an identification problem:

4.4 P4 (RG-flow reversal): REFUTED 
Paper IV observed higher -mode energy share  declining with temperature in most episodes  
windows and offered two interpretations: (a) genuine microstructure — stress concentrates 5 
dynamics in the level mode as dealers widen quotes and granular price discovery collapses; (b) 
artifact — hot bins contain fewer, more autocorrelated observations. The live pipeline, with 
different aggregation and different period, measures RG residual correlation =  0.202, which 
shown to have regime depend and drifting properties. The companion diagnostic paper’s panic 
result — T₅ declining monotonically from −0.003 (2023) to −0.128 (2026), the parallel -shift 10 
signature — supplies exactly the potential-landscape geometry that interpretation (a) requires: 
in stress, the landscape shifts in parallel and dynamics collapse onto the level coordinate. 
Verdict: P4 is refuted as a criticality claim. The VIX microstructure field does not approach 
a Wilsonian critical point under VVIX heating; it approaches a single-mode absorbing state.  
4.5 Unification: PARTIAL, WITH A STRUCTURAL CEILING 15 
The correlation between field -derived Gibbs free energy and directly computed G rises from 
Paper IV’s mean 0.024 to  0.6751 (daily; EMA variant 0. 6718; raw trading 0. 4936; cross 
0.6314; delta-unify 0.0404) under the dual -temperature pipeline. We grade this against the 
ceiling conditional on the regime specified and set by thermometer agreement, 𝑐𝑜𝑟𝑟(𝑇𝐶, 𝑇𝐾) 
= 0.8288: the observed unification (R² ≈ 0.6751) sits well below the ceiling (R² ≈ 0.8288), and 20 
the residual correlates with the illiquidity-residual NEI of Sun (2026e) (NEIr_ρ ranging −0.003 
to −0.144, sign-flipping by regime). Verdict: Paper IV’s strong claim — “G is derived, not 
posited” — is withdrawn. The weak claim — thermodynamics and field statistics share 
substantial common variance, with a measurable, economically interpretable gap — is 
affirmed. The gap is itself informative: it is the portion of market “heat” that is neither implied-25 
vol nor term-structure-implied, i.e., the liquidity-fragmentation component.

temperature program, honestly audited, has yielded one robust law, one priced state variable,
