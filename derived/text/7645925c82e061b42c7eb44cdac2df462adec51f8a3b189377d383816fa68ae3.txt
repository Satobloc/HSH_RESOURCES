Abstract
The apparent structural resemblance between atomic and planetary systems has played an important role in the
historical development and conceptual interpretation of atomic models. Although the planetary picture provides
an intuitive representation of a compact central object surrounded by smaller constituents, modern atomic physics
demonstrates that the underlying physical descriptions of atomic and planetary systems are fundamentally different. In
this work, we develop a quantitative dimensionless-scaling framework to examine the limits of the atomic–planetary
analogy through a comparison between a gold-197 atomic system and the Sun–Earth system. Characteristic length,
velocity, time, mass, energy, force, and angular-momentum scales are evaluated under explicitly stated physical
assumptions and subsequently expressed through normalized ratios. The atomic quantities derived from the classical
model are treated as characteristic scales rather than literal electron trajectories or quantum observables. For the
initial Gold-197–Sun–Earth comparison, the normalized characteristic length and velocity ratios are approximately
95.94 and 395.82, respectively, while the corresponding mass and energy ratios are approximately 1.0864 and
2.39×10 −50. These results demonstrate that an apparent structural resemblance does not imply comparable physical
scales or dynamical equivalence. The analysis is subsequently extended through sensitivity studies and comparisons
across different characteristic atomic and planetary scales in order to determine the robustness of the observed
relationships. The resulting framework provides a quantitative approach for distinguishing the positive structural
aspects of the atomic–planetary analogy from the large-scale physical differences imposed by electromagnetic
interactions, gravitation, and quantum mechanics.

1. Introduction
The analogy between atomic and planetary systems has
played an important role in the historical development of
atomic theory. In the early twentieth century, the plan-
etary picture provided a useful conceptual framework
for describing a positively charged central nucleus sur-
rounded by electrons. Bohr’s 1913 model incorporated
this picture into an early quantum description of the atom
and used classical mechanical concepts together with
quantum conditions to account for the observed hydrogen
spectrum [1, 2, 3]. Subsequent historical studies have
emphasized that the development of the old quantum
theory was closely connected with mathematical ideas
originating in celestial mechanics [4, 5, 6]. The analogy
between planetary motion and atomic motion therefore
has both historical and conceptual significance.
Despite this structural similarity, atomic and planetary
systems are not physically equivalent. Planetary motion
is predominantly governed by gravitation and can often
be described to a high degree of accuracy using classical
mechanics. Atomic systems, in contrast, require quan-
tum mechanics for their fundamental description, while
electromagnetic interactions determine their characteris-
tic electronic structure. Consequently, an electron in an
atom cannot be regarded as a classical particle following
a definite planetary orbit. The historical development
of the Bohr model itself illustrates this distinction: simi-
larities between planetary and atomic systems provided
useful guidance, but important differences between the
two systems were essential to the development of quan-
tum theory [ 4, 7]. Thus, a structural correspondence
between two physical systems does not by itself imply a
dynamical or physical equivalence.
The distinction between structural analogy and phys-
ical equivalence raises a quantitative question. If cor-
responding characteristic scales are constructed for an
atomic system and a planetary system, can their similar-
ities and differences be expressed independently of the
units used to describe them? Dimensionless quantities
provide a natural framework for addressing this ques-

tion because they permit comparisons between systems
whose absolute length, mass, velocity, and time scales
differ by many orders of magnitude. Such a framework
can also reveal whether an apparent correspondence
observed in one physical quantity is accompanied by
comparable behavior in other quantities.
In the present study, we develop a dimensionless
characteristic-scale framework for a direct comparison
between a Gold-197 atomic system and the Sun–Earth
system. Gold-197 is selected as a reference atomic
system because its nuclear charge and mass number
provide explicitly defined parameters for constructing
characteristic nuclear and atomic scales. The Sun–Earth
system provides a corresponding planetary reference
characterized by the solar mass, Earth mass, Earth–
Sun separation, and Earth’s orbital motion. Within the
adopted model, characteristic length, velocity, timescale,
mass hierarchy, interaction energy, interaction force, and
angular momentum are constructed for both systems.
A central feature of the present analysis is that the
atomic quantities obtained from the classical Coulomb-
orbit construction are not interpreted as exact quantum-
mechanical observables. They are used only as character-
istic scales within a clearly defined mathematical model.
This distinction is particularly important for Gold-197,
which is a many-electron quantum system and cannot be
represented literally by a single classical electron orbit.
The purpose of the classical construction is therefore to
establish a consistent scale for comparison rather than
to propose a classical replacement for quantum atomic
theory.
The main research gap addressed here is the lack of a
unified multi-quantity comparison in which the atomic–
planetary analogy is tested simultaneously across several
dimensionless characteristic scales. A correspondence
in one quantity, such as a mass hierarchy, does not
necessarily imply correspondence in length, velocity,
timescale, energy, force, or angular momentum. More-
over, the numerical values of such ratios can depend on
the characteristic scales adopted for the atomic system.
A meaningful assessment therefore requires not only
the calculation of the dimensionless ratios but also an
examination of their sensitivity to the underlying model
parameters.
Accordingly, the present work has two primary ob-
jectives. First, we construct and evaluate dimensionless
ratios for the seven characteristic quantities listed above
for the Gold-197–Sun–Earth comparison. Second, we
investigate the robustness of these ratios by varying
the characteristic atomic length, nuclear charge, and
mass number. This allows us to distinguish numeri-
cal correspondences that persist under changes in the
model parameters from those that arise primarily from a
particular choice of characteristic scale.
The central research question is therefore whether a
dimensionless characteristic-scale framework can pro-
vide a quantitative criterion for distinguishing structural
similarity from physical equivalence between atomic
and planetary systems. Rather than searching for a single
universal scaling factor, we examine each physical quan-
tity independently and determine the degree of scale
correspondence or separation that it exhibits. The re-
sulting framework is intended to provide a reproducible
quantitative description of the atomic– planetary analogy
while explicitly retaining the physical limitations of the
underlying classical and two-body approximations.

3. Mathematical Framework and Dimensionless
Scaling
The comparison between the Gold-197 atomic system
and the Sun–Earth system is formulated in terms of
dimensionless characteristic-scale ratios. The objective
is not to construct a transformation that maps one phys-
ical system onto the other, but to quantify the relative
separation between corresponding characteristic scales.
Because the physical meaning of a characteristic quan-
tity differs between the two systems, a single universal
normalization cannot be imposed on every observable.
Instead, each dimensionless ratio is defined from a phys-
ically corresponding pair of characteristic quantities.
This approach ensures that the normalization remains
explicit and reproducible for every comparison.
The framework considers seven characteristic quanti-
ties: length, velocity, timescale, mass hierarchy, interac-
tion energy, interaction force, and angular momentum.
All dimensional quantities are calculated first in SI units,
after which the corresponding dimensionless ratios are
constructed.

3.8. Interpretation of the Dimensionless Framework
The seven ratios defined above are intentionally inde-
pendent comparisons rather than components of a single
universal scaling factor. Consequently, agreement in one
ratio does not require agreement in any of the others.
This distinction is central to the analysis. If a normal-
ized ratio is close to unity, the result indicates numerical
similarity of the specific characteristic quantity under the
adopted normalization. It does not establish dynamical,
structural, or physical equivalence between the atomic
and planetary systems.
The resulting framework therefore provides a quanti-
tative measure of scale correspondence while retaining
the physical distinctions between the Coulomb-based
atomic reference model and the gravity-based planetary
reference model. The numerical evaluation of these
ratios is presented separately in Sec. 5, while their de-
pendence on the adopted atomic and nuclear parameters
is examined in Sec. 6.

computation is to evaluate the characteristic quantities
defined in Sec. 3, maintain consistent SI units, construct
the corresponding dimensionless ratios, and perform the
parameter-dependent sensitivity analysis described later
in the paper.
All physical constants and system parameters are
specified explicitly at the beginning of the calculation.
The Gold-197 system is defined using𝑍= 79 ,𝐴= 197 ,

7. Cross-Scale Comparative Interpretation
The results obtained in Secs. 5 and 6 can be interpreted
more meaningfully when the seven dimensionless ratios
are considered together rather than individually. The
purpose of this section is therefore not to introduce
additional calculations, but to synthesize the different
characteristic-scale comparisons and identify the pattern
that emerges across the atomic and planetary systems.
7.1. Hierarchy Versus Dynamical Scales
The most notable feature of the comparison is the differ-
ence between the mass hierarchy and the other character-
istic quantities. For the reference Gold-197–Sun–Earth
comparison, the normalized mass hierarchy is close to
unity,
(87)𝑅𝑀≈1.0864.
This indicates that the ratio between the central-body
mass and the orbiting-body mass is numerically com-
parable in the two selected systems under the adopted
definitions.
This correspondence does not, however, extend to the
characteristic dynamical scales. The velocity, timescale,
energy, force, and angular momentum ratios are substan-
tially displaced from unity. The contrast is particularly
pronounced for the timescale, energy, force, and angular-
momentum comparisons.
The result demonstrates that similarity in a dimen-
sionless hierarchy does not imply similarity in the com-
plete set of physical scales. Consequently, the atomic–
planetary analogy is better regarded as a partial structural
correspondence than as a universal scaling relation.
7.2. Near-Unity and Non-Unity Correspondence
A useful distinction can be made between numerical
correspondence and physical equivalence. A dimen-
sionless ratio close to unity indicates that two selected
characteristic quantities have comparable normalized
magnitudes. It does not establish that the corresponding
systems possess the same equations of motion, interac-
tion mechanism, or microscopic structure.
The present results provide a clear example of this
distinction. The near-unity value of𝑅𝑀 identifies a nu-
merical correspondence in the central-to-orbiting-body
mass hierarchy. In contrast, the large departures of the
other ratios from unity demonstrate that this correspon-
dence is not accompanied by a general matching of the
characteristic scales.
The length and velocity comparisons also show that
the two systems cannot be described by a single scale-
independent conversion factor. Their normalized values
remain finite and quantitatively comparable, but are not
close to unity. The timescale comparison provides an
even stronger separation between the two systems.
7.3. Interaction Scales and Physical Interpretation
The energy and force comparisons require particular care
because the two systems are governed by different inter-
actions. The atomic reference scale is constructed from
a Coulomb interaction, whereas the planetary reference
scale is constructed from Newtonian gravitation.
Therefore, the very small values obtained for the nor-
malized energy and force ratios should not be interpreted
as universal statements about the fundamental relative
strengths of electromagnetism and gravity. They quan-
tify the separation between the specific characteristic
scales defined by the present model.
Similarly, the angular-momentum comparison is based
on a classical characteristic angular momentum for the
atomic reference system. Because the electronic struc-
ture of Gold-197 is fundamentally quantum mechanical,
this quantity should not be interpreted as a quantum
angular-momentum eigenvalue.
7.4. Implications of the Sensitivity Analysis
The sensitivity analysis in Sec. 6 provides an additional
test of the interpretation. The numerical values of the
dimensionless ratios change systematically when the
characteristic atomic length, nuclear charge, or mass
number is varied. This demonstrates that the exact
numerical values are model-dependent.
Nevertheless, the principal qualitative pattern remains
pronounced throughout the explored parameter ranges:
the mass hierarchy can approach unity, whereas the
characteristic dynamical and interaction scales generally
remain strongly separated.
This distinction is important because it prevents the
reference Gold-197 result from being interpreted as a
universal property of all atomic systems. The observed
correspondence is associated with the specific charac-
teristic scales and parameters adopted in the present
model.
7.5. Structural Analogy Versus Physical Equivalence
Taken together, the seven characteristic ratios support a
clear distinction between structural analogy and physical
equivalence. The atomic and planetary systems share
a useful mathematical correspondence in the sense that

both can be represented using central-body, orbiting-

these correspondences do not produce a common scale

The framework therefore supports a restricted inter-
pretation of the atomic–planetary analogy. It can be used
to identify specific dimensionless similarities between

evidence for a general dynamical equivalence between

8. Discussion
The quantitative results obtained in this study provide a
more detailed assessment of the long-standing analogy
between atomic and planetary systems. Historically, the
planetary picture played an important role in the devel-
opment of early atomic theory, particularly in the Bohr
model. The connection between celestial mechanics and
the old quantum theory has also been examined exten-
sively from a historical and mathematical perspective
[1, 4, 5, 14]. The present analysis does not attempt to
revive the planetary model as a physical description of
the atom. Instead, it uses the analogy as a starting point
for a quantitative comparison of characteristic scales.
The most striking result is the contrast between the
normalized mass hierarchy and the other characteris-
tic quantities. For the reference Gold-197–Sun–Earth
comparison, the normalized mass hierarchy is close to
unity,
(88)R𝑀≈1.0864,
whereas the normalized energy ratio is approximately
(89)R𝐸≈2.39×10 −50.
Thus, the relative dominance of the central body
over the orbiting body can be numerically similar un-
der the adopted normalization, while the associated
interaction-energy scales differ by approximately fifty
orders of magnitude. This result demonstrates that a
correspondence in one dimensionless quantity cannot be
interpreted as evidence for a general scaling equivalence
between the two systems.
A similar distinction appears in the length and velocity
comparisons. The normalized characteristic length and
velocity ratios are approximately 95.91 and 395.82, re-
spectively. These values indicate that the corresponding
dimensionless scales are not equal to unity, although
they remain finite and can therefore be compared quanti-
tatively. The results should consequently be interpreted
as measures of relative scale separation rather than as
scaling constants that transform an atomic system into a
planetary system.
The timescale comparison provides an even stronger
distinction. The normalized characteristic timescale is
approximately

The atomic characteristic process represented by the
simplified classical model therefore occurs on a timescale
enormously shorter than the orbital timescale of the
Earth–Sun system. This large separation is consistent
with the fundamentally different characteristic scales
associated with atomic and macroscopic dynamical sys-
tems.
The force comparison should be interpreted with
particular caution. The quantity

The same caution applies to the characteristic energy

This distinction is essential because the historical

replaced the notion of definite classical electron trajec-

provided useful conceptual and mathematical guidance
but were ultimately superseded by quantum-mechanical
descriptions [4, 5, 6].
The principal contribution of the present study is
therefore the systematic organization of this distinction
through dimensionless characteristic-scale comparisons.
Rather than assigning a single qualitative verdict to
the atomic–planetary analogy, the framework identifies
which quantities exhibit numerical correspondence and
which display extreme scale separation. The results
indicate that the analogy cannot be represented by a
single scale-independent transformation between atomic
and planetary systems.
Several limitations must nevertheless be emphasized.
The atomic model uses a simplified central Coulomb
interaction and a selected characteristic atomic length.
Gold is a many-electron relativistic atom, and a com-
plete description would require an appropriate quantum-
mechanical treatment. Similarly, the Sun–Earth system
is treated as an idealized two-body system, whereas
actual planetary dynamics involve additional bodies and
perturbations. These limitations do not invalidate the
scaling exercise, but they define the scope within which
its results should be interpreted.
Future work can extend the framework by replacing
the single Gold-197 reference system with a systemati-
cally selected set of atomic systems and by comparing
them with multiple planetary systems. Such an extension
would allow the dimensionless parameter space to be
explored more comprehensively and would provide a
stronger test of whether any non-trivial scaling relation-
ships persist across different physical systems.[13]
9. Limitations and Scope
The quantitative framework developed in this study
is intentionally simplified. Its purpose is to compare
selected characteristic physical scales between an atomic
and a planetary system rather than to provide complete
dynamical models of either system. The interpretation of
the results must therefore remain within the assumptions
defined in Sec. 2.
9.1. Limitations of the Atomic Model
The atomic reference system is represented through a
central Coulomb characteristic-scale construction. This
approximation does not describe the complete electronic
structure of Gold-197. A real Gold-197 atom is a many-
electron quantum system in which electron–electron
interactions, screening, relativistic effects, and quantum-
mechanical state structure contribute to the electronic
properties.
Consequently, the characteristic velocity, timescale,
energy, force, and angular-momentum quantities con-
structed in this work should not be interpreted as exact
observables of a Gold-197 electron. They are model-
defined scales introduced to enable a controlled dimen-
sionless comparison.
The adopted characteristic atomic length, 𝑟Au =
0.144 nm, is another source of model dependence. Dif-
ferent definitions of an atomic size or characteristic
electronic length can lead to different numerical values
of the dimensionless ratios. The sensitivity analysis in
Sec. 6 partially quantifies this dependence.
9.2. Nuclear-Radius Approximation
The nuclear radius is represented using the empirical
relation
(92)𝑅𝑁 =𝑟 0𝐴1/3.
This relation provides a convenient characteristic nu-
clear scale, but it does not constitute a complete descrip-
tion of nuclear structure. The actual charge distribution
and nuclear radius can depend on nuclear deformation,
proton and neutron distributions, and the specific defini-
tion of the measured radius.
Therefore, the nuclear radius used in the present
analysis should be understood as a reference scale rather
than as a complete structural description of the Gold-197
nucleus.

9.3. Limitations of the Planetary Model
The planetary reference system is treated as an idealized
Sun–Earth two-body system. In the real Solar System,
planetary motion is affected by the gravitational pertur-
bations of other bodies, non-circular orbital motion, and
additional dynamical effects.
These effects are outside the scope of the present
characteristic- scale comparison. The two-body ap-
proximation is adopted to provide a well-defined and
reproducible planetary reference scale.
9.4. Interpretation of Energy and Force Ratios
The interaction-energy and interaction-force ratios re-
quire particular caution. The atomic quantities are
constructed from a Coulomb reference scale, whereas
the planetary quantities are constructed from Newtonian
gravitational scales.
The resulting ratios therefore represent comparisons
of the specific characteristic quantities defined in this
model. They should not be interpreted as universal
dimensionless measures of the fundamental relative
strengths of electromagnetism and gravity.
Similarly, the angular-momentum comparison uses a
classical characteristic angular momentum for the atomic
reference system. It does not represent a comparison
between a quantum angular-momentum eigenvalue and
a planetary orbital angular momentum.
9.5. Dependence on the Normalization Scheme
The dimensionless ratios depend not only on the physi-
cal parameters but also on the characteristic quantities
selected for normalization. A different physically mo-
tivated normalization scheme could therefore produce
different numerical ratios.
For this reason, the present framework should be
regarded as a specific comparative methodology rather
than as a unique universal mapping between atomic and
planetary physics. The conclusions are restricted to
the characteristic scales and normalization definitions
explicitly specified in this work.
9.6. Scope and Future Extensions
The framework can be extended by replacing the sim-
plified atomic model with quantum-mechanical charac-
teristic quantities. For example, future studies could
use experimentally determined atomic radii, effective
electronic length scales, quantum binding energies, or
expectation values of relevant observables.
A broader comparison could also include multiple
atomic species rather than Gold-197 alone. Varying
atomic number, mass number, and electronic structure
across a systematically selected set of atoms would allow
the persistence of the observed scaling behavior to be
tested over a wider parameter space.
On the planetary side, the framework could be ex-
tended to multiple planetary systems and to additional
characteristic quantities such as orbital eccentricity, or-
bital period, angular frequency, and multi-body dynami-
cal parameters.
Such extensions would provide a stronger test of
whether any dimensionless correspondences identified
in the present study are specific to the selected Gold-
197–Sun–Earth comparison or represent more general
patterns across physically distinct systems.
The scope of the present work is therefore deliber-
ately limited to establishing and testing a reproducible
characteristic-scale framework. The framework does not
claim that atomic and planetary systems obey identical
dynamics or can be transformed into one another by a
universal scaling law.
10. Conclusion
This study developed a dimensionless characteristic-
scale framework for quantitatively examining the long-
standing analogy between atomic and planetary systems.
A Gold-197 atomic reference system was compared
with the Sun–Earth system using characteristic length,
velocity, timescale, mass hierarchy, interaction energy,
interaction force, and angular momentum.
The results demonstrate that the degree of correspon-
dence depends strongly on the physical quantity consid-
ered. Among the quantities examined, the normalized
mass hierarchy is closest to unity, with
(93)𝑅𝑀≈1.0864.
This numerical correspondence, however, is not ac-
companied by a similar correspondence across the re-
maining characteristic scales. The normalized timescale,
energy, force, and angular-momentum ratios remain sep-
arated from unity by many orders of magnitude, while
the length and velocity ratios also differ substantially
from unity.
The sensitivity analysis further shows that the numer-
ical values of the dimensionless ratios depend systemati-
cally on the characteristic atomic length, nuclear charge,
and mass number. Nevertheless, the principal qualita-
tive distinction between the near-unity mass hierarchy
and the strongly separated dynamical and interaction
scales remains evident throughout the parameter ranges
explored in this study.
The central conclusion is therefore that the atomic–
planetary analogy represents a limited structural and
dimensional correspondence rather than a general phys-
ical or dynamical equivalence. A similarity in one
dimensionless characteristic quantity cannot be taken
as evidence that the underlying systems share the same
dynamics, interactions, or microscopic structure.
