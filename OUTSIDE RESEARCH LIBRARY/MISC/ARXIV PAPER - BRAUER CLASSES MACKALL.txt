REFINED INDEX OBSTRUCTIONS FOR BRAUER CLASSES ON AN ABELIAN VARIETY
EOIN MACKALL
Abstract. We produce refined index obstructions, generalizing recently constructed index obstructions due to de Jong and Perry, for topologically trivial Brauer classes on smooth and projective complex varieties. We show that our refined obstructions are more stringent than previous obstructions and, as a consequence, we produce more counterexamples to the integral Hodge conjecture. Throughout this work, we focus on algorithmic aspects of these obstructions and we illustrate many of these aspects through the concrete examples of complex abelian varieties.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background. The integration and application of Hodge theory to the area of Brauer groups and Azumaya algebras has had tremendous success in recent years. The most substantial of this success is certainly
the recent proof of the period-index conjecture for abelian threefolds due to Hotchkiss and Perry, [HP24], but there have also been new constructions of counterexamples to the integral Hodge conjecture due to Hotchkiss [Hot22] and, separately, de Jong–Perry [dJP22], that one can directly point to. Notable as well is the pioneering work of Kresch [Kre03] on representability of Brauer classes by quaternion Azumaya algebras that anticipated this development.
The point of this paper is to further elucidate the algorithmic nature of this connection that has gone relatively unnoticed or, at least, has lacked direct focus by the broader algebra community. Specifically, we focus on the explicit and exact methods of de Jong and Perry [dJP22] that enabled their constructions of new counterexamples to the integral Hodge conjecture. While this aspect of the authors’ work was not their main goal, we believe that there should be an immediate and direct appeal to researchers and users of Azumaya algebras.

1.2. Refined Obstructions. In [dJP22], the authors point out that any algebraic condition that one can impose on the generic fiber of the relative Severi–Brauer scheme for an Azumaya algebra — representing the topologically trivial Brauer class α ∈ Br(X) of a smooth projective complex variety X — can be used to deduce conditions analogous to but possibly more general than [dJP22, Theorem 5.10]. Theorem 1.1 is the culmination of a sequence of results from Section 3 of this article. Throughout this section, we focus on the particularly nice case of a very general complex abelian variety for motivation. The section culminates in an example (Example 3.8) where the refined index obstructions from Theorem 1.1 can be explicitly shown to provide stricter constraints than the index obstructions of de Jong and Perry [dJP22, Theorem 5.10] alone. 1.3. Applications. Compared to Section 3 of this article, where our focus is on building the refined index obstructions of Theorem 1.1, in the final section of this article, Section 4, we focus on the application of the algorithmic aspects of this work in more detail.
Among the results in this section, we include a simple analysis (Propo- sition 4.1) on the degrees of failure of the de Jong–Perry index obstruc- tion algorithm for topologically trivial Brauer classes α on a complex abelian variety X whose period per(α) is not coprime to (dim(X)−1)!. We also provide some data (see Figures 1–24) to the effect of how sharp this analysis is, and to how it may also apply to our new refined index obstruction algorithm.
We also include an example (Example 4.6), found with the aid of a computer, of an indecomposable topologically trivial Brauer class α ∈ Br(X) of period per(α) = 2 and index ind(α) = 4 on a very general abelian threefold X. While indecomposable Brauer classes with these invariants are most likely not new, we remark that it is interesting that they can be constructed so readily with the help of the de Jong–Perry and our refined index obstruction algorithms.
Notation.
• Given a complex algebraic variety X, we write Xan for the analytification of X.
• For a group G and a topological space Y , we write Hi(Y, G) for the singular cohomology of Y with coefficients in G.
• For a paracompact, Hausdorff, locally contractible space (such as Xan for a smooth complex variety X), we identify the derived cohomology Hi(X, G) of a constant sheaf associated to a group G with the singular cohomology Hi(X, G).

3. Reduced power operations and index bounds
In this section we give a refinement of the theorem of de Jong and Perry for bounding the index of a topologically trivial Brauer class on a smooth projective complex variety X. Afterwards, we consider the possible obstructions to the period-index conjecture granted by both the theorem of de Jong and Perry, and this new refinement, in the case of a very general abelian variety. We show that, in this particular case, these obstructions actually all vanish. We conclude this section with some more examples of the failure of the integral Hodge conjecture.

4. Additional applications to Azumaya algebras
In this section we highlight some applications to the area of Azumaya algebras that are directly accessible through work of Hotchkiss [Hot22], the obstructions of de Jong–Perry [dJP22], and the results of this paper. We focus on the interpretation of all of these methods as an algorithm for producing lower bounds on the index for a given Brauer class on an abelian variety, and how this can be applied to produce new examples.

4.2. Indecomposable Azumaya algebras. Algorithms that allow one to determine lower bounds on the index of a Brauer class α ∈ Br(X) can also be used to analyze other interesting algebraic properties of the Brauer class α, e.g. decomposability of α. In this subsection, we show how we can leverage these algorithms of de Jong–Perry and the previous subsection to obstruct decomposability of a Brauer class.
Definition 4.4. Let X be a smooth, projective variety. Let α ∈ Br(X) be a Brauer class on X. We say that α is decomposable if there exists

20 EOIN MACKALL
a Brauer class β ∈ Br(X) such that both
ind(β) < ind(α), ind(α − β) < ind(α).
If no such β exists, then we say that α is indecomposable.
Remark 4.5. Let X be a smooth projective variety. Then α ∈ Br(X) is decomposable if and only if there exists an Azumaya algebra A in the class α and Azumaya algebras B, C on X satisfying:
(1) ind(B) < ind(A) and ind(C) < ind(A), (2) and A ∼= B ⊗ C.
An Azumaya algebra A that admits B, C satisfying (1) and (2) above is said to be decomposable. Analogously, an Azumaya algebra that is not decomposable is said to be indecomposable.
It follows that if α ∈ Br(X) is indecomposable, then every Azumaya algebra A in the class of α is indecomposable.
For a long time, determining whether there existed indecomposable central simple algebras of prime period p > 2 and index p2 was an open problem. This problem was eventually resolved by Karpenko [Kar95b], who produced obstructions to decomposability coming from degrees of certain naturally defined algebraic cycles.
In the case of algebras of period 2 and index 4, a classical theorem of Albert [Alb39, XI §6] shows that all such algebras are products of two quaternion algebras (thus motivating the question for higher primes p). This theorem does not generalize to an a arbitrary Azumaya algebra over a more general scheme, however, as can be deduced from a theorem of Antieau-Auel-First [AFW19, Theorem A] on the existence of such algebras lacking an involution of the first kind.
We give an example, in the same vein as [AFW19, Theorem A], of an indecomposable Brauer class α ∈ Br(X) of period 2 and index 4 on an abelian threefold below (Example 4.6). It follows that no Azumaya algebra of degree 4 representing this class admits such an involution, although we do not know if α is even represented by such an algebra.
