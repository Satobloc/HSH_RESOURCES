ABSTRACT. In this Part 2 of our article we give a detailed discussion of the compatibility between the ana- lytic Gysin maps we have defined in Part 1 and the topological Gysin maps defined by the second author. A significant roˆle is played by a bordism-like description of K-homology due to Jakob which is closely related to the geometric K-homology theory of Baum and Douglas. We give a self-contained proof of the equivalence of the former with the analytic K-homology theory of Kasparov. As an intermediate step towards proving our main result we use Thom’s transversality theorem to describe Gysin maps compatibly with Jakob’s definition of K-homology.

In contrast to Part 1 we allow f to be a normally nonsingular inclusion of arbitrary finite CW-complexes (as opposed to Witt pseudomanifolds), but when f is a fibration we require that the associated structure group is compact Lie to define the topological Gysin map. Via the Mostow-Palais equivariant embedding theorem, this enables sufficient control over fiberwise embeddings into Euclidean spaces over the base. More generally, it is often possible to work with block bundles and replace fiberwise with blockwise embeddings. In that setting, a topologically Gysin map had been defined by the second author in [Ban24, Ban25]. That map agrees with the topological Gysin maps considered here, since locally trivial fiber bundles are in particular block bundles, and the Thom classes correspond under the map that forgets the projection and only remembers the blocks.
An interesting part of our analysis is the use of a bordism description of K-homology, analogous to the geometric K-homology groups of Baum-Douglas [BD82]. Thus we make use of the theory developed by Jakob in [Jak98] where any multiplicative cohomology theory h∗ on finite CW-complexes is provided with a geometric description h′∗ of its dual homology theory h∗. Classes in h′∗(X,A) are represented by 4-tuples (M,u(νM),x, f) with M a compact smooth manifold with (possibly empty) boundary, u(νM) an h∗-orientation of the stable normal bundle of M, x ∈ h∗(M), and f : (M,∂M) −→ (X,A) a continuous map. Two representatives represent the same class if they are related by diffeomorphism, bordism, or vector bundle modification. Jakob shows that h′∗ is naturally equivalent to h∗. We start out in section 2, establishing some naturality results in analytic K-homology concerning the interplay between Gysin maps, pull-back maps, and cup products with orientations. Section 3 reviews the Siegel-Sullivan orientation and its lift by the second author to spectra. In section 4, we study the geometric descriptions of K-homology and K-homology with 2 inverted. Sections 5 and 6 show the compatibility of Gysin maps for oriented normally nonsingular inclusions in K-homology with 2 inverted and ordinary K-homology, respectively. Finally section 7 establishes the compatibility of Gysin maps in the submersive case.

4. BORDISM-LIKE DESCRIPTION OF K-HOMOLOGY AND THE ISOMORPHISM WITH Kan(−) ∗
A significant roˆle in our arguments will be played by a bordism-like description of K-homology due to Jakob [Jak98] which is closely related to the geometric K-homology theory of Baum and Douglas. In this section we will recall Jakob’s definition, apply it to both ordinary K-theory and K-theory with 2 inverted, and establish isomorphisms with both analytic K-homology and geometric K-homology.
4.1. Bordism-like description of K∗(−) and K∗(−)[21]. Recall that given a multiplicative cohomology theory h∗, represented by a ring spectrum h, Jakob provides a bordism-type description h′∗ of the associated homology theory h∗ where a description means more precisely that there exists an explicit isomorphism φ : h′∗ (X ) → h∗ (X ) with interesting functoriality properties. We shall also refer to h′∗ as the geometric description of h∗.
Let Th(ξ) denote the Thom spectrum of a stable vector bundle ξ. Recall that an h∗-Thom class (or h- orientation) of ξ is a class u(ξ ) ∈ 􏰈h0 (Th ξ ) which restricts to ±1 ∈ π0 (h) on every fiber, where 1 denotes the unit of the ring coefficient ring π∗(h). The geometric group h′∗(X,A) of a pair (X,A) of spaces is obtained by starting with quadruples of the form (M, u(νM ), x, f ), where M is a compact smooth manifold with (possibly empty) boundary, u(νM) is an h-orientation of the stable normal bundle νM, x ∈ h∗(M) is a cohomology class, and f : (M,∂M) → (X,A) is a continuous map. Let us briefly write uM := u(νM). For n-dimensional M, the stable Thom class uM determines a homology class [M,∂M]h ∈ hn(M,∂M), the h∗-fundamental class of (M,∂M), such that there is a Poincare ́-Lefschetz duality isomorphism hi(M) → hn−i(M,∂M), x 􏰁→ x∩[M,∂M]h. 7. THE SUBMERSIVE CASE
In this section we compare the analytic and topological Gysin maps associated to submersions. In Part 1 we have defined the analytic Gysin map for fiber bundles of Witt spaces but it is clear that the construction only requires that the fibers be Witt spaces and works equally well for fiber bundles of finite CW-complexes as long as the fibers are Witt spaces. (The reason that we assumed in Part I that the base was also a Witt space was so that we could show that the Gysin map sent an orientation of the base to an orientation of the total space.) On the topological side, we will describe Gysin maps for fiber bundles associated to principal bundles with compact Lie structure group. The main result of this section is then the compatibility of the two definitions in this case (so one could think of the Gysin map defined in Part 1 as extending the topologically defined map beyond compact Lie structure groups).
