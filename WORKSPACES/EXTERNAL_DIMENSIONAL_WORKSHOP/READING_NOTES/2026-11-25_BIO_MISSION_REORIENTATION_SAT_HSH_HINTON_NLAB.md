## 4. Biological implications: working hypotheses, not a mechanism catalogue

Use the SAT/H(s)H lens explicitly and at the right level of abstraction. Candidate questions include:

- Organisms as temporally extended four-dimensional structures, not merely present-time 3D bodies.
- Cells, tissues, organs and organisms as nested histories or substructures, with their relations represented within a larger worldtube.
- Development as a structured history through successive configurations, rather than only a sequence of snapshots.
- Lineage, cell division, tissue renewal and organismal persistence as distinct kinds of continuity/branching.
- Morphogenesis and biological form as possible outcomes of nested geometry, constraints, interactions and history-dependent organization.
- Organisms and biosphere as possible cross-scale filamentary/superhelical structures, while distinguishing a useful structural conjecture from the much stronger claim that all life is literally one unified organism.
- Biological systems as possible places where physical geometry, temporal organization, material continuity and information-bearing structure meet.

These are SAT/H(s)H-led interpretive questions. Contemporary biology is used to determine what structures and mechanisms actually exist, not to replace the ontology with a long conventional mechanism survey. A biological mechanism needs enough overview to establish the real phenomenon, the relevant scales and the known causal constraints; detailed specialist depth is warranted only when it discriminates between candidate mappings.

## 5. Comparison labels and evidence discipline

Every proposed correspondence should be tagged as one of:

- **Prior art / historical transmission:** documented intellectual or mathematical ancestry.
- **Independent convergence:** structurally similar construction with no demonstrated transmission.
- **Formal translation:** a defined mapping preserving specified structure.
- **Mainstream intersection:** shared mathematical/physical territory without equivalence.
- **Analogy / heuristic:** suggestive but not structure-preserving or derived.
- **Divergence:** superficially similar concepts that differ in objects, dimensions, assumptions or predictions.
- **Speculative extension:** a proposed SAT/H(s)H implication not yet derived or tested.

For each source, record whether it is Nathan-authored, assistant-generated, collaborative transcript, historical primary source, contemporary review, primary scientific paper or secondary summary. A source's presence in the archive is not proof of its truth or authority. Strange presentation is not grounds for dismissal; polished presentation is not grounds for promotion.

Keep external research downstream of SAT archive recovery: first understand what SAT actually says and where it came from, then use Hinton/nLab/physics literature to test lineage, convergence and divergence. External sources provide comparison, prior-art control, empirical constraints and conceptual translation; they do not silently steer or rewrite SAT.
