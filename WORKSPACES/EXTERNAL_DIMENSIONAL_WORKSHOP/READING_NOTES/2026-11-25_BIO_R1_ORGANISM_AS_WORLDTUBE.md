**Question:** What does it mean to represent one developing organism as a four-dimensional physical structure whose familiar anatomy appears as successive three-dimensional sections?

## Result

Represent the organism's full physical existence over its lifespan as an extended four-dimensional structure:

\[
\mathcal W_{\mathrm{org}}\subset\mathcal M_{3+1},\qquad
\mathcal O_t=\mathcal W_{\mathrm{org}}\cap\Sigma_t .
\]

Here \(\mathcal W_{\mathrm{org}}\) is the organism's worldtube, \(\Sigma_t\) is a three-dimensional time-slice, and \(\mathcal O_t\) is the organism's spatial configuration at that slice. The successive \(\mathcal O_t\) sections need not resemble one another: an embryo, a juvenile, and an adult are different sections of one temporally extended organismal structure.

Under the stipulated **ᛟ lens**, this is a literal physical reading, not merely a diagram of a three-dimensional body plus an abstract timeline. The familiar present-day anatomy is a section through the full organismal structure; it is not the whole temporally extended organism.

## What standard physics already supplies

Worldtubes are standard kinematics for extended bodies. In relativistic continuum mechanics, one may describe a material body by a three-dimensional space of material points and a map \(X^\mu(\lambda,\zeta)\) into spacetime. Holding a material label \(\zeta\) fixed while varying \(\lambda\) traces that point's worldline; the collection of material-point worldlines forms the body's worldtube. The intersection with a spatial slice gives the body's configuration at that time.

This is stated explicitly in Nishita Jadoo, *Extended Body Motion in General Relativity* (PhD thesis, North Carolina State University, 2023), §3.2, pp. 18–19. James Owen Weatherall's “Classical Spacetime Structure,” in *The Routledge Companion to Philosophy of Physics* (2022), likewise describes an extended body's worldtube as bounded in each spatial slice, with each intersection giving its configuration.

So the basic worldtube/slice architecture is **not a new SAT/H(s)H mathematical construction**. The specifically SAT/H(s)H commitment in this workshop is to read that four-dimensional extension as the organism's physical ontology, rather than only as a useful representation.

## What this immediately makes visible

- **Persistence across change:** the organism is not identified with any one snapshot; each anatomy is a section of the larger history.
- **Morphological development:** growth, folding, organ formation, changing proportions and later remodeling become changes in the shape and internal organization of successive sections.
- **Temporal paths:** cells, tissues, cilia, organizers, signals and other constituents/processes can be located within the same four-dimensional developmental history.
- **Relations between events and structures:** a feature at one stage and a later feature can be treated as temporally separated parts of one extended history, rather than as unrelated pictures.

For the current BIO material, this gives a clean representational home for the mouse node, its cilia and flow, sensory response, signaling fields and subsequent organ morphogenesis already reconstructed in ledger §76. It does not yet map that example in detail; that is the next bite.

## The biological complication: a living organism is not a sealed rigid parcel

The standard material-body construction can use a fixed reference space whose material points retain their labels and trace worldlines. That is a useful baseline, but it is not by itself a complete biological identity model.

An organism grows, exchanges matter with its environment, turns over cells, produces daughter cells by division, loses cells, differentiates lineages, and reorganizes its tissues. A simple picture in which exactly the same material constituents occupy a fixed body throughout the full interval would therefore be inadequate.

For the biological application, distinguish:

1. **Organism-level worldtube:** the four-dimensional region/history of the living, organized organism.
2. **Constituent histories:** worldlines or worldtubes of particular cells, tissues, and material components.
3. **Lineage and turnover relations:** events where one cell's history branches into daughter-cell histories, where cells enter or leave a tissue, or where material crosses the organism's boundary.
4. **Organizational fields and relations:** the changing spatial and functional organization that makes the evolving region count as this organism, rather than merely as an arbitrary volume of matter.

These are requirements for a sufficiently rich biological model, not mechanisms supplied by the bare worldtube definition. The next work must not silently treat cell identity, organism identity, or the organismal boundary as already solved.

## Boundary of the result

**Established in standard physics:** an extended material body can be represented in spacetime by a worldtube; its intersections with spatial slices describe successive configurations; material constituents can be tracked by worldlines inside it.

**Stipulated SAT/H(s)H interpretation:** the organism is physically four-dimensionally extended, and its familiar three-dimensional anatomy is a time-slice of that full structure.

**Not established by this bite:** a unique criterion for organismal identity across matter exchange; a complete treatment of cell division and lineage branching; a new biological causal mechanism; or any extra predictive constraint not already present in the underlying biological model.

The useful question carried forward is therefore not “can an organism be drawn as a worldtube?” Standard spacetime mechanics already does that. It is: **what additional structure is needed to represent a living organism's continuity, internal reorganization and cross-scale causal history as one four-dimensional physical object, and does that representation make anything explicit that ordinary developmental descriptions leave implicit?**
