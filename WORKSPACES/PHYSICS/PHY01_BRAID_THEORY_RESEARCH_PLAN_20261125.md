## Purpose

Preserve the PHYS-01 analysis as the physics-specific case study within the broader cross-disciplinary braid and geometric-formalization programme. The umbrella scope, including the nLab and n-Category Café source observatory and applications across mathematics, physics, quantum geometry, quantum computing, robotics and other fields, is recorded in WORKSPACES/COMMON/BRAID_AS_CROSS_DISCIPLINARY_GEOMETRIC_LENS_20261125.md.

The task has two separate products:

1. A source-grounded account of braid theory and its related mathematical structures, origins, transformations and uses across topology, geometry and mathematical physics.
2. A conditional comparison asking which, if any, of those structures can represent the actual SAT/H(s)H filament, timesheet, finite-core and worldtube histories after their geometry is source-defined.

The second product must not determine or rewrite the first. Nor may an attractive braid formalism be imported into the physical construction merely because the vocabulary fits.

## Research strands

### 1. Classical braid theory

Recover primary sources and exact hypotheses for:

- geometric braids and Artin braid groups;
- pure braid groups and ordered configuration spaces;
- unordered configuration spaces and indistinguishable objects;
- generators, relations, isotopy, braid closures and links;
- braid actions on groups, algebras, categories and state spaces.

Trace historical development from primary mathematical sources. Separate documented transmission from shared antecedents, independent convergence and later reinterpretation.

### 2. Dimension and configuration-space dependence

Construct the actual configuration spaces before naming their fundamental groups.

| Objects and ambient setting | Candidate structure to investigate | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| Labeled points in a plane | Pure braid groups | Ordered configuration space |
| Indistinguishable points in a plane | Artin braid groups | Unordered configuration space |
| Indistinguishable point particles in ordinary three-space | Permutation group | Standard point-particle configuration and collision assumptions |
| Embedded circles, rings or links in three-space | Link and embedding spaces; motion groups | Extended object, isotopy and boundary/orientation rules |
| Strings, sheets and higher-dimensional objects | Higher-dimensional embedding and motion structures | Intrinsic dimension, codimension and ambient dimension |

These are routing categories, not substitutes for theorem-level source reading. Boundaries, punctures, ambient topology, labels, orientation and collision rules can change the answer.

### 3. Extended-object motion groups

Investigate motion groups for embedded circles, rings, links, ribbons, strings and surfaces. Include loop-braid and braid-permutation structures only where their defining configuration spaces and object hypotheses fit.

Keep the following separate:

- static linking or knot type;
- a path through a configuration or embedding space;
- a loop or homotopy class of motions;
- a braid/motion-group element;
- intersection of worldtubes;
- a dynamical interaction or force;
- quantum-statistical action on a state.

### 4. Braided categories and mathematical physics

Trace supported mathematical bridges among:

- braid-group actions and braided monoidal categories;
- Yang–Baxter equations and their precise algebraic role;
- groupoids and higher categorical generalizations;
- topological field theory and extended observables;
- exchange statistics, anyons and topological phases;
- topological quantum computation.

For each connection, label whether it is a definition, theorem, mathematical representation, model-dependent realization or physical interpretation. Do not identify categorical degree with an extra physical dimension.

### 5. Historical and cross-disciplinary lineage

Build a dated source map. Check primary texts and editions before constructing a lineage. Distinguish:

- direct documented influence;
- citation or institutional transmission;
- shared mathematical antecedent;
- independent convergence;
- structural analogy;
- retrospective reinterpretation.

Do not infer Hinton-to-braid-theory, Hinton-to-category-theory or SAT-to-modern-mathematics influence from resemblance alone. Search internal archives first; the currently active Mersearch lane-2 request is the existing acquisition dependency and must not be duplicated or overwritten.

## Physical-realization gate for SAT/H(s)H

No braid group, braid invariant or braided categorical action may be assigned until the following are source-defined:

1. actual spatial cross-section and topology of the core;
2. intrinsic dimension, codimension, orientation and boundary structure;
3. labeled, distinguishable, identical or permutation-quotiented status of objects;
4. allowed configuration space and its collision, overlap and reconnection rules;
5. permitted isotopies and deformations, distinguished from physically allowed dynamics;
6. time orientation and the relation between configuration-space paths and complete worldtube histories;
7. equivalence relation under which two histories define the same motion class;
8. a map from a source-defined history to a group element, isotopy class or categorical morphism;
9. invariants and observables that discriminate topological structure from projection artifacts.

A two-dimensional diagram crossing is not, by itself, a physical intersection, linking event or braid generator. A diagram represents a mathematical class under specified projection and isotopy conventions.

The framework-internal use of winding or Interbraid remains at its recorded epistemic status. An unwrapped winding count is not automatically an element of an Artin braid group, a pure braid group, a loop-braid group or a higher categorical braid.

## Questions

- **PHY-Q36:** What primary sources establish the development of geometric braids, Artin groups and pure braid groups?
- **PHY-Q37:** Under exactly which hypotheses do planar point exchanges yield braid groups while three-dimensional point exchanges yield permutations?
- **PHY-Q38:** Which configuration spaces and motion groups describe embedded rings, links, ribbons, strings and surfaces in three- and higher-dimensional spaces?
- **PHY-Q39:** How do braid-group actions, braided monoidal categories, Yang–Baxter structures, exchange statistics and topological field theories connect mathematically?
- **PHY-Q40:** Given the actual H(s)H finite core, what is its configuration space and corresponding fundamental or motion group?
- **PHY-Q41:** Which apparent crossings survive changes of projection and admissible isotopy?
- **PHY-Q42:** If a motion group is identified, what independent map connects its elements to quantum-state transformations or exchange phases, and what discriminates it from ordinary permutation action?
