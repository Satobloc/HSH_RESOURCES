## The dimensional turn-count discriminator

A scalar turn counter may map to a winding number only after its configuration space is specified.

- Two ordered points in a plane have a relative-position vector in the punctured plane; its loops have an integer winding class.
- For two unordered points in a plane, exchange generates the two-strand Artin braid group, isomorphic to the integers. A full 2 pi rotation of a chosen ordered relative vector corresponds to the pure-braid subgroup, not automatically to a single exchange generator.
- For two indistinguishable point particles in ordinary three-space, the standard unordered configuration-space fundamental group is S2, recording exchange parity rather than an unrestricted integer winding history.
- Extended loops/rings in three-space require their own motion/embedding group. Neither the planar Artin braid model nor the point-particle permutation model can be assumed.

Thus the BR-006 integer might map to a winding or pure-braid class in a specifically defined planar two-object model, and its parity might map to an exchange class in a standard three-dimensional point-particle model. Neither mapping follows from the formula alone. Relative angle, lift, endpoints, labels, collisions, spatial dimension and allowed homotopies must be specified.

This is a mathematical discriminator for ℬ, not an assignment of planar or three-dimensional statistics to H(s)H.

## Why internal braiding differs from particle exchange

A triple-braid internal structure asks how constituent strands are embedded and constrained to form one composite object. An exchange braid asks how several distinguishable or indistinguishable objects move in a configuration space. The inputs differ:

- Internal composite: identify strands, ambient embedding, endpoints/closures and equivalence of internal configurations.
- Exchange/motion: identify objects exchanged, spatial configuration space, labels or quotient-by-permutation, excluded collisions and allowed loops/homotopies.

A Borromean- or Hopf-like section could be a readout of a higher-dimensional composite, but requires an explicit source-defined projection and invariant comparison. A familiar drawing does not establish the higher-dimensional embedding or physical identity.
