## Question

Does a moving family of point-valued filament intersections on a two-dimensional film or a three-dimensional timesheet carry the same braid group?

No. Under the standard configuration-space assumptions, the ambient dimension of the leaf changes the exchange group.

## Historical comparator: Hinton's two-dimensional film

In *The Fourth Dimension* (1906), chapter IV, Hinton describes a spiral crossing a moving film. At any instant the intersection is a point; the successive positions of that point trace a circle in the film. Hinton then extends the picture to multiple filamentary structures and a plane world. He explicitly calls the wider consciousness/readout hypothesis symbolic.

For multiple indistinguishable point intersections moving in a two-dimensional film without collision, the relevant unordered configuration space is the planar point-configuration space. Its fundamental group is the Artin braid group B_n. For labeled points the corresponding fundamental group is the pure braid group P_n.

Hinton's passage is therefore a useful geometrical comparator for planar braid histories, but it does not itself define an Artin braid group or claim a physical braid mechanism.

## Conditional SAT comparison: three-dimensional timesheet

The archived RMS Spacetime Filaments snapshot describes the time surface as a three-dimensional surface propagating through a four-dimensional construction. The 2026-07-03 SAT synthesis likewise treats the worldline and timesheet as primitives. The 2026-10-09 clarification describes inherited SAT as a worldline plus time wavefront, not a curve wrapped around a separate carrier.

Suppose, conditionally, that:

1. each leaf of the timesheet family is a simply connected three-manifold such as R^3 or S^3;
2. each of n distinct worldlines meets every leaf once and transversely;
3. the intersections are point-like;
4. the points are indistinguishable, remain distinct, and may move freely in all three spatial directions of the leaf;
5. collisions are excluded and the leaf-identification rule is fixed.

Then the readout is a path in the unordered configuration space C_n(M^3). For the standard M^3 = R^3 or S^3 case, its fundamental group is S_n, the permutation group. The ordinary planar Artin braid group is not the generic exchange group for freely moving point intersections across a three-dimensional leaf.

### Two-point derivation

For two ordered points in R^d, the configuration space decomposes as:

F_2(R^d) ≅ R^d × (R^d minus {0}).

The second factor deformation-retracts to the sphere S^(d-1).

- In d=2, the ordered configuration space has fundamental group π1(S^1)=Z, the two-strand pure braid group. Quotienting by exchange gives the two-strand Artin braid group B_2, also isomorphic to Z. An exchange is a half-turn of the relative vector; a full turn corresponds to the pure-braid subgroup.
- In d=3, the ordered configuration space has π1(S^2)=0. The unordered space has the free exchange quotient S^2/(v ~ -v) = RP^2, whose fundamental group is Z_2, isomorphic to S_2. Only exchange parity remains for standard point-particle motion.

This is the configuration-space reason that a planar crossing diagram cannot be transplanted uncritically to freely moving points on a three-dimensional timesheet.

## Ways a richer braid structure could still arise

The three-dimensional point-particle result is conditional. It does not rule out every braid-like structure in the SAT/H(s)H construction. A richer structure could arise if the native geometry supplies one of the following:

1. **A physically specified two-dimensional constraint:** intersection points are confined to an embedded surface within the three-dimensional timesheet. The relevant configuration space may then be planar or surface-braid-like.
2. **Extended intersection objects:** the intersection of a finite-core worldtube with a timesheet is not point-like. Rings, links, ribbons or other extended objects have embedding and motion spaces different from point-particle configuration spaces.
3. **Nontrivial leaf topology or boundary conditions:** punctures, excluded regions, boundaries or nontrivial spatial topology can alter the configuration-space fundamental group.
4. **Internal constituent braiding:** several constituent strands form one composite object. This is not the same problem as exchanging several completed particle intersections.
5. **Higher-dimensional embedding motion:** the relevant strands, sheets or cores have nontrivial embedding spaces and allowed deformations in the full ambient geometry.

Each candidate needs a source-defined object and a corresponding mathematical construction. A projected crossing alone does not supply the missing two-dimensional constraint, extended core or topology.

## Direct consequence for the internal SAT braid proposal

The July 2026 SAT synthesis proposes braiding worldlines as a representational grammar for color confinement, including triple-braid quarks and double-braid mesons. Before identifying that proposal with the Artin braid group, one must determine what the braid parameter and transverse configuration space actually are.

Questions to resolve:

- Are the proposed strands worldlines, portions of worldlines, filament constituents, or paths of intersection points?
- Does the braid live internally within a composite, or describe exchange of distinct particle intersections?
- Is there a genuine two-dimensional configuration surface, or does each point move throughout a three-dimensional timesheet?
- Does a 3D knot/link diagram represent an invariant of a higher-dimensional embedding, or only a projection?
- Does the H(s)H finite core intersect the timesheet as a point, region, ring, loop or another object?
- What transformations and homotopies preserve the source-defined physical identity?

## Decision rule

Do not assign B_n merely because a drawing contains three strands or projected crossings. First identify the object, leaf dimension, configuration space, labels, collision exclusions and allowed paths. Then compute or recover the relevant fundamental group / motion group. If the actual object is an extended core, derive its embedding or motion group from that core instead of using the point-particle result.
