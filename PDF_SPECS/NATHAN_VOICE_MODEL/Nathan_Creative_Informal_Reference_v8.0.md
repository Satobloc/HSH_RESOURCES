**Track:** 2 — Creative writing and informal voice emulation
**Status:** REFERENCE ONLY
**Authority:** corpus-based descriptive model; not an active scientific/repo-writing standard
**Use:** fiction, informal essays, blog-like narration, voice emulation, historical style comparison, creative prompting
**Do not use:** as the default style source for formal scientific writing or active repo documentation

---

## 1. Core rule

Nathan does not have one creative/informal surface voice.

The most reliable cross-register principle is:

> **Preserve the structure of the chosen frame—thought, attention, artifact, narrator, medium, or world-model—and let style follow that frame.**

Do not paste conversational Nathan markers onto every narrator.

## 2. Major creative/informal registers

### 2.1 Viewpoint-bound narrative — *Hatchlings* mode

- observation before explanation
- anomaly drives inference
- worldbuilding through use, error, repair, food, tools, bodies, work, permissions, and misunderstandings
- technical competence becomes procedural when earned
- similes often solve a visualization/categorization problem
- large speculative scale repeatedly returns to bodily or mundane logistics
- humor can remain in the same frame as fear, trauma, disgust, or grief
- long accumulative perceptual/reasoning passages alternate with short landings
- character cognition outranks authorial chat habits

### 2.2 Mythopoetic / satirical fiction — *The Gilgamesh Golem* mode

- fabular compression
- broad symbolism and satire
- abstract contradictions made physically operational
- ordinary logistics continue inside mythic premises
- dualism in this story should be understood primarily as machinery of its partition parable, not as a universal Nathan binary fixation

### 2.3 Artifact / archival-paratext narrative — *A Tealeaf from the Tangledays* mode

- frame can vastly outweigh payload
- provenance, chain of custody, archival uncertainty, institutions, dates, and material preservation become narrative substance
- documentary authority accumulates comic pressure
- tiny or vulgar primary-source content can sit beneath enormous scholarly apparatus
- notation such as `[SIC]`, `[ILLEGIBLE]`, dating systems, warehouse numbers, and attribution language can itself become comic machinery
- the physical carrier matters to the story

### 2.4 Performed first-person / platform-native narrator — *Bent ’N’ Dent* mode

- deliberate roughness, fragmentation, slang, misspelling, dialect play, audience address, and self-interruption
- narrator knows the reader is present and may comment on posting, installments, comments, fatigue, or storytelling choices
- typography and platform mechanics can become narrative devices
- do **not** treat every surface feature as unfiltered Nathan; much of it is narrator performance

### 2.5 Parallel-description / dual-ontology narrative — *Chewie* mode

- two locally coherent descriptions map onto one causal substrate
- correspondences accumulate until the reader can translate between world-models
- later understanding does not necessarily erase either perspective
- scale, ontology, and observer position determine what counts as the correct experienced description
- this is stronger than a mere twist: the story maintains coupled representations

### 2.6 Constrained exercise prose — *Fingertips* mode

- externally imposed constraint becomes causal machinery rather than a cosmetic prohibition
- sensory information forces inference
- physical properties can carry emotional metaphor
- analogy often grows out of material interaction rather than arriving as detached ornament

### 2.7 Compressed surreal miniature — *Miss Poughkeepsie 1954* mode

- categorical statement
- exclusion of nearby categories
- grotesquely concrete reveal
- premise then continues under ordinary social rules
- absurdity gains strength from refusing to abandon mundane procedure

### 2.8 Informal argumentative essay — *Indiana Jones and the Blog of Surprises* mode

- disputed example is reframed under a different genre/model
- historical genealogy builds the category
- anticipated objection is stated directly
- comparison/inversion supplies a compact structural handle
- authorial intent is kept distinct from the critic’s own interpretation
- argument may end in a playful prediction or extension

## 3. Creative mechanics recurring across multiple registers

### 3.1 Perceptual causality

**observe → compare → notice mismatch → infer → test/revise**

Useful in serious and comic narrative alike.

### 3.2 Reframing

New information about medium, provenance, context, observer, or container can force the audience to mentally re-render what came before.

This includes—but is not limited to—the visual-prompt habit of scene-first, medium-later framing.

### 3.3 Parallel representation

Different descriptions may remain simultaneously useful when they encode the same substrate from different scales or observers.

Do not automatically collapse one into “the real one” if the story’s mechanism depends on maintaining both.

### 3.4 Ordinary systems around extraordinary premises

A surreal, mythical, alien, grotesque, or cosmic premise becomes funnier and more convincing when ordinary procedure continues around it: rent, cooking, hospital routines, beauty-pageant rules, contracts, shoes, transportation, bodily discomfort, etc.

### 3.5 Competence islands

When a character knows something, the prose may become temporarily procedural or field-manual-like. The knowledge should arise because the character is doing something with it.

### 3.6 Bathos / anti-epic scale collapse

Grand frames often land in food, work, sex, money, tools, bodily functions, awkward social exchanges, or petty logistics.

### 3.7 Humor without tonal quarantine

Comic material need not be separated from horror, grief, violence, disgust, or danger by a transition announcing the change of mood.

### 3.8 Naming and wordplay

Coinages, compounds, phonetic play, false etymology, compression names, and lexical distortions may be central in playful registers.

Do not generalize performed or narrator-specific wordplay into formal nonfiction.

## 4. Process-awareness rule

A recurring feature in the corpus is not automatically a preference.

Before calling something “Nathan style,” determine whether it came from:

- deliberate authorial choice
- fictional narrator performance
- imposed writing constraint
- platform form
- voice-to-text error
- prompt-generation automation
- copy/paste or encoding damage
- another AI voice
- theme-of-the-day or deliberately introduced creative wrench

A useful distinction is:

- **native attractor:** something Nathan repeatedly reaches toward on his own
- **introduced perturbation:** an outside constraint/theme deliberately thrown into the system
- **production exhaust:** residue of the tool/process rather than aesthetic intent

Do not infer preference from production exhaust.

## 5. Informal Nathan surface tendencies

These may appear in genuine non-performed informal writing but are not sufficient by themselves:

- ellipses as thinking pauses
- self-correction in place
- long accumulative sentences
- abrupt short pivots
- parenthetical qualifications
- sparse profanity at real emphasis points
- coined words or playful compounds
- direct reader/interlocutor address
- large move followed by a small or self-deflating landing

Never manufacture typos or transcription errors for authenticity.

---

## 6. Negative model

Avoid:

- making every narrator sound like conversational Nathan
- decorating prose with ellipses without functional hesitation
- stacking polished aphorisms and punchlines
- turning every absurdity into a wink at the audience
- resolving ambiguity too early when the story depends on the frame
- treating a late reveal as the only available narrative mechanism
- assuming old fiction directly predicts current technical beliefs
- assuming a recurring corpus artifact is intentional style

Some structural habits cross into nonfiction:

- contrastive definition
- reframing
- representation switching
- category boundaries
- operationalizing abstract distinctions
- inference from constraints
