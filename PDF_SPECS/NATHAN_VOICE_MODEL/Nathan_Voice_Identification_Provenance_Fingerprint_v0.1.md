## 1. Governing principle

The identification target is not a bag of mannerisms.

The strongest Nathan signature is the recurring way language is used to **expose, test, qualify, point to, and manipulate the structure of thought for another mind**.

Surface habits vary by medium, tempo, fluency, dictation, genre, and degree of composition. Deep habits often survive those changes.

Therefore:

> **Identify clusters of structural behavior first; use punctuation, spelling, ellipses, typos, and cadence only as supporting evidence.**

This is a provenance aid, not a biometric authorship detector. It should never be treated as proof of authorship without source/context support.

## 2. Highest-value deep markers

### 2.1 Language as an interface between minds

Nathan reports no habitual running internal monologue. Verbalization often functions as an interface through which a substantially nonverbal or not-yet-verbal structure is rendered for another mind and, secondarily, inspected by Nathan himself.

Consequences visible in text may include:

- serializing several simultaneously active relations;
- local reformulation as wording reveals a mismatch;
- preserving an odd construction when it better exposes the intended structure;
- using wording itself as a control surface rather than merely a carrier of propositions.

This is a deep marker. Do not infer it from one messy sentence alone.

### 2.2 Short backward revision window

Nathan frequently revises the immediately preceding clause or half-sentence while continuing forward.

Typical purposes:

- precision of hedging;
- accuracy-to-intent;
- qualification of self-certainty;
- correcting category placement;
- adding subtext or provenance;
- preserving tentativity;
- changing what the reader should notice.

The revision is often local rather than a wholesale restart.

Importantly, the result may become less conventionally smooth if that roughness better exposes the actual state of the thought.

### 2.3 Multiplex wording

A phrase may simultaneously:

- make a literal claim;
- calibrate certainty;
- comment on its own wording;
- wink at the reader;
- point to an earlier distinction;
- mark provenance or conceptual ancestry;
- act as a mnemonic handle;
- carry a second tonal/intellectual channel.

The presence of one such sentence is not decisive. Repeated use of compatible multi-channel wording is highly characteristic.

### 2.4 Maximal interpointing of connexion

Nathan often prefers wording that connects the immediate statement to several already-active nodes in the discussion.

The locally simplest synonym is not always preferred if another word can point backward, sideways, and downward at once.

This can create unusually dense semantic cross-linking without necessarily increasing sentence length.

### 2.5 Anticipated-reader composition

Nathan reports habitually reading through the eyes of anticipated readers, including often in nominally private writing.

Look for text that seems to be simultaneously asking:

- What do I mean?
- What will this reader think I mean?
- Which distinction will they miss?
- What unintended stronger claim does this wording invite?
- Will they recognize that this odd word/spelling is marked?
- Can I make the intended surface visible even if full understanding is not yet possible?

This often produces local clarification, paired contrast, parenthetical repair, or deliberate markedness before a likely misreading can occur.

### 2.6 Faithful exposure before normalization

Nathan may prefer a strange but faithful phrase to a clean paraphrase that erases the actual contour of the thought.

The objective is often:

> full understanding if possible; recognition if not; at minimum, preservation of the correct visible surface.

This is a strong discriminator from assistant prose that optimizes aggressively for smoothness.

### 2.7 Object / representation / interpretation separation

Nathan repeatedly distinguishes:

- thing from model;
- ontology from representation;
- observation from interpretation;
- mechanism from analogy;
- necessary from sufficient;
- current definition from historical form;
- artifact from enterprise.

This appears across scientific, methodological, legal/formal, and conversational material and should receive high evidentiary weight.

### 2.8 Integration back into the product

Exploration often branches, but Nathan repeatedly attempts to bring the result back into the artifact being built.

A recurring work pattern is:

> explore → isolate useful distinction → return to product → incorporate.

Short pivots such as “Alright. So.” or “Ok, now.” may function as return-to-product signals when the shared task-state is active.

Do not treat these phrases as fixed semantic tokens; their function depends on conversational position and tempo.

## 3. Tempo and shared-state markers

At high conversational tempo, Nathan may use very small utterances to operate on an already-active shared representation.

Examples of possible functions:

- retain this result;
- reject only the last refinement;
- reopen a branch;
- return to the product being built;
- hold the current structure while another distinction is introduced;
- redirect attention without restating context.

Thus fragments such as:

- “Right.”
- “No, but—”
- “Almost. So…”
- “Alright. So.”
- “Ok, now.”

cannot be classified by wording alone.

### Identification rule

When evaluating a short Nathan candidate, inspect:

1. the immediately preceding shared task;
2. conversational timing/turn density where available;
3. what branch was active;
4. whether the utterance changes task-state rather than content-state.

A fragment in a live exchange may carry much more information than the same words in isolation.

## 4. Markedness and lexical/orthographic partitioning

Nathan may recruit available formal differences into semantic service.

Possible channels:

- spelling variants;
- punctuation;
- italics;
- scare quotes;
- capitalization;
- hyphenation;
- deliberate morphology;
- coinage;
- unusual but intelligible phrasing.

### Confirmed example: programme / program

Nathan uses:

- **programme** for an organized scheme of activity, methodology, research enterprise, institutional initiative, school of thought, or sustained agenda;
- **program** for software, broadcast items, printed schedules/leaflets, and similar discrete informational/executable artifacts.

The contrast is semantic, not merely British/American spelling preference.

### Accident versus adjudication

Do not overread typos.

A typo is merely a typo unless Nathan actually recruits it into use.

Conversely, an accidental form can become meaningful after explicit adjudication if it exposes a useful distinction.

The identification model must therefore ask:

> Was this form intentionally authored, subsequently adopted, or merely accidental?

---

## 5. Coinage behavior

Nathan can coin, bend, borrow, or reassign words quickly when he needs a handle.

But coinages do not all have the same status.

Classify them where possible as:

- stable terminology;
- temporary precision tool;
- mnemonic / hat-hanging label;
- indexical pointer;
- metatextual marker;
- tonal play;
- illustrative/performed wording;
- accidental form later promoted;
- unresolved / unknown.

Do not assume every nonstandard word belongs in a glossary.

Do not autocorrect a sensible nonstandard form until its function is understood.

## 6. Epistemic calibration as one channel, not the whole voice

Nathan often locates uncertainty precisely.

Typical patterns:

- strong proposition plus narrow qualification;
- correction of a certainty word rather than abandonment of the underlying idea;
- different verbs for different evidentiary relations;
- limitation treated as guidance for what to test or where to seek help.

However, do not reduce Nathan’s voice to boundary policing or hedging. The same precision machinery is used for humor, subtext, provenance, reader positioning, conceptual cross-linking, and lexical distinction.

## 7. Surface features: useful but lower authority

These can support identification when clustered with deeper markers:

- ellipses as genuine pauses;
- accumulative syntax;
- self-interruption;
- direct address to a reasoning partner;
- visible correction;
- parenthetical qualification;
- occasional terse handoff fragments;
- coined or reassigned terminology;
- inconsistent proofreading despite conceptual care;
- abrupt register shift during genuine excitement;
- “big move, small landing” rhythm;
- sparse cussing at genuine emotional peaks;
- occasional scare quotes around a contested or provisional word.

None is decisive alone.

---

## 8. Causal provenance test

Before treating any recurring feature as Nathan’s voice, ask what produced it.

Possible causes include:

- deliberate Nathan wording;
- voice-to-text substitution;
- typo;
- transcription error;
- automated prompt-generation grammar;
- platform formatting;
- character/narrator performance;
- assignment constraint;
- copy/paste damage;
- assistant or NotebookLM prose;
- externally imposed theme;
- experimental perturbation;
- quoted source material.

**Direct Nathan correction outranks model inference.**

This test is mandatory for provenance work.

## 9. Cross-language stress-test value

The Alberr / young-Einstein roleplay corpus is unusually informative because Nathan communicates extensively through reduced-fluency German.

Its value is not as an English-style sample.

Its value is that several deep traits remain visible despite severe surface-language constraint:

- local certainty correction;
- distinction between ontology and observables;
- methodological reduction to primitives;
- explicit operational use of limitations;
- reader/interlocutor awareness;
- humor persistence;
- metaphor carrying more than one layer;
- insistence on historical/epistemic locality and prevention of future-information leakage.

Therefore:

> **High authority for deep voice structure; low authority for English surface style.**

## 10. Register and medium variation

Do not expect one surface Nathan.

Likely registers include:

- rapid text / directive;
- live exploratory reasoning;
- dictated stream;
- notebook technical shorthand;
- formal operational prose;
- formal argumentative prose;
- historical formal science;
- fiction / performed narrator;
- stranger-facing explanatory prose.

Identification should ask whether the candidate is plausible **for the register and medium**, not whether it resembles one canonical surface style.

## 11. Negative / anti-signals

These should reduce confidence when presented as supposed Nathan traits without deeper support:

- mechanically inserted ellipses;
- manufactured typos;
- constant coinage for novelty;
- generic “quirky scientist” voice;
- uniform informality across all genres;
- ritual self-deprecation;
- blanket uncertainty;
- endless category policing without humor, relational awareness, or conceptual purpose;
- assistant-like closing offers such as “Shall we proceed?” used habitually;
- performative role declaration (“As your co-investigator…”);
- excessive enthusiastic affirmation with no substantive friction;
- clean generic academic prose with no evidence of Nathan’s structural distinctions when the source task clearly requires them.

## 12. Suggested provenance confidence labels

For corpus work, use confidence labels rather than binary authorship declarations.

### NATHAN-STRONG

Multiple independent deep markers + context/provenance support; no obvious competing production source.

### NATHAN-LIKELY

Several compatible structural/surface markers, but source attribution is incomplete or mixed.

### MIXED / INTERLEAVED

Nathan content clearly present but assistant, source, notebook, or generated material is interwoven.

### UNCERTAIN

Insufficient evidence or features explainable by multiple sources.

### NON-NATHAN-LIKELY

Strong assistant/source/other-author characteristics and little Nathan structural evidence.

These labels should accompany source metadata whenever possible.

## 13. Emulation use

This fingerprint may occasionally be used to communicate something difficult to Nathan in a form likely to be recognizable or absorbable.

If used for emulation:

- reproduce structural behavior before surface quirks;
- do not manufacture spelling mistakes or VTT noise;
- use local qualification where it changes meaning;
- allow multiplex phrasing where useful;
- preserve distinction markers;
- exploit already-shared context instead of redundantly rebuilding it;
- match conversational tempo and task-state;
- avoid turning every sentence into a performance of “Nathan-ness.”

For peer-reviewed scientific prose, **do not use this document as the primary writing target**. Use `Nathan_Peer_Review_Writing_Fingerprint_v0.1.md`.

---

## 14. Compact identification rule

> **Nathan is most reliably identified not by ellipses, typos, or coinages, but by the repeated use of language as a high-bandwidth interface for exposing the actual contour of thought to an anticipated reader: local self-revision, precise but multi-purpose markedness, representation/ontology separation, reader-aware qualification, conceptual cross-linking, and context-sensitive manipulation of shared task-state. Surface quirks support that identification only after their production cause is known.**
