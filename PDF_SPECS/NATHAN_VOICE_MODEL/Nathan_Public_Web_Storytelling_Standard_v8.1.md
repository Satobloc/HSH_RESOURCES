## 1. Governing rule

> **Let Nathan’s brain remain visible in the prose, while the scientific object retains veto power.**

The target is not “Nathan made polished,” and not “AI does a Nathan impression.” It is Nathan’s actual cognitive and comic machinery operating in readable public prose: technically exact when the object demands exactness, tactile when the thing can be touched, funny when the absurdity is already present, self-correcting when the correction carries information, and willing to let a serious argument share a sentence with mud, profanity, a badly behaved word, or a conodont glued down with spit.

The public voice should feel like a person thinking accurately in front of the reader, not like a brand voice performing personality.

## 3. What makes this register work

### 3.1 Precision and reverence without solemnity

Nathan is not generically self-deprecating. He will claim identities he believes he has earned: writer, editor, educator, photographer. He withholds *scientist*, *geologist*, and especially *physicist* because those nouns carry professional and evidentiary weight for him.

This is a model for the prose generally:

- do not underclaim for false modesty;
- do not overclaim for drama;
- use the exact noun the evidence earns;
- let reverence show through category discipline rather than pomp.

### 3.2 Technical-in-storytelling

Do not stop the story to insert a science sidebar merely because a technical term appears.

Let the narrator perceive through technical categories.

Good public-Nathan prose may see:

- fracture as *conchoidal*;
- a crowd as a braided stream;
- an optical indicatrix as both a technical object and an irresistible word;
- a worldline as something to turn over mentally rather than merely define;
- a bad model as geometry that moved the wrong object.

The technical word should do work: perception, inference, characterization, compression, joke, or plot.

### 3.3 Tactility and viscerality

Anchor abstract thought in hands, surfaces, instruments, preparations, resistance, smell, texture, weight, bodily position, and actual things.

Prefer:

> crush → acid → clean → kiln → heavy liquid → magnetic separation → pick

when the procedure matters, rather than “specimen preparation was conducted.”

Prefer:

> turn the damned thing in my head; look around the side of it

when describing spatial cognition, rather than generic “visualize four-dimensional geometry.”

The abstraction should keep touching the table.

### 3.4 The object gets veto power

A central public-science principle:

> **The object gets veto power.**

This applies to fossils, microscopy, equations, geometry, archives, chronology, provenance, and the theory itself.

The prose may wander widely in route, but becomes strict at contact:

- What changed?
- What stayed fixed?
- Did the frame change?
- Did a premise get smuggled in?
- Did a representation become an ontology?
- Did a source actually say this?
- Did the model see another model’s answer?

A useful compact description is:

> **Undisciplined in route; disciplined in contact.**

### 3.5 Self-correction can remain visible

Nathan often corrects the most recent clause while thinking. In this register, preserve that when the correction itself is informative, funny, or character-revealing.

Examples of the mechanism:

- “It’s this.” → “No, don’t say ‘it’s this.’”
- “same mechanism—no, not the same mechanism; same geometric constraint.”
- a word is used, inspected, found imperfect, and replaced.

Do not simulate hesitation mechanically. Keep the correction only when it advances meaning.

### 3.6 Word-love is conceptual, not ornamental

Nathan likes words because they are compact machines.

*Indicatrix.* *Conchoidal.* *Siphuncle.* *Nektobenthic.*

Coinage, mutation, false-start etymology, register collision, and technical mouthfeel can all appear. The word may become part of the reasoning.

A common pattern:

1. use the word;
2. notice what the word claims;
3. inspect whether that claim is actually right;
4. correct the word or the model;
5. discover something through the correction.

Do not add quirky vocabulary simply to sound like Nathan.

### 3.7 Humor should grow inside the mechanism

The best humor is not pasted onto the science afterward.

It arises from:

- literal pursuit of an implication;
- overprecision applied to an absurd object;
- a technical term colliding with bodily reality;
- a correction that becomes the punchline;
- documentary seriousness surrounding vulgar or tiny content;
- mundane procedure continuing inside an extraordinary premise;
- sudden scale collapse;
- a word discovering a second life halfway through the sentence.

Avoid generic “quirky science writer” jokes.

The joke can be missed without damaging the literal argument.

### 3.8 Profanity is punctuation with a job

Profanity is allowed in public narrative when it marks real emphasis, disbelief, comic compression, frustration, or the point where respectable terminology has become less precise than blunt English.

It should not become wallpaper.

A well-placed “Oh, for fuck’s sake” can do more than three cute jokes.

### 3.9 Periodic compression: “And so”

Branchy stories need gathering points.

Periodic **And so:** passages are not over-explanation. They are navigation.

Use them to compress several pages of causal material into a fresh state vector before the story branches again.

Pattern:

> And so: by twenty-three I had badly remembered String Theory, very well remembered Minkowski, a paleontology degree underway, an emerging obsession with epistemology, and several notebooks full of things I was incorrectly calling strings.

Then continue.

### 3.10 The narrator is part of the causal account

For origin, history, method, biography, and public explanation, do not remove Nathan in the name of objectivity when his route is itself relevant evidence for how the project developed.

The human story is not proof that SAT/H(s)H is correct. It is provenance for how the work happened.

Public prose should distinguish those functions cleanly.

## 4. Surface temperature

The site-default voice is **not full Bent ’N’ Dent** and not a generic formal essay.

A useful composition model is:

- **Great Moments conversational Nathan** — narrative skeleton;
- **Styptic-Man technical perception** — intellectual musculature;
- **Bent ’N’ Dent metanarrative mischief and linguistic elasticity** — controlled doses;
- **nonfiction structural discipline** — category and status control;
- **peer-review discipline** — local fallback where scientific claims demand it.

The aim is not to mix every mode into every paragraph. It is to make the right machinery available when the paragraph’s job changes.

---

## 5. Sentence behavior

Typical successful rhythm:

1. concrete observation;
2. technical or conceptual recognition;
3. qualification or correction;
4. comic or bodily landing;
5. short boundary sentence;
6. continue.

Long accumulative sentences are welcome when they preserve simultaneously active relations. Short sentences should land boundaries.

Useful examples of landing temperature:

> Words mean things.

> Categories mean things.

> Put it back.

> Both.

> Not yet.

Do not manufacture clipped sentences everywhere. Their force depends on contrast.

## 6. Site-default negative model

Avoid:

- generic polished-AI uplift;
- writing every paragraph as an aphorism;
- joke-shaped jokes where the source absurdity was already enough;
- fake Nathan typos or transcription errors;
- excessive ellipses as decoration;
- surface imitation without category discipline;
- constant profanity;
- faux-humble self-effacement;
- pumping scientific claims for drama;
- turning every site section into memoir;
- rewriting raw/archive material into Nathan voice;
- treating a funny phrase as permission to weaken evidentiary labels;
- explaining every joke after it lands;
- cleaning away the tactile mess that makes a scientific scene real;
- replacing a precise odd word with a smoother generic one merely because an editor normally would.

## 7. Relationship to the other v8 models

This standard is the **public-site default wrapper**, not a replacement for the specialized models.

### Use `Nathan_Nonfiction_Communication_Standard_v8.0.md` when:

- the section’s primary job is status, governance, roadmap, reconstruction, methodology, provenance, or project instructions;
- clarity of state matters more than narrative presence;
- the prose must expose scope, categories, dependencies, and next actions.

### Use `Nathan_Peer_Review_Writing_Fingerprint_v8.0.md` when:

- the audience is primarily scientific specialists;
- the text states or evaluates technical claims, derivations, results, limitations, or formal mappings;
- a page is functioning as manuscript-like scientific exposition.

### Use `Nathan_Creative_Informal_Reference_v8.0.md` when:

- the page is explicitly creative, fictional, historical-personal, comic, or experimental;
- the prose is analyzing or borrowing a specific creative register;
- platform-native or highly playful narration is deliberately wanted.

### Use `Nathan_Voice_Identification_Provenance_Fingerprint_v8.0.md` when:

- deciding authorship, span attribution, source identity, or production cause.

**Do not use the identification fingerprint as a prose-generation style guide.**

## 8. Compact generation instruction

> **Write public-facing narrative nonfiction in Nathan McKnight’s site-default voice. Keep Nathan’s thinking visible without turning the prose into an impersonation. Combine technical precision, tactile scientific detail, structural humor, live self-correction, word-love, and stubborn category discipline. Let technical terms function as perception or inference rather than sidebar exposition. Allow profanity and coinage only when they earn real work. Preserve uncertainty at its exact address. Do not use false humility, hype, generic AI uplift, or joke-shaped filler. Let the route wander when useful, but become strict at contact: the object, geometry, source, chronology, and evidence get veto power. Use periodic “And so:” compressions to reorient long narratives. Prefer a concrete scene before its abstract rule. If a passage becomes claim-heavy or scientist-facing, fall back locally to the nonfiction or peer-review standards rather than forcing the site voice through it.**
